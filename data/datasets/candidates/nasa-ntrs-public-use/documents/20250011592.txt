<p>Background</p>
<p>Managing cryogens is a critical technology for NASA’s spaceflight missions</p>
<p>Cryogenic fluid management (CFM) challenges
Long term propellant storage
Pressure control for nominal operations
Propellant transfer in microgravity
Conditioning propellants with cryocoolers</p>
<p>NASA’s CFM Modeling Portfolio
Develop, enhance and validate computational fluid dynamic (CFD) models</p>
<p>Propulsion Fluid Dynamics Branch at NASA Marshall supports several projects with CFD analyses
Human Landing System (HLS), Space Launch System (SLS), Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS), etc.</p>
<p>Develop and validate Loci-Stream-VOF CFD solver to solve complex thermo-fluid and multi-physics problems for CFM
Cryogenic tank operations/phenomena: self-pressurization, autogenous pressurization, helium pressurization, mixing (depressurization with jets, sprays, etc.), venting, ullage collapse, tank fill/drain, liquefaction, etc.</p>
<p>Multi-purpose Hydrogen Test Bed (MHTB) Experiment[1]</p>
<p>[1] Hastings, L.J., Flachbart, R.H., Martin, J.J., Hedayat, A., Fazah, M., Lak, T., Nguyen, H., Bailey J.W., ‘Spray Bar Zero-Gravity Vent System for On-Orbit Liquid Hydrogen Storage”, NASA TM-212926, 2003</p>

<p>Self-pressurization Experiments and Computational Model</p>
<p>NASA MSFC Test Stand 300 – MHTB tank tests</p>
<p>Al 5083 tank of diameter and height 3.05 m with two 2:1 oblate hemispheres</p>
<p>Flight-scale – filled with liquid hydrogen (LH2)</p>
<p>Four self-pressurization tests selected for validation</p>
<p>Primary comparison metric: Average pressurization rate</p>
<p>[1] Hastings, L.J., Flachbart, R.H., Martin, J.J., Hedayat, A., Fazah, M., Lak, T., Nguyen, H., Bailey J.W., ‘Spray Bar Zero-Gravity Vent System for On-Orbit Liquid Hydrogen Storage”, NASA TM-212926, 2003</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide3_rId3"></div>
<p>[1]</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide3_rId4"></div>
<p>Computational Model</p>
<p>Two phase model: sharp interface method </p>
<p>Turbulence Model: IDDES (Hybrid RANS/LES closure) and RANS</p>
<p>Asymmetric interface turbulence transport modeling: Wall-like turbulence on gas side of the interface</p>
<p>Phase change: Gas kinetic theory-based Hertz-Knudsen-Schrage model</p>
<p>1-deg axisymmetric CFD Model with refined mesh</p>
<p>Pressure transience from the four self-pressurization tests </p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusion</p>
<p>Validated multiphase-flow modeling methodology for CFM Modeling Portfolio for MHTB self-pressurization using 50% fill level, 51 W heat-leak test.</p>
<p>25% and 90% fill-level tests predictions were unsatisfactory, which prompted a detailed investigation</p>
<p>Flow physics insights from spatial heat leak distribution study</p>
<p>Strong sensitivity to spatial distribution of heat leak rate on pressurization rate</p>
<p>Phasic heat distribution is skewed towards liquid due to liquid phase’s high specific heat and effective thermal conductivity (laminar + eddy conductivity) and high conductivity of the aluminum tank wall</p>
<p>Despite most of the heat distributed to the liquid phase, the small amount of heat rate absorbed by the ullage vapor dictates the self-pressurization rate</p>
<p>Future work</p>
<p>Investigate effect of heat leak distribution with higher fidelity modeling of tank features</p>
<p>The study provides detailed insights into self-pressurization mechanisms and, together with the heat leak sensitivity analysis technique, enables improvements in self-pressurization predictions for NASA’s spaceflight missions</p>
