Background (continued)
Design techniques to control propellant locations within tanks is commonly requested.

Organizations are often interested in solutions for standard tank designs.
Excludes various CFM technologies (many need flight experience to reduce risk of use)
Excludes propellant management devices (can be heavy for large tanks)

Typical propellant management needs include:
Vapor only venting
Unsubmerged ullage gas diffuser for pressurization
Propellant only extraction for engine start or propellant transfer
Propellant settled to mitigate ullage collapse and boiloff

Performing in-space vehicle maneuvers is one way to manage propellants with standard tank designs.

A variety of settling and unsettling events were simulated with computational fluid dynamics (CFD) to address some typical propellant management needs for a generic tank; results are presented herein.



4
Simulation Methodology
Used Loci/STREAM-VoF for every simulation
Pressure based Navier-Stokes solver
Volume of fluid (VoF) method of distinct phase representation
Used a generic 2 m diameter tank with hemispherical domes
2 m tall barrel section
~4 million unstructured hexahedral volume cells
Used a non-inertial reference frame to specify vehicle dynamics
Inputs varied by simulation
Simulated LOX and GOX at different conditions
Saturated:
1 bar ullage pressure
Unsaturated:
4 bar ullage pressure
90 K minimum liquid temperature
250 K maximum ullage temperature
Initially stratified around the gas-liquid interface
Used an implicit Hertz-Knudsen model for phase change
Evaporation and condensation at gas-liquid interfaces
Accommodation coefficient of 0.001 for every simulation

Effects of Roll on Settling Efficiency
Liquid CG transients shown below demonstrate the time to reach nearly periodic slosh motion.
Settling efficiency drops sharply beyond this point due to low slosh damping.
Rolling a vehicle prior to settling can distribute liquid circumferentially and reduce lateral slosh motion.
Long duration rolling to achieve a uniform distribution of liquid circumferentially results in symmetric slosh motion which persists longer.
This effect is not well illustrated in the plotted metrics.

Propellant Dynamics
Settling was performed for 2 tank configurations.
The anti-slosh baffle aided propellant breakup during initial large amplitude lateral sloshing.
This behavior will enhance ullage collapse.

Effects of Baffles on Settling Efficiency
Liquid CG transients shown below demonstrate the time to reach nearly periodic slosh motion.
High damping was maintained across the wave amplitude space in the baffled tank enabling more rapid and complete settling than in the bare tank.
Halting settling during slosh motion will allow the propellant to unsettle again due to residual liquid momentum.
Having some structure like a baffle above the liquid once settling is halted gives the liquid a place to pin to via surface tension.
High liquid momentum can overpower surface tension though so the final settled state must be chosen carefully.

Ullage Collapse during Slewing
Various transient metrics quantifying ullage collapse are shown in the plots.

Significant mixing occurred when purely rotating.
Ullage temperature and pressure drop
Initial condensation as the bulk liquid warmed from subcooled to saturated
Trailing evaporation as ullage pressure approaches complete collapse and liquid becomes saturated

Little mixing and thus ullage collapse occurred when including translation.

18
Propellant Dynamics after Engine Cutoff
Slosh motion prior to engine cutoff facilitated unsettling as liquid momentum is unbounded without vehicle acceleration.
Delaying settling allowed propellant to mix with the hot ullage gas through initial unsettling and the following high amplitude sloshing.
Settling before propellant moves away from its initial state prevents most mixing.

19
Ullage Collapse after Engine Cutoff
Various transient metrics quantifying ullage collapse are shown in the plots.

Without settling after engine cutoff, complete ullage collapse was approached.

Delayed settling mirrored the initial ullage collapse observed without settling but retained excess ullage pressure once mixing was suppressed as expected.

Ullage collapse was significantly mitigated when settling was started promptly following engine cutoff.




20
Conclusion
Propellant dynamics during in-space vehicle maneuvers were investigated with CFD analysis.
Objective was to identify strategies for addressing CFM using vehicle maneuvers.

Bulk propellant dynamics were found to be insensitive to settling acceleration magnitude.
Dependent upon tank configuration and initial propellant distribution
Settling costs reduce as average acceleration reduces, but maneuver duration increases.

Rolling a vehicle prior to settling can distribute liquid circumferentially and reduce settling maneuver duration.

Anti-slosh baffles can significantly reduce settling requirements but inherently aid ullage collapse when unsettling.

Slew maneuvers can be designed to translate the vehicle during rotation to avoid unsettling.
Settling should be done as soon as possible following engine cutoff to avoid unsettling.
