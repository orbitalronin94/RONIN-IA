<p>Intro to ECLSS
• Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) – everything it takes to keep 
</p>
<p>crew members alive on the International Space Station (ISS)
• Atmosphere Control – making sure the cabin air has the right mixture of oxygen &amp; nitrogen, 
</p>
<p>without too much carbon dioxide or other trace contaminants
• Pressure control
• Temperature and humidity control
• Carbon dioxide (CO2) removal
• Oxygen (O2) production
• Trace contaminant control
• Air monitoring
</p>
<p>• Water Recovery – recycling condensation and urine to provide drinking water for crew and 
water for O2 generation
</p>
<p>• Condensate collection from cabin humidity
• Waste collection
• Urine processing
• Condensate/distillate processing
• Brine processing
• Potable water dispensing
</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>History of ECLSS on ISS
• Early ECLSS consisted of consumable systems
</p>
<p>• LiOH cans removed CO2
• O2 and Nitrogen flown in Airlock tanks, replenished via transfer from space shuttle
• Water flown in bags, replenished via space shuttle fuel cell generation
• Hand-held sensors
</p>
<p>• More regenerable systems arrived over the course of multiple ISS assembly missions
• Atmosphere Revitalization (AR) rack – brought Carbon Dioxide Removal Assembly (CDRA), Major 
</p>
<p>Constituent Analyzer (MCA), Trace Contaminant Control System (TCCS) – 2001
• Oxygen Generation System (OGS) Rack - 2007
• Water Recovery System (WRS) Racks – Urine Processor Assembly (UPA) and Water Processor Assembly 
</p>
<p>(WPA) – 2008
• Systems were improved-upon and redesigned as necessary when issues were 
</p>
<p>identified
• In 2015 – Exploration Capabilities, now under Mars Capability Office (MCO), initiated 
</p>
<p>effort to demonstrate Exploration-Class ECLSS via primarily the upgrade of existing, 
core systems
</p>
<p>• MCO funds the ISS Program Vehicle Office/OB to initiate and integrate these technology demonstrations (tech 
demos)
</p>
<p>• Parallel efforts for technology development also funded by MCO – fly as payloads
</p>

<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>ISS Demonstration Approach
• Develop a testbed that mimics exploration spacecraft ECLSS as much as possible
</p>
<p>• ISS architecture presents limitations such as rack layout, 120VDC power, MIL-STD-1553 
command and data handling systems, varying crew size
• Arcturus Data System developed and in operation to provide alternative for data 
</p>
<p>connectivity
• Internal cooling – limited capacity for large new users, especially given Commercial Element 
</p>
<p>work
• Objective is to mature performance and reliability predictions of the core system components 
</p>
<p>(e.g. Urine Processor Assembly (UPA) Distillation Assembly or Oxygen Generation Assembly 
(OGA) Cell Stack) and enable repackaging/layout of the systems when actual mission profiles 
and spacecraft designs are established
</p>
<p>• Close key technology gaps
• For ease of integration into ISS, overall system is divided into two strings:
</p>
<p>• Air String
• Water String
</p>
<p>• Environmental Monitors are installed as their functions dictate, such as water 
monitoring devices inline with the WPA
</p>

<p>Air String Upgrades
• Condensing Heat Exchanger (CHX)
</p>
<p>• Part of the Common Cabin Air Assembly (CCAA) – which performs cabin air temp and humidity control, 
collecting condensate for processing
</p>
<p>• ISS Legacy CHX will be upgraded to incorporate a modified hydrophilic coating with improved properties for 
microbial control, siloxane resistance, and overall lifetime
</p>
<p>• Current coating starts to degrade after 2-4 years – weldment rejuvenation has been accomplished but it is suspected 
that particulate liberates and contributes to Liquid Check Valve clogging – most common cause of Water Separator ORU 
failure
</p>
<p>• It took ~10 years of integrated ISS ops to identify that siloxanes in the cabin atmosphere react to form dimethylsilanediol
(DMSD) which impacts WPA Cat Reactor life.  One mechanism of DMSD production is specifically via condensation on 
the existing CHX coating.1,2
</p>
<p>• Goal to enable longer periods between CHX dryouts or eliminate need altogether (CHX dry-out drives 
temperature changes on ISS to collect condensate elsewhere – not feasible in a small spacecraft)
</p>
<p>• Build will make use of additive manufacturing – reduces time, complexity, and cost of future builds 
• Project: NASA direct contract to Collins for CHX, Boeing contract for integration into CCAA with delivery in 
</p>
<p>2025
• Water Separator – sends condensate collected by the CHX into the wastewater bus for 
</p>
<p>processing
• Technology will remain with current state-of-the-art (SOA) rotary separator that pairs well with hydrophilic 
</p>
<p>CHX.
• Trace Contaminant Control System (TCCS) – legacy system is considered SOA
</p>
<p>• Additional filtering (charcoal) added to the inlet of the CHX to remove volatile siloxanes from cabin 
atmosphere
</p>
<p>• Performance is still being evaluated – condensate sample return did confirm overall reduction in atmospheric 
and condensate-based siloxane levels
</p>
<p>• Appear to be at a steady-state DMSD level now which has been manageable
• Some ground testing planned for catalyst obsolescence – future system would likely be integrated into cabin 
</p>
<p>airflow differently (in-line with CCAA vs. independent)
7</p>

<p>Air String Upgrades
</p>
<p>8
</p>
<p>• Carbon Dioxide Removal (CO2) System – Carbon Dioxide Removal 
Assembly (CDRA)
</p>
<p>• Historical Problems
• Bed containment issues that resulted in Zeolite dusting from 
</p>
<p>Adsorption/Desorption Beds, caused clogging of Air Selector Valves and 
Check Valves, and drove the need for frequent changeouts
</p>
<p>• Temp sensor failures
• Heater sheet shorting after years of ops 
• Honeywell blower obsolete
• CO2 moisture introduction into Sabatier compressor which led to 
</p>
<p>compressor/accumulator failure
• CDRA concentrated ISS trace contaminants which poisoned Sabatier reactor 
</p>
<p>catalyst
</p>
<p>• New technology/system required for robust, reliable CO2 removal sized 
for 4-crew at 2mmHg inlet ppCO2 with similar or less mass, power, 
volume compared to CDRA (close technology gap)</p>
<p></p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>• Carbon Dioxide Removal (CO2) System – Four Bed CO2 (FBCO2) Scrubber
• Logical evolution of CDRA
</p>
<p>• Upgraded beds
• More robust air valves
• Improved heater design
• Calnetix Blower installed 2/13 – replaces Honeywell blower and sized to improve performance 
</p>
<p>to objective 4-crew removal at 2mmHg inlet ppCO2
</p>
<p>• Developed by MSFC ECLSS - began operating Sep 2021 – 13 months cumulative on-
orbit runtime
</p>
<p>• Failure History
• UA Honeywell Blower/Commercial Off the Shelf (COTS) Celeroton Blower Controller comm. 
</p>
<p>issues cause intermittent blower speed faults and FBCO2 shutdown. Nuisance for flight control 
team
</p>
<p>• Non op periods
• Shared Vacuum Exhaust System with other payloads that needed access
• Basic Express Rack firmware controller issues
• FBCO2 Arcturus comm system issues
</p>
<p>• Calnetix blower installation occurred 2/13 with activation 2/15
• Acoustics blanket brought levels to within acceptable limit
• Performance assessment pending resolution of software issues
</p>
<p>• New fluid/electrical jumpers and software required to integrate with air string in Lab (i.e. 
connect to other systems)

<p>Air String Updates (cont’d)
• CO2 Reduction System 
</p>
<p>• Sabatier operated on ISS until Oct 2017 (~2 years runtime), was returned following 
reactor failure for upgrade and demonstration
</p>
<p>• Root cause determined to be catalyst poisoning from contaminants that were concentrated from ISS 
atmosphere in CDRA and the Oxygen Generation Assembly
</p>
<p>• Liquid water also passed through to CO2 accumulators and caused corrosion (condensation in 
compressor)
</p>
<p>• Sabatier 2.0 will provide better protection for the reactor and allow for its 
maintenance, and will interface with new CO2 accumulator tanks
</p>
<p>• Addition of a getter which removes poisons from mixed CO2 /H2 input stream to Sabatier reactor
• Reactor position within system is relocated for on-orbit replacement
• Addition of humidity sensors to detect moisture and safe Sabatier
</p>
<p>• Sabatier 2.0 Preliminary Design Review (PDR) occurred 11/1, delivery 2025
• ISS can be ready to support additional CO2 Reduction technologies if they mature 
</p>
<p>sufficiently in time for flight demonstration
• Methane Reduction takes Sabatier waste product methane and recovers additional H2 which can be 
</p>
<p>fed back into Sabatier to improve efficiency (waste product is carbon)
• Technologies currently in development need both footprint and relatively high levels of power and 
</p>
<p>heat rejection
</p>

<p>Air String Updates (cont’d)
• O2 Generation System: Advanced Oxygen Generation Assembly (AOGA) 
</p>
<p>• Failure history:
• Pump lock-ups due to material incompatibility (one bearing issue corrected, 
</p>
<p>another being addressed currently with Niflor coating in Quick Disconnect 
connectors)
</p>
<p>• Cell Stack failure due to fluoride generation reducing pH in recirculation loop 
– addition of activated carbon filter in recirculation loop addressed this
</p>
<p>• Back Pressure Regulator (BPR) failure over time – component within H2
Orbital Replacement Unit (ORU)
</p>
<p>• Cell stack failure due to material thinning/shorts
• AOGA Objectives:
</p>
<p>• Utilize current industry standard cell stack design features: chemically 
stabilized Nafion membranes with cerium doping for reduced material loss 
through electrolysis, sintered plate membrane supports (rather than fine 
mesh screens that allow material creep over time which causes shorts)
</p>
<p>• Redesign for maintainability
• Change from a single sealed dome that houses the cell stack and Rotary Separator 
</p>
<p>Accumulator to two, maintainable domes
• Crew ability to replace components inside domes will be demonstrated – key 
</p>
<p>improvement for mass/volume reduction
• Dome seal design is being finalized after much technical discussion and additional 
</p>
<p>requirement

<p>Water String Upgrades
• Urine Processing System
</p>
<p>• Urine Processor Assembly (UPA) is being upgraded to correct design 
weaknesses and improve maintainability
</p>
<p>• Improvements have been incremental throughout operational life 
• Upgraded bearing material in Distillation Assembly (DA)
• Changed from a disposable brine tank, Recycle Tank Filter Assembly (RFTA) to a reusable, 
</p>
<p>advanced version (ARFTA) with fill/drain valve external to the system for ease of crew use
• Recovery from precipitation event caused by excessive sulfates in the brine loop and DA 
</p>
<p>resulted in the change from legacy sulfuric acid pretreat to phosphoric/chromic acid ‘alternate’ 
pretreat
</p>
<p>• UPA operations on ISS advanced NASA’s understanding of metabolic changes in crew (i.e. significantly 
higher levels of calcium and sulfates in urine) as well as contributed to a focus on better hydration
</p>
<p>• Accepted % recovery of pre-treated urine (PTU) was increased slowly over time in order to 
minimize risk of precipitation (involved continuous analysis of returned brine filters)
</p>
<p>• Fluids pump and purge pump were both modified to changed to a planetary gear design to 
improve design life
</p>
<p>• Purge pump modified to reduce squeeze on the tubing
• Upgraded DA installed on ISS in 2020 – system runtime has surpassed all other 
</p>
<p>DA units (7234 hrs as of 6/13 which is 165% DA life)
• DA eliminated several failure modes including belt slippage via use of a toothed gear belt and 
</p>
<p>fluid leak path through bearings
16

<p>Water String Upgrades
• Urine Processing System (cont’d)
</p>
<p>• Purge Pump and Separator Assembly (PPSA) - smaller, more efficient, maintainable at a 
lower level and potentially more reliable
</p>
<p>• Installation on-orbit planned for end of June
• PPSA combines legacy purge pump and separator filter ORUs into footprint of purge pump
</p>
<p>• ARFTA Quantity Insight – crew inspection require to ensure adequate fill/drain of ARFTA at 
start and end of concentration cycles
</p>
<p>• Sensor reduces crew time and enables automated concentration cycle
• Ground demonstration in development
</p>
<p>• Fluids Pump – legacy design is a 3-channel pump used to move different fluids through 
UPA and driven by a single motor
</p>
<p>• New design separates channels into independent pumps 
• More efficient design, independent control (advantageous for dormancy) and replaceable/repairable (reduces 
</p>
<p>mass/volume of spares)
• Ground prototype funded with flight demo unfunded to date
</p>
<p>• Joint team: MSFC, JSC and ARC established to implement recommendations from a study identifying 
areas that would benefit from additional failure insight – initial goal is ground demonstration of 
automation capabilities for exploration (late 2023)
</p>

<p>Water String Upgrades
• Water Processing System 
</p>
<p>• Water Processing Assembly (WPA) on ISS upgraded to address key reliability issues 
and optimize based on lessons learned
</p>
<p>• Prevalence of siloxanes, their conversion to DMSD, and subsequent impact on Cat Reactor life was 
unknown prior to extended operations on ISS
</p>
<p>• Addition of charcoal beds upstream of CHX reduced siloxanes in the condensate and reduced Cat 
Reactor life
</p>
<p>• Biofilm growth in the Wastewater Tank clogged a valve downstream – led to an operational control 
for biofilm growth in the tank via regular cycling of the full bellows travel, and installation of a filter 
downstream (External Filter Assembly originally intended just for new Cat Reactor flushing)
</p>
<p>• Multifiltration Bed (MF Bed) config reduced from two beds to one
• Improves system ability to withstand siloxanes in condensate – how 
</p>
<p>contaminants collect and are pushed off beds was something learned over 
time (“breakthrough cycles”)
</p>
<p>• Better posture for dormancy (less hardware to replace post-dormancy)
• MF Bed redesigned for improved sorbent material (performance) and extend 
</p>
<p>operational life – first installed Oct 2020
18</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Water String Upgrades
• Water Processing System (cont’d) 
</p>
<p>• Catalytic Reactor (Cat Reactor) – Demo Catalytic Reactor ORU will address 3 primary 
improvements
</p>
<p>• New Catalyst: a new catalyst developed under NASA funding. Ground tests at MSFC showed this 
catalyst will improve oxidation efficiency, and specifically improve oxidation of DMSD
</p>
<p>• Metal Seals: new metal seals will replace the existing soft seals that lead to failure
• Every Catalytic Reactor ORU has failed due to external leakage from degraded seals
</p>
<p>• O2 Flow Rate Adjustment: testing has shown increased O2 flow will improve oxidation efficiency
• Additionally, the O2 Filter ORU will be modified to remove the filter element, not needed and eliminates a 
</p>
<p>consumable
</p>

<p>Conclusion
• ISS Vehicle is being fully utilized as platform for demonstrating Exploration ECLSS 
</p>
<p>technology
• Continually identifying system failure modes and correcting designs
• Increasing understanding of system reliability for successful/efficient spares planning
</p>
<p>• Many challenges related to these demonstrations have already been addressed
• Largest areas of forward work
</p>
<p>• Completing Integrated Air String
• Developing Integrated Ground Test Bed
• Integrating methane reduction if tech matures sufficiently for ISS flight demo
</p>
<p>ECLSS Capability Advancements on ISS continue – we still have much to learn
• Discussions ongoing to ensure we are utilizing ISS fully and reducing risk for near-term NASA 
</p>
<p>Programs (Orion, Gateway, Commercial Leo Destination Program) in addition to Exploration 
missions
</p>
<p>• Need to incorporate ECLSS lessons learned to avoid reinventing the wheel
• Crew survival means minimizing risks that may be imposed by procurement or partnership 
</p>
<p>strategies
• Drafting a list of recommended NASA standard interfaces/components for better compatibility 
</p>
