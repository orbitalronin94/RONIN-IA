<p>STMD Monthly </p>
<p>Space Technology 
Research Grants Program</p>
<p>NASA Space Technology Graduate Research Opportunities (NSTGRO)  - ~$84k per year, ~2 to 4 years</p>
<p>Graduate student research in space technology; research conducted on campuses and at NASA Centers and not-for-profit R&amp;D labs</p>
<p>Early Career Faculty (ECF) - ~$200k per year, up to 3 years</p>
<p>Focused on supporting outstanding faculty researchers early in their careers as they conduct space technology research of high priority to NASA’s Mission Directorates </p>
<p>Early Stage Innovations (ESI) - up to $250k per year – NTE $650k total, up to 3 years</p>
<p>University-led, possibly multiple investigator, efforts on early-stage space technology research of high priority to NASA’s Mission Directorates </p>
<p>Paid teaming with other universities, industry, and non-profits permitted</p>
<p>Lunar Surface Technology Research (LuSTR) Opportunities - up to $2M total, up to 2 years</p>
<p>University-led efforts addressing high priority lunar surface challenges</p>
<p>Short duration, high value grants with emphasis on technology development and  potential infusion</p>
<p>Paid teaming with other universities, industry, and non-profits encouraged</p>
<p>Space Technology Research Institutes (STRI)  - up to $3M per year, planned for 5 years</p>
<p>University-led, integrated, multidisciplinary teams focused on high-priority early-stage space technology research for several years</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Engage Academia: tap into spectrum of academic researchers, from graduate students to senior faculty members, to examine the theoretical feasibility of ideas and approaches that are critical to making science, space travel, and exploration more effective, affordable, and sustainable. </p>
<p>Accelerate development of groundbreaking high-risk/high-payoff low-TRL space technologies</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>ACCESS STRI: Motivations</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide5_rId2"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide5_rId3"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide5_rId4"></div>
<p>Missions like operational crewed Moon/Mars exploration and Mars sample return demand an ability to quantify entry system reliability prior to flight</p>
<p>Key Needs:</p>
<p>Risk and performance based architecture trades</p>
<p>Establish system reliability for flight certification</p>
<p>Bridge ground-to-flight testing gap; reduce system risk</p>
<p>Support sustainability and block upgrades</p>
<p>Mars Sample Return</p>
<p>Human Mars Exploration</p>
<p>Artemis</p>
<p>Topic designed to complement and supplement in-house research, leveraging an academic base created from NASA &amp; DOD entry system funding. Institute products address multiple EDL gaps and are directly relevant to the “Land 20 Tons” and “EDL to Enable Planetary Science” Envisioned Future Priorities. </p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>ACCESS STRI: Intended Scope</p>
<p>Objective: Fundamentally advance the SoA in modeling and simulation of entry systems for future NASA missions; emphasis on tools to predict system response to off-nominal events of critical importance.</p>
<p>Required Elements:</p>
<p>Detailed modeling of kinetic rates and physical processes that govern key chemical kinetic, material behavior, and fluid dynamic phenomena. [295, 296, 324, 325]</p>
<p>Tightly-coupled multi-disciplinary analysis of aerodynamics, aerothermodynamics, shock layer radiation, material response, and thermostructural behavior. [329]</p>
<p>High-fidelity modeling of thermal protection material damage initiation/propagation, and failure mode identification. [295, 296, 1440]</p>
<p>Rigorous techniques for uncertainty quantification and established methodology to make physics-based reliability estimates for NASA entry system concepts. [323]</p>
<p>Other Considerations:</p>
<p>Model validation is key across all objectives</p>
<p>Effective solution will likely require emerging heterogeneous Peta- and Exascale computing technology [330]</p>
<p>[xx]:  EDL&amp;PL Gap Addressed</p>
<p>ACCESS is NASA’s Primary investment in 323 and 329 at this time</p>
