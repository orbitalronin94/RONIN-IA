<p>3
</p>
<p>• Windows Material Database
– A database that provides flight relevant data to designers, allowing for quick trade studies 
</p>
<p>between transparent materials.
• The database contains 4 types of materials: Acrylics, Polycarbonates, Glasses, and Ceramics (various 
</p>
<p>brands), and each is tested using the same method.
– Acrylic (AC) brands
</p>
<p>» GKN Cast, GKN Stretched, Plexiglass, Poly II, SAR (coated)
– Polycarbonate (PC) brands
</p>
<p>» Lexan 9034, Lexan MR10 (coated), Makrolon (coated, also now Tuffak), &amp; Zelux
– Glasses and Ceramic Brands
</p>
<p>» Corning – Fused Silica (FS), Tosoh – Fused Quartz (FQ), &amp; Surmet - ALON
– Each material is taken through a suite of material and environment testing
</p>
<p>• Environments: Flammability, Atomic Oxygen, Hypervelocity Impact, Thermal Vacuum, and Radiation 
&amp; UV
</p>
<p>• Testing: Optical, Creep, Strength, and Thermal Property
– Materials are tested optically before and after exposure to determine if there was a degradation in optical quality 
</p>
<p>after exposure. Then, the materials were tested for strength after exposure.
</p>

<p>Glass &amp; Ceramics vs. Polymers
</p>
<p>Shot 2 – Stainless Steel @ 5 km/sShot 1 – Soda Lime @ 5 km/s
</p>
<p>• The geometry of the defect for 
glasses and ceramics is unique 
with each shot.
</p>
<p>• This led to a 35-shot matrix being 
used to cover any potential 
difference with material size, 
density, and speed. 
</p>
<p>• Polymers do not have a unique 
response to HVI and are not as 
notch sensitive as glasses and 
ceramics
</p>
<p>• A modified 12-shot matrix was 
used to cover different material 
sizes, density, and speeds.
</p>
<p>• Shots chosen: # 2, 4, 5, 9, 12, 13, 
17, 19, 21, 26, 32, &amp; 33</p>
