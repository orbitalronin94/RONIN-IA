<p>NASA Space Nuclear Propulsion (SNP) 
Overview
</p>
<p>Vision
Robust and enduring access to destinations throughout the solar system
</p>
<p>Mission
Revolutionize space travel through the development and demonstration of robust and high 
performing nuclear propulsion systems
</p>
<p>Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP)
Fission is a thermal power source, the reactor acts as a heat 
exchanger for high efficiency, high thrust applications (long or 
short duration missions)
</p>
<p>Nuclear Electric Propulsion (NEP)
Fission is a thermal power source which is converted to 
</p>
<p>electricity to power electric propulsion thrusters to enable fast 
transit to far away destinations</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>1. Develop nuclear propulsion capabilities that can be directly leveraged or readily evolved to meet the 
Agency’s future missions, including human exploration of Mars and deep space science
</p>
<p>2. Mature the enabling technologies and demonstrate the engineering performance of integrated nuclear 
propulsion systems in relevant operational environments
</p>
<p>3. Collaborate with other government organizations, industry, and academia to support the development 
and commercial infusion of nuclear propulsion capabilities and supporting infrastructure
</p>

<p>DRACO Flight Demonstrator Background
DARPA/NASA Partnership
</p>
<p>DRACO program is a partnership between NASA and 
DARPA to design, build, launch, and demonstrate the 
nation’s first in-space Nuclear Thermal Rocket Engine 
(NTRE)
</p>
<p>NASA responsible for managing the design, development, 
fabrication, testing of the NTRE
</p>
<p>DARPA responsible for managing the design and 
development of the integrated flight vehicle, in-space 
flight operations, and all nuclear launch safety and 
approval processes
</p>
<p>US Space Force procuring the Vulcan/Centaur for March 
2027 Launch as part of FY24 two-year task order
</p>
<p>Nuclear Thermal 
Rocket Engine
</p>
<p>Flight System comprised of 3 primary modules
</p>
<p>Cryogenic Tank (LH2)
Spacecraft Bus
</p>
<p>DRACO Objective: 
Demonstrate a high-assay low enriched uranium (HALEU) Nuclear Thermal Rocket Engine in space in FY27</p>
<p></p>

</p>
<p>• Pathfinder for interagency regulatory basis for launching and operating a fission-
based systems in space
</p>
<p>• An informed understanding of the technology gaps that need to be addressed to 
develop future operational flight systems</p>

<p>Draco Mission Overview
</p>
<p>Nuclear Launch Safety
• DRACO vehicle is the launch vehicle payload
• Reactor includes inhibits to prevent turning on reactor
• Reactor activated after successful orbit insertion
</p>
<p>ODMSP compliant (2000 km)
</p>
<p>DRACO Vehicle
- Low thrust for demo 
</p>
<p>(preserves LH2)
- Capable of high Isp but 
</p>
<p>allowed to test at low Isp
</p>
<p>Objective of mission is to collect data 
on novel HALEU NTR behavior</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>DRACO Flight Demonstrator
High Assay Low Enriched Uranium (HALEU) fuel
Modular core design scalable to higher thrust for Mars 
Reactor core operating temperature &gt;2700K
4+ hour engine life with repeatable starts and stops 
Mission duration limited by cryogenic propellent supply
</p>
<p>Human Lunar Lander/Mars Transportation Study (2023)
Fuel: High Assay Low Enriched Uranium (HALEU)
Thrust per engine: 15000-25000 lbf
Chamber Temperature: 2700-2800K 
Engine Life: 4 hrs at rated thrust performance
Cryo Management: Active, Near-Zero Boiloff
</p>
<p>Demonstration Reactor Mars Mission Reactor
</p>
<p>Extensibility
• Core power &amp; size increases by adding 
</p>
<p>fuel elements; fuel element &amp; moderator 
block design remains the same
</p>
<p>• Increase number of control drums; 
control drum design remains the same
</p>
