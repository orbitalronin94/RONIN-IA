</p>
<p>• The highest CO2 emissions were in Southern Alabama, which is consistent with 
</p>
<p>pine plantations being harvested in that area.

<p>Team Members
</p>
<p>The balancing of  atmospheric carbon dioxide (CO2) sources and sinks is 
</p>
<p>fundamental to curbing climate change. Forests draw CO2 from the atmosphere and 
</p>
<p>accumulate carbon in tree biomass and soil over time, but forest loss releases the 
</p>
<p>carbon stored in aboveground biomass (AGB) back to the atmosphere. This project 
</p>
<p>assessed the feasibility of  using different earth observations to quantify CO2
</p>
<p>emissions from forest cover loss across Alabama and Tennessee. The team 
</p>
<p>generated four maps of  stable forest and forest cover loss from 2016 through either 
</p>
<p>2019, 2021, or 2022, using data from Landsat 5 Thematic Mapper, Landsat 7 
</p>
<p>Enhanced Thematic Mapper Plus, and Landsat 8 Operational Land Imager, and 
</p>
<p>open-source land cover datasets, and validated the maps using Sentinel-2 satellite 
</p>
<p>imagery. The Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation (GEDI) and Advanced 
</p>
<p>Topographic Laser Altimeter System satellite light detection and ranging instruments 
</p>
<p>measured tree canopy height, yielding AGB density estimates. The team calculated 
</p>
<p>average annual CO2 emissions from forest cover loss, based on forest loss area and 
</p>
<p>average AGB density of  stable forest per county. Validation showed that emissions 
</p>
<p>calculated using the National Land Change Dataset for forest cover loss and GEDI 
</p>
<p>for AGB density were most accurate. The team’s partners — the Land Trust of  
</p>
<p>North Alabama, Alabama Forestry Commission, American Forest Foundation, 
</p>
<p>Tennessee Department of  Air Pollution Control, and Tennessee Division of  Water 
</p>
<p>Resources — can compare these remote sensing results against existing ground-
</p>
<p>based observations to help inform where to focus forest management resources to 
</p>
<p>minimize CO2 emissions from forest loss.
