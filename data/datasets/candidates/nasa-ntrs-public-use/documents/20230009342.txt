<p>Background &amp; Introduction
• Liquid rocket engines generally require turbopumps to provide necessary pressure 
</p>
<p>head to fuel and oxidizer propellants.
• Distortions, cavitation, &amp; other unsteadiness in fluid flow field generate harmonic 
</p>
<p>and narrow-band excitation onto all flow path structural components 
‒ Structural Dynamic Analysis required to assess response for both High Cycle Fatigue 
</p>
<p>and Ultimate Failure.
• Many uncertainties in natural frequencies of 
</p>
<p>Pump-Side Components (Inducers and 
Impellers) including effects of:
1. Young’s Modulus during operation in cryogenic 
</p>
<p>propellant (-423°F); 
2. Fluid-added mass;
3. Components operate with a tight tip clearance, 
</p>
<p>which alters mass affects;
4. Compressibility/interaction with acoustic 
</p>
<p>modes.
• Inability to measure natural frequencies during 
</p>
<p>operation requires verified analytical capabilities
</p>
<p>Typical Turbopump</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Motivation
</p>
<p>• Inducer in Low Pressure Fuel (Liquid Hydrogen, LH2) Pump in RS25 
Engine for Space Launch System (SLS) predicted to operate at 
resonance with Higher-Order Cavitation (HOC) excitation driver.
</p>
<p>• RS25 essentially same engine as well-tested Space Shuttle Main 
Engine, but operational conditions puts HOC more into range.
</p>
<p>• Extensive response analysis performed, but due to uncertainties, 
analysis predicted failure even for SSME operation, which was 
clearly not accurate. 
</p>
<p>• Empirical, non-physics-based damage-fraction analysis therefore 
performed based upon one of the tested units, and acceptance 
criteria stemming from this analysis were imposed upon the 
inducer and the operation of the turbopump. 
</p>
<p>• Some of resulting speed and operational restrictions are quite 
undesirable, though, and possibly could be severe if testing shows 
the magnitude of HOC are greater than expected. </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Comprehensive Inducer Test/Analysis Program
</p>
<p>• Team is completing integrated test/analysis program to quantify uncertainties.
• Natural frequency testing of sub-scale water-flow test inducer in LH2 using 
</p>
<p>unique facilities of MSFC’s Cryogenic Test Laboratory.
• Phase I completed 2017
</p>
<p>–Cantilever Beam 
–Same Titanium alloy as RS-25 Inducer.
‒ Simple geometry allowed high-fidelity, accurate modeling &amp; comparison to 
</p>
<p>academic methods for precise correlation.
• Phase II-A test completed 2018, analysis 2019-2020.
</p>
<p>–Cantilever beam with walls close to longitudinal edges to match experiment 
in  literature.
</p>
<p>–Sub-Scale Stainless Steel Inducer in water with “medium” tight tip clearance.
–Sub-Scale Stainless Steel Inducer in LH2, without tight tip clearance.
</p>
<p>• Test of Inducer in LH2 with tight tip clearance in planning stages.
• Authors have not found any documentation of modal testing in LH2 in the 
</p>
<p>literature.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Effects of Tight Tip Clearance in Literature
</p>
<p>• Some useful background work published by Harrison (’07) using micro-
cantilever beams, and analytical techniques by Green &amp; Sader (’97).
</p>
<p>• Most important publication used here is Xiu &amp; Davis ’18.
– Analytical technique and experiment conducted on beams fixed on both short 
</p>
<p>and long sides, with varying clearance from a wall along long edge.
– Fixed along longitudinal wall is analogous to inducer blade.
– Results include detailed curves of fluid-added mass as a function of a non-
</p>
<p>dimensionalized boundary distance. 
– Although not quite analogous, test at MSFC performed with beam fixed on short 
</p>
<p>edge to provide methodology validation.
• Recently authors discovered comprehensive review by Dehkharquani ’18 of 
</p>
<p>significant amount of work in the last 2 years focusing on water turbines, and 
test/analysis program by DeLaTorre, ’16.
– Will provide validation for this effort.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Effects of Compressibility in Literature
• Davis, et al, ’08, investigated 
</p>
<p>acoustic/structure system modes that 
exist when acoustic and structure 
modes of the same nodal diameter also 
have close the same frequency 
(eigenvalue veering).
</p>
<p>• The independent acoustic and structure 
modes are replaced by system modes
with both structural and acoustic 
response, and which are below and 
above the crossing of the structural &amp; 
acoustic-only lines.
</p>
<p>• This means that tracking of modes of 
interest via both Modal Assurance 
Criteria and visual animation is critical.
</p>
<p>Used by permission, Davis, 
Virgin, Brown ‘07</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Cantilever Beam Tight Tip-Clearance Modal Test &amp; Analysis
</p>
<p>• Phase II-A testing performed to quantify 
the effect of tight-tip clearance on the 
fluid-added mass, and in turn the natural 
frequency
– Two removable and adjustable side plates 
</p>
<p>added to the cantilever beam ping-test 
setup described in Phase I. 
</p>
<p>– Purpose to validate theoretical predictions 
and optimize meshing techniques. 
</p>
<p>• Water, air, and liquid Nitrogen test to 
provide another temperature data point 
for the Young’s Modulus versus 
Temperature relationship discussed in 
Phase I.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Acoustic Modal Analysis of Cant Beam tight clearance
</p>
<p>• ANSYS modal acoustic models were generated for the tight 
clearance test configurations
– Due to tight fluid clearances surrounding the beam, fluid volumes 
</p>
<p>were sliced as needed to better control mesh densities throughout the 
volume.  
</p>
<p>– General practice was to ensure at least two elements through the tight 
fluid clearance.  
</p>
<p>– Testing included 2 possible clearances (.06” and .02”) on either side, 
each combination of these modeled.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Tight-Tip-Clearance Cantilever Beam Results
</p>
<p>• Focusing on the first cantilever mode since it is most similar to the 
inducer mode of interest, results are excellent, with the numerical 
results matching test generally within 3%.   
</p>
<p>• 2-sided, .020” clearance on each side.
</p>

<p>Sub-Scale Inducer Modal Test/Analysis in Water
• To enable examination of realistic geometry, testing initiated on sub-scale Low 
</p>
<p>Pressure Fuel Pump inducer previously built for water flow testing.
• Problematic mode for RS25 is 2nd in-phase 0 nodal diameter (ND); sequential order 
</p>
<p>within this family refers to number of half-waves along periphery starting at leading 
edge. 
</p>
<p>• Inducer glued to a block to exercise the hub flexibility and the block placed on a foam 
pad to enable well-defined boundary conditions.
</p>
<p>• Modal punch device used to apply impact loads to the inducer, Laser Doppler 
Vibrometer (LDV) to measure response, which was processed to generate natural 
frequencies and mode shapes.  
– Acrylic strip placed on surface of water to 
</p>
<p>enable smooth optical transition for laser beam.
</p>
<p>• Model correlation proved problematic. 
• Inducer exhibited mode localization due to 
</p>
<p>mistuning, which made the model correlation 
difficult. 
</p>
<p>• Addition of water does appear to tune the 
system somewhat, which makes sense as the 
fluid-added mass is entirely symmetric.</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>Inducer Ping test in LH2 Modeling, Correlation Procedure
</p>
<p>• Strain gage analysis was conducted and modal 
matches between air and LH2 were made based on 
the same relative resonant peak location and the 
same strain gage phasing.
</p>
<p>• ANSYS finite element models were constructed for 
the cryostat air and cryostat LH2 tests, facility star 
piece and columns included in the model. 
</p>
<p>• MAC’s and judgement required to match modes 
due to limited gages.
1. Determine the mode of interest in air in the cryostat.
2. Select the analytical mode of interest in 
</p>
<p>incompressible LH2.
3. Select analytical mode of interest in compressible 
</p>
<p>LH2.
4. Compare ANSYS cryostat compressible LH2 mode of 
</p>
<p>interest to measured modes in LH2 cryostat test.
- Measured mode of interest in LH2 originally 
</p>
<p>believed to be 3480 Hz, but phase information is 
not understood and the total reduction from air 
and estimated reduction from cryo air appears out 
of family with other matched modes, so 3548 Hz 
mode tracked instead.
</p>
<p>Modeling of entire 
cryostat found to be 
necessary for accurate 
acoustic results.</p>
<p></p>

<p>Conclusions &amp; Future Work
• Integrated Test/Analysis program performed at NASA/MSFC to examine effect of a 
</p>
<p>number of factors complicating natural frequency prediction of LH2 pump structures.
• For mode of interest, natural frequency reduction in tank of water -58%, large volume of 
</p>
<p>LH2 almost -5% , for all measured modes, between 5% and 8%.  
• Tip clearance effects measured for cantilever beam and inducer are substantial.  
</p>
<p>– For cantilever beam, 1st natural frequency reduced as much as 20% in water and 9% 
in LH2 with tight clearance on both sides.
</p>
<p>– Tight tip clearance modal test in water for subscale inducer showed natural frequency 
of mode of interest reduced by 15% and reduction as high as 21% for other modes.  
Calibrated ANSYS model predicted tip clearance effect to within 2.5% of test results.
</p>
<p>– The analytical trend of results for tip clearance effect were quantified and agree 
reasonably well with theory (correction from paper).  The average tip clearance effect 
is -11% for the range of tip clearances analyzed for the full scale induce.
</p>
<p>• A third phase of inducer testing with a tight tip clearance in LH2 is being considered to 
further validate analytical capability to predict the tip clearance effect for the inducer.
</p>
<p>• Calibrated ANSYS modal acoustic techniques extended to SLS RS-25 LPFP full-scale 
inducer, effects due to elastic modulus, large volume, tip clearance, and compressibility 
tracked &amp; quantified separately.  
– These adjustments initially appear to mitigate inducer HOC resonance concerns for 
</p>
<p>SLS.  
– The demonstrated success to model structures in turbopump structures in LH2 with 
</p>
<p>tight clearances will benefit future flight programs and other pump and water 
turbine industries as well.</p>
