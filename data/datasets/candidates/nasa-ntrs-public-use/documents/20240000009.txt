<div class="slide-content"><p>Introduction</p>
<p>The SLS propulsion system induces an energetic launch environment the vehicle must withstand</p>
<p></p>
<p>Water is injected into the mobile launcher hole (MLH) to mitigate the environment on the vehicle</p>
<p>The ignition overpressure/sound suppression (IOP/SS) system consists of six water nozzles per SRB and 16 water nozzles for the Core Stage</p>
<p></p>
<p>Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations of the multiphase launch environment conducted to support SLS</p>
<p>Comparison to Artemis I flight data provided a validation opportunity</p>

<p>SLS Launch Environment</p>
<p>The solid rocket booster (SRB) transient generates the most energetic portion of the SLS launch environment</p>
<p>Includes igniter shock (IS), ignition overpressure (IOP), and duct overpressure (DOP)</p>
<p></p>
<p>Igniter shock results from activation of the SRB igniter</p>
<p>The shock and ‘cold’ flow precedes the hot combustion gases</p>
<p></p>
<p>IOP and DOP result from the SRB plume transient</p>
<p>The plume accelerates through the MLH and trench as the motor pressure rises</p>
<p>Compression of ambient air and vapor in the plume’s path forms the pressure sources</p>
<p>Large magnitude, low frequency waves generated when the sources release</p>
<p>IOP is the upward traveling portion while DOP is the downward traveling portion</p>

<p>Multiphase CFD Predictions Support the SLS Program</p>
<p>The plume-water interaction physics during the SLS launch are highly non-linear and difficult to model</p>
<p>Multiple, hot, supersonic rocket plumes</p>
<p>Multiphase flow (gas and liquid) + phase change</p>
<p>Dependent on both large and small length/time scales</p>
<p></p>
<p>Significant uncertainty existed about the plume-water interactions prior to the Artemis I flight </p>
<p>Lack of integrated SLS tests prior to flight</p>
<p>Significant configuration differences between SLS, Shuttle, and subscale testing</p>
<p></p>
<p>SLS Program relied on CFD to inform design and launch risks</p>
<p>Supported hydrogen burn-off igniters (HBOIs) operation, water system design, debris transport, environment development, etc.</p>
<p>Multiphase CFD evolved over time, conducted 5 simulations in total with different models and flow conditions</p>
<p>Those simulations that most closely represents the Artemis I launch are presented here</p>

<p>Model Comparison</p>
<p>Two different fidelity multiphase models have been fielded</p>
<p>Loci/Stream-Volume of Fluid (VoF) pressure-based solver (high fidelity)</p>
<p>Gas-liquid interface tracked explicitly via geometric reconstruction</p>
<p>Surface tension applied to the interface</p>
<p>Liquid structures properly breakdown to smaller droplets in response to external forces</p>
<p>Accuracy (and expense) dependent on mesh resolution</p>
<p>Loci/CHEM-Real Fluids (RF) density-based solver (low fidelity)</p>
<p>Gas-liquid interface not tracked (no surface tension, liquid diffuses non-physically)</p>
<p>Water acts as a dense fluid with appropriate thermodynamic/phase change properties</p>
<p>Captures water mass and general location, but neither full water density nor droplets/splashing</p>
<p>Results reasonable where:</p>
<p>Interface details less important</p>
<p>Numerical diffusion timescales longer than fluid convective timescales</p>
<p>Three different simulations will be examined presently</p>
<p>One VoF, two RF, two different water flowrates</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusion</p>
<p>Qualitative validation achieved by comparing CFD flowfields to Artemis I flight video</p>
<p>Both solvers successfully predicted the initial SRB plume-water interaction</p>
<p>CFD outward velocity jets match flight-observed outward water spray</p>
<p></p>
<p>Quantitative validation achieved by comparing CFD to flight pressures at the base heat shield</p>
<p>Both solvers successfully predicted the SRB igniter shock, secondary igniter shock, and the magnitude of the IOP mitigation</p>
<p>CFD suggests IOP forms as the MLH pressurization diffracts from the confined MLH duct into the larger flame trench volume</p>
<p>More work needed to fully understand the launch environment</p>
<p>More details in the accompanying paper</p>
<p></p>
<p>This analysis considered successful validation of both the Loci/STREAM-VoF and Loci/CHEM-RF CFD solvers for multiphase launch environments</p>
<p>CFD successfully predicted IS propagation and IOP magnitude reduction</p>
<p>Validation provides confidence in future use of these tools on multiphase launch environments</p>
