<p>Background</p>
<p>Managing cryogens is a critical technology for NASA’s spaceflight missions</p>
<p>Cryogenic fluid management (CFM) challenges
Long term propellant storage
Pressure control for nominal operations
Propellant transfer in microgravity
Conditioning propellants with cryocoolers</p>
<p>NASA’s CFM Modeling Portfolio
Develop, enhance and validate computational fluid dynamic (CFD) models</p>
<p>Propulsion Fluid Dynamics Branch at NASA Marshall supports several projects with CFD analyses
Human Landing System (HLS), Space Launch System (SLS), Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS), etc.</p>
<p>Develop and validate Loci-Stream-VOF CFD solver to solve complex thermo-fluid and multi-physics problems for CFM
Cryogenic tank operations/phenomena: self-pressurization, autogenous pressurization, helium pressurization, mixing (depressurization with jets, sprays, etc.), venting, ullage collapse, tank fill/drain, liquefaction, etc.</p>
<p>Multi-purpose Hydrogen Test Bed (MHTB) Experiment[1]</p>

<p>Primary comparison metric: Average pressurization rate</p>

<p>Computational Model</p>
<p>Eulerian Model</p>
<p>Two phase model: sharp interface method </p>
<p>Turbulence Model: IDDES (Hybrid RANS/LES closure)</p>
<p>Asymmetric interface turbulence transport modeling: Wall-like turbulence on gas side of the interface</p>
<p>Phase change: Thermal equilibrium and Hertz-Knudsen-Schrage’s (kinetics-based)</p>
<p>Lagrangian particle modeling:</p>
<p>Spray cannot be resolved in Eulerian phase</p>
<p>Monte-Carlo modeling using parcel approach</p>
<p>Two-way momentum (Clift-Gauvin) and energy coupling (Ranz-Marshall) with Eulerian flow</p>
<p>Particle phase change: Thermal equilibrium and Hertz-Knudsen-Schrage model</p>
<p>Turbulence scattering: Sawford’s acceleration scattering model</p>
<p>Particle breakup model (Chryssakis and Assanis [2]): based on Weber number</p>
<p>Droplet-droplet collision model (O’Rourke [3]): based on collision Weber number, velocity vectors, and droplet size</p>

<p>Modeling Sensitivities (1/2)</p>
<p>At a parcel injection rate (PIR) producing about 1 particles/parcel, prediction is independent of PIR.</p>
<p>IDDES turbulence closure is marginally better at predicting pressurization rate at the end of the test</p>
<p>Asymmetric interface turbulence modeling compares best with the test – consistent with several validations [4][5][6]</p>

<p>Break-up Modeling is Crucial</p>
<p>Particle break-up and coalescence modeling is based on known flow physics</p>
<p>Depressurization rate is strongly affected by injected particle size</p>
<p>Large particle surface area enhances heat/mass transfer</p>
<p>Difficult to measure droplet sizes. They were not characterized in testing, but physics-based modeling does not require a priori particle diameter information</p>
<p>Particles with injection diameter of 1.7 mm are scaled 4x for visualization</p>
<p>Every 100th particle is displayed</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusion</p>
<p>Validated Loci-Stream and Lagrangian-Eulerian multiphase flow modeling technique for CFM Modeling Portfolio.</p>
<p>Identified important models crucial for reliable modeling</p>
<p>Asymmetric interface turbulence transport in Eulerian phase change modeling</p>
<p>Weber number based Lagrangian droplet break-up and collision model for physically accurate heat/mass transfer with Eulerian domain</p>
<p>Future work</p>
<p>Validate with VOF method for morphing interfaces</p>
<p>Validate VOF to Lagrangian particle conversion based on droplet size</p>
<p>A successful, and parameter-free validation against flight-scale tank test builds confidence in use of CFD methodology for CFM modeling for human spaceflight projects.</p>
