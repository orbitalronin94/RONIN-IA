
































Assessment of Mars 2020 Forebody Heating Predictions with 

Coupled Material Response

Tom West and Chris Johnston

NASA Langley Research Center



Motivation and Objective

• Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and material 

response models are typically assumed uncoupled 

during design and analysis

• “Correct” physics model has the CFD and material 

response coupled such that

– Pyrolysis and char species are treated explicitly in the 

boundary layer

– Surface chemistry modeled through finite rate processes

• Measurements obtained from within the Mars 2020 

heatshield provided an opportunity to assess impact of 

flow field-material response coupling

• Objective: Investigate the impact of coupled material 

response modeling on predicting near-surface in-depth 

forebody thermal protection system (TPS) temperatures 

on Mars 2020

MEDLI2 Forebody Instrumentation and Flight Data

• Mars 2020 instrumented with the Mars Entry Descent and Landing 

Instrumentation 2 (MEDLI2) package for measuring in-flight pressure, in-

depth TPS temperatures, and backshell heating

• 11 thermocouple (TC) plugs installed on the heatshield, denoted as 

MEDLI2 Thermal on Heatshield (MTH)

• MTH01, MTH02, and MTH03 had three TCs at different depths, all others 

had one TC.

Turbulent Heating



Computational Model

• Flow solver: LAURA 5.6, 20 species, two temperature thermochemical nonequilibrium

• Coupled 1-D material response model with equilibrium and nonequilibrium ablation wall 

boundary models 

• Coupled radiation with HARA (CO2 and CO infrared bands)

• Turbulence modeled with Cebeci-Smith algebraic model - transition modeled based on 

observed heating augmentation in the MEDLI2 flight data

Material and Surface Chemistry Models

• NuSil coated PICA (PICA-N) 

– Silicone based NuSil coating inhibits recession of PICA 

heatshield - seen in MEDLI & MEDLI2 flight data and in limited 

arc-jet testing in CO2

– Modeled by using a PICA model with a zero char rate 

• Two surface chemistry models

– Model 1: CO oxidation “Mitcheltree” model with 10% 

catalytic efficiency and N2 catalysis with 5% catalytic 

efficiency

– Model 2: N2 and O2 catalysis both with 5% catalytic 

efficiency

• Equilibrium ablation model over predicts heating 
throughout the trajectory leading to over-prediction of 
temperature

– Due to nearly full recovery of CO2 on the vehicle surface

– At 60s, CO2 boundary layer edge mass fraction < 10% and 
> 90% at wall

• Temperatures are also over predicted further in-depth

• Uncoupled ablation shown to be a reasonable 
approximation of equilibrium for NuSil coated PICA at 
Mars 2020 like entry conditions

• Agreement with flight data at TCs near the stagnation 
region better than TCs further away

Modeling Turbulence and Transition

• Near-shoulder region subject to turbulence after about 

71s

• Purely laminar boundary layer seen to significantly 

underpredict surface heating

• Transition induced at the tile seam upstream of MTH02

• Gradient after transition similar to flight data, but peak 

temperature still over predicted due to excess heating 

early in the trajectory

Nonequilibrium Ablation

• Two surface chemistry models yield different 

temperatures within the TPS material

– Underprediction of near surface heating with the N2 and 

O2 catalysis models

– Overprediction early in the trajectory with the N2 and CO2

model

• N2 and CO2 model temperatures similar to equilibrium 

ablation early in trajectory due to CO2 recombination

• Peak temperature within about 10 K with the N2 and 

CO2 model

• Significant improvement over equilibrium surface 

chemistry

• Refinement of the surface chemistry model needed

Conclusions

• Objective of this work was to assess the impact of flow field - material response coupling on 

reproducing temperatures within the Mars 2020 forebody thermal protection system

• Coupled equilibrium ablation overpredicts in-depth temperatures due to excess heating

• Nonequilibrium ablation improves prediction of peak near-surface in-depth temperature 

using an N2 catalysis & CO oxidation chemistry model on the surface – excess heating still 

observed early in the trajectory

• Strong need for a detailed surface chemistry model to accurately model physics on the 

vehicle surface
