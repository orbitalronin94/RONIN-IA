<p>SIZING &amp; PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS OF A 
MEGAWATT-CLASS ELECTRIFIED AIRCRAFT 
PROPULSION (EAP) SYSTEM FOR A PARALLEL 
HYBRID TURBOPROP CONCEPT
</p>
<p>ICAS PAPER 2024_0884
</p>
<p>Dahlia Pham, Carl Recine, and Ralph Jansen
</p>
<p>NASA Ames Research Center</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>INTRODUCTION &amp; BACKGROUND
</p>
<p>• Electrified Powertrain Flight Demonstration (EPFD) project
</p>
<p>– Collaboration between NASA &amp; U.S. industry partners to integrate, test, and demonstrate 
</p>
<p>Megawatt-class Electrified Aircraft Propulsion (EAP) systems
</p>
<p>• With rapid emergence of novel, multi-MW hybrid-electric aircraft systems and 
</p>
<p>concepts being developed:
</p>
<p>– How can we evaluate the impacts of aircraft electrification on vehicle-level performance?
</p>
<p>– What are the mission capabilities for a multi-MW, true parallel hybrid concept assuming 
</p>
<p>near-term EAP technology performance levels?
</p>
<p>2</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>CONCEPT OVERVIEW
</p>
<p>True Parallel Hybrid (TPH) Architecture
</p>
<p>• Dual-sided, hybrid-electric powertrain with 
</p>
<p>separately maintained EAP system alongside 
</p>
<p>conventional gas turbine engines
</p>
<p>• Featured on magniX EPFD Demonstrator
</p>
<p>TPH Large Turboprop Freighter (LTF) Concept
</p>
<p>• Four-engine turboprop concept featuring dual-
</p>
<p>sided, multi-MW, hybridized EAP system with 
</p>
<p>parallel turbine and electric motor drive system
</p>
<p>3</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>EAP COMPONENT-LEVEL PERFORMANCE PARAMETERS
</p>
<p>• Near-term EAP technology levels informed by power/energy requirements for 
</p>
<p>freighter mission carrying normal design payload &amp; literature review:
</p>
<p>• Total weight for EAP system includes battery, AC/DC cabling, thermal 
</p>
<p>management systems (TMS), gearbox, and inverter weight
</p>
<p>4</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>INTEGRATED MODELING &amp; SIMULATION ENVIRONMENT
</p>
<p>• Integrated M&amp;S environment for parametric modeling &amp; mission analysis for 
</p>
<p>conventional and hybrid-electric powertrain and vehicle concept
</p>
<p>• For fixed vehicle, payload &amp; 
</p>
<p>EAP system specifications:
• Max. motor power 
</p>
<p>(EHPMAX)
</p>
<p>• Battery specific energy 
</p>
<p>(SEBATT)
</p>
<p>• Determine battery sizing &amp; 
</p>
<p>range performance
</p>
<p>5</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>HYBRID-ELECTRIC MISSION PROFILE FOR TPH LTF STUDY
</p>
<p>• Concept of Operations (ConOps) for hybrid-electric aircraft includes 
</p>
<p>power/energy management assuming 20% min. state-of-charge &amp; compliance 
</p>
<p>with FAA Part 25/121 airworthiness and reserve mission requirements
</p>
<p>6
Segment definitions provided in paper. </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>PROPULSION SYSTEM MODELING (BASELINE &amp; HYBRID-ELECTRIC)
</p>
<p>• Electric motor model developed using MotorXP while conventional gas 
</p>
<p>turbine engine weight &amp; performance synthesized using NPSS &amp; WATE++
</p>

<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>UNCERTAINTY PROPAGATION &amp; PERFORMANCE VARIABILITY
</p>
<p>Monte Carlo (MC) Simulations
</p>
<p>• Variations in EAP component-level parameters 
</p>
<p>captured stochastically
</p>
<p>Motor Sizing Impacts on Cruise Thrust-Split
</p>
<p>• Cross-over point at 1.910 MW where thrust 
</p>
<p>contributions from both powertrains equal
</p>
<p>8</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>PERFORMANCE IMPACTS FOR NOMINAL EAP TECHNOLOGY LEVELS
</p>
<p>• For 300 NM mission, nominal TPH LTF configuration results in 42.7% block 
</p>
<p>fuel savings with increased takeoff distance &amp; reduced rate-of-climb
</p>
<p>9</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>RESULTS OF VEHICLE-LEVEL SENSITIVITY STUDIES
</p>
<p>Energy Consumption per Nautical Mile (ECNM)
</p>
<p>• Battery specific energy density has highest impact 
</p>
<p>and variability in ECNM reduction 22.3-27.4%
</p>
<p>Fuel Burn per Nautical Mile
</p>
<p>• Total block fuel savings for TPH LTF concept 42-63% 
</p>
<p>for ~300 NM range &amp; 25,000 lb payload
</p>
<p>10</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>RESULTS OF VEHICLE-LEVEL SENSITIVITY STUDIES
</p>
<p>Battery Specific Energy (SEBATT)
</p>
<p>• Battery technology improvement results in 
</p>
<p>increased range performance &amp; fuel savings
</p>
<p>Motor Efficiency
</p>
<p>• Improvements in motor efficiency result in 
</p>
<p>improved range performance capabilities:
</p>
<p>11</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>CONCLUSIONS
</p>
<p>• Integrated approach to sizing &amp; capturing vehicle-level performance sensitivities to variations in 
</p>
<p>EAP technology levels in the 2020-2035 timeframe
</p>
<p>• Key drivers for overall vehicle performance improvements:
</p>
<p>– Battery specific energy
</p>
<p>– Maximum EAP system power output 
</p>
<p>– Motor efficiency
</p>
<p>• Improving pack-level battery specific energy (SEBATT) from 250 Wh/kg to 400 Wh/kg results in 
</p>
<p>~25% Energy Consumption per Nautical Mile (ECNM) reduction &amp; 40-63% fuel savings for short-
</p>
<p>haul missions (300 NM)
</p>
<p>– Informing how focused improvements to EAP component-level specifications enhance mission performance 
</p>
<p>capabilities for multi-MW freighter concept
</p>
