<p>ABSTRACT 
Effective deployment of machine-learning (ML) models 
</p>
<p>could drive a high level of efficiency in aircraft engine 
</p>
<p>conceptual design. Aero-Engines AI is a user-friendly app that 
</p>
<p>has been created to deploy trained machine-learning (ML) 
</p>
<p>models to assess aircraft engine concepts. It was created using 
</p>
<p>tkinter, a GUI (graphical user interface) module that is built into 
</p>
<p>the standard Python library. Employing tkinter greatly 
</p>
<p>facilitates the sharing of ML application as an executable file 
</p>
<p>which can be run on Windows machines (without the need to 
</p>
<p>have Python or any library installed). The app gets user input 
</p>
<p>for a turbofan design, preprocesses the input data, and deploys 
</p>
<p>trained ML models to predict turbofan thrust specific fuel 
</p>
<p>consumption (TSFC), engine weight, core size, and 
</p>
<p>turbomachinery stage-counts. The ML predictive models were 
</p>
<p>built by employing supervised deep-learning and K-nearest 
</p>
<p>neighbor regression algorithms to study patterns in an existing 
</p>
<p>open-source database of production and research turbofan 
</p>
<p>engines. They were trained, cross-validated, and tested in 
</p>
<p>Keras, an open-source neural networks API (application 
</p>
<p>programming interface) written in Python, with TensorFlow 
</p>
<p>(Google open-source artificial intelligence library) serving as 
</p>
<p>the backend engine. The smooth deployment of these ML 
</p>
<p>models using the app shows that Aero-Engines AI is an easy-to-
</p>
<p>use and a time-saving tool for aircraft engine design-space 
</p>
<p>exploration during the conceptual design stage. Current version 
</p>
<p>of the app focuses on the performance prediction of 
</p>
<p>conventional turbofans. However, the scope of the app can 
</p>
<p>easily be expanded to include other engine types (such as 
</p>
<p>turboshaft and hybrid-electric systems) after their ML models 
</p>
<p>are developed. Overall, the use of a machine-learning app for 
</p>
<p>aircraft engine concept assessment represents a promising area 
</p>
<p>of development in aircraft engine conceptual design. 
 
</p>
<p>Keywords: aircraft engine, machine learning, tkinter, Python, 
</p>
<p>TensorFlow

<p>INTRODUCTION 
More and more organizations are adopting a data-driven 
</p>
<p>approach to decision-making. With the vast amounts of data 
</p>
<p>collected and tracked in recent times, machine-learning (ML) 
</p>
<p>applications are gaining popularity across multiple industries. 
</p>
<p>The aircraft engine industry has amassed and stored significant 
</p>
<p>quantities of data over the years. These big data sets, sourced 
</p>
<p>from multiple origins such as the database of currently 
</p>
<p>manufactured engines, ongoing development projects, 
</p>
<p>previously completed development projects, and 
</p>
<p>unmanufactured designs, hold tremendous potential as a 
</p>
<p>knowledge asset for future engine projects. 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>  Designing an aircraft engine is a complex, 
</p>
<p>interdisciplinary process that requires significant time and 
</p>
<p>effort. Engine designers encounter a formidable challenge 
</p>
<p>during the conceptual design phase - how to rapidly evaluate 
</p>
<p>the performance of a specific engine design given the aircraft's 
</p>
<p>mission requirements and various design parameters. The 
</p>
<p>number of potential engine configurations could be vast, 
</p>
<p>requiring designers to rely on system analysis and simulation to 
</p>
<p>estimate performance. Consequently, designers must conduct a 
</p>
<p>comprehensive propulsion system study for each possible 
</p>
<p>configuration, which can be time-consuming, particularly when 
</p>
<p>dealing with a large design space. 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p> By leveraging the power of machine learning (ML) 
</p>
<p>algorithms to learn from the existing engine data sets, it is 
</p>
<p>possible to develop ML models that can quickly and accurately 
</p>
<p>assess new aircraft engine concepts, providing valuable insights 
</p>
<p>and reducing the time and resources required for the engine 
</p>
<p>concept assessment process. A ML model can identify patterns 
</p>
<p>and trends that may not be immediately apparent to human 
</p>
<p>analysts, leading to more informed decision-making and 
</p>
<p>ultimately resulting in the development of better aircraft engine 
</p>
<p>concepts. The ability to assess new engine concepts quickly and 
</p>
<p>accurately can be a competitive advantage in aircraft engine 
</p>
<p>development. 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>  Previously, the author focused on training/developing the 
</p>
<p>ML models that allow for quick estimation of engine TSFC, 
</p>
<p>system weight, and core size during the conceptual design </p>

</p>

</p>

</p>
<p>app, Aero-Engines AI, is described in this paper. 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p> While the development of ML models is essential for their 
</p>
<p>applications, the models would only be of value if they are 
</p>
<p>actively deployed in a production environment where they can 
</p>
<p>be used to solve practical problems. Thus, effective ML model 
</p>
<p>deployment is just as important as ML model development. ML 
</p>
<p>model deployment involves integrating trained ML models, 
</p>
<p>developed in a R&amp;D environment, into a production 
</p>
<p>environment. It is a critical step that must be done so an 
</p>
<p>organization can use the models to solve problems. Seamless 
</p>
<p>deployment of trained ML models into production is essential 
</p>
<p>for putting the models to practical use.  
</p>
<p>  
</p>
<p>  Aero-Engines AI, a Windows app, has been created to 
</p>
<p>deploy the trained ML models for aircraft engine concepts 
</p>
<p>assessment. It was created using tkinter, a GUI module that is 
</p>
<p>built into the standard Python library. Employing tkinter greatly 
</p>
<p>facilitates the sharing of ML application as an executable file 
</p>
<p>which can be run on Windows machines (without the need to 
</p>
<p>have Python or any library installed). MS Windows platform 
</p>
<p>was chosen for the deployment to reduce complexity and for 
</p>
<p>ease of access. The structure of the app is shown in Figure 1. 
</p>

<p> The app is user-friendly. It is simple to learn, easy to 
</p>
<p>navigate, and its use is intuitive enough that it does not require 
</p>
<p>an instruction manual. The development process of Aero-
</p>
<p>Engines AI consists of five steps: 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>• Engine data collection, augmentation, and 
</p>
<p>preparation 
</p>
<p>• ML models training 
</p>
<p>• ML models testing and evaluation 
</p>
<p>• App design for ML models deployment 
</p>
<p>• Monitoring and updating 
</p>

<p>ENGINE DATA COLLECTION, AUGMENTATION, AND 
PREPARATION 
</p>
<p>• Engine data collection 
</p>
<p>Current version of the app has only turbofan assessment 
</p>
<p>capability (will be expanded to include other engine types such 
</p>
<p>as turboshaft, hybrid-turbofan, etc., in the future versions). The 
</p>
<p>basic engine architecture is an axial-compressor turbofan. The 
</p>
<p>engine database consists of 145 manufactured (commercial) 
</p>
<p>engines [4 to 10] and 39 engines that were studied previously 
</p>
<p>in various NASA aeronautics projects. These commercial 
</p>
<p>engines capture over half-a-century of engine technology 
</p>
<p>improvements and lessons-learned, which would minimize the 
</p>
<p>prediction uncertainties of the ML models. The NASA engine 
</p>
<p>data were the system-study results for various NASA 
</p>
<p>aeronautics projects [11–16]. The engine database is shown in 
</p>
<p>Appendix A.

<p>• Data augmentation 
</p>
<p>Data augmentation is an important technique that is 
</p>
<p>commonly used in ML to improve the performance and 
</p>
<p>generalizability of a training model. The process entails 
</p>
<p>creating additional data points from the existing training data 
</p>
<p>by applying various transformations and modifications to the 
</p>
<p>data. Data augmentation increases the diversity and quantity of 
</p>
<p>training data, improving the model's performance for its task, 
</p>
<p>and making it more adaptable to changes in the data. For this 
</p>
<p>study, the data augmentation was performed by scaling up the 
</p>
<p>current engines by 10%. For example, the sea-level static (SLS) 
</p>
<p>engine thrust and weight were increased by 10%, while keeping 
</p>
<p>the other operating parameters such as bypass ratio (BPR), 
</p>

</p>

</p>
<p>predictive models. The testing set consisted of the remaining

<p>engines that were unseen by the training models and was </p>

<p>ML MODELS TRAINING 
Once the data was ready, the next step was to select the 
</p>
<p>appropriate algorithms that would be used to train the ML 
</p>
<p>models. This can involve choosing from a variety of machine 
</p>
<p>learning algorithms and tuning the parameters and 
</p>
<p>hyperparameters of the models to optimize their performance 
</p>
<p>on the specific problem or task. 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>As reported in [1, 2, 3], the ML models for TSFC, engine 
</p>
<p>weight, and core size predictions were constructed using 
</p>
<p>supervised deep-learning and K-nearest neighbor algorithms 
</p>
<p>[17], which analyzed patterns in an open-source database of 
</p>
<p>research and production turbofan engines. Additional ML 
</p>
<p>models were developed since then for the turbomachinery stage 
</p>
<p>count prediction, using K-nearest neighbor regression 
</p>
<p>algorithm. These models were trained, cross-validated, and 
</p>
<p>tested using Keras, an open-source neural networks API written 
</p>
<p>in Python, with TensorFlow as the backend engine. These 
</p>
<p>models were trained and deployed in Keras [18], an open-
</p>
<p>source neural networks API written in Python, with TensorFlow 
</p>
<p>[19] serving as the backend engine. Keras provided the building 
</p>
<p>blocks for developing the deep-learning models, and 
</p>
<p>TensorFlow handled the tensor computations and 
</p>
<p>manipulations. 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>Depending on the ML model, either L2 or Dropout 
</p>
<p>regularization technique (where neuron outputs are dropped out 
</p>
<p>randomly) [20 and 21] was applied to prevent the DNN from 
</p>
<p>overfitting the training data. A grid-search routine was used to 
</p>
<p>determine the regularization parameter, dropout rate, number of 
</p>
<p>epochs, batch size, and the number of ‘neurons’ in the hidden 
</p>
<p>layers that give the lowest training error. The Adam 
</p>
<p>optimization algorithm [22] was used to update the network 
</p>
<p>weights during each epoch.  
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p> Totally, nine ML models were trained for engine TSFC, 
</p>
<p>weight, core size (last stage HPC blade height), fan diameter, 
</p>
<p>and turbomachinery stage count predictions, respectively. The 
</p>
<p>training and cross validation of these ML models are described 
</p>
<p>in detail in [1, 2, and 3]. 
</p>
<p> 
</p>

</p>

<p>these ML models are described in detail in [1, 2, 3]. The results 
</p>
<p>showed that these ML models are an effective tool for

<p> 
APP DESIGN for ML MODELS DEPLOYMENT 
</p>
<p>After the ML models were developed, trained, and 
</p>
<p>tested, they were integrated into the user-friendly app, Aero-
</p>
<p>Engines AI, that allows for the easy and intuitive assessment 
</p>
<p>of engine concepts. Aero-Engines AI is a Windows app that 
</p>
<p>deploys trained ML models to assess aircraft engine concepts. 
</p>
<p>The app was created using tkinter [23], a GUI (graphical user 
</p>
<p>interface) module that is built into the standard Python library. 
</p>
<p>And pyinstaller [24], a Python package, was used to convert 
</p>
<p>the python scripts into an executable file that can be run on 
</p>
<p>Windows machines. The conversion greatly facilitates the 
</p>
<p>sharing of ML applications with other Windows users (who do 
</p>
<p>not need to have Python, or any library installed in their 
</p>
<p>computers). 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>The app design aimed to provide a user-friendly 
</p>
<p>experience with a simple point-and-click feature. The input 
</p>

<p>MONITORING AND UPDATING 
Monitoring and updates are important aspects of ML app 
</p>
<p>development, as they help ensure that the app continues to 
</p>
<p>perform well and provide accurate predictions or 
</p>
<p>recommendations over time. To ensure optimal performance of 
</p>
<p>the current ML models, it's crucial to keep track of the changing 
</p>
<p>engine data and its effect on their overall functionality. While 
</p>
<p>the commercial engine data in the current database remain 
</p>
<p>static, the NASA engine data are obtained through research on 
</p>
<p>aeronautics studies for three generations of aircraft - near, mid, 
</p>
<p>and far term. Each generation has associated goals for 
</p>
<p>reductions in noise, emissions, fuel burn, and field length 
</p>
<p>relative to present-day aircrafts. These aircraft generations are 
</p>
<p>labeled as 'N+1', 'N+2', and 'N+3', respectively. The research 
</p>
<p>for 'N+2' and 'N+3' is aimed at enabling new vehicle 
</p>
<p>configurations that meet NASA's ambitious technology 
</p>
<p>objectives. As the NASA engine data could be revised over 
</p>
<p>time, the ML models must be updated periodically to consider 
</p>
<p>the impact of such updates. 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>SUMMARY 
Aero-Engines AI, a user-friendly Windows app, has been 
</p>
<p>created using tkinter, a GUI module that is built into the 
</p>
<p>standard Python library. This app is designed to deploy trained 
</p>
<p>ML models to assess various aircraft engine concepts. These 
</p>
<p>ML models were trained, cross-validated, and tested in Keras, 
</p>
<p>an open-source neural networks API written in Python, with 
</p>
<p>TensorFlow serving as the backend engine. The assessment 
</p>
<p>results are presented in terms of engine TSFC, weight, core size, 
</p>
<p>and turbomachinery stage counts. The seamless deployment of 
</p>
<p>these ML models through the app demonstrates that Aero-
</p>
<p>Engines AI is an efficient and easy-to-use tool for exploring the 
</p>
<p>design space of aircraft engines during the conceptual design 
</p>
<p>stage. The current version of the app focuses on predicting the 
</p>
<p>performance of conventional turbofans. However, after the 
</p>
<p>development of their ML models, the app's scope can be easily 
</p>
<p>expanded to include other engine types, such as turboshaft and 
</p>
<p>hybrid-electric systems. 
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>The success of the ML application will depend on the 
</p>
<p>quality and quantity of data available for training, as well as the 
</p>
<p>deployment of the ML model itself. Careful consideration of 
</p>
<p>these factors is crucial to ensure the optimal performance of the 
</p>
<p>ML system. Overall, the use of a machine-learning app for 
</p>
<p>aircraft engine concept assessment represents a promising area 
</p>
<p>of development in aircraft engine conceptual design. 
</p>
