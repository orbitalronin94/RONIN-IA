<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Challenges of Landing at the Lunar South Pole</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Characteristics of the South Pole</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Characteristics of the South Pole
</p>
<p>• Earth is upside down and spins 
backwards
</p>
<p>• Same side of the moon always faces 
the Earth
</p>
<p>• Sun and Earth only rise above the 
horizon ~2 to 7 deg 
</p>
<p>• Sun casts long shadows
• Earth is in a 2-week cycle: visible two 
</p>
<p>weeks, not visible two weeks
• Terminator, where light meets darkness, 
</p>
<p>is not smooth
• Constantly changing lighting effects at 
</p>
<p>the pole
</p>
<p>Kaguya “Earth-Set” from the Lunar South Pole
</p>
<p>Lunar South Pole terminator rotates around the pole.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Characteristics of Near Rectilinear Halo Orbit
• ~6.5 day period
• Visible from Earth 100%
• Periapsis of ~1500 km and apoapsis nearly 70,000 km
• Due to variations in Moon and NRHO, approach path 
</p>
<p>varies orbit-to-orbit but has general left to right 
direction
</p>
<p>• Other Considerations:
• Assumes Gateway fixed NRHO, so can only descend to 
</p>
<p>the surface once every 6.5 days (~55 opportunities to 
land per year)
</p>
<p>• Earth/Moon orbital mechanics and SLS/Orion limitations 
reduce ability to get to NRHO to ~28 NRHO departure 
opportunities per year</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Key Artemis III Landing Requirements
</p>
<p>1. Land within 6 deg latitude of the South Pole
2. Surface slope for landing (100m): &lt;10 deg
3. Surface slope limit for EVA  (2km): &lt;20 deg
4. Direct with Earth (DWE) communication; assume no comm relay is 
</p>
<p>available
5. Lighting
</p>
<p>a. Surface Lighting
b. Approach lighting
</p>
<p>6. Land within 100 m of a target
</p>
<p>Objective: Identify locations that are viable for landing and meet all requirements and constraints</p>
