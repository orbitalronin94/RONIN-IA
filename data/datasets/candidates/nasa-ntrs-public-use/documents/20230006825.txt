<p>Stirling cycle machine development</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Stirling cycle machines have been developed for 
terrestrial and space applications since 1800s  
• 1816 – Robert Stirling patented the first Stirling cycle engine 
</p>
<p>which he called “The Economiser”. Kinematic engines 
contain linkage arms and contact bearings.  
</p>
<p>• 1964 – Mr. Beale developed a working prototype of what he 
would call the “Free-Piston Stirling Engine” in his patent. 
Free-piston engines contain close running or non-contacting 
clearance seals to enable long life.
</p>
<p>• 1970s-1990s – Development of terrestrial Stirling cycle 
machines focused on predictable operation and life limiting 
aspects (TRL 2-3)
</p>
<p>• 2000s – Development of terrestrial and space designs 
focused on deployment into systems and demonstrating life 
and reliability (TRL 4-5)
</p>
<p>• 2020s – Development has focused on robustness (TRL 5-6)</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>Life and reliability testing has demonstrated long life 
capability in NASA’s Stirling Research Laboratory
• NASA and it’s DOE partners started maturing Stirling 
</p>
<p>cycle machines for potential use in space in 1999
• Flexure and gas bearing machines tested
• Conversion efficiency ranges between 25-39% 
• Convertors run 24/7 unattended with autonomous 
</p>
<p>response systems and performance data archiving in 
NASA Glenn’s Stirling Research Laboratory 
</p>
<p>• Long duration testing ensures design of life limiting 
mechanisms, geometric stability of non-contact running 
clearances, and materials compatibility
</p>
<p>• Stirling convertors have operated at full power longer 
than needed to support a 14-year mission
</p>
<p>• Engineering methods needed to make a reliable 
long-life Stirling convertor have been established</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Commercial applications have demonstrated the ability 
to produce large quantifies of free-piston Stirling units
• Microgen, Netherlands (Gas Bearings)
</p>
<p>- Application: Combined heat and power
- Design based on licensing agreement with Sunpower, built in China
- Power output: 1,000 We to 1,700 We
</p>
<p>- Units: Over 15,000 units produced  
- Hours: Over 250 million hours of operation accumulated 
- Temperatures: Hot side: 180-560 oC, Cold side: 6-70 oC 
- Fixed operating frequency
</p>
<p>• Qnergy, US (Flexure Bearing)
- Application: Solar and remote power
- Power output: 1,200 We to 5,600 We
</p>
<p>- Units: Over 1,000 units produced  
- Hours: Over 10 million hours of operation accumulated 
- Temperatures: Hot side: 550-650 oC, Cold side: -40-100 oC 
- Floating operating frequency
</p>

<p>Technology Demonstration for Fission Surface Power
</p>
<p>Technology Demonstration Unit (TDU)
• Electrically heated system test in 2016
• Single 12 kWe gas bearing Stirling convertor 
</p>
<p>used to demonstrate full scale ¼ power output
• Demonstrated maximum system net power of 
</p>
<p>9.6 kW and system efficiency of 18.7%
Kilopower Reactor Using Stirling 
TechnologY (KRUSTY)
• Nuclear reactor test 2018 using HEU core
• Two 80 We gas bearing Stirling convertors used 
</p>
<p>to demonstrate full scale ¼ power output
• Tested full power and various fault conditions 
• Demonstrated maximum system net power of 
</p>
<p>183 We and conversion efficiency of 30-34%
FSP TDU installed in 
</p>
<p>Vacuum Facility 6 at GRC
</p>
<p>KRUSTY installed at the 
Nevada National Security Site</p>
<p></p>

<p>MARVEL for Clean Terrestrial Power
</p>
<p>• Microreactor Applications Research Validation and EvaLuation 
(MARVEL)
</p>
<p>• Microreactor designs are currently under development in the 
United States as part of an aggressive government plan to 
combat climate change and reach net-zero emissions by 2050
</p>
<p>• DOE is developing MARVEL 
- Liquid-metal cooled microreactor 
- Four (4) free-piston Stirling engines 
- 100 kilowatts system power output 
- High-assay, low-enriched uranium (HALEU) fuel 
- Built using off-the-shelf components allowing for faster construction
- Located in the Transient REActor Test (TREAT) facility at Idaho 
</p>
<p>National Laboratory
</p>
<p>• Looking forward to learning more!

<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>• Stirling power conversion reduces fuel consumption of a limited supply, 
system waste heat and emitted radiation
</p>
<p>• Free-piston Stirling convertors have been developed for terrestrial and space 
applications since the 1960s
</p>
<p>• Commercial units have operated &gt;250M hrs on 15,000 units, demonstrating 
mature production capability for terrestrial applications
</p>
<p>• Space designs have been demonstrated from 55 We to 25,000 We
</p>
<p>• Reliable long-life Stirling convertors continue to operate beyond 16 years
• Aerojet Rocketdyne’s generator design estimated 293 We at BOM, 19.5% 
</p>
<p>efficiency, and a specific power of 2.6 W/kg with power conversion redundancy 
• Stirling convertors have been matured to TRL 5-6 for RPS applications 
</p>
<p>and to TRL 4-5 for Fission reactor applications</p>
<p></p>
