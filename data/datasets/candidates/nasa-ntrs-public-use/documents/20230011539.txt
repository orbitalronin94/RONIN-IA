<p>Introduction
</p>
<p>Flight Instrumentation and Surface Assets
</p>
<p>Rocket Plume-Surface Interaction (PSI) is a complex, 
multi-phase discipline that describes the lander 
</p>
<p>environment due to the impingement of hot rocket 
exhaust on regolith of planetary bodies 
</p>
<p>Risks Posed by PSI
</p>
<p>Apollo flight experience cannot be extrapolated to 
HLS for PSI à new data and modeling required
</p>
<p>11-deg Tilt on Apollo 15 Lander (NASA)
</p>
<p>Visual Obscuration During Apollo 16 Landing (Metzger, 2010)
</p>
<p>• All propulsive landers experience PSI
• Visual and sensor obscuration, vehicle stability and tilt, 
</p>
<p>localized augmentation of heating, pressure, and 
abrasion damage, debris/ejecta
</p>
<p>Moving Beyond Apollo
• NASA’s Human Landing System (HLS) will take 
</p>
<p>astronauts to the Lunar surface using commercial 
vehicles from SpaceX and Blue Origin, as part of the 
Artemis program
</p>
<p>• Each vehicle is considerably larger 
and uses a different concept of 
operations than that from Apollo
</p>

<p>Propulsion Elements

<p>98 mm GOX/ABS Hybrid Motor (Utah State)
</p>
<p>• Highly under-expanded exhaust plume used to match 
peak and radially scaled distributions for surface 
impingement pressure and shear
</p>
<p>• Firing durations: 4-6 seconds per test run
• PSI testing will use two separate propulsion elements:
• Inert, heated ethane plume (Purdue Univ.)
• GOX/ABS hybrid rocket motor (Utah State Univ.)
• Thrust &lt; 50 lbf
</p>

<p>Vacuum Test Facility
• 60-ft vacuum sphere at NASA Langley Research Center
• Historically used to test PSI for Apollo and Viking
• Provides very large volume to minimize ambient pressure 
</p>
<p>rise during PSI testing

<p>PSI Test Concept
</p>
<p>• Objective: To reduce risk to the HLS lander(s) through 
acquisition of lunar-relevant ground test data to quantify 
and reduce uncertainty in PSI predictions through 
improvements to and validation of modeling capabilities
</p>
<p>• Target scaled flight conditions and an Apollo condition
• Parameters: 
• Nozzle height above regolith
• Plume total pressure
• Plume mass flow rate
</p>
<p>• Target 30 runs per propulsion type:
• Inert Ethane: June – Sept 2024
• Hybrid Motor: Jan – Apr 2025
</p>

<p>Summary
• PSI poses risks to all propulsive landers, as well as 
</p>
<p>nearby surface assets and infrastructure
• PSI testing will reduce risk to HLS lunar landers operating 
</p>
<p>outside of environments and experience from Apollo
• Targeted PSI testing of this type was last conducted using 
</p>
<p>vacuum facilities for the Apollo and Viking programs
• These test data will not be applicable for Mars, but the 
</p>
<p>ability to conduct relevant testing and eventual validation 
of generalized erosion models will extend to PSI for Mars
</p>
