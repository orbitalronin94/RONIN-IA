<div class="slide-content"><p>Galileo Probe Overview</p>
<p>Jovian entry on 7 December 1995 traveling at 47 km/s</p>
<p>Sphere-cone with a 22.2 cm nose radius and a 44.86 degree half angle frustum</p>
<p>Carbon phenolic TPS material (FM 5055G) adapted with opacified resin to mitigate radiative heating</p>
<p>Forebody outfitted with 10 analog resistance ablation detector (ARAD) sensors and four thermistors</p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Pre-Flight Predictions vs Observed Recession</p>
<p>Pre-flight predictions made by Moss and Simmonds in 1982</p>
<p>Viscous Shock-Layer (VSL) solver with thermochemical equilibrium, coupled radiation (tangent slab) and ablation (steady-state equilibrium)</p>
<p>Binary species diffusion with constant Schmidt number</p>
<p>Fully turbulent using two-layer eddy viscosity model from Cebeci</p>
<p>Flowfield modeled with 11% He and 89% H by volume</p>
<p>Measured recession returned from the 10 ARAD sensors showed:</p>
<p>Likely overprediction of recession in the stagnation region</p>
<p>Significant under prediction of recession on the flank</p>

<p>Previous Attempts to Reconcile Observations</p>
<p>Matsuyama et al. (2005) performed thermochemical equilibrium analysis with coupled radiation and ablation</p>
<p>Tangent-slab and binary diffusion with constant Sc</p>
<p>Injection induced turbulence model</p>
<p>Increased He to 13.6% by volume</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Objectives and Approach</p>
<p>Objectives</p>
<p>Revisit Galileo forebody physics modeling approaches</p>
<p>Employer higher model fidelity beyond a “heritage” approach and relax significant assumptions</p>
<p>Investigate the impact of parametric uncertainty and identify key drivers</p>
<p></p>
<p>Modeling approaches Investigated</p>
<p>Coupled ablation</p>
<p>Forebody shape change due to recession </p>
<p>Three-dimensional radiation</p>
<p>Multicomponent diffusion</p>
<p>Ionization potential lowering</p>
<p>State-specific H modeling</p>
<p>Flowfield ray-tracing</p>
<p>Freestream Radiation Absorption</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Coupled Ablation and TPS Modeling</p>
<p>Coupled Ablation</p>
<p>Increase shock standoff – massive blowing from the TPS</p>
<p>Lowers convective heating</p>
<p>Strongly absorbing species form in the boundary layer from ablation products</p>
<p>Partial blockage of incident radiative flux</p>
<p>Forebody shape change due to recession </p>
<p>Preliminary analysis showed the nose should blunt during the descent</p>
<p>Observed nose sharpening leads to less radiative heating</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Impact of Diffusion Modeling</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Previous studies assumed binary diffusion with constant Schmidt number – well suited for Earth and Mars entry modeling</p>
<p></p>
<p>Vastly different molecular weights of species prohibit this assumption</p>
<p></p>
<p>Rigorous Stefan-Maxwell multicomponent diffusion modeled needed to relax assumptions – results in higher convective heating due to increased H diffusion</p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Three-Dimensional Radiative Heating and H Ionization</p>
<p></p>
<p>Flowfield-radiation coupling known to be important</p>
<p>Tangent-slab is typically used for computing radiative heating</p>
<p>Results in about 10% over-prediction of forebody radiative heating compared to 3D radiation via ray-tracing</p>
<p>H Ionization potential lowering </p>
<p>High electron number density increases probability of H ionization</p>
<p>Lowering the potential for H ionization leads to a 10% decrease in final recession</p>
