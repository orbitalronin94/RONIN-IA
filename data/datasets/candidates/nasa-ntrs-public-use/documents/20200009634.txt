Energy, Climate and NASA
In the US, residential and commercial building infrastructure combined
consumes about 40% of total energy usage and emits about 39% of total
CO2emission (DOE/EIA “Annual Energy Outlook 2013”). Thus, increasing
the energy efficiency of buildings is paramount to reducing energy costs
and emissions.
Building codes, as used by local and state enforcement entities are
typically tied to the dominant climate, defined according to a building
climate zone which is based upon a 30-year average of local to regional
surface observations.
Guidelines for these codes, applied to all residential and commercial
buildings, are developed by Department of Energy (DOE) and ASHRAE
(formerly known as the American Society of Hearting, Refrigeration and
Air-Conditioning Engineers).
Using NASA MERRA(-2), we are now working to provide long-term
global surface meteorological parameters to provide key surface
temperature, humidity, wind and precipitation parameters for building
energy performance planning, targeting and verification.

Summary and Conclusions

• Building Energy Managers and Architects need consistent
long-term surface meteorological and solar parameters to
design specifications for new/modified buildings and
monitor energy performance

• NASA’s MERRA-2 data products are being evaluated for
usage by ASHRAE to improve global specification of climate
zones and more detailed design conditions

• Near-real time data products are useful for monitoring
building energy performance using decision support tools
like RETScreen

• NASA LaRC is tailoring the relevant parameters from
MERRA-2, CERES/GEWEX SRB for usage in energy
efficiency; the POWER web site provides parameters in a
variety of data formats using GIS tools.
