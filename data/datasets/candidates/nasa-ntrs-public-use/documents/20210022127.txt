Space Nuclear Propulsion Overview 2

Why is Nuclear Propulsion Attractive for Human 
Missions to Mars?

Short Stay Opposition Mission with Earth Departure  Dates 2028-2045

• Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP) and Nuclear Electric Propulsion (NEP) allows 
for shorter total mission time and shorter trip time (Less exposure to galactic 
cosmic radiation and zero-g)

• Nuclear allows mission robustness and potential abort scenarios
• Fewer rocket launches can save operation time, money, and reduce risk
• Current efforts in NTP are initial steps towards advanced space nuclear power 

and propulsion, which could eventually help enable exploration and development 
of the solar system

Space Nuclear Propulsion Overview 5

How Does Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP) Work?

• Propellant heated directly by a nuclear reactor and thermally expanded/accelerated 
through a nozzle (Low molecular weight propellant – typically Hydrogen)

• Thrust directly related to thermal power of reactor:  100,000 N ≈ 450 MWth at 900 sec

• Specific Impulse directly related to exhaust temperature: 830 - 1000 sec (2300 - 
3100K)

• Specific Impulse improvement over chemical rockets due to lower molecular weight of 
propellant (exhaust stream of O2/H2 engine runs much hotter than NTP)

Space Nuclear Propulsion Overview 6

Development Plan for NTP

Overall Nuclear Thermal Propulsion Strategy:

Develop a subscale nuclear engine that demonstrates a 
viable NTP propulsion system and is scalable to the Mars 
mission engine needs. Evolve to Mars engine system as a 
part of a Mars NP propulsive stage.

1. Develop a HALEU solid solution fuel form that can 
provide reactor exit temperatures > 2700 K 
(corresponds to > 900s Isp).

2. Design a reactor with an eye toward capability to be 
scaled to a size to support Mars mission engine 
needs. (Scaling is to be an engineering solution 
rather than further tech development.)

3. Design and develop a subscale engine (Key 
attribute goals: 10K-15K lbf thrust, 900s Isp)

4. Determine Demonstration option (either ground test 
or demonstration flight).

• Development of a technology maturation plan (TMP) and definition of requirements to 
guide early stage NEP development (TMP development already started in FY21)

• Industry and Government to continue with parallel technology maturation efforts, 
including 

– Early phase risk reduction for high risk elements, targeting near-term TRL 
advancement payoff 

– Fabrication and test of prototype systems 

– A focus on subsystems with historically long lead times for development and/or 
acquisition 

– Modeling & Simulation (M&S) efforts to drive tasking for FY23 (and beyond) (SNP-
sponsored M&S work on reactor already started in FY21)

– System-level analytical assessments of technology/design options to support down-
selections in the NEP system (work already started in FY21)

Development Plan for NEP

SNP plans to use expected NEP funding to target a few high-payoff activities, and is actively 
developing a plan that expands NEP work to a far greater range if more funding becomes available 



Space Nuclear Propulsion Overview 9

Conclusions: Why Nuclear, Why Now?

• Space fission propulsion systems are game changing 
technologies for space exploration and cis-lunar mobility.

• First generation NTP systems provide significant benefits to 
sustained human Mars exploration and other missions.
- Imagine Earth-Mars transit times of 120 days; imagine 540 day total 

Mars mission times; imagine reduced crew health effects from 
cosmic radiation and exposure to microgravity; imagine robust 
architectures including abort capability. 

• Advanced space fission power and propulsion systems will 
enable extremely ambitious space exploration and 
development.
