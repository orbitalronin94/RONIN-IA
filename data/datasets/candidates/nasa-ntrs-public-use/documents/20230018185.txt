<p>Introduction
</p>
<p>➢Cryogenic transfer systems are 
</p>
<p>essential for the development of 
</p>
<p>future ground and low-g systems
</p>
<p>➢Two-phase flow will be present in 
</p>
<p>these systems
</p>
<p>➢Currently there is a need for more 
</p>
<p>accurate, direct cryogenic data-
</p>
<p>anchored models for various 
</p>
<p>boiling and two-phase phenomena
</p>
<p>3

<p>LH2 Storage Tank and transfer line at 
</p>
<p>NASA's KSC</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Introduction, Cont’d
</p>
<p>➢ Existing room-temperature based boiling correlations do a poor job at predicting 
</p>
<p>cryogenic flow phenomena
</p>
<p>➢ For steady state or heated tube, the disparity between these existing correlations and 
</p>
<p>cryogenic data is as high as 400%
</p>
<p>➢ New universal cryogenic flow boiling correlations were recently developed and 
</p>
<p>anchored to the largest cryogenic flow boiling database assembled in the world 
</p>
<p>to-date
</p>
<p>➢ Current work will show implementation of these new correlations into Thermal 
</p>
<p>Desktop (TD) and compare against TD’s built-in code for two historical datasets 
</p>
<p>using liquid hydrogen
</p>
<p>➢ Recent work has been performed to validate the new cryogenic correlations for other 
</p>
<p>cryogens and results show an improvement over TD’s built-in correlations
</p>
<p>4</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>New Universal Correlations
</p>
<p>➢New Universal Cryogenic Correlations were developed at 
</p>
<p>Purdue University in collaboration with NASA Glenn
</p>
<p>➢Correlations were developed for the location of onset of 
</p>
<p>nucleate boiling (ONB), pre-Critical Heat Flux (CHF) heat 
</p>
<p>transfer coefficient, post-CHF heat transfer coefficient, and the 
</p>
<p>location of the CHF. Subcooled film boiling was not included
</p>
<p>➢The aim was then to create a smooth, continuous predictive 
</p>
<p>curve for wall superheat as a function of heat flux by patching 
</p>
<p>the individual correlations together
</p>
<p>7</p>
<p></p>

<p>Conclusion
</p>
<p>➢ Universal Correlations show modest improvement over Thermal 
</p>
<p>Desktop’s Built-in Correlations for liquid hydrogen
</p>
<p>When subcooled film boiling points are excluded Universal 
</p>
<p>Correlations predict Lewis wall temperatures within 20% vs. 69% for 
</p>
<p>Built-in
</p>
<p>➢ Universal Correlations predict Hendricks wall temperatures within 
</p>
<p>38% vs. 54% for Built-in
</p>
<p>➢ Future work includes using Universal Correlations for predicting 
</p>
<p>transfer line performance in future systems
</p>
<p>➢ Correlations have now been validated for 4 different cryogens 
</p>
<p>across a range of flow conditions
24</p>
