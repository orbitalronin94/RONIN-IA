<p>01 OPERATIONAL CONTEXT &amp; PROGRAM BACKGROUND 
</p>
<p>Predicting which runway, a departing aircraft will be used is one of the foundational inference problems in 
air traffic flow management. At major hub airports, runway assignment drives downstream predictions of 
departure fix loading, sector sequencing, and Traffic Management Initiative (TMI) effectiveness.  
</p>
<p>The NASA Digital Information Platform (DIP) sub-project developed the legacy departure runway prediction 
models through a structured collaboration with FAA system specialists and airline flight operations 
personnel. These models emerged from iterative cycles of hypothesis, SME review, operational testing with 
live NAS data, and performance analysis — conducted in real operational settings, not simulations. 
</p>
<p>Live Operational Validation — What That Means 
</p>
<p>When this document refers to "operational validation," it means the models ran against live FAA data feeds, 
their outputs were evaluated by working TMCs, and the feedback loop involved real airline operations 
personnel making actual traffic management decisions. This is a significantly higher bar than retrospective or 
simulation-based validation. 
</p>
<p> Critical Context: The legacy models described here are an operationally hardened standard born based on 
live FAA deployment. They are presented here as the performance bar — not as artifacts being 
transferred. The case this document makes is for the agentic development methodology that can produce 
equivalent or better models at a fraction of the time investment. 
</p>
<p>Scope of This Analysis 
</p>
<p>This analysis focuses on the one-hour prediction horizon, the most operationally meaningful window for 
departure runway assignment. Results are presented for two airports with distinct operational profiles: 
</p>
<p>• DFW: Dallas/Fort Worth International, one of the highest-throughput facilities in the NAS 
</p>
<p>• IAH: George Bush Intercontinental, a complex multi-runway facility with significant configuration 
variability and known legacy model deficiencies 
 
</p>
<p>02 THE LEGACY EXPERT MODEL — OPERATIONAL BASELINE 
</p>
<p>The legacy ML model represents the state of practice in NASA-validated departure runway prediction. Its 
development required close SME partnership to identify, curate, and validate the feature set. 
</p>
<p>Architecture &amp; Feature Set 
</p>
<p>Implemented using XGBoost with expert-selected hyperparameters, the model draws on eight features 
identified through SME collaboration and historical data analysis: 
</p>
<p>• Departure Fix / Route Sequence: planned waypoint exit path from the terminal area 
</p>
<p>• Airport Configuration: active arrival and departure runway set at time of prediction 
</p>
<p>• Proposed Departure Time: estimated wheels-up time at prediction moment 
</p>
<p>• Wake Turbulence Category: aircraft weight class driving separation requirements 
</p>
<p>• Last ASDE-X Position: surface surveillance position, relevant as aircraft approaches departure 
</p>
<p>• Filed Flight Plan: route structure filed with FAA TFMS </p>

<p>03 THE AGENTIC AI PLATFORM 
</p>
<p>NASA has developed an Agentic AI platform that autonomously executes the full ML development lifecycle, 
from raw database exploration through feature engineering, model selection, hyperparameter optimization, 
and performance evaluation. The platform represents a strategic capability: it decouples ML development 
throughput from the availability of domain experts. 
</p>
<p> Scope Note: The Agentic AI models presented in this report were developed after the operational 
deployments described in Section 01. They were not part of the live FAA environment runs. This is 
presented as independent validation: a fully automated system, given the same data and evaluation 
framework, produces results that meet or exceed expert-crafted development. 
</p>
<p>Platform Architecture 
</p>
<p>The platform, illustrated in Figure 3, is built around Large Language Models (LLMs) primarily OpenAI GPT 
models, with an architecture that supports LLM substitution as requirements evolve, integrated with an on-
premises Model Context Protocol (MCP) server.  
</p>
<p>The platform interacts with the DIP's Fuser database (Apache Parquet / DuckDB) containing: 
</p>
<p>• Flight-level operational summaries and trajectory histories 
</p>
<p>• Airport configuration state timelines 
</p>
<p>• Time-Based Flow Management (TBFM) data 
</p>
<p>• Traffic Management Initiative records (GDP, MIT restrictions) 
</p>

<p>Agent 1: Feature Engineering Agent 
</p>
<p>The feature engineering agent applies a bootstrap-aggregation-inspired methodology to rapidly assess the 
predictive value of all available database features, without requiring a human expert to pre-select 
candidates. In each run: 
</p>
<p>• Sample broadly: N=10 independent random feature subsets are drawn from the full database 
</p>
<p>• Train efficiently: Lightweight classifiers are trained on each subset using a two-week data window 
</p>
<p>• Rank objectively: Features are ranked by model performance contribution and importance scores 
across all subsets 
</p>
<p>• Output: A prioritized feature list, free of expert familiarity bias, that drives the optimization agent 
</p>
<p>This approach systematically surfaces signals that SME-driven selection might miss — particularly derived or 
interaction features that emerge from the data rather than from operational intuition. 
</p>
<p>Agent 2: Model Selection &amp; Optimization Agent 
</p>
<p>Given the top-ranked features, the model optimization agent conducts a comprehensive hyperparameter 
search using Optuna, an open-source Bayesian optimization framework. For this study, a LightGBM classifier 
was evaluated across the following search space:

<p> 
Feature Discovery: The Critical Differentiator 
</p>
<p>The most analytically significant result is not the aggregate accuracy numbers alone — it is what the 
engineering agent discovered through experimentation with the data. 
</p>
<p>At IAH: The agent retained the established predictors (departure fix, configuration, wake turbulence, 
departure time) and independently identified configuration change timestamps and estimated fix-crossing 
times as important predictors, features available in the DIP database but never surfaced through the manual 
expert process. These additions directly explain the IAH performance gain and is visualized in Figure 6. 
</p>
<p>At DFW: Fewer novel predictors emerged — informative. The SME process at DFW was particularly 
thorough, and the legacy feature set appears near optimal for that facility's operational dynamics. This is a 
case where agentic validation confirms the quality of prior expert work. 
</p>
<p> Key Finding: The agentic platform does not merely replicate expert knowledge — it extends it. At IAH, it 
found features that the prior expert analysis missed, and those features drove measurable performance 
improvements on an already operationally validated system. At DFW, it independently confirmed that the 
expert feature set was near-optimal. 
</p>

<p>05 THE CASE FOR AGENTIC ADOPTION 
</p>
<p>This study provides independent, data-backed validation that agentic ML development is not just a 
convenience — it is a promising methodology for predictive modeling in operational aviation environments. 
The implications for FAA facilities and industry partners are direct. 
</p>
<p>Why the Agentic Approach Wins Pound-for-Pound 
</p>
<p>• Broader feature coverage: The agent explores the full database systematically, unconstrained by 
expert familiarity or cognitive bias. It surfaces novel predictors that SME processes might miss. 
</p>
<p>• Faster time-to-capability: Feature engineering cycles that took weeks of expert coordination now 
complete in automated runs. The development cycle is compressed dramatically. </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>

</p>

</p>
<p>• Reproducible and auditable: All agent decisions are logged. Hyperparameter searches are 
deterministic given a fixed seed. The process can be independently verified. 
</p>
<p>• Scalable without proportional SME investment: Generalization to new airports, new prediction 
horizons, and new operational targets can be onboard without a full expert engagement cycle each 
time. 
</p>
<p>• Bias-free model selection: The agent evaluates model architectures and hyperparameters objectively, 
without the methodological familiarity bias that individual researchers bring to manual selection. 
</p>
<p>What This Does Not Replace 
</p>
<p>The agentic platform is an accelerant for development, not a replacement for operational validation. Expert 
review of agent-selected features and final model behavior remains essential before any live deployment. 
What changes is where expert time is spent: from exhaustive feature search and manual hyperparameter 
tuning to targeted validation and operational integration. 
</p>
<p> Forward Position: All future predictive models in NASA's air traffic management research pipeline will be 
developed through the agentic platform. The combination of operationally validated knowledge 
embedded in the DIP architecture and the efficiency of autonomous ML development positions NASA to 
deliver greater capability to FAA and industry partners on a significantly compressed timeline. 
</p>
<p> 
06 CONCLUSIONS 
</p>
<p>This white paper documents a pivotal transition in NASA's predictive modeling program for the National 
Airspace System. The departure runway prediction models at DFW and IAH represent the operationally-
hardened state of practice — the product of live FAA deployment, expert collaboration, and iterative 
refinement under real operational conditions. 
</p>
<p>The Agentic AI platform meets that bar — and at IAH, clears it. Key conclusions: 
</p>
<p>• Agentic feature engineering surfaces novel predictors that rigorous SME processes did not identify, 
directly improving IAH prediction accuracy. 
</p>
<p>• Agent-generated models achieve operational-grade performance across two airports with 
significantly different operational profiles. 
</p>
<p>• Consistent hyperparameter convergence across five independent experiments demonstrates 
platform stability and reproducibility. 
</p>
<p>• The case for adoption is data-backed: the methodology produces equivalent or superior results in 
automated, repeatable cycles, without the significant time consumed by the experts.  
</p>
<p>For FAA and industry stakeholders evaluating NASA's predictive modeling capabilities, the message is clear: 
the agentic development approach is the right methodology going forward. It is faster, broader, more 
reproducible, and produces results that match or exceed what the expert-intensive hand-crafted 
development achieved. The operational knowledge embedded in NASA's DIP platform provides the 
foundation; the agentic pipeline provides the velocity. 
</p>
