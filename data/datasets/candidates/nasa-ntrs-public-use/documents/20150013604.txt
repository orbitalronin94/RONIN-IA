INTRODUCTION 

In accordance with the request of the Materiel Center, 

Wright Field, supplementary spin-tunnel tests have been made 

on a 1/20-scale model of the-Bell P-39D airplane. 	 The 

purpose of these tests was t . obtain information as to the 

spin characteristics of the current production version of 

-	 the airplane designated as the P-39D-l.	 It is understood 

that the Bell Aircraft Corporation has been requested by 

the Army Air Forces to perform flight spin demonstrations 

of the P-39D-1. 

Tests were made for the normal loading of the airplane 

in the clean condition and for a loading simulating removal 

of wing-gun ammunition. 

Mr..F. D. Townsend, Bell Aircraft Corporation repre-

sentative, witnessed most of the tests.'

Comparison between model and airplane results (references 

2 and 3) indicates that because of scale and tunnel effects, 

lack of detail in the model, and differences in operators' 

technique, the spin-tunnel results are not always in complete 

agreement with full-scale spinning data.	 In general, for a

given loading condition and control setting, the model steady-

spin-results have shown a somewhat lower angle of attack, a 

somewhat higher rate of descent, and from 50 to 100 more 

outward sideslip at a given angle of attack.	 The comparison 

made in reference 3 showed that 80 percent of the model 

recoveries predicted satisfactorily the corresponding full-

scale recoveries, and that 10 percent overestimated and 10 

percent underestimated the full-scale recoveries.

DISCUSSION 

There was no fin offset on the model for the tests 

witnessed by the Bell representative although the airplane 

has its fin offset 2 1 , leading edge to the left. 	 Results 

obtained when the model was spun to the right and to the 

left varied slightly, and, as the results from the right 

spins appeared slightly conservative, the regular test 

program was run to the right.	 Subsequent check tests, 

made with the correct fin offset indicated little effect of 

this factor, : and accordingly, the results for the model 

without fin offset are presented. 

The results of the current tests were as follows: 

For normal loading, clean condition, and normal 

spinning control configuration (rudder full with the spin, 

elevator full up, and ailerons neutral), the spinwas 

oscillatory, the angle of attack varying from approximately 

350 to 500.	 Recovery took place in lt to 2 turns by 
rapid full rudder reversal. When the rudder was merely 

neutralized, however, recovery characteristics were unsatis-

factory.	 Setting the elevator at neutral or down before 

reversing the rudder seriously retarded recovery. It is 

felt on the basis of previous results on the P-39D model, 

that moving the elevator down briskly after the rudder has 

been completely reversed will aid recovery somewhat. 	 In

general, aileron-against settings (left aileron upand

right aileron down in a right spin) aided recovery, whereas 

aileLron-with settings retarded recovery.	 When ailerons were 

full with the spin and the elevator full down, the model con-

tinued spinning even though the rudder was full against the 

spin.	 With the mass distribution along the wings decreased 

(IX and I	 decreased 25 percent 'x) to simulate the airplane 

condition when the wing-gun ammunition is expended, recoveries 

by rapid full rudder reversal were satisfactory from all 

elevator-up spins.	 Setting the elevator at neutral or down

before reversing the rudder led to slow recoveries unless the 

ailerons were full with the spin.	 For this loading, it 

should be noted there was a partial reversal of aileron effect	 b 

in that when the elevator was neutral or down, setting ailerons 

full with the spin expedited recovery.

CONCLUSIONS 

The model results indicate the following spin characteris-

tics for the P-39D-1 airplane:



-7-

1. The recovery characteristics of the P-39D-1 

airplane will be quite similar to those . obtained for 

the P-39D airplane. 

2. In the normal clean condition, for the normal spin 

control configuration, the spin will be oscillatory. 

Recovery will-be satisfactory provided the stick is held full 

back while-the rudder Is reversed fully and rapidly . .	 It 

-is recommended that the stick be moved briskly forward after 

the rudder has been completely reversed. 

3. For the normal loading, setting ailerons full 

against the spin will generally steepen the spin and expedite 

recovery. When wing-gun airnunition has been expended, 

however, ailerons should be set full with the spin to 

expedite recovery.
