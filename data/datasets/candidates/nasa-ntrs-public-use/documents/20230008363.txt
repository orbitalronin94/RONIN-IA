<p>3OFT 2023
</p>
<p>Brief Introduction of RST
</p>
<p>• RST Payload is composed of the Optical Telescope
Assembly (OTA), Coronagraph Instrument (CGI), and 
Wide Field Instrument (WFI)
</p>
<p>• CGI and WFI share the same Primary and Secondary 
Mirrors
</p>
<p>• RST WFI channel has field 0.8 x 0.4 deg2, which is 
about 100x larger than the Hubble Space Telescope!</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>4OFT 2023
</p>
<p>Brief Introduction of Wide Field Instrument
</p>
<p>Element Wheel Assembly
Focal Plane Assembly
</p>
<p>• WFI includes Element Wheel Assembly (EWA), Focal Plane Assembly (FPA), and Calibration system 
</p>
<p>FPA integrates 18 H4RG detectors 
Each detector is a 4096 x 4096 array of 10 μm pixels
FPA is mounted on a hexapod to allow tip/tilt/focus 
adjustment
</p>
<p>Prism
</p>
<p>Grism
EWA houses:
• 8 science filters, Grism, Prism, and a dark element
• All elements are confocal
• Element operating temperature is ~175K</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>Grism and Prism As-built Modeling work</p>
<p></p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>6OFT 2023
</p>
<p>Grism and Prism as-built modeling work simulates the lab alignment and testing activities of 
Grism and Prism assemblies by the following steps on optical models:
</p>
<p>1. Apply as-fabricated values of each optical elements
2. Apply metrology data 
3. Tweak/optimize alignment/fabrication parameters within uncertainty range to let Zernike 
</p>
<p>coefficients of each field match to the coefficients from interferometric testing
4. Double check the spectral dispersion performance of as-built models to make sure it 
</p>
<p>matches to the results from spectral verification testing
After steps 1~4, we got room temperature as-built models of Grism/Prism
</p>

<p>7OFT 2023
</p>
<p>Starting from room temperature as-built model:
5. Apply room temp-to-cryo changes from STOP analysis and cryogenic metrology data
6. Adjust/optimize elements’ positions within the ancillary metrology uncertainty ranges to 
</p>
<p>let  models WFE matches to the cryo interferometric testing result by comparing low-
order Zernike coefficients
</p>
<p>7. Adjust higher-order Zernikes determined by STOP modeling based on cryogenic 
interferometric measurements
</p>
<p>8. Add mid-spatial SFE if needed  
After step 8, the cryo as-built Grism/Prism models are ready
</p>

<p>10OFT 2023

<p>Flow Chart of Flight Performance Prediction - EDIT
</p>
<p>• Currently, no as-built telescope but telescope component information 
• So, we perform a Monte Carlo of a pseudo as-built telescope which includes telescope mirrors’ as-fabricated 
</p>
<p>information, alignment errors and compensators’ adjustment, plus on-orbit perturbations and on-orbit refocus 
performed in Telescope+Filter operational mode.
</p>
<p>• Grism and Prism mode follow a simplified procedure without any refocus adjustments and inheriting the FPA 
position from Filter mode</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>Grism End of Life Performance Prediction
</p>
<p>After an EOL Monte Carlo 
simulation, the 95th percentile trial 
is selected; then we find the 
maximum RMS WFE at the 
boundary of the lowest RMS 95% 
of the focal plane area.
</p>

which meets the requirement 
(160nm).
</p>

<p>13OFT 2023
</p>
<p>• We generated as-built Grism and Prism models based on data at the component and 
assembly level and augmented the data with STOP results to inform and bound them
</p>
<p>• We have both room temperature and cryogenic models that mimic the test results within 
the tests’ experimental errors
</p>
<p>• We predicted the observatory level end-of-life performance of Grism and Prism, both meet 
requirements with adequate margin
</p>
<p>• Going forward, these models will be further refined as the testing progresses and will 
serve as starting points for on orbit models that can be used for science simulations
</p>
