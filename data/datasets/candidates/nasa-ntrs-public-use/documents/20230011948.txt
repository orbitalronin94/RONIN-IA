<p>Hot Isostatic Pressing (HIP)
</p>
<p>• HIP is a process of heating and 
pressurizing inert gas around a specimen.
</p>
<p>• This is used to collapse surface pores, 
close cracks, and initiate recrystallization.
</p>
<p>• Often used to obtain better 
microstructure and more isotropic 
properties in AM parts.

<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>1. Recrystallization and texture change did not occur during high-vacuum annealing, but did occur 
in hot isostatic pressing.
</p>
<p>2. HfO2 formed in all specimens and grew exponentially with temperature until saturated. Oxide 
formation showed a strong negative correlation with hardness.
</p>
<p>3. Anisotropy of the AP and HIPed specimens was driven by grain aspect ratio, while anisotropy in 
HVAed specimens depended on oxide growth.
</p>
<p>4. Overall, HVA and HIP cannot be used interchangeably but both may serve as viable options for 
future material engineering endeavors.
</p>
