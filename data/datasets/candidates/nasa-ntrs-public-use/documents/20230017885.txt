<p>NASA Ames Research Center has been working on enabling the vision of 
spacecraft “swarms”  for over a decade. 
• Spacecraft Swarms:
</p>
<p>– Groups of spacecraft cooperating with each other to achieve a goal
– Acts as a single unit versus needing to command and control each spacecraft separately
– Include autonomy so that they can change behavior based on engineering or science 
</p>
<p>measurements
• Advantages:
</p>
<p>– Multi-point science data collection (e.g. Helioswarm)
– Improved overall robustness due to systems redundancy 
– Potential for upgrades by adding additional spacecraft to the swarm
– Reduced communication needs for large group of spacecraft 
– Enables opportunistic science
</p>
<p>NASA Ames – Spacecraft Swarms
</p>
<p>Starling is a mission to test and demonstrate key 
technologies that are essential to this vision. </p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>Starling Project Summary
• NPR 7120.8 Research and Technology Project
• Starling is flying 4 x 6U spacecraft in LEO
• Launched via Rocket Lab Electron
</p>
<p>(July 18, 2023)
• 575km, sun-synchronous orbit
• 6-month mission ops for original swarm technology 
</p>
<p>experiments
• The Starling mission will test multiple swarm technologies
</p>
<p>• Reconfiguration and Orbit Maintenance Experiments 
Onboard (ROMEO)
</p>
<p>• Mobile Adhoc Network (MANET)
• Starling Formation-Flying Optical Experiment (StarFOX)
• Distributed Spacecraft Autonomy (DSA)
</p>
<p>• Mission extension to Starling 1.5 via software upload to 
demonstrate autonomous collision avoidance with SpaceX 
Starlink constellation
</p>
<p>• Funding
• Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD)
</p>
<p>• NASA Small Spacecraft Technology (SST) Program
• NASA Game Changing Development (GCD) Program

<p>Swarm Technologies in Starling
Cluster flight control algorithms: 
</p>
<p>(ROMEO – Autonomous maneuver planning and execution)
• Demonstrates onboard autonomous maneuver planning and execution to maintain 
</p>
<p>relative vehicle positions.  Utilizes Cluster Flight Application (CFA) software from 
Emergent Space Technologies.
</p>
<p>Network communication protocols:
(MANET – Crosslink/Networking)
• Uses the BATMAN infrastructure-less adhoc network for mobile devices to 
</p>
<p>demonstrate a reliable self-configuration network in space applicable to 100s of 
spacecraft. Crosslink radios provided by Cesium Astro.
</p>
<p>Navigation algorithms: 
(StarFOX –Absolute and Relative Navigation)
• Enables onboard vision-based trajectory estimation of agents in swarm. Uses 
</p>
<p>standard star trackers and the Absolute and Relative Trajectory Measurement System 
(ARTMS) autonomous angles-only swarm navigation architecture developed by 
Stanford’s Space Rendezvous Lab. 
</p>
<p>Autonomous reactive operations software:
(DSA – Distributed Spacecraft Autonomy)
• Demonstrates autonomous reconfiguration of observation tactics in response to 
</p>
<p>external sensor data. Objective is to maximize the scientific productivity of a 
spacecraft swarm by quickly reacting to changing conditions.
</p>
<p>The intent is to test each technology to 
understand what works, what are the 
limitations, and what is still needed in 
terms of swarm technology development.</p>
