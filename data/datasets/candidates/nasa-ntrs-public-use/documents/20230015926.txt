<p>Motivation</p>
<p>Phenolic resins, commonly utilized in spacecraft heatshield fabrication, have historically encountered challenges due to their inherent brittleness [1]. To overcome this issue, researchers have explored the incorporation of additives into the polymer backbone to enhance flexibility and durability [2].</p>
<p>This study aims to improve the flexibility and durability of highly crosslinked polymers by incorporating long-chain aliphatic silicon-based additives with varying molecular during material synthesis.</p>
<p>By increasing the flexibility of these brittle polymers, they can be effectively employed in constructing intricate curvatures for heatshields, eliminating the need for gap fillers.</p>
<p>Abstract</p>
<p>In this study, six additives were investigated, comparing them to a phenolic baseline. Various characterization techniques, including thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA), nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), were employed to assess the properties of the materials.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Discussion</p>
<p>NMR uses a magnetic field and radio waves to identify the molecular structure of a polymer and was used in this study to verify the incorporation of additives into the network structure of the phenolic resin.</p>
<p>FTIR spectroscopy was employed to corroborate the findings obtained from NMR analysis. It revealed the presence of silicon oxide and silicon hydroxide, particularly in Additive B. The notable absence of these peaks in the baseline spectrum strongly suggests a chemical interaction between Additive B and the phenolic resin matrix. </p>
<p>Samples were subjected to DMA to assess their flexural strength under linear loading conditions ranging from 0.1 to 20 MPa. The lower flexural modulus observed in samples containing Additive B and K indicates improved flexibility.</p>
<p>TGA was conducted to ensure that the modifications did not compromise char yield. Notably, both Additive B and K exhibited only a 3% reduction in char yield compared to the Baseline, suggesting that the increased flexibility did not come at the expense of this critical thermochemical property.</p>
<p>The thermal conductivity results illustrated that phenolic resin with Additive K and B displays reduced thermal conductivity in comparison to the Baseline. Conversely, additives with higher molecular weight polymers exhibit increased thermal conductivity. This demonstrates the potential for tailoring thermal properties while maintaining flexibility in heat shield applications.</p>
<p>Conclusion</p>
<p>These analyses collectively highlight the successful integration of silicon-based additives into phenolic resin to enhance flexibility without compromising other essential properties, positioning these modified resins as promising candidates heat shield materials.</p>
<p></p>
<p>NMR data show significant peaks corresponding to the amide group for Additive B indicating its reaction and incorporation into the phenolic resin network. Additive D displayed a lower silica content and incomplete reaction due to hinderance. </p>
<p>FTIR analysis demonstrates successful incorporation of polysiloxane-based additives into the  phenolic resin structure.</p>
<p>DMA results indicate increased flexibility of phenolic resin for additives B and K.</p>
<p>TGA data demonstrates that while Additive B effectively increased the flexibility of Baseline materials, it did not compromise char yield. </p>
<p>Additives B and K provide lower density than Baseline as seen in Table 1.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Future Work</p>
<p>Infusion into Carbon Matrix</p>
<p>The infusion of improved phenolic solutions into carbon matrices presents an intriguing prospect. The future goal of integrating these modified resins into carbon-based materials is to develop advanced composites with tailored mechanical and thermal properties for the application of heatshield technologies.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Space Environment Testing:</p>
<p>To further assess the properties of phenolic samples with these additives, samples were sent to the Materials International Space Station Experiment (MISSE-19). Exposure of the additives in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) environment will provide insights into how these materials perform in the harsh conditions of space, offering valuable data for space-based applications and reentry systems.</p>
