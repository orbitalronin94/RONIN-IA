<p>Formation and Evolution of Pits in DSMC
In order to model the formation and evolution of etch pits, we have 
developed a new capability within the DSMC code SPARTA (Stochastic 
PArallel Rarefied-gas Time-accurate Analyzer) [4]. It can model the 
material removal in the presence of active sites, leading to the formation 
of etch pits. In this work, we focus on FiberForm®, a commonly used base 
material within many of NASA’s thermal protection system materials 
(TPS). The microstructure of virgin FiberForm (FF) was obtained directly 
from X-ray microtomography experiments (Fig. 2).
</p>
<p>1Analytical Mechanics Associates Inc. at NASA Ames Research Center, Moffett Field, CA 94035, USA
</p>
<p>With the onset of pitting, the surface area of the FiberForm structure 
initially increases, and the newly generated surface is highly reactive due 
to the presence of active sites along the perimeter of etch pits. However, 
after a maximum surface area is reached, the surface area begins to drop 
and will eventually drop to zero as the material is fully etched away. This 
directly influences the reactivity, which can be expected to evolve 
nonlinearly and highly dependent on the pitting geometry.
</p>
<p>DSMC Pitting Simulation Results on FiberForm 
</p>
<p>Thermal conductivity and Young’s modulus of elasticity are observed to 
degrade due to pitting in both the in-plane (IP) and through-the-thickness 
(TT) principal directions. Higher the number of initial defects, greater the 
decrease in both the material properties. The microstructure is observed to 
influence the property values. Future work will extend the microstructure 
size to analyze if this influence persists.  Pitting weakens the FiberForm 
structure significantly, and it is, therefore, crucial to understand in detail 
the degradation of the material properties.
</p>
<p>References
</p>
<p>Erosion of carbon due to oxidation does not occur uniformly but through 
the formation of localized etch pits because of active surface sites [1]. 
These active sites are formed due to the defects on the carbon surface and 
have much higher reactivity than non-defective sites. Thus, active sites are 
the first to react during ablation, resulting in their removal. This causes all 
the neighboring atoms to be defective and increases their reactivity, thus 
leading to localized carbon removal around these active sites [2]. These 
etch pits can have a detrimental effect on the thermal properties and 
structural integrity. In this work, we are first studying the formation and 
evolution of etch pits on carbon fiber structures within direct simulation 
Monte Carlo (DSMC). Then, the effect of etch pits on the material 
properties is analyzed using the Porous Microstructure Analysis (PuMA) 
software [3]. Understanding and analyzing the pitting process will allow 
us to better model the recession and roughness evolution of carbon-based 
ablative Thermal Protection Systems (TPS), more accurately predict their 
degradation in terms of material properties, and potentially uncover 
previously unknown failure mechanisms.
</p>
