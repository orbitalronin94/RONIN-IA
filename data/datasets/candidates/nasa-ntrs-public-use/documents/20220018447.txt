Introduction
• When intercalibrating analogous sensor channel reflectances the spectral band 

difference induced TOA reflectance disparity must be accounted for using a 
spectral band adjustment factor (SBAF). 
• The SBAF is specific to the underlying Earth viewed scene and sensor pair spectral response 

function (SRF)
• Radiative transfer predicted at sensor pair reflectances or hyper-spectral 

reflectances convolved with the sensor pair SRFs pseudo reflectances provide 
site specific SBAFs
• Multiple site inter-calibration spectrally adjusted reflectances determine the SBAF 

uncertainty to eventually reveal the true sensor calibration difference
• The VIIRS M5 and I1 reflectance ratios provides an opportunity to validate SBAF 

coefficients spatially over an image
• Having the disparate M5 and I1 band SRFs on the same sensor eliminates the temporal, 

spatial and angular matching noise of sensor pair SNO events
• The 4 I01 0.375-km pixel reflectances are averaged into the M 750-km pixel so that M and I 

band reflectances can be compared spatially over the granule
• The M and I bands are calibrated against the same solar diffuser, so that the M/I reflectance 

ratio should be temporally constant

Conclusion

• Radiative transfer predicted at sensor pair reflectances or hyper-spectral 
reflectance convolved with the sensor pair SRFs pseudo reflectances provide site 
specific SBAFs
• Multiple site inter-calibration SBAF adjusted reflectance ratios eventually revealed the true 

sensor calibration difference

• The VIIRS M5 and I1 reflectance ratios provides a way to validate the 0.64µm 
channel SBAF coefficients
• The SCIAMACHY 2nd order and GOME-2 linear fit SBAFs were optimal in providing spatially 

uniform M5/I1 spectrally corrected reflectance ratios over all-sky tropical ocean scenes –
correcting both clear-sky and bright clouds scenes with differing SBAF spectral adjustments

• Dome-C and DCC SBAFs had small SBAF adjusted reflectances, whereas Libya-4 had large 
SBAF adjusted reflectances

• The DCC, Dome-C and Libya-4 sited were spectrally uniform spatial sites
• Cloud contamination impacted the Libya-4 SBAF more than the Dome-C site
• The Hyperion spectral resolution is too coarse to be convolved with the M5 SRF
