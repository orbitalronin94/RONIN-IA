NASA’s Aeronautics Research Mission Directorate, Fundamental Aeronautics Program
The Subsonic Fixed Wing Project conducts research to improve prediction methods and
technologies that will produce lower noise, lower emissions, and higher performing subsonic
aircraft for the Next Generation Air Transportation System.
Subproject: FW Structural Efficiency: Lightweight, multi-objective and multifunctional
structures (e.g., structures that can carry load and attenuate noise at the same time) are being
developed to reduce the weight of both airframes and engines. Adaptive and flexible wing
structures with aeroelastic tailoring offer significant weight reduction due to the elimination
of a heavy actuation system. Extensive use of polymer matrix composites (PMCs) in airframe
and engine structures (where applicable) will offer significant weight reduction opportunities.
Research on composite technology is focused on developing alternate processes that will
enable fabrication of large structures, increasing the temperature capability toward greater
usage in engine and supersonic airframe structures, developing advanced structural concepts,
and modeling damage initiation and progression in composite structures.
Element: FW SE High Aspect Ratio Wing - Actuator/Structure Integration

Fiber optics in this effort: Use multi-core fiber optic shape sensing to measure the
shape of flexible surfaces during flight with the possibility of using the shape
measurements as feedback in the control system.

Near-term research areas
•

How should a shape sensing fiber be installed/secured onto a flexible aerospace structure?
– Want the fiber to bend, not stretch, as the structure flexes.
– Want to avoid pinch points and micro-bending.
– Want the fiber to move and twist according to shape changes, free from external twisting
influences.
– Avoid adhesives if possible to ease installation.
– Possible solution: Teflon sleeving-avoid securing at opposite ends of a straight section that may
experience tension or compression.

•

How is accurate installation ensured…or how is installation position documented?
– Past efforts show how difficult it can be to position a fiber at exactly where the test plan
specifies.
– Possible solution: Lay the sleeving out as close to the design plan as possible and use
photogrammetry to measure the exact installation location post-install.

•

How is accuracy verified?
– Possible solution: ground-based testing with photogrammetry providing 3rd party data.

•

What general corrections/calibrations can be done to improve accuracy?
– Solution TBD

• The Basics

Triangulation
–

•

Triangulation is the principle used by photogrammetry to produce 3dimensional point measurements. By mathematically intersecting converging
lines in space, the precise location of a target can be determined. In
photogrammetry the two-dimensional (x, y) location of a target on the image is
measured to produce the lines. By taking pictures from at least two different
locations and measuring the same target in each picture a "line of sight" is
developed from each camera location to the target. If the camera location and
aiming direction are known, the lines can be mathematically intersected to
produce the XYZ coordinates of each targeted point.

Resection
–

•

The fundamental principle used by photogrammetry is triangulation. By taking
photographs from at least two different locations, so-called "lines of sight" can
be developed from each camera to points on the object. These lines of sight
(sometimes called rays owing to their optical nature) are mathematically
intersected to produce the 3-dimensional coordinates of the points of interest.

Resection is the procedure used to determine the final position and aiming
(called the orientation) of the camera when a picture is taken. Typically all the
points that are seen and known in XYZ in the image are used to determine this
orientation.

Bundle Adjustment
–

The Bundle Adjustment is the program that processes the photographic
measurements to produce the final XYZ coordinates of all the measured points.
In order to do this, it must triangulate the target points, resect the pictures and
self-calibrate the camera.

Photogrammetry to fiber x,y,z
• The fiber is sleeved in Teflon-impregnated fiber glass braiding.
• Retro-reflective tape strips (2 mm thick) wrap the sleeving at nonspecific locations along the sleeving.
• The photogrammetry system gives the (x,y,z) locations of the
retroreflective points along the sleeving.
• The photogrammetry (x,y,z) points are spline-fitted with point
number serving as the arbitrary parametric variable.
• The resultant fit is numerically evaluated for arc length vs.
parametric variable.
• Specific (x,y,z) locations along the fiber can then be determined
using interpolated arc length/parametric variable values and the
photogrammetry spline fit.
• The results presented here refer to the locations of FBG’s along the
fiber (1 cm intervals).

Error sources
• FBG measurement errors
• External twisting influences on the fiber, either
from sleeve installation or sleeve twisting during
the flap flexing
• Nonlinear elastic behavior in the fiber
• Core arrangement not consistent along fiber-leads
to incorrect curvature and bend direction
calculations.
• Errors are allowed to integrate, i.e. there are not
correction routines using the intersection points of
the fiber

Conclusion & Future work
• Photogrammetry as a verification tool for fiber
optic shape sensor position has been
demonstrated.
• However, further investigation is required in:
– Core arrangement calibration
– Nonlinear elasticity calibration
– Correction algorithms utilizing known intersection
points along the fiber
– “Smarter” fiber layout – measurements respond
well to curved fiber, not so well to straight fiber
