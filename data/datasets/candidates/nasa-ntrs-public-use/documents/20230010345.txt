<p>Problem Statement
</p>
<p>11</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Bonded Composite Joints
</p>
<p>❑Usage expected to increase in next generation aircraft
➢Currently requires extensive use of structurally redundant fasteners
</p>
<p>❖Slow to install -&gt; hole drilling, fastener installation, inspection and sealing
</p>
<p>❖Lost of envisioned manufacturing advantages of bonding
</p>
<p>❑Lightweight adhesively-bonded parts 
➢Have shown some great benefits, including improvements in:
</p>
<p>✓Mechanical properties, fuel efficiency, flight range and payload
</p>
<p>✓Reduction in aircraft operation costs
</p>
<p>➢More efficient than mechanical joints
✓Ability to distribute load over a much broader area
</p>
<p>12
</p>
<p>Future aircraft</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Bonded Composite Joints
</p>
<p>❑Lightweight adhesively-bonded parts 
➢Problem: Development, qualification and mainstream adoption
</p>
<p>❖Impeded by the lack of reliable and quantitative NDE method or technique for quality 
assurance
</p>
<p>❖Current commercial NDE typically detect gross bonding defects (e.g., delaminations, 
voids, major porosity, etc.)
o Albeit, in a more qualitative (Pass/Fail) manner
o These techniques fail to detect weak or kissing bonds
</p>
<p>❖Bond strength is currently ensured by process control and semi-destructive testing
➢Proposed Solution: A novel phase-based ultrasonic technique
</p>
<p>✓Developed at NASA Langley Research Center (LaRC)
✓ Based on a constant frequency phase-locked loop (CFPLL) technology
</p>
<p>➢Objective: Perform a Measurement System Analysis (MSA) study to evaluate 
the novel phase-based ultrasonic system for bond quality assessment
✓Novel system’s precision, repeatability and reproducibility (R&amp;R) 
</p>
<p>▪ Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) method
▪ Components of variation in the measurement process
</p>

<p></p>

<p>17

<p>Constant Frequency Phase-locked Loop*
</p>
<p>❑ Comparing the received ultrasonic waveform reflected off 
bonded interface
➢ To a reference waveform of the same frequency
</p>
<p>❑ Comparing the two signals allows measurement of the phase 
shift in the bonded joint
➢ With high resolution and low uncertainty
</p>
<p>*Yost, W. T.; Cantrell, J. H.; Kushnick, P. W. Fundamental Aspects of Pulse Phase-Locked Loop Technology-based Methods for Measurement of Ultrasonic 
</p>
<p>Velocity. Journal of the Acoustical Society of America 1992, 91(3), 1456-1468
</p>
<p>❑ When repeated over a band of frequencies around the anti-
resonant frequency
➢ Results to a phase spectral response
</p>
<p>❑ Quality of the bond
➢ Could be inferred from the resulting phase spectral response</p>
<p></p>

<p>Experimental Setup
</p>
<p>18</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>❑Simplistic borosilicate (BS) adhesively bonded glass specimens:
</p>
<p>➢ Used for proof of concept
</p>
<p>✓ Can this novel phase-based NDE technique assess bond strength?
</p>
<p>➢ Optically transparent, smooth surfaces and no microstructure
</p>
<p>✓ Helps to eliminate any confounding effect in experimentations
</p>
<p>✓ Creates a path towards phase-based ultrasonic NDE of:
</p>
<p>▪ Metallic bonded joints, 
</p>
<p>o Grain noise becomes relevant, and
</p>
<p>▪ Ultimately, bonded composite joint,
</p>
<p>o Multi-layer laminate and fiber interactions present more complexity
</p>
<p>❑ Gage Repeatability and Reproducibility (Gage R&amp;R)
</p>
<p>➢ How precise is the novel measurement device for bond strength?
</p>
<p>➢ Performed on these bonded glass specimens 
</p>
<p>✓ Produced under various degrading conditions, including:
</p>
<p>▪ Epoxy adhesive with varying % of Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) coverage area
</p>
<p>▪ FM209-IU adhesive with varying thermal conditions
</p>
<p>▪ UV-cured NOA60 adhesive at varying UV exposure times
</p>
<p>✓ Coupons: 5.08 cm x 5.08 cm and 1.10 cm thick. Bondline: ~100 μm thick
</p>
<p>19
</p>
<p>Test Samples for the Gage R&amp;R Study</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>20
</p>
<p>Data Collection for Gage R&amp;R Study
</p>
<p>❑Laboratory setup of the ultrasonic phase-based instrument
➢ Highly-damped 10 MHz contact transducer of 0.25” diameter
</p>
<p>✓ Placed in the center of each sample
</p>
<p>✓ Held in place using a screw clamp at constant load of ~5 lbs
</p>
<p>✓ Excited with tone-burst
▪ Swept over frequency range from 8.5 MHz – 11.75 MHz
</p>
<p>▪ 10 KHz step size
</p>
<p>➢ Ultrasonic phase measurements from each of the test samples
✓ Obtained in pulse-echo mode
</p>
<p>✓ By two separate operators
▪ 3 times each in a randomized manner
</p>
<p>▪ Taking out test sample from the fixture between measurements
</p>
<p>▪ Using best practices to reposition transducer for new measurement
</p>
<p>➢ Computer was used to control the phase measurement circuit
✓ Taking phase measurements of the 1st reflected signal
</p>
<p>▪ From the adhesive surface at each frequency
</p>
<p>▪ Recording the measured phase
</p>
<p>✓ Analysis region selected where the measured phase was constant
▪ Stable as possible over the entire frequency range 
</p>

<p>Conclusion and Future Work
</p>
<p>27</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>❑Measurement System Analysis (MSA) conducted
➢ To assess the quality of the novel ultrasonic phase-based bond strength instrument
</p>
<p>➢ Specifically, the instrument's precision (repeatability and reproducibility) has been evaluated
</p>
<p>✓ Through a Gage R&amp;R study using the ANOVA (Analysis of Variance) method
</p>
<p>➢ Study results show this new device can precisely measure interfacial stiffness 
</p>
<p>✓ Adhesively bonded BS specimens
</p>
<p>✓ Most of the measurement variation coming from the samples instead of the instrument
</p>
<p>▪ Potentially due to bondline thickness variation across the samples
</p>
<p>❑ Ultimate goal is to measure bond strength of composite bonded samples
➢ This study on comparatively simplistic BS specimens provide roadmap for further development
</p>
<p>✓ Laboratory-based instrument → field instrument
</p>
<p>❑Reproducibility error ~0% (for the level of precision reported)
➢ Use of such devices could be independent of the operator
</p>
<p>✓ Useful as an in-service equipment to measure bond quality for aerospace parts in use
</p>
<p>❑Future Work: Accuracy (bias, linearity and stability) study
➢ To get a full picture of the measurement variation of this novel device
</p>
