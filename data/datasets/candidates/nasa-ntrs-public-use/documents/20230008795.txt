<p>Introduction and Motivation
</p>
<p>Due to the complex fabrication process of CMC blades, larger blade
trailing edge thicknesses and larger leading-edge radii of curvature are
expected. These features significantly alter aerodynamic performance
especially near the trailing edge and thus the loss profile.
</p>
<p>Large Eddy Simulation (LES) shows some success to capture such characteristics of 
CMC blades [8]. However, there is still challenging issue such as a grid requirement.
</p>
<p>CMC blade coordinates 
tested at NASA GRC [1]
</p>
<p>• Lyall, et al. [2], Keadle, et al. [3] and Arko-, et al. [4] used 
RANS to investigate the front- and aft-loaded designs at a 
relatively low free-stream turbulence (FST) (Tu~3.1-3.3%).
</p>
<p>• Medic and Sharma [5] and Bolinches-Gisbert et al. [6] used 
LES to investigate the LPT blades with (Tu=4%) and without 
FST for 30,000 &lt; Re &lt; 150,000 and Re ~ 50,000,
</p>
<p>• Pichler et al. [7] performed a grid convergence study for LPT 
DNS at a Re=60,000 with the aim of resolving the background 
turbulence with 18, 72 and 200 million points 
</p>
<p>• Miki and Ameri [8] performed LES with 20 million points 
(157,000 &lt; Re &lt; 314,000 ) and RANS for the CMC blades 
(Tu=10~13%) and compared the results against the data by 
Giel et al. [1] 
</p>
<p>- aft-loaded
- mid-loaded
- front-loaded</p>

<p>Grid Resolution Test (20M Mesh) [8]
</p>
<p>The 20M mesh seemed to be satisfied with the conventional grid resolution 
requirements for LES.
</p>
<p>- Pope Criterion is a good indicator to check whether the grid resolution in the 
freestream region adequately resolves the turbulence.
</p>
<p>- Near the wall, we use very fine mesh (1.25x10-6 [m]) to resolve the BL and y+1 is 
~1 for most of the surface including the suction side. 
</p>
<p>- In the midspan wall region, on the average, x+1 value is of ~10 and z+1 is of ~20.</p>

</p>
<p>LES (20 M) grid [8]                       LES (50M) grid (Current study)                        
</p>
<p>- In order to resolve these issues, we decided to improve the quality of mesh near the

region are the same.)
</p>
<p>- The span of the domain is 0.2×Cx and 0.3 × Cx, and the spanwise grid resolutions are 
32 and 64 for the LES (20M) and LES (50M) meshes. 
</p>
<p>- Near the end of the suction side and the trailing edge, the grid resolution is increased 
for the LES (50M) grid by three folds compared to the LES (20M) grid. </p>

<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>We utilized Large eddy simulation to calculate the pressure loading profile, the total 
loss distribution, and the integrated pressure loading profile of the front-loaded 
CMC blade at the high-freestream turbulence (Tu=13%). 
</p>
<p>• In our previous studies [8], we reported the issues (a small recirculation bubble 
at the end of the suction side and the ``shifted’’ wake profile), which are resolved 
by local grid refinement at the trailing edge and expanding the span.
</p>
<p>• With the new mesh, there is no recirculation bubble observed due to the fact the 
boundary layer is energized more from the momentum transfer from the free 
stream turbulence. 
</p>
<p>• The transition to the turbulent boundary layer (bypass transition) was observed 
(not separation reattachment previously reported [8])
</p>
<p>• The increase in the span of the blade seem to have no noticeable effect on the 
solution.
</p>
<p>• The fuller velocity profile alters the direction of the shear layer departing from the 
trailing edge, which reduces the shift of the wake profile.
</p>
