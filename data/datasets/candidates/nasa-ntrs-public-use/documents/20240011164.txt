<p>A new type of planetary rover
First NASA lunar rover
</p>
<p>• Designed for the “dynamic” lunar environment
• Emphasis on high operational cadence and traverse speed
• Significantly lower cost than Mars rovers (but higher risk)
</p>
<p>Interactive operations
• “Real-time” mission control: rover operations + science team
• Single waypoint driving (approx. 4 m / command cycle)
• Hybrid of human exploration (Shuttle, Space Station) and Mars rover 
</p>
<p>mission operations
Hybrid avionics and software
</p>
<p>• BAE RAD 750 v3 (rad-hard, 198 MHz PPC, similar to 1997 PowerMac G3) + 
SP0 (rad-tolerant, 1 GHz PPC, similar to 2010 iPhone 4) computing
</p>
<p>• Flight software is split between on-board and ground
• Ground software uses Robot Operating System 2 (ROS2)</p>

<p>7
</p>
<p>Mission Planning
Objectives
</p>
<p>• Develop activity, resource, and traverse plans
• Strategic and tactical plans, including contingency handling
• Robustly achieve full science success under uncertainty
• Real-time replanning, particularly when operating in science stations
</p>
<p>(A few) constraints
• Measure distribution and state of lunar polar volatiles
• Solar-powered rover with limited battery (shadow endurance)
• Lunar summer “season” (~100 Earth days max)
• Line-of-sight data communications to Earth
• 20 km max drive, 15 deg slopes, 20 cm step obstacles (design limit)
• 10 cm/s drive speed while prospecting (instrument limit)
• 1 cm/s “Speed Made Good” (average rate of progress)
• Moving shadows cast by rover and terrain (0.1 to 1.8 cm/s)</p>
