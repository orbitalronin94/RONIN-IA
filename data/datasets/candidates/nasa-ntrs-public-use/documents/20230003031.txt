<p>LUNAR LANDER LANDING MODELING AND SIMULATION IN 
</p>
<p>SIMSCAPE 
</p>
<p>Wei Wu+ 
</p>
<p>The landing gear is a critical subsystem of a lander for a successful landing 
</p>
<p>on the moon or a planet. During a landing event, structural loading, energy 
</p>
<p>absorption and toppling stability are important factors for the landing gear 
</p>
<p>development. These aspects can be studied using simulations. 
</p>
<p>The landing gear comprising the primary struct, secondary structs, 
</p>
<p>footpad, and joints, is easily modeled using Simscape Multibody toolbox.  
</p>
<p>The energy absorption mechanisms in the structs is readily modeled using 
</p>
<p>Simulink and Multibody blocks based on the relative motions of the inner 
</p>
<p>and outer cylinder of the structures. During touchdown, the footpads strike 
</p>
<p>the lunar surface, and the contact forces can be modeled including the soil 
</p>
<p>mechanical properties. Slosh dynamics are included using a mechanical 
</p>
<p>pendulum model. Tip-over controllers are developed using Simulink 
</p>
<p>blocks and toolboxes.  
</p>
<p>This model was intensively tested manually then additionally with Monte 
</p>
<p>Carlo simulation. It offers the opportunity for landing stability study, i.e. 
</p>
<p>the max slope a lander can land without tipping over in 3D realistic landing 
</p>
<p>situations. 
</p>
<p>INTRODUCTION/MODELING 
</p>
<p>The landing gear is a critical subsystem of a lander for a successful landing on the moon or a planet. 
</p>
<p>For the Apollo program, Lunar module (LM) landing gear touchdown dynamics was both 
</p>
<p>experimentally tested (Reference 1) and simulated by computer (Reference 2). During a landing 
</p>
<p>event, structural loading, energy absorption and toppling stability are important factors for the 
</p>
<p>landing gear development. 
</p>
<p>Landing legs usually have a primary strut and two secondary struts equipped with an energy 
</p>
<p>absorption mechanism, as with the LM landing gear shown in Figure 1(a) (Reference 3). The 
</p>
<p>landing leg structure can be easily modeled using Simscape Multibody toolbox. The Simscape 
</p>
<p>Multibody toolbox’s Body Elements, Joints, Frames and Transforms libraries contain blocks which 
</p>
<p>can be graphically connected to model the leg struts, the footpad, the joint connecting the primary 
</p>
<p>strut to the secondary struts, and joints connecting the legs to the vehicle main body. The energy 
</p>
<p>absorption mechanisms in the struts, such as a spring-dashpot damper, can be easily modeled using

<p>During a lander touchdown, the footpads strike the lunar surface and there is dynamic interaction 
</p>
<p>between the landing legs and the lunar soil. It is important to model the footpad and soil interaction 
</p>
<p>in a landing performance assessment.  The surface can be model as a plane using the Infinite Plane 
</p>
<p>block. The contact forces can be modeling using the Spatial Contact Force block, where the normal 
</p>
<p>force is computed based on the footpad penetration, specified soil stiffness, and damping coefficient 
</p>
<p>and the friction force is computed based on the normal force and the soil friction coefficient. The 
</p>
<p>soil mechanical properties computed based on Apollo touchdown data can be found in Reference 
</p>
<p>4. In the simulation, the landing contact normal force is initially modeled using a linear spring-
</p>
<p>damper method provided within Simscape. But this method is too conservative without considering 
</p>
<p>the inelastic deformation of the lunar regolith and increased soil stiffness with increasing footpad 
</p>
<p>penetration. A custom nonlinear contact normal force model is also developed to simulate the 
</p>
<p>landing events with increased accuracy. 
</p>
<p>SIMULATION 
</p>
<p>Having modeled the landing leg structure, energy attenuation, and contact forces, landing dynamics 
</p>
<p>under various landing modes (Reference 5) can be simulated given the vehicle states and local slope 
</p>
<p>at touchdown. The model can be instrumented to output key variables such as stroke, penetration 
</p>
<p>depth, tilt angle and vehicle body rates, so the structural integrity and toppling stability for a given 
</p>
<p>landing leg design can be assessed against the requirements. Figure 2(a) shows the lander stable 
</p>
<p>landing on a 4.6 degrees slope for the passive 2-2-over landing mode (Reference 5) and Figure 2(b) 
</p>
<p>shows the primary structure stroke distances. </p>

<p>To successfully land on steeper slopes, hot gas thrusters can be fired during touchdown to prevent 
</p>
<p>the vehicle from tipping over via feedback control logic. Because Simscape is embedded inside 
</p>
<p>Simulink, it is very easy to model different tip-over control strategies using various Simulink blocks 
</p>
<p>and toolboxes. For higher fidelity modeling, the slosh dynamics of the liquid propellant in tanks 
</p>
<p>can be easily modeled using a pendulum mechanical model using Simscape Multibody library.

<p>CONCLUSION 
</p>
<p>First, this model was intensively tested manually then through Monte Carlo simulation. It offers 
</p>

</p>
<p>in 3D realistic landing situations.
