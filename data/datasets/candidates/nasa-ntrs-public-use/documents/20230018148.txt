<div class="slide-content"><p>Artemis I launch debris hazards routinely assessed</p>
<p>Two expected debris concerns identified and cleared</p>
<p>Tools and processes developed to assess Day-of-Launch (DOL) unexpected debris</p>
<p>Debris hazards increased due to tests and environments leading up to launch</p>
<p>Green Run, Wet Dress Rehearsals, Tanking Test, Hurricanes, Launch Attempts</p>
<p>Multiple unexpected debris hazards assessed and accepted by the program before DOL, including thermal materials, processing debris, and potential pad debris from weather events</p>
<p>DOL unexpected debris was minimal, easily resolved</p>

<p>Expected Debris Assessments</p>
<p></p>
<p>SLS vehicle materials are less vulnerable to debris impacts than Shuttle materials; however,</p>
<p>Longer vehicle results in higher impact energies on aft components</p>
<p>Liquid engine locations closer to Booster nozzles increase risk of throat plug impacts</p>
<p>Expected debris includes thermal materials, ice, nozzle plug 	materials and other minor miscellaneous materials</p>
<p>Assessments of expected debris consist of </p>
<p>Debris transport analyses</p>
<p>Impact assessments</p>
<p>Risk assessments for identified concerns</p>
<p>Identified issues reviewed by SLS Program for acceptance                       	or mitigation</p>
<p>3</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><div class="embedded" id="slide4_rId2"></div>
<p>Two Expected Debris Concerns Accepted as Low Risk</p>
<p>Core Stage thermal protection system materials identified as concerns to the Booster Aft Separation Motor Single Fairing</p>
<p>Probabilities assessed for debris release, impact, and damage</p>
<p>Booster Throat Plug foam identified as concern to the liquid engine nozzles</p>
<p>Complex debris transport mechanism examined</p>
<p>Impact tests onto ‘waterfall’ performed for availability for transport</p>
<p>Fragment distribution assessed from test data </p>
<p>Probability of damage calculated, given availability and transport to the nozzle surfaces</p>
<p>SLS Program determined the risks were low and acceptable</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Day-of-Launch (DOL) Debris Process</p>
<p>Evaluating launch debris as a hazard assessment resulted in limited known design thresholds, requiring new process for DOL support</p>
<p>Developed DOL Launch Debris Process, coordinated with SLS Elements, Cross Programs, and imagery teams and processes</p>
<p>Debris Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) </p>
<p>review scans to identify potential debris Items of Interest (IOIs)</p>
<p>perform needed assessments supporting risk evaluations, by Program management and mission management teams</p>
<p>DOL Debris Transport and Impact Assessment Tools developed</p>
<p>IOI evaluations coordinated over voice loops across four NASA Centers and several contractor sites</p>
<p>DOL Process exercised for multiple unexpected debris sources during simulations, tests, and launch attempts</p>
<p>Only one minor unexpected debris item required assessment on DOL</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide5_rId2"></div>
<p>5</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Debris Activities During Pre-Launch Tests</p>
<p>Core Stage Green Run Wet Dress Rehearsal and Hot Fire Tests</p>
<p>Four cycles of cryogenic loading/unloading, two hot-fire cycles, and a year of environment exposure</p>
<p>Cracks developed in TPS foam and were repaired as needed</p>
<p>Integrated Vehicle Wet Dress Rehearsals (WDR) 1 through 4</p>
<p>After Rollout and WDRs debris was observed and evaluated:  foreign object debris, minor vehicle TPS damage, expected ice accumulation (potential debris), and unexpected tower debris</p>
<p>Unexpected items observed were corrected before launch</p>
<p>Launch Attempts 1 and 2 </p>
<p>Noted unexpected items on the tower, unexpected ice growth on the Core Stage, and TPS foam cracks at LH2/IT flange</p>
<p>All evaluated with DOL process or removed before launch</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide6_rId2"></div>

<p>Tanking Test Through Launch</p>
<p>Tanking Test</p>
<p>All cryogenic tanks successfully filled</p>
<p>Unexpected tower debris evaluated</p>
<p>Rollback for Hurricane Ian</p>
<p>Potential tower debris removed, and repairs completed in VAB</p>
<p>Weathering Hurricane Nicole</p>
<p>Imagery scans and walkdowns assured minimal unexpected debris</p>
<p>Minor tower softgoods damage mitigated to prevent debris release</p>
<p>One vehicle thermal material loosened and required assessment</p>
<p>Artemis I Launch, November 16, 2022</p>
<p>Areas of ice growth evaluated, confirmed within expectations</p>
<p>Several known TPS cracks monitored, previously assessed for risk</p>
<p>One minor TPS material assessed and cleared just before launch</p>

</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Pre-Launch Risk Assessments</p>
<p>Planned and unplanned tests and processes increased the risk of debris release</p>
<p>Loads induced by cryogenic tanking cycles, from rollbacks, and from unexpected weather events resulted in two notable thermal materials damaged </p>
<p></p>
<p>Foam cracks on the Core Stage indicated debris releases could be larger than previously defined for expected debris, and noted as Items of Concern (IOC)</p>
<p>Repair of most cracks was completed during roll-back to the VAB before launch</p>
<p>Remaining cracks were evaluated, and risk assessments completed</p>
<p>Thermal materials on Orion hardware were observed loosened in prelaunch events</p>
<p>Unexpected loose material from this location was evaluated and noted as an IOC</p>
<p>The loose materials were removed during roll-back to the VAB before launch</p>
<p>Remaining material was assessed as potential debris and risks evaluated</p>
<p></p>
<p>Risk assessments were completed and accepted by the SLS Program and mission management before launch</p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><div class="embedded" id="slide9_rId2"></div>
<p>Conclusions</p>
<p>An integrated approach to DOL debris assessment across NASA SLS, Cross Program, and contractor organizations led to successful and efficient evaluations of unexpected launch debris and a successful Artemis I launch</p>
<p>Awareness of potential launch debris issues has improved, and mitigation activities more accomplished as a result of the activities leading up to launch</p>
<p>The many evaluations of expected and unexpected launch debris for Artemis I provide a sound basis for support to Artemis II and beyond</p>
