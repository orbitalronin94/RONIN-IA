<p>Design of Safe Separation Bounds for Temporally Deconflicted
</p>
<p>Trajectories under Bounded Uncertainties
</p>
<p>Javier Puig-Navarro∗
</p>
<p>Analytical Mechanics Associates, Hampton, Virginia, 23666, USA
</p>
<p>Benjamin N. Kelley†, Matthew P. Vaughan‡, and B. Danette Allen§
</p>
<p>NASA Langley Research Center, Hampton, Virginia, 23666, USA
</p>
<p>This paper explores the derivation of safe separation bounds for a heterogeneous group of =
</p>
<p>Uncrewed Aerial Systems (UAS) that are assigned temporally deconflicted trajectories. Com-
</p>
<p>pared to spatially deconflicted trajectories, temporal deconfliction can lead to higher traffic
</p>
<p>capacities and a more efficient use of the available airspace. One challenge with this type of
</p>
<p>deconfliction is that collisions can occur if some cooperating UAS are behind or ahead of sched-
</p>
<p>ule. To overcome this risk, this paper derives a lower bound on the safety distance between
</p>
<p>two heterogeneous UAS in the presence of bounded uncertainties. This safety distance can
</p>
<p>be leveraged to inform trajectory generation algorithms. The proposed bound establishes a
</p>
<p>rigorous safety margin when the fleet deviates from the planned trajectories, both temporally
</p>
<p>and spatially. For its derivation the paper assumes the UAS implement a distributed coordina-
</p>
<p>tion algorithm that allows the fleet to maintain their schedules synchronized within a bounded
</p>
<p>temporal error, and a path-following algorithm that lets the vehicles track a target that moves
</p>
<p>along the planned trajectory with a bounded spatial error.
</p>
<p>I. Nomenclature
</p>
<p>UAS = uncrewed aerial systems
</p>
<p>CoM = center of mass
</p>
<p>C = real time
</p>
<p>C38 = desired time for the 8th UAS as planned in the trajectory generation phase
</p>
<p>b8 = virtual time for the 8th UAS
</p>
<p>pi = position of the CoM of the 8th UAS
</p>
<p>pdi = desired position for the CoM of the 8th UAS
</p>
<p>pTi
= position of the virtual target of the 8th UAS
</p>
<p>eTi
= target-tracking error for the 8th UAS
</p>
<p>3B8, 9 = safety distance between the 8th and 9th UAS
</p>
<p>2B8, 9 = safety coefficient for the separation problem between the 8th and 9th UAS
</p>
<p>A8 = radius of a bounding sphere for the frame of the 8th UAS with center at pi
</p>
<p>II. Introduction
As the diversity and number of vehicles in the airspace increase, it is important to aid traffic controllers, automate
</p>
<p>certain tasks, and certify that travel plans are safe within specified margins, even in the presence of uncertainties. In
</p>
<p>addition, as the air traffic demand grows [1], increasing the traffic capacity of the existing infrastructure may require
</p>
<p>less conservative solutions that maintain or improve the levels of safety that the aviation industry has historically
</p>
<p>operated under. In this context of soaring traffic demand, the inclusion of unmanned systems in the airspace poses
</p>
<p>an added challenge. In some scenarios, UAS operators resort to ad hoc safe separation bounds that are sometimes

<p>informed with Monte Carlo simulation results. This paper attempts to characterize the trajectory-tracking performance
</p>
<p>of any UAS with the goal of moving closer to standardization. To this end, this paper proposes that UAS implement
</p>
<p>two algorithms: a path-following algorithm that attempts to track a point in space with a bounded spatial error, and
</p>
<p>a time-critical coordination algorithm that is tasked with synchronizing the schedules of the cooperating UAS that
</p>
<p>operate in mid and close proximity. The performance of these algorithms is characterized by generic bounds, based
</p>
<p>on existing algorithms [2, 3]. These are then leveraged to derive a lower bound for the safe separation distance. The
</p>
<p>contribution of this paper is the definition of a safety coefficient that provides a normalized notion of the safety margins
</p>
<p>embedded in the solutions of the trajectory generation problem. This use of a safety coefficient resembles standard
</p>
<p>practices in structures and materials problems.
</p>
<p>This paper is organized in three main sections. Section III captures the properties of the trajectories considered,
</p>
<p>and the characteristics of the relevant underlying algorithms that control the UAS. These are formalized through a
</p>
<p>series of assumptions. These assumptions are then used to define the problem of the design of safe separation bounds
</p>
<p>for temporally deconflicted trajectories in the presence of uncertainties. Section IV provides a solution through the
</p>
<p>derivation of a safe separation bound for each pair of heterogeneous UAS. Last, Section V presents the conclusions of
</p>
<p>this paper, and suggests potential applications and extensions of the results presented.
</p>

<p>V. Conclusion
This paper defines and solves a safe separation problem for a fleet of = heterogeneous UAS that are assigned
</p>
<p>temporally deconflicted trajectories. This work assumes that the vehicles are equipped with a path-following algorithm
</p>
<p>that can track a virtual target that slides along their trajectories within some bounded spatial error. To coordinate the
</p>
<p>UAS as the mission evolves, even in the presence of external disturbances, the fleet is also equipped with a distributed
</p>
<p>time-critical coordination algorithm. This protocol attempts to synchronize the vehicle schedules within a bounded
</p>
<p>temporal error. The paper leverages these bounded spatial and temporal errors to propose a lower bound for the safety
</p>
<p>distance between the vehicle trajectories. Embedded in the computation of the safe separation distance is a safety
</p>
<p>coefficient that provides a normalized notion of the safety margins built into the trajectory generation problem. If
</p>
<p>chosen within the bounds provided in this paper, the safety coefficients guarantee safe separation among all cooperating
</p>
<p>UAS. Future work could leverage the proposed bound to identify areas of improvement in path-following, time-critical
</p>
<p>coordination, and trajectory generation to safely improve the traffic capacity of the airspace. A clear extension of this
</p>
<p>work could involve the design of well-clear geometries that resemble current standards as in detailed in [5, 6], instead
</p>
<p>of the bounding sphere used here.
