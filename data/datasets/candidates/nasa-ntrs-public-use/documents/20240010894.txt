<p>CRYOGENIC FLUID MANAGEMENT PORTFOLIO PROJECT (CFMPP) 2
CFMPP Overview and Update
</p>
<p>September 5, 2024
</p>
<p>CFM Portfolio Project (CFMPP)
• Project Objective: Mature CFM technologies essential to NASA’s future missions in science and 
</p>
<p>exploration which utilize both chemical and nuclear in-space propulsion, landers, and in-situ resource 
utilization
</p>
<p>• Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) portfolio project comprised of over twenty individual CFM 
technology development activities
– Includes non-flight demo activities related to Lunar/Mars surface system applications (ex. ISRU liquefaction) 
</p>
<p>• Partnered leadership and execution by NASA’s Marshall Spaceflight Center and Glenn Research Center
– MSFC PM: Jeremy Kenny; GRC Deputy PM: Lauren Ameen
</p>
<p>• CFMPP aims to close technology gaps, with focus on integrated CFM systems development and 
demonstration, to advance the national goals of landing on the Moon and Mars
</p>
<p>• Technology entrance minimum of TRL 4, with project end state objective of TRL 7 (Flight Demonstration)
– CFMPP has developed and implemented a rigorous TRL assessment and advancement process 
</p>
<p>• CFMPP leverages Agency-wide mission developments and expertise for prioritization of activities 
</p>
<p>To mature CFM technologies critical for space flight applications, CFM flight demonstrations are required.  
Flight demonstrations must be of appropriate scale, utilize integrated systems, and be performed in extended 
</p>
<p>microgravity and thermal environments</p>
<p></p>

<p>CRYOGENIC FLUID MANAGEMENT PORTFOLIO PROJECT (CFMPP) 8
CFMPP Overview and Update
</p>
<p>September 5, 2024
</p>
<p>2020 Tipping Points - SpaceX
On-Orbit Large-Scale Cryogenic Propellant Management and Transfer 
</p>
<p>Demonstration
</p>
<p>Overview:  Demonstration of large-scale on-orbit cryogenic fluid transfer of liquid 
oxygen (up to 3 metric tons) between tanks on a Starship in orbit and management 
to provide a basis for operational use of in-space refueling technology. 
</p>
<p>Mission Overview:
• Demonstration conducted as part of a Starship flight
• Active settling maneuver with settled transfer between propellant tanks located 
</p>
<p>within Starship (not ship-to-ship)
• Flight-3 included special demonstration hardware specific to Tipping Point
</p>
<p>• ≤ 1 day mission duration
</p>
<p>Flight Demonstration Completed on Starship Flight-3 on March 14, 2024!
• Conducted the large-scale propellant transfer, moving several metric tons of 
</p>
<p>propellant from one tank to another, while in space
• Storing and transferring cryogenic propellant in orbit has never been attempted 
</p>
<p>on this scale before. This is a huge milestone toward providing NASA with the 
technologies it will need for both a long-term presence on the moon and its 
boldest missions to deep space destinations such as Mars and beyond.   </p>
<p></p>

<p>CRYOGENIC FLUID MANAGEMENT PORTFOLIO PROJECT (CFMPP) 9
CFMPP Overview and Update
</p>
<p>September 5, 2024
</p>
<p>Successful RFMG Demo on CLPS IM-1
Radio Frequency Mass Gauge (RFMG) Technology
Measures tank RF spectrum to provide tank fill level; the spectrum changes with 
fill level, since the dielectric fluid (e) slows the speed of light
• Tech has been in development for over 15 years at Glenn Research Center
</p>
<p>RFMG successfully flew and operated onboard the Intuitive Machines IM-1 
Nova-C (Odysseus) lunar lander as part of Commercial Lunar Payload 
</p>
<p>Services Program! 
</p>
<p>• Mission Objectives:
– Support pre-flight and flight operations during CLPS Program with Intuitive 
</p>
<p>Machines (IM-1) Nova C lander.
– Demonstrate RFMG system operations at multiple fluid levels for both 
</p>
<p>liquid oxygen (LOX) and liquid methane (LCH4) propulsion tanks 
throughout IM-1 mission.
</p>
<p>• NASA team participated multiple in pre-launch activities, including RF fluid 
simulations and propellant tanking during wet-dress rehearsals
</p>
<p>This technology was actively used on throughout IM-1 mission, where it 
not only provided critical data to advance the RFMG technology but also 
</p>
<p>assisted the IM-1 team during maneuvers and the lunar landing.
</p>
<p>Propellant loading of IM-1 tank at LC 39A
</p>

<p>CRYOGENIC FLUID MANAGEMENT PORTFOLIO PROJECT (CFMPP) 10
CFMPP Overview and Update
</p>
<p>September 5, 2024
</p>
<p>Low-Leakage Hydrogen Valves
NASA team at Marshall Space Flight Center has developed a patented valve 
technology that utilizes a seat and poppet concept enabling self-alignment, 
greatly reducing leakage rates.
</p>
<p>• Activity Goal: 
– Develop valves that are more tolerant of imperfections and reduce internal leakage rates by 
</p>
<p>an order of. Traditional internal leakage rates up to 2000 Standard Cubic Inches per Minute 
(SCIM)
</p>
<p>– Low-leakage rates are required for future long-duration cryogenic missions
</p>
<p>• In 2023, CFMPP team successfully developed and tested a relief valve design 
on small scale at MSFC
– Test results indicated leakage rates under 1.0 SCIM
– Achieved TRL 4 criteria
</p>
<p>• CFMPP started new activity in 2024 to continue valve technology development 
and begin tech transfer and infusion with industry 
– Procure a high-fidelity prototype valve from a commercial partner which will 
</p>
<p>enable a future test campaign to bring the low-leakage valve design to TRL 6 with 
the exception of the electromechanical actuator
</p>
<p>– Contract(s) starting in October 2024
</p>

CFMPP Overview and Update
</p>
<p>September 5, 2024

<p>Cryogenic Fluid Modeling
• NASA has spent the past 20 years developing and validating multi-node and CFD 
</p>
<p>(Computational Fluid Dynamics) modeling tools for cryogenic propellant applications 
</p>
<p>• Current CFM Modeling Portfolio supports stakeholders through development and 
validation of pre-predictive models against cryogenic experimental data
</p>
<p>• CFM Modeling Team uses experimental ground and flight data to model cryogenic 
fluid phenomena
</p>
<p>– Quality of modeling is dependent on the quality of the experimental data; flight demonstrations which 
are expensive and rare
</p>
<p>– With more confident models, systems can be designed with reduced margin and greater performance.
</p>
<p>• Modeling will be complete when the CFM risks are acceptable to the 
project/stakeholders, with definition of success actively in dialogue across the 
Agency
</p>
<p>• Multiple occurrences of industry modeling tool infusion/transfer and knowledge 
sharing with Industry Partners
</p>
