The title of my presentation if Flow Physics Study of Sweeping Jet Actuation on an N-A-C-A 0015 Swept Wing Configuration. <div class="slide-content"><p>Motivation</p>
<p>Cruise drag reduction of simplified high-lift system</p>
<p>3.3 count reduction in excrescence drag – 2.25% reduction in fuel burn </p>
<p>Hartwich et al., NASA/CR-2016-21170 </p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Takeoff L/D improvements of AFC aileron</p>
<p>2.5% – 5% net improvement in L/D at takeoff</p>
<p>Shmilovich et al., NASA Technical Report, document ID 20220006731</p>
<p></p>
<p>2</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-notes"><p>We are motivated by the fact that AFC is a technology that has the potential to improve the aerodynamics efficiency of a commercial transport.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Two examples are shown.</p>
<p></p>
<p>In the first, Hartwich et al showed that a 3.3 count reduction in excrescence drag is possible by applying active flow control to simplify a high-lift system</p>
<p></p>
<p>In the 2nd example, Shmilovich et al indicate that a 2.5% to 5% net improvement in takeoff L/D (lift-to-drag ratio) may be possible by applying AFC to a deflected aileron.</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-notes"><p>The test was performed in the Basic Aerodynamics Research Tunnel located at the NASA Langley Research Center.  </p>
<p></p>
<p>The model tested is a 30 deg swept wing, semispan model that is sidewall mounted in the Tunnel. The chord is 0.3 meters and the semispan is 0.61 meters.  </p>
<p></p>
<p>It has an N-A-C-A 0015 cross-section normal to the leading edge.  </p>
<p></p>
<p>The flap was tested at deflection angles of 0 and 20 degrees in this study and the results are included in the paper.   </p>
<p></p>
<p>The results obtained with the flap deflected to 20 degrees will be presented.  </p>
<p></p>
<p>Transition dot of 0.2mm applied to the upper surface at around 1.14cm from the LE to achieve a fully turbulent BL</p>
<p></p>
<p>The model has the capability of LE Actuation – the slot for it was left opeN</p>

<div class="slide-notes"><p>On the left hand side,  we present the lift increment versus momentum coefficient or cmu supplies to the array of SWJ actuators with wing at an angle of attack of 4 deg.  The figures indicates that we were able to achieve a lift increment of 0.4 using a cmu of approximately 2.5%.  </p>
<p>____________</p>
<p></p>
<p>On the right hand side we show Cp distributions.  We decided to focus on the cmu value that generated a Cp of 0 at the TE.  The cp distribution on the right hand side  Indicates that with cmu=0 the flow over the flap is separated.  </p>
<p></p>
<p>As cmu is increased we see a nice pressure recovery over the flap and a trailing edge cp equal to 0 is achieved with a cmu of approximately 1%. </p>
<p>______________________ </p>
<p>For the remainder of the presentation the SwJ AFC results presented used a Cmu=1.1%.  This Cmu value generated a cmu of 0.26 which represents a 26% improvement in CL at this angle of attack.</p>
<p></p>
<p>NPR ~ 2 ( freq ~ 1330Hz) for the inboard and mid-board and out-board sections were set NPR ~1.7 (freq ~ 1250Hz)</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Summary</p>
<p>Active flow control (AFC) applied to a 30° swept wing with an NACA 0015 cross section normal to the leading edge.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Sweeping jet (SWJ) actuators with a Cm = 1.1% attached the flow when the flap deflected 20° and Rec=750,000.  </p>
<p></p>
<p>Flow visualization and particle image velocimetry (PIV) combined with force and moment data aided in understanding the flowfields with and without AFC.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Spanwise component of flow caused streamwise vortices produced by SWJ actuator to be inclined toward wing tip. </p>
