<p>FY20/21 High-Speed Market Studies
 The overwhelming number one request that industry had for NASA from the Jan 
</p>
<p>2020 Commercial High-Speed Workshop was to conduct an independent market 
study to determine the viability of high-speed / hypersonic commercial travel. 
</p>
<p> NASA subsequently funded two contracts, one to Deloitte/SpaceWorks and one to 
SAIC/Bryce, to study the market given three specific tasks:
• Define the market – how many people would be willing to travel and how much revenue could be 
</p>
<p>generated
• Define the business case – can an aircraft (Mach 2-5+)be fielded to serve this market and be 
</p>
<p>able to generate enough profit to make it economically viable
• What are the barriers preventing this market from becoming reality
</p>
<p> Deloitte and SAIC spent six months studying the problem; final reports are public
• SAIC Report: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210015471
• SAIC Presentation: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210015472
• Deloitte Report: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210014711
• Deloitte Presentation: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210014932
</p>
<p>215 Nov 2023</p>

<p>General Approach
Deloitte/SpaceWorks
</p>
<p> Examined commercial, private, cargo
 Evaluated demand / price elasticity by surveying 
</p>
<p>willingness to pay (actual $) for various flight time 
savings (correlated to Mach 2, 3, 4, 5, 6) across 
three proxy routes
</p>
<p> Developed list of 90 city pairs deemed favorable for 
high-speed travel considering multiple factors
• City pairs are predominantly over water
• Five additional over-land routes would add 24% annual 
</p>
<p>revenue (four are over US)
 Developed “Level 0/1” aircraft sizing model to drive 
</p>
<p>costs and environmental factors
• Sizing model anchored to existing (Concorde, Tu-144) 
</p>
<p>and proposed aircraft
• Estimates for sonic boom, LTO noise &amp; emissions
• Costs calculated using Galorath SEER 
• Average IRR primary cost output; required to be equal 
</p>
<p>across manufacturers and operator; minimum 25% IRR 
to be “viable” 
</p>
<p>• Single aircraft type operating model
</p>
<p>SAIC/Bryce
 Examined commercial, private, cargo
 Evaluated demand / price elasticity by speed (Mach 
</p>
<p>2, 3, 4, 5) and willingness to pay (1.5x, 2.5x, 5x, 10x 
subsonic premium fair price)
</p>
<p> Surveyed 150 high net worth individuals and 
executive travelers
</p>
<p> 800 long-haul city pairs considered
 Demand &amp; revenue growth forecasted thru 2070
 Interviewed 50+ engineering SMEs, aircraft 
</p>
<p>developers, industry experts and observers 
 Business case subtracts costs from available 
</p>
<p>revenue, what remains is available for RDT&amp;E
• Costs include operating costs, marginal aircraft 
</p>
<p>manufacturing costs, and industry ecosystem profit
• Operating costs use multipliers over current aircraft 
</p>
<p>operating costs on a per seat mile basis
• 25% profit for manufacturers and operator
• Single aircraft type operating model
</p>

<p>SpaceWorks Initial Follow-on Tasks (Sep21)
1. Initial Deloitte and SAIC efforts use single “aircraft type” model; first task 
</p>
<p>here was to explore whether a “two type” model was better or worse 
economically (e.g. only a 737 vs both 737 &amp; 777) with A/C split by range
</p>
<p>2. Evaluate economic cases for specific matrix of aircraft requirements
• 20 and 50 PAX, Mach 2, 3, 4, &amp; 5, for “short-range” and “long-range” versions
• All on Jet-A, and include an “optimized” short- and long-range A/C
</p>
<p>3. Develop “baseball card” format for ease of data comparison
4. Evaluate “alternate fuels” (SAF, LNG, LH2) across same matrix
5. What impact does inclusion of overland flights have? 
6. What impact does delay of IOC have (does optimal PAX or Mach 
</p>
<p>change)? 
7. Develop Discrete Event Simulation (DES) to provide additional fidelity / 
</p>
<p>insight into “two type” operational model

<p>Key Findings from Follow-on (Phase 1)
 Overall, when modeling a single Jet-A fueled aircraft type that serves a 
</p>
<p>subset of our 90 candidate transoceanic passenger routes that lie within 
its unrefueled range, the Mach 2-2.5, 4,500-5,500 nmi, and 40-50 
passenger aircraft had the best business cases
</p>
<p> Safety, noise compliance, emission, compliance, and sonic boom remain 
significant challenges to this industry
</p>
<p> Trade results: 
• A two-type model with A/C split by range offered minimal improvement over single 
</p>
<p>type A/C operating model
• Jet-A had the best economics initially, transitioning to SAF over time (assumed lower 
</p>
<p>future cost of SAF and adequate producibility); jury still out on LNG, LH2 largely 
infeasible due to A/C physical size (size=cost)
</p>
<p>• Adding overland routes (still U.S. based) increases market &gt; 5X; capital outlay for 
single operator becomes restrictive; optimal PAX increases, ticket prices lower
</p>
<p>• Delayed IOC improves economics (market grows, tech advances), but 
environmentals (impacts not modeled here) also likely to get more stringent
</p>

<p>Phase 2 Trade Study Summary
 Demand-based two-type aircraft model still showed only modest 
</p>
<p>improvement over single type aircraft model 
 No premium found in new market data for “same day businessman” 
</p>
<p>scenario; DES model incorporated flight scheduling / manifest
 Tech stops modestly improve addressable market for 
</p>
<p>shorter range aircraft (helps highspeed aircraft more) 
• Potential to open up Asian routes to east coast cities
</p>
<p> Competitive analyses:
• Even split of demand lowered operator outlay; all else 
</p>
<p>similar to single type / single operator
• Similar to mixed fleet; splitting demand between manufacturers has adverse effect on 
</p>
<p>entire business case
• Most promising results; advantage of splitting risk between operators and maximizing 
</p>
<p>route/demand capture via tech stops; supports “leader/follower” scenario

<p>SEI Recommendations
</p>
<p>15 Nov 2023 19
</p>
<p> NASA and FAA should continue their efforts to enable and permit overland supersonic flight
• Potential to drastically increase market size by six-fold and increase access to providers
• Provides more robust business cases for supersonic / hypersonic developers and operators
</p>
<p> In the meantime, enact a two-phased “leader-follower” strategy to allow markets and technology to 
mature
• First-to-market transoceanic “leader” aircraft in Mach 1.5-2 range aimed at addressing high demand routes
• “Follower” aircraft designed to address growing and/or newly emerging markets via further technology improvements (longer range,
</p>
<p>higher speeds, etc.)
• Allows for initial regulatory requirements and certification processes to be established and matured for lower speed systems (Mach 1.5 
</p>
<p>to 2) before attempting higher speeds (Mach 3+)
</p>
<p> Continue investments in supersonic and hypersonic aircraft technologies, 
particularly in the areas of:
• Engine fuel efficiency and emissions
• Takeoff noise
• Aircraft and engine structures/materials
</p>
<p> Continue investment in SAF with further exploration of LNG and LH2 viability
• Supply of all alternative fuels needs to be orders of magnitudes greater
• LNG shows promise while LH2 is economically viable for some simulated solutions
</p>
