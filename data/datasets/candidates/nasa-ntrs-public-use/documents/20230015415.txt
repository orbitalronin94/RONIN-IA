<div class="slide-content"><p>NASA HR-1: A HEE Resistant Fe-Ni Superalloy</p>
<p>Hydrogen Environment Embrittlement (HEE) is the degradation of mechanical properties while a material is under stress in a gaseous hydrogen environment </p>
<p>Material susceptibility influenced by alloy composition and microstructure</p>
<p>NASA HR-1 derived from JBK-75 and designed to be hydrogen resistant</p>
<p>Developed at NASA MSFC M&amp;P Lab in 1990-1992</p>
<p>NASA HR-1 is 15 - 25% stronger than JBK-75</p>
<p>Higher Ni/Ti, additions of Co &amp; W, lead to higher strength </p>
<p>W retards  precipitation and increase HEE resistance </p>
<p>It has good weldability, castability, and cold-workability</p>
<p>NASA HR-1 was identified as a candidate material for rocket engine applications operating in extreme H2 pressures</p>
<p>Requirements include yield strength, ductility, low cycle fatigue life, thermal conductivity, and HEE resistance</p>
<p>Additive manufacturing has enabled the use of NASA HR-1 for rocket engine applications</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>NASA HR-1 Composition Optimization for AM</p>
<p>Initial adjustments to NASA HR-1 composition were made to attempt to make the material more suitable to AM</p>
<p>Changes from the wrought composition are expected to lead to drops in strengths</p>
<p>During the early characterization, it was observed that the LP-DED process promoted significant Ti segregation and grain boundary -phase precipitation more than observed in wrought</p>
<p>-phase is detrimental to the ductility of the material as well as to the hydrogen environment embrittlement (HEE) resistance of the material</p>
<p>Composition of NASA HR-1 was modified using PHACOMP analysis to help avoid -phase formation in the AM material</p>
<p>Adjustments in composition are expected to drop the yield strength of the material while improving ductility and LCF life.</p>
<p>HEE resistance and thermal conductivity are expected to remain similar or slightly improve</p>

<p>Challenges for DED NASA HR-1 development: Chemical and microstructural inhomogeneities  </p>
<p>The as-deposited alloys exhibit varying degree of chemical segregation and microstructural gradient.  </p>
<p>Wrought alloys can be optimized by hot/cold rolling (or forging) and heat treatments to remove chemical segregation and microstructural inhomogeneity, but the microstructure of AM-materials can only be optimized by heat treatment</p>
<p>Therefore, development of appropriate heat treatment is critical to the success of AM alloy development. </p>
<p>Development for LP-DED NASA HR-1 aimed to optimize the heat treatment for microstructure and practicality</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Mitigation of -phase Through Solution 
and Aging Treatments</p>
<p>A time-temperature-precipitation (TTP) diagram was developed for -phase in both initial composition and HT, and checked after composition and HT optimization</p>
<p>The standard 718C/16h aging treatment was unable to reduce the brittle -phase to an acceptable level for LP-DED NASA HR-1.  Reduction of temperature to 704°C or 690°C with two step aging cycles were examined to avoid -phase formation</p>
<p>Minimal -phase formation was observed with the two step aging cycles that were developed and no grain boundary -phase was observed to be continuous around the grains</p>
<p>690°C/16h furnace cooled to 648°C/16h or to 621°C/16h were examined</p>
<p>While two step aging cycles minimized -phase formation, a moderate drop in yield strength (5-15%) was expected to result</p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>LP-DED NASA HR-1 Tensile Results</p>
<p>All the samples were 1070W round bars subjected to the same stress relief, homogenization, and solution treatment previously baselined</p>
<p>Two aging cycles were tensile tested in temperatures ranging from -195°C to 982°C: 718°C/16h (1-Step) and 690°C/16h furnace cooled to 621°C/16h (2-Step)</p>
<p>The two sets of data had similar trends to each other and to the wrought material with the tensile strengths significantly dropping around 648°C which is considered the maximum recommended operating temperature for the alloy</p>
<p>Powder reuse was examined at RT for 1x and 2x reused powder and at 648°C for 1x reused powder.  No significant effects of powder reuse were observed</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>LP-DED NASA HR-1 LCF Results</p>
<p>LCF tests at 1% and 2% total strain and R=-1 were performed at temperatures ranging from -195°C (LN2) to 760°C</p>
<p>The 2-step aging cycle tended to have improved fatigue life over the 1-step age at most temperatures tested for both strain ranges, however the fatigue life converged more above 648°C</p>
<p>No significant impact of 1x or 2x powder reuse on LCF properties was observed for any temperatures tested</p>
<p>At room temp, both aging cycles had similar LCF behavior to wrought NASA HR-1 in GHe (historical data)</p>

<p>GH2 Tensile Results</p>
<p>A case study in high pressure hydrogen tensile testing examined effects of surface finish and strain rates</p>
<p>Strain rates varied from 0.005-0.0001 in/in/min and surface finish between machined and LSG per ASTM G142</p>
<p>GH2 testing performed at 5000 psi at room temp and compared to GN2, GHe, and Air tests</p>
<p>A minor drop in ductility was consistently observed for 2-step age while a more notable drop ~7% was observed for 1-step age</p>
<p>1-Step age drop was attributed to test time due to slow strain rate at 0.0005 in/in/min</p>
<p>No significant drops in strength noted in either aging conditions</p>
<p>Fractography showed GH2 affected zones with quasi-cleavage near surface that increase in area with slower strain rates</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Low Cycle Fatigue in High Pressure GH2</p>
<p>LCF testing in 34 MPa GH2 was performed on LP-DED NASA HR-1 at 2% strain range and R=-1
 </p>
<p>For both aging cycles, the LCF life was comparable in GH2 at 2% strain and was much higher than the wrought material in GH2
 </p>
<p>The LP-DED material has similar LCF life to wrought in 34 MPa GHe 
 </p>
<p>Powder reuse was also shown to not have an impact at 1x and 2x reuses
 </p>
<p>Both aging cycles achieved LCF life generally within scatter of the data in air at room temperature</p>
<p>Average cycles to failure around 800-1000 cycles for both aging cycles in GH2</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Summary and Future Work</p>
<p>Desirable, consistent microstructures have been shown to be achievable and repeatable in LP-DED NASA HR-1 and the issues of Ti segregation and η-phase precipitation mitigated</p>
<p></p>
<p>LP-DED NASA HR-1 has been shown to have similar trends in strength and ductility to wrought, but strengths were lower and ductility was higher for LP-DED material</p>
<p>LCF behavior is comparable to the wrought material in air and LP-DED NASA HR-1 has a much smaller debit in LCF life in GH2 compared to wrought</p>
<p>Further testing to round out GH2 LCF testing possible for future</p>
<p></p>
<p>LP-DED NASA HR-1 has been proven to have excellent resistance to HEE in high pressure GH2 up to 34 MPa and a good candidate alloy for several liquid rocket engine applications</p>
<p></p>
<p>L-PBF NASA HR-1 still in development at NASA MSFC</p>
<p>Region of good parameters establish</p>
<p>Evaluating process sensitivities and mechanical properties</p>
