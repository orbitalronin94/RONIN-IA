<p>Background
• The International Space Station (ISS) Air Quality Monitors 
</p>
<p>(AQMs) monitor trace concentrations of 23 targeted volatile 
organic compounds in the ISS atmosphere 
</p>
<p>• Two units with differing GC column chemistries are 
used to cover the target list
</p>
<p>• Target compounds were chosen based on years of 
archival data
</p>
<p>• Compounds on the target list had one or more of the 
following characteristics
</p>
<p>• Compounds frequently detected in spacecraft atmosphere 
(ethanol, acetone, xylenes, and 2-butanone)
</p>
<p>• Compounds with significant toxicity at low concentrations, 
even though they are detected infrequently on spacecraft 
(acrolein, benzene)
</p>
<p>• Compounds that can affect ECLSS (siloxanes and alcohols)
• The target list is fluid, dictated by experience and 
</p>
<p>changes in materials of construction or ECLSS 
</p>
<p>• The data from the AQM is used by NASA toxicologists as 
part of the assessment of air quality aboard the ISS
</p>
<p>• Some archival sampling using grab sample containers (GSCs) 
continues (~ every 45 days; 1 GSC is always in the U.S. LAB)
</p>
<p>• Samples collected at visiting vehicle ingress
• The AQM can also provide data for ECLSS troubleshooting
• In case of contingency, the AQM can help to monitor clean 
</p>
<p>up efforts</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Successes</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Support for Investigation of Potential Ammonia Leak
• On January 14, 2015, an alarm indicated an ammonia leak near Node 2
</p>
<p>• Crew evacuated to the Russian Service Module safe haven
• There were pressure fluctuations in the US LAB indicative of an ammonia leak
• Crew did not smell ammonia or experience symptoms
</p>
<p>• ECLSS engineers reviewed downlinked data and suspected a false alarm.  
There were only two options to verify, however:
</p>
<p>• Send the crew, with PPE into the unknown LAB atmosphere to take Dräger tube 
measurements
</p>
<p>• Control the AQM from the ground to analyze the atmosphere and downlink the data
• The AQM can provide trending data for ammonia based on a shifted RIP
</p>
<p>• AQM was activated and data was received that showed no ammonia in the 
atmosphere 
</p>
<p>• Given the seriousness of the situation, a subsequent analysis was performed using 
other techniques. These verified that no ammonia was present</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Air Quality Monitor (AQM) Role in Resolution of 
Potential Ammonia Leak
</p>
<p>• AQM data confirmed the analysis of the ECLSS personnel, and the 
crew was sent back to the LAB (with PPE) with high confidence that 
they were not entering a harmful environment
</p>
<p>• The AQM permitted confirmation of the LAB atmosphere situation 
without putting the crew or their safe haven at risk
</p>

<p>Analysis of Atmosphere for Siloxanes
</p>
<p>• Polydimethylsiloxanes (PDMS) found to
lead to formation of DMSD in Water
Processor Assembly leading to
decreased lifetime of Multifiltration
Beds
</p>
<p>• To remove the problematic DMSD
precursors, HEPA filters in Node 1 were
replaced with filters containing activated
charcoal on May 12, 2015
</p>
<p>• Decreases in PDMS were seen, but
DMSD levels remained high
</p>
<p>• It was thought that transient higher
siloxane levels may be present in Node 3
near the condensing heat exchanger
</p>
<p>• AQM 1005 moved to Node 3 to monitor
siloxane levels during condensate
collection following routine 30-day dry-
out of CHX
</p>
<p>• Siloxane concentrations in Node 3 found
to be similar to those in LAB
</p>

<p>Recent Concerns
</p>
<p>2-Propanol</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>SpaceX-Demo1 (SpX-DM1)
</p>
<p>• Docked on March 3rd, 2019
• AQM runs started within 30 minutes of hatch opening
• First ingress GSC collected at hatch opening
• Report of unknown odor reported by crew in Russian segment service 
</p>
<p>module, so expedited AQM results were requested
• Results were nominal except for 2-propanol (IPA)
</p>
<p>• AQM1 reported 5.9 mg/m3
</p>
<p>• Expedited analysis underestimated true AQM value (saturation)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>ISS Nominal IPA
</p>
<p>• IPA regularly seen in pre-flight offgas
tests
</p>
<p>• Upon docking, offgas products will 
diffuse through the stack
</p>
<p>• AQMs in LAB v/s visiting vehicle hatch
• Concentrations measured by the AQM at 
</p>
<p>first ingress should be lower than that 
collected in GSCs
</p>
<p>• IPA never observed by AQM above 0.8 
mg/m3 when started at first ingress
</p>
<p>• 7-day/180-day SMAC for IPA is 150 
mg/m3; total alcohol guideline for U.S. 
Water Recovery System is 5 mg/m3</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Increased Atmospheric Monitoring
• High IPAAQM led to MCC-H request 
</p>
<p>to monitor atmosphere more 
often (daily/as needed)
</p>
<p>• After initial drop to 2.2 mg/m3 on 
March 4th, IPAAQM returned to 
saturated levels for remainder of 
docked operations
</p>
<p>• Other compounds were stable
</p>
<p>• After undock on March 8th, IPAAQM
returned to nominal levels
</p>
<p>• Contingency GSCs collected 
during docked ops were returned 
and analyzed shortly after SpX-
DM1 return.
</p>

<p>Conclusions from Ground Testing
• Powering On
</p>
<p>• If there is no IPA present, powering on the AQM results in no IPA contamination in the initial clean/no 
sample runs
</p>
<p>• If the AQM is powered on in an atmosphere containing IPA, contamination is present
• If the IPA is removed, then clean runs will successfully remove the IPA
</p>
<p>• If the AQM is powered on in an atmosphere containing IPA, and that source remains, then IPA accumulates 
in the system, regardless of the scrubbing provided by the sieve cartridges
</p>
<p>• Normal Ops
• If there is an extremely large transient [IPA], a large peak will be seen in the sample runs
</p>
<p>• If the IPA is removed, clean runs will remove the IPA from the system
• If high [IPA] is present in the atmosphere, it will be pulled into the AQM during clean/no sample runs via the 
</p>
<p>purge, leading to contamination
• If this source is removed, the AQM functionality can be recovered following clean runs
</p>
<p>• These results suggest that continued operation of the AQM in the presence of 
elevated IPA could lead to interference in normal target compound measurements </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>IPA Anomaly Conclusions
• If our understanding of what happened during SpX-DM1 and NG-11 is 
</p>
<p>correct, then it suggests that our approach to calibration may not be 
appropriate
</p>
<p>• Current (and legacy) calibration methods utilize a “slip-stream” 
approach, in which the AQM sample pump pulls from an ~ 150 
cc/min flow of standard
</p>
<p>• Purges included with the methods simply pull from the lab 
atmosphere
</p>
<p>• This assumes that potentially-high analyte concentrations in-flight are 
transient
</p>
<p>• If the elevated analyte concentrations are long-term, we could 
overreport due to carryover</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>Benzene Anomaly Conclusions
</p>
<p>• Initial detection/reporting of benzene by the AQM in 2020 led to a large amount of concern 
by multiple NASA organizations
</p>
<p>• Deeper analysis of the data indicated that it was unlikely to be benzene
• Archival samples confirmed that no benzene was present
</p>
<p>• Ground testing of the AQM showed that benzene was not being produced in the system
• Trace VOCs found in archival samples did not coelute/interfere with benzene detection
• No identification of “unknown peak” was achieved
</p>
<p>• Repeat of “benzene anomaly” in early 2022 was easily attributed to the same(?) interfering 
peak; No concern from the toxicologists</p>

<p>Where is the Elevated IPA Coming From?
• No significant releases of IPA were reported during NG-11 docked operations, and 
</p>
<p>no payloads containing large quantities of IPA delivered or operating in this 
timeframe.
</p>
<p>• However, new hardware that could be releasing IPA into the ISS atmosphere was
delivered on NG-11
</p>
<p>• Charcoal HEPA Integrated Particle Scrubbers (CHIPS)
• The filters were processed in a facility that uses IPA, and a final cleaning was 
</p>
<p>performed with IPA
• The charcoal could potentially adsorb IPA and release it into the atmosphere
</p>
<p>• Thermal Amine Scrubber (TAS)
• CO2-removal Technology Demonstration
• Ground testing showed elevated IPA in some samples
• Acrylonitrile also present in some samples
</p>
<p>• Traces of acrylonitrile detected in ISS GSCs returned on SpX-18, SpX-20, and 
SpX-DM2</p>

<p>Testing Setups
</p>
<p>Legacy Science Verification Testing (SVT) setup
Only the sample inlet is exposed to the test mixture; 
the purge inlet pulls from the laboratory atmosphere

<p>Updated Kintek setup
The sample and purge inlets are both exposed to the 
</p>
<p>test mixture at the same time</p>

Immersion 
Setup

carryover (below detection 
limits) in the legacy testing 
setup, in which only the 
sample inlet is exposed to 
analytes
</p>

exposed to analytes, the 
carryover is apparent very 
quickly, and the reported 
concentration is increased
