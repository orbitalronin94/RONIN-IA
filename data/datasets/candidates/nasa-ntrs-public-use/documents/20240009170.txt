<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Eleven Principal Aircraft from My Career
(1970-2024)
</p>
<p>• North American T-28 Trojan (N502NA)*
• Helio Courier*
• DeHavilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter (N508NA)*
• General Dynamics F-106B Delta Dart (N816NA)*
• Learjet Lear 28/29 (N566NA)*
• Boeing B-757-100 (N575NA)
• Cirrus SR22 (N501NA)
• Beechcraft B200 (N529NA)
• Dassault HU-25A Guardian (N524NA)
• Gulfstream C-20B Gulfstream III (N520NA)
• Boeing 777-246ER (N577NA)
</p>
<p>• Served as Flight Test Engineer and Project Engineer
• Bold signifies still maintained and operated by RSD</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>North American T-28 Trojan (N502NA)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>North American T-28 Trojan (N502NA)
</p>
<p>• Utilized to calibrate guidance beam generated by NASA Wallops 
radar
• My first flight test experiment and as flight test engineer
• First exposure to NASA Wallops assets and personnel
• Lessons:  
• Flight test techniques and protocols were not taught in the academic 
</p>
<p>curriculum – on the job training (OJT) mentoring required
• Research Pilots are different from everybody else, including Flight Test 
</p>
<p>Engineers</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Helio Courier</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Helio Courier
</p>
<p>• Leased vehicle used to evaluate light STOL operations
• Published first NASA publication (NASA TM)
• Lessons:
</p>
<p>• Select the appropriate aircraft for the experiment
• Piloted Induced Oscillations (PIO) can corrupt your results
• Mentorship is essential/critical
• Teamwork is essential (“You cannot do it all by yourself”.)
• Manage by walking around
• Take detailed notes
• Do not write NASA reports like an English major – introduction to the “Grammar 
</p>
<p>Ladies”
• Even an experiment with marginal results can result in an informative report</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>DeHavilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter 
(N508NA)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>DeHavilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter, 1 of 2
</p>
<p>• First mission - selected to evaluate crosswind landing gear during 
STOL operations at NASA Wallops (aircraft leased from 
manufacturer)
• Lessons:
• NASA cannot perform all modifications inhouse – DeHavilland designed, 
</p>
<p>fabricated and installed the crosswind landing gear
• Detailed records are required during solicitation, award and performance 
</p>
<p>of the contract
• Be prepared to accept results that differ from the a priori expectations
• Be prepared for industry to not incorporate your results
• Prepare detailed cockpit check lists for the FTE and coordinate with the 
</p>
<p>pilot’s check list</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>DeHavilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter, 2 of 2
</p>
<p>• Second mission - used to evaluate airborne commercial lightning 
detector during Storm Hazards 1978 in Norman, OK (aircraft 
purchased by NASA prior to Storm Hazards Program)
• Lessons:
• Be prepared to change the sampling paradigm – instead of flying the 
</p>
<p>aircraft in the vicinity of thunderstorms, the aircraft was operated from a 
parked position on the ramp, and let the storms come to the aircraft
• Be prepared for the vendor to challenge your results</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>General Dynamics F-106B Delta Dart 
(N816NA)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>General Dynamics F-106B Delta Dart (N816NA)
• Selected to collect direct-strike lightning data (electromagnetic) and the correlation of 
</p>
<p>other storm hazards to aircraft lightning strikes during Storm Hazards 1979-1986
• My first multiagency research mission (NOAA and FAA)
• Lessons:
</p>
<p>• Become an SME in the subject:  “The library is over there, and the phone is on your desk.”
• Select an aircraft with appropriate safety features:  twin intakes, autoignition, overhead canopy rail, 
</p>
<p>canopy glass with imbedded gold foil, ejection seats, excusive use of certified Jet-A/JP-5 fuel
• Consult with lightning SMEs regarding additional precautions:  seal all fasteners in fuel tanks and 
</p>
<p>conduct “hot and cold zap” tests of the aircraft on the ground before each season
• Flight in such an aircraft required extensive crew training (e.g., physiological (altitude chamber), 
</p>
<p>emergency egress (ejection seat and water survival) and cockpit procedures)
• Repeat lesson - Be prepared to accept and defend results that differ from the a priori expectations:
</p>
<p>• Aircraft trigger the lightning strikes, do not intercept them
• Aircraft lightning strikes do not correlate with the areas of heaviest precipitation and turbulence
• Aircraft lightning swept attachments to the aircraft exterior are not limited to the sharp edges and along the 
</p>
<p>fuselage</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Learjet Lear 28/29 (N566NA)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Learjet Lear 28/29 (N566NA)
</p>
<p>• Selected for Joint NASA/USAF  Airborne Field Mill Program to 
correlate airborne electric fields with ground-based 
measurements to develop Launch Commit Criteria for Natural 
and Triggered Lightning
• Mission created following lightning strike to NASA Johnson T-38 
</p>
<p>over southern California and loss of Atlas Centaur during launch 
from Cape Canaveral
• Last mission in which I served as a Flight Test Engineer
• Lesson
• Lessons learned from the previous Storm Hazards Program were 
</p>
<p>applicable to this mission</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Boeing 757-100 (N557NA)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Boeing 757-100 (N557NA)
• Acquired to replace NASA Boeing 737 as transport category aircraft for ARMD-
</p>
<p>funded missions
• RSD designed, fabricated and installed an Aft Flight Deck (AFD) from which 
</p>
<p>the aircraft could be flown with the ship’s pilots in the cockpit providing safety 
oversight
• Incorporating the AFD was the most complex task undertaken by RSD 
</p>
<p>including the B-777 (no ties into the ship’s flight controls or Flight 
Management System)
• ARMD cancelled effort due to change in the Agency’s priorities
• Lessons:
</p>
<p>• NASA as a Federal agency is subject to politics
• Be flexible –
</p>
<p>• Removed AFD and installed it in Building 1268 complex as a fixed-base simulator
• Began supporting SMD-funded missions</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Cirrus SR22 (N501NA)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Cirrus SR22 (N501NA)
• Selected by NASA along with the Lancair Columbia 300 (LC40) to 
</p>
<p>support the Advanced General Aviation Technology Experiment(AGATE); 
however, AGATE partners required NASA to provide only one aircraft –
RSD selected the SR22.
• Equipped with the GA Baseline Research System (GABRS) and UAS 
</p>
<p>Surrogate capability (both designed, fabricated and installed by RSD)
• Recently added modified commercial belly pod to contain EPA science 
</p>
<p>instrumentation (nadir viewing)
• Lesson:
</p>
<p>• Even “simple” GA aircraft can accommodate highly complex research 
instrumentation systems</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Beechcraft B200 (N529NA)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Beechcraft B200 (N529NA)
• Selected as a Mission Management asset for the Center.  Later transitioned to 
</p>
<p>research aircraft for ARMD and SMD-funded missions
• Dual nadir portals installed by vendor with the FAA approval (STC) for the 
</p>
<p>modifications
• B200 supported first SMD-funded missions performed by RSD
• SMD missions are bid and proposal versus ARMD, which was directed 
</p>
<p>research
• Supported Heliophysics/SMD by serving as imaging platform for sounding 
</p>
<p>rocket launches
• Supported first RSD foreign deployments:  Canada, Greenland, South Korea, 
</p>
<p>Spain and Gabon
• Lesson:
</p>
<p>• Flight test techniques and protocols utilized by RSD were applicable to SMD missions</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Dassault HU-25A Guardian (N524NA)
</p>
<p>• Former U.S. Coast Guard aircraft acquired to provide NASA with 
business class aircraft to support science missions:  “weapons 
bay”, pylons and existing workstation
• RSD installed multiple external science probes and internal racks 
</p>
<p>as necessary
• Lesson:
• Repeat:  Flight test techniques and protocols utilized by RSD were 
</p>
<p>applicable to missions on these aircraft</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Gulfstream C-20B Gulfstream III (N520NA)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Gulfstream C-20B Gulfstream III (N520NA)
• Acquired from U.S. Air Force to replace HU-25A, providing RSD with 
</p>
<p>global reach:  Greenland, South Korea, Taiwan, Philippines, Thailand, 
Sao Tome and Principe and South Africa
• Dual nadir portals designed, fabricated and installed by commercial 
</p>
<p>vendor
• Dropsonde design based on design provided by NASA Johnson –
</p>
<p>beginning of Integrated Product Team for Gulfstream aircraft at NASA 
Langley, Johnson and Armstrong
• Lessons:
</p>
<p>• Repeat:  Flight test techniques and protocols utilized by RSD are applicable to 
missions on this aircraft
• Repeat:  Detailed records are required during solicitation, award and 
</p>
<p>performance of the contract</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Boeing 777-246ER (N577NA)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Boeing 777-246ER (N577NA)
• Acquired by NASA to replace the DC-8, which was maintained and operated by 
</p>
<p>NASA Armstrong.  Provides NASA with large transport category aircraft for 
deploying multiple payloads simultaneously globally
• Initial baseline being designed by multi-Center IPT with support from 
</p>
<p>commercial vendors
• Portals to be installed off site by commercial vendor
• Initial Operational Capability scheduled for end of CY25
• Lessons:
</p>
<p>• Repeat:  Flight test techniques and protocols utilized by RSD are applicable to missions 
on this aircraft (in particular, the B200, B-757, HU-25A and Gulfstream III)
</p>
<p>• Repeat:  Detailed records are required during solicitation, award and performance of the 
contracts
</p>
<p>• Close coordination between the members of the IPT are essential (analyses, design 
reviews with minutes, ICDs, action items with closures)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Top Ten Lessons from My Flight Test Career
• Safety first
• Mentorship is essential/critical
• Teamwork is essential
• Maintain close coordination between the members of your IPT (analyses, 
</p>
<p>design reviews with minutes, ICDs, action items with closures)
• Take detailed notes – documentation
• Consult with the SMEs in your subject area as you become a SME
• Apply test techniques and protocols from your prior missions to your current 
</p>
<p>mission as appropriate and applicable
• Be prepared to accept and defend your results but be open to valid criticism
• Select the appropriate vehicle for your mission, considering capabilities and 
</p>
<p>safety
• Prepare yourself for the mission</p>
