<div class="slide-content"><p>Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP) Engine Intro</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide4_rId3"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide4_rId4"></div>
<p>Base of LH2 Tank</p>
<p>Helium</p>

<p>Turbopumps</p>
<p>Propellant heated directly by a nuclear reactor and thermally expanded/accelerated through a nozzle</p>
<p>Specific Impulse improvement over chemical rockets due to lower molecular weight of propellant</p>
<p>Challenges</p>
<p>Regulatory approval </p>
<p>Exhaust gas may contain fission products </p>
<p>Requires capture or processing of exhaust gas</p>
<p>Exhaust system design to accommodate extreme temperatures, high flow rates, and velocities </p>

<p>No need to mix LOX and LH and ignite to create combustion </p>
<p>Less propellant used to produce more thrust/velocity </p>
<p>Technology for human-rated Mars missions</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>History/Background</p>
<p>Nuclear Furnace (NF-1)</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>6</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide6_rId2"></div>
<p></p>
<p>Nuclear Furnace Testing</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide6_rId3"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide6_rId4"></div>
<p>NF-1 developed at the end of Rover/NERVA</p>
<p>Pre-regulatory environment </p>
<p>~4 years design and development </p>
<p>~$165M ($FY20) including design, fab, testing</p>
<p>NF-1 provided first successful demonstration of exhaust processing “scrubbing” exhaust of radionuclides</p>
<p>Only Rover/NERVA reactor test with filtered exhaust before burning hydrogen in flare stack and operated successfully</p>
<p>Included slots for 49 core elements</p>
<p>Composite fuel achieved better corrosion performance, while carbide fuel had cracked extensively near center of reactor</p>
<p>4 tests performed at full power (44MW)</p>
<p>Exit gas temperature above 4000R for 121 minutes and above 4400R for 109 minutes total</p>
<p>Cancelled in July 1972 when Rover/NERVA ended</p>
</div>

<div class="slide-content"><p>History/Background</p>
<p>Rocket Exhaust Capture System (RECS)</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>8</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide8_rId2"></div>
<p></p>
<p>Engine hydrogen exhaust is burned at high temperatures with oxygen and produces steam to be cooled, condensed, and collected for controlled processing and disposal</p>
<p>Total capture of exhaust gas </p>
<p>Risk around the combustion process of oxygen and hydrogen to produce steam</p>
<p>Limited by capture volume (Dewar)</p>
<p>High cost and requires a subscale demonstration of facility </p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>History/Background</p>
<p>Post Run Capture System (PRCS)</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>10</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide10_rId3"></div>
<p></p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide10_rId4"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide10_rId5"></div>
<p>Shift from certification testing to technology demonstration test</p>
<p>Releases exhaust gas into open air during start-up/nominal operations </p>
<p>Heavily relies on fuel integrity and ability to retain majority of fission products</p>
<p>Shutdown/cooldown redirected to accumulation tanks for total capture </p>
<p>Regulatory approval risk</p>
<p>20 psia internal backpressure</p>
<p>Designed for 12.5k lbf thrust engine test</p>
<p>8:1 nozzle area ratio for engine test article </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-notes"><p>Cost came in higher than desired </p>
<p>20 psia</p>
<p>10</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>HECS Introduction</p>
<p>High-pressure Exhaust Capture System</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>11</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide11_rId3"></div>
<table><tr>	<td>Feature </td>	<td>PRCS</td>	<td>HECS</td></tr>
<tr>	<td>Vertical Pipe Diameter</td>	<td>11 ft</td>	<td>6 ft</td></tr>
<tr>	<td>Vertical Pipe Length/Test Stand Height</td>	<td>54 ft</td>	<td>22 ft</td></tr>
<tr>	<td>Header Diameter</td>	<td>8 ft</td>	<td>1.5 ft</td></tr>
</table>
<p>Higher internal backpressure in the system (300 psia) compresses exhaust gas volume </p>
<p>Compressed volume allows for more tanks and total capture</p>
<p>Favorable for regulatory approval </p>
<p>Requires engine test article change</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide11_rId4"></div>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-notes"><p>HECS 5k thrust test designed too that further reduces test stand height and footprint </p>
<p>12.5k lbf thrust test and 5k lbf thrust test evaluated </p>
<p>Lower thrust is smaller piping diameters, smaller piping length, thus reducing test stand height </p>
<p>Less tanks, less footprint/foundation </p>
<p>Cost savings </p>
<p>11</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>HECS Testing Article </p>
<p>NTP Engine with 2:1 Nozzle Area Ratio (AR)</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>12</p>
<p>A smaller AR reduces the diameter at the nozzle exit and length of the overall nozzle, which improves the structural integrity, thus allowing for higher pressure loading upon the nozzle. </p>
<p>The smaller AR also causes the exhaust gas to exit the nozzle plane at a lower velocity.</p>
<p>8:1 Nozzle Ratio for PRCS</p>
<p>2:1 Nozzle Ratio for HECS</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide12_rId3"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide12_rId4"></div>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-notes"><p>Pressure differential from chamber pressure and backpressure is important for proper engine performance </p>
<p>12</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>HECS CONOPS</p>
<p>Prestart </p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>13</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide13_rId3"></div>
<p>System is inert and purged</p>
<p>Accumulation tanks are filled with water </p>
<p>Tank drain valves and isolation valves closed </p>
<p>Water spray system on and water supply valves open</p>
<p>Drain valve at base of duct opened to control water level</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-notes"><p>CONOPS will be further matured as test article and test plan is further developed/refined </p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>HECS CONOPS</p>
<p>Start-up/Nominal Operations</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>14</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide14_rId2"></div>
<p>Water spray system on and water supply valves open</p>
<p>Drain valve at base of duct opened to control water level</p>
<p>Start-up/nominal tanks valve open </p>
<p>Drain valve for start-up/nominal tanks open</p>
<p>Controls internal backpressure</p>
<p>Shutdown/cooldown tank valves closed and isolated</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>HECS CONOPS</p>
<p>Shutdown </p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>15</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide15_rId2"></div>
<p>Water spray system on and water supply valves open</p>
<p>Drain valve at base of duct opened to control water level</p>
<p>Start-up/nominal tanks full and associated valves closed</p>
<p>Shutdown/cooldown tank isolation valves open</p>
<p>Drain valve for shutdown/cooldown tanks open</p>
<p>Controls internal backpressure</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>HECS CONOPS</p>
<p>Cooldown</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>16</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide16_rId2"></div>
<p>Water spray system on and water supply valves open</p>
<p>Drain valve at base of duct closed to accumulate water </p>
<p>Creates water seal and reduces purge volume</p>
<p>Isolation valve in header pipe closes </p>
<p>Valve in cooldown piping opens to direct flow straight to tanks at a lower pressure </p>
<p>Drain valve for shutdown/cooldown tanks open</p>
<p>Controls internal backpressure</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>HECS CONOPS</p>
<p>Post-cooldown</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>17</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide17_rId2"></div>
<p>Water spray system off and water supply valves closed</p>
<p>Shutdown/cooldown tanks full and valves close </p>
<p>Drain valves close </p>
<p>Purge on, evacuating exhaust gas from main vertical pipe </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Exhaust Gas Processing</p>
<p>Processing of Cooldown Flow and Extensibility Option </p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>18</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide18_rId2"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide18_rId3"></div>
<p>Processes cooldown flow with real time processing system</p>
<p>Smaller, COTS components utilized due to much lower flow rate</p>
<p>Reduces number of cooldown tanks and allows for longer testing duration</p>
<p></p>
<p>Removes accumulation tank configuration and replaces with a full-scale real-time processing system</p>
<p>Utilizes existing infrastructure to reduce cost for a development/certification test</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusion </p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023</p>
<p>19</p>
<p>Ground testing provides ability for PIE of components, more data collection, ability to halt and restart testing to replace/inspect components/systems</p>
<p>HECS provides a lower cost, shorter lead time option for a technology demonstration test of a NTP engine </p>
<p>HECS total capture capability provides favorability for regulatory approval</p>
<p>Technology demonstration test matures NTP engine TRL without high initial investment </p>
<p>Maturation of TRL through technology demonstration testing reduces risk for development, certification, and/or flight testing </p>
