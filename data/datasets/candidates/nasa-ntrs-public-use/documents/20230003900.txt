<p>Prior Efforts to Augment Heat Transfer with Roughness
</p>
<p>• LEWICE/LEWICE3D
Ø Reads in CFD flow solution
Ø CFD solution only used for trajectory calculation
Ø Heat transfer coefficient calculated via integral boundary layer with sand-grain roughness 
</p>
<p>(Schlichting)
• RANS Models that incorporate roughness effects
Ø Wilcox
Ø Aupoix
Ø Menter
</p>
<p>• GlennICE 
Ø Reads in CFD flow solution
Ø Uses heat transfer coefficient from CFD
</p>
<p>– CFD may or may not have included roughness effects
– Need to account for cases where CFD did not incorporate roughness (such as Spalart-
</p>
<p>Allmaras)

<p>Heat Transfer Augmentation in GlennICE
</p>
<p>• Heat transfer coefficients are supplied to GlennICE via two modes
Ø Reading surface heat transfer coefficient (HTC) directly
Ø Reading surface heat fluxes and computing HTC
– Requires two flow solutions with different wall temperature boundary conditions
– These flow solutions are user-supplied as GlennICE does not directly interact with 
</p>
<p>the CFD solver
• Roughness can be incorporated via turbulence model or by adjustment in 
</p>
<p>GlennICE
Ø Adjustment via turbulence model could require modifications to flow solvers
– Adjustments we can't make since we do not develop CFD solvers
</p>
<p>• Heat transfer in GlennICE can be augmented by a constant or by using an 
augmentation based on roughness height
Ø Roughness height calculated by McClain correlation
</p>
<p>5</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>SAE INTERNATIONAL
</p>
<p>McClain Roughness Correlation
</p>
<p>• Experiments conducted over several years in IRT to measure roughness
Ø Uses scanned ice shapes (short time sprays) for a variety of conditions 
Ø Tests performed on multiple geometries
</p>
<p>• An experimentally-derived correlation was produced that provides 
roughness distribution on a given geometry
Ø Utilizes both surface and freestream variables
</p>
<p>• Roughness height correlation may not be applicable to ice crystals or 
geometries other than wings.
</p>
<p>• The purpose of this effort is to empirically relate roughness height 
distribution to HTC augmentation
</p>

<p>Iterative Process for Heat Transfer Augmentation
</p>
<p>• Roughness depends on freezing fraction
• Freezing fraction depends on htc
• HTC depends on roughness
• Iteration process 
Ø Use initial htc to find freezing fraction
Ø Use freezing fraction to find roughness
Ø Use roughness to find new HTC
Ø Process iterates to convergence
Ø Number of roughness iterations is also a user 
</p>
<p>input
</p>

<p>SAE INTERNATIONAL
</p>
<p>Empirical Case Study Process
</p>
<p>• Three user inputs need to be defined
Ø Augmentation multiplier
Ø Ideal rime limit
Ø Number of roughness iterations (fixed at 6 iterations in this study)
</p>
<p>• Selected 95 cases from LEWICE validation database
Ø All cases were large (accumulation parameter based on diameter over 3)
Ø All cases were glaze (stagnation freezing fraction less than 0.5)
Ø Data consists of 2D tracings on straight wing airfoil cross-sections
– Six airfoils represented
</p>
<p>11</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>SAE INTERNATIONAL
</p>
<p>Process to Determine Correlation Coefficients
</p>
<p>• Create grids in Pointwise
Ø Rhino used to create smooth airfoil geometries
</p>
<p>• Run FUN3D on all 95 conditions
Ø Grid sensitivity study performed 
Ø No variation in turbulence model (SA-neg used for all cases)
</p>
<p>• Run GlennICE on all 95 conditions for using one combination of 
correlation parameters
</p>
<p>• Obtain ice shape measurements using THICK utility
Ø Horn angles and horn thicknesses
Ø THICK utility introduces its own errors
</p>
<p>• Repeat GlennICE runs for another combination of correlation parameters
</p>

<p>SAE INTERNATIONAL
</p>
<p>Conclusion
</p>
<p>• User inputs successfully calibrated to experimental data
Ø Defaults set to augmentation of 5 and ideal rime limit of 9 mm (product = 45)
</p>
<p>• Augmentation multiplier and ideal rime limit exhibit similar behavior
Ø Results plotted as the multiple of those factors 
</p>
<p>• Similar behavior on all cases above a multiplier of about 20
• Augmentation used is far higher than the measured augmentation of heat 
</p>
<p>transfer due to roughness
Ø Augmentation will need to be re-evaluated if multi-shot capability is 
</p>
<p>implemented.
• This study only examined differences in geometry
Ø Changes in aerodynamic performance were not examined.
</p>
