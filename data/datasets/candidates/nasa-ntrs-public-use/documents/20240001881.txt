<div class="slide-content"><p>Adaptable Deployable Entry and Placement Technology (ADEPT) Project Background</p>
<p>ADEPT FY12-FY13</p>
<p>Focus on 6m Venus ADEPT (Low m/CdA to deliver 1000kg lander with peak decel &lt; 30 g’s)</p>
<p>Carbon fabric arc-jet tested 100-240 W/cm2</p>
<p>Successful demonstration of 2m Ground Test Article</p>
<p>ADEPT FY14</p>
<p>Demonstration carbon-fabric stitched joint</p>
<p>Major budget reduction. Project re-plan from 6m Venus to 1m scale for ‘cubesat class’ secondary payload mission infusion</p>
<p>ADEPT FY15</p>
<p>Continuation of ‘FTE-only’ project status</p>
<p>Focus on 0.7m Aero-loads wind tunnel test &amp; 
0.35m SPRITE-C Pathfinder arcjet test</p>
<p>Planning for 0.7m sub-orbital sounding rocket flight</p>

<p>0.7m development. Deployment Prototype.  FTE-only</p>
<p>ADEPT FY17-18</p>

<p>FREE-FLIGHT DYNAMICS TESTING</p>
<p>IMPACT TESTING</p>
<p>Drop testing enabled testing of various impact attenuators, characterization of impact load and demonstrated survivability of the on-board memory cards and battery design robustness.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Impact testing also enabled battery safety procedures to be rehearsed in the event of severe damage to the rechargeable lithium-ion batteries upon impact.</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Assessed the dynamic behavior of ADEPT SR-1 at sub-sonic speeds, and aided in the selection of center of mass location to maximize likelihood of nose first impact. Testing performed in the Langley Vertical Spin Tunnel.</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Static and dynamic aerodatabase predictions were used to perform preflight trajectory analysis of vehicle performance and helped inform risk and safety management.</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>GTA Results Summary &amp; Findings</p>
<p>48</p>
<p>Results Summary:</p>
<p>GTA demonstrated reliable and repeatable ADEPT deployment</p>
<p>Only one unsuccessful deployment (caused by operator error – latch not reset)</p>
<p>Achieved fabric tension level requirement of &gt;= 100 lbf/in at mid-gore location</p>
<p>Fabric showed no significant degradation from the repeated stow-deploy cycles</p>
<p>Demonstrated general manufacturability of an ADEPT aeroshell</p>
<p>Fabric to rib interface maintained tension &amp; was mostly adequate – (see findings)</p>
<p>ADEPT GTA formed an acceptable deployed aeroshell shape (except possibly at trailing edge)</p>
<p>Findings / Lessons Learned:</p>
<p>Good alignment and structural compliance allowed for successful deployments despite the mechanism being over-constrained</p>
<p>Reduce over-constraints where practical</p>
<p>Fabric in-plane stiffness is very high (actually dominated over the structure lateral stiffness)</p>
<p>Need to make allowances for the structure to be adjusted to the fabric</p>
<p>Recommend that the fabric be allowed to rest on top of the ribs (not continuous attachment)</p>
<p>Allow some adjustability in final rib deployment angle to accommodate the as-built cone angle of the carbon fabric skirt  (don’t expect to hit an exact rib deploy angle &amp; desired tension level at the same time  deploy to the angle that provides the proper tension)</p>
<p>Trailing edge treatment (rib tip radius with single fabric layer stretched between) was unsatisfactory  recommend a different trailing edge design for a better outboard shoulder</p>
