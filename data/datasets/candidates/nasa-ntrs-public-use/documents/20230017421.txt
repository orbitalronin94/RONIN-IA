<p>Mobility Challenges
</p>
<p>• Transportation shapes society, enabling 
economic and social interactions
</p>
<p>• The quality of life is impacted by ever growing 
commute time
</p>
<p>• Transportation is increasingly driven by door-to-door challenges
</p>
<p>Air Mobility can convert our 2D mobility system to a 3D mobility system, 
increasing mobility options for both people and goods</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>4
</p>
<p>Air Mobility Data and Reasoning Challenges
</p>
<p>• Urban air operations require decisions based on 
diverse and dynamic data, including vehicle, 
airspace, weather, infrastructure, payload, and 
customer data
</p>
<p>• Decisions need to be made where the data exist and, 
increasingly, data may be too large or dynamic to centralize
</p>
<p>• The envisioned density of operations will only be achieved if decision 
processes continue to move from purely human decisions, to machine-
intelligence based decisions with human oversight
</p>
<p>• Machine-intelligence generally requires greater volumes of digital data of 
explicitly known applicability, quality, and many other “ilities”</p>
<p></p>
</div>

</p>

<p>Critical Barriers &amp; Minimum Success Criteria
</p>
<p>Critical Barrier​ Minimum Success Criteria​
</p>
<p>Limited Interoperability​
</p>
<p>Demonstrate successful data exchange and 
interpretation between three participants (a 
consumer, data provider and a reasoning 
provider)​
</p>
<p>Limited data standardization​ Create and use standard metadata schema and 
format for two datasets​
</p>
<p>Clear Economic Incentives Demonstrate one monitory transaction for data 
exchange​
</p>
<p>Insufficient Current and 
Accurate Data Where it is Needed 
to Make Airspace Operations 
&amp; Management Decisions​
</p>
<p>Demonstrate integration between two data and 
two reasoning services with 100 data and 
reasoning simulated consumers​</p>
<p></p>

</p>
<p>Critical Barriers &amp; Minimum Success Criteria
</p>
<p>Critical Barrier​ Minimum Success Criteria​
Inability to Switch 
Between Different Service 
Providers​
</p>
<p>Demonstrate swapping one data provider and 
adding another one at run time​
</p>
<p>Lack of Economic Incentives Integrate 1 external data service and 
one external reasoning service.​
</p>
<p>Reusable Reasoning Services 
are not Easily Accessible​
</p>
<p>Demonstrate integration with two reasoning 
services to support real time decisions.​
</p>
<p>Adverse Impact to Safety 
Related Decisions without High-
Reliability and Low-Latency 
Data Interconnect to Edge 
Computing Devices​
</p>
<p>Demonstrate one service deployed at 
a physical or simulated Edge node with latency 
less than 20ms for safety related data less than 
1KB in size​</p>

<p>Data &amp; Reasoning Fabric – Original Proposed Solution
</p>
<p>DRF is envisioned to provide access to data and AI-driven decision support tools to:
</p>
<p>• Provide transportation for people and 
cargo to places previously not served or
underserved by aviation.
</p>
<p>• Enable increased flights in congested 
urban areas
</p>
<p>• Support the growth of AAM operations 
that depend on accurate, reliable, and 
current data</p>
<p></p>

</p>
<p>DRF Mission Statement and Approach
</p>
<p>• Develop a DRF Accelerator which includes prototype instantiations of key DRF features, 
including data and reasoning services, a DRF marketplace software architecture sufficient 
to engage end-user representatives​
</p>
<p>• Exercise the DRF Accelerator with representatives of key service providers and end user 
stakeholder communities in order to demonstrate that the DRF Accelerator provides key 
data and reasoning functions​
</p>
<p>• Assess the DRF Accelerator to determine if the minimum viable system concept (MVSC) 
can overcome the identified critical barriers across the desirability, viability and feasibility 
perspectives​
</p>
<p>By completion of the DRF CAS Project, provide the information required by ARMD management 
to make an informed decision whether and how to continue investing in DRF</p>

<p>DRF Support Scope for CAP Mission
</p>
<p>To support the lightning induced fire detection flight mission of CAP, the use of the DRF aims to 
facilitate:
</p>
<p>• PRE FLIGHT: Streamlined access to decision support data that includes:
1. Lightning strike data [sourced from the GOES 16 and GOES 17 satellites managed 
</p>
<p>by NASA/NOAA]
2. Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) data [sourced from GOES satellite]
3. Existing fire data [sourced from the VIIRS equipment onboard the Suomi NPP 
</p>
<p>satellite]
4. Weather data [sourced from openweathermap.org data]
5. Wind data [sourced from openweathermap.org data]
6. Population density data [sourced from US Census Bureau data]
7. Route planning [internally developed by NASA DRF team]
</p>
<p>• IN FLIGHT: Coordinated information gathering and exchange among flight entities for:
1. Detection and processing of fire markers [internally developed by NASA DRF team]
2. Notification of mission status and task assignments [internally developed by NASA DRF team]
</p>
<p>• POST FLIGHT: Information sharing for the fire response team that includes:
1. Publishing of actionable data [internally developed by NASA DRF team]</p>

<p>Transition – ATM-X Extensible Traffic Management (xTM)
</p>
<p>• Discovery and Synchronization Service (DSS):
o The purpose of the DSS is to discover relevant operations and to ensure 
</p>
<p>information that is shared and distributed in a federated network is 
synchronized to enable a Common Operating Picture
</p>
<p>o The current DSS represents a technical risk to future UAM efforts, DRF is 
considering proposing to expand DRF capabilities to meet UAM DSS 
requirements and find commonalities across xTM domains
</p>
<p>• Located in between the PSUs or USSs and the 
supplemental data service providers (SDSPs), DRF 
facilitates:
</p>
<p>• Discovery
• Accessibility
• Accountability
• Interoperability 
</p>
<p>• Within ATM-X, DRF technology is being considered to 
support decentralized data exchanges for diverse 
extensible traffic management data exchanges</p>
