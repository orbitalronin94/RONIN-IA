<p>The SLD Challenge
</p>
<p>Quantifying Liquid Water Content (LWC) in Supercooled Large Droplet (SLD)
environments is critical for aircraft certification.
</p>
<p>Thermal anemometry remains the standard for in-situ measurements, but it struggles in
large-droplet regimes.
</p>
<p>The core problem: Impact-induced mass loss (splashing and aerodynamic stripping)
causes severe signal deficits, frequently exceeding 40% for standard cylindrical elements.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 2 / 23</p>

<p>IRT Facility Capabilities
</p>
<p>The NASA Icing Research Tunnel (IRT) is a closed-loop, refrigerated wind tunnel.
</p>
<p>Capable of simulating both 14 CFR Part 25 Appendix C and Appendix O (SLD)
meteorological envelopes.
</p>
<p>Features an array of air-atomizing nozzles with independent control over water and air
pressures.
</p>
<p>This architecture crucially allows for the decoupling of LWC from the droplet Median
Volume Diameter (MVD) during testing.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 4 / 23</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Thermal Anemometry Fundamentals
</p>
<p>Hot-wire probes infer LWC from the electrical power required to evaporate impinging
water.
</p>
<p>The sensing elements are maintained at a constant target temperature (approximately
140 ◦C).
</p>
<p>The Fundamental Assumption: This measurement principle assumes that all impacting
water remains on the wire long enough to vaporize. If droplets splash or shed, the sensor
systematically under-reads the true LWC.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 5 / 23</p>

<p>Geometry Matters
</p>
<p>Testing spans three specific micro-scale element geometries:
</p>
<p>021 Wire: A 0.53mm cylindrical wire.
083 Wire: A 2.11mm or 2.4mm reverse half-pipe (convex face to flow).
Half-Pipe: A concave element optimized for retention.
</p>
<p>Different probe housings inherently alter the local aerodynamic flow fields approaching
the wires:
</p>
<p>The Multi-Element (MW) shroud induces a bluff-body deceleration.
The Ice Crystal Detector (ICD) airfoil induces local flow acceleration.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 7 / 23</p>
<p></p>

<p>Breaking Calibration Circularity
</p>
<p>Standard practice often calibrates hot-wires against facility cloud standards that were
originally derived from other master hot-wires.
</p>
<p>To break this circularity, this experiment utilizes the Isokinetic Probe 2 (IKP2).
</p>
<p>The IKP2 uses total evaporation thermodynamics (rather than convective heat transfer)
to provide a true, independent ground truth for Total Water Content (TWC).
</p>

<p>The Efficiency Framework
</p>
<p>The overall collection efficiency of a hot-wire sensor is driven by three distinct physical
probabilities:
</p>
<p>Etot = Ecoll · Eret · Eevap
</p>
<p>Ecoll: Aerodynamic collision efficiency.
</p>
<p>Eret: Mechanical mass retention efficiency.
</p>
<p>Eevap: Thermodynamic evaporation efficiency.
</p>
<p>To understand the true mechanics of retention (Eret), we must mathematically isolate it
from aerodynamic capture and thermal evaporation.
</p>

<p>Validating Aerodynamics &amp; Thermodynamics
</p>
<p>Aerodynamics (Ecoll):
Verified via 3D Lagrangian particle tracking.
For large droplets (MVD &gt; 50 µm), inertia easily overcomes flow streamlines, resulting in
Ecoll ≈ 1.0. Aerodynamic bypass is not the cause of the signal deficit.
</p>
<p>Thermodynamics (Eevap):
Verified via a high-fidelity 1-D electro-thermal model.
A simulated “stress test” at extreme liquid loading (3.5 gm−3) confirms the sensors maintain
a high thermal margin, ensuring Eevap ≈ 1.0.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 12 / 23</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Isolating Mass Retention (Eret)
</p>
<p>With aerodynamics and thermodynamics mathematically decoupled, we can isolate the
residual efficiency.
</p>
<p>Because the droplets successfully impact the sensor (Ecoll ≈ 1.0), and the sensor possesses
the thermal capacity to vaporize them (Eevap ≈ 1.0), the observed SLD deficit is strictly
mechanical.
</p>
<p>High-inertia droplets physically splash or are stripped off the wire by aerodynamic shear
before a complete phase change can occur.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 13 / 23</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Weber vs. Sommerfeld Scaling
</p>
<p>The Weber Number (We):
Scales disruptive kinetic energy against restorative surface tension.
Successfully predicts droplet deformation but is insufficient for predicting mass ejection. A
droplet can flatten at a high We without splashing if viscous forces dampen it.
</p>
<p>The Sommerfeld Number (K):
</p>
<p>Defines the complete physical descriptor: K = We1/2Re1/4
</p>
<p>Successfully captures the physical splashing threshold by incorporating the stabilizing effect
of viscous dissipation within the spreading lamella.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 14 / 23</p>
<p></p>

<p>The Splashing Threshold (Kcrit)
</p>
<p>The critical splashing threshold (Kcrit) is not a universal constant; it maps directly to
probe geometry and local aerodynamics.
</p>
<p>Airfoil Housings (ICD): Flow acceleration over the airfoil increases the effective impact
energy of the droplet.
</p>
<p>Bluff-Body Housings (MW): The shroud decelerates local flow, reducing impact energy.
</p>
<p>Consequently, droplets reach the splashing threshold at a significantly lower freestream
Kcrit on the airfoil compared to the bluff body.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 17 / 23</p>
<p></p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>The Hazard of “Artifact Knees”
</p>
<p>A nonlinear least-squares solver will unconditionally return a breakpoint (x0) for a
piecewise model, because the model has no valid state without one.
</p>
<p>When data are purely linear or too scattered, the resulting breakpoint is an artifact: it is
physically meaningless and statistically insignificant.
</p>
<p>The Diagnostic Signature: These artifact knees “chase” the initial guess across the
data range rather than converging to a stable value.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 20 / 23</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Auditable Model Selection
</p>
<p>To separate real physics from fitting artifacts, we require unanimous agreement across
three independent criteria:
</p>
<p>1 F -test for Nested Models: Does the piecewise model reduce the residual sum of squares
more than chance allows? (p &lt; 0.05).
</p>
<p>2 Breakpoint Stability Test: Does the breakpoint converge to the same value regardless of
the optimizer’s starting guess? (sx0 &lt; 0.15).
</p>
<p>3 Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC): Does the model improvement justify the extra
parameter penalty? (∆BIC &lt; 0).
</p>
<p>Any disagreement yields a conservative linear selection flagged as “indeterminate”.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 21 / 23</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>The Verdict &amp; Uncertainty Propagation
</p>
<p>The Five-Element Verdict:
Two Piecewise: MW-HP and ICD-083 (clean, stable breakpoints).
One Linear: MW-083 (clean linear unanimous selection).
Two Indeterminate: MW-021 and ICD-HP (disagreement between F -test and stability;
defaulted to linear).
</p>
<p>Hybrid Uncertainty Propagation:
Taylor expansion (Kline–McClintock) is used by default for speed and reproducibility.
The logic dispatches to Monte Carlo only near the breakpoint, where the discontinuous
derivative invalidates a Taylor expansion.
</p>
<p>R. Chen (NASA GRC) SLD Mass Retention &amp; Model Selection August 17–20, 2026 22 / 23</p>

<p>Conclusion: The Current SoTA
</p>
<p>Summary: We have replaced assumed model forms with explicit, auditable decisions.
</p>
<p>Methods Focus: The Sommerfeld-based piecewise framework, paired with multi-criteria
selection, represents the current State of the Art for hot-wire SLD correction.
</p>
<p>The Path Forward:
The indeterminate results emphasize that data scatter remains a challenge.
Future work requires more testing for repeatability and confidence.
We aim to refine these geometry-specific coefficients as the dataset grows.
</p>
