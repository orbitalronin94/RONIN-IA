<p>Introduction
</p>
<p>07/19/2023 ICES-2023-51 2
</p>
<p>• The SOA on oxygen recovery onboard the ECLSS at the ISS is a complex, heavy, and power-consuming
</p>
<p>system that recovers approximately 50% of the oxygen (O2) from metabolic carbon dioxide (CO2).
</p>
<p>• For future long-duration beyond LEO, O2 recovery systems must be highly reliable and efficient and
</p>
<p>recover a minimum of 75% O2 from metabolic CO2. An alternative technology development effort
</p>
<p>currently underway at NASA MSFC has the potential to increase O2 recovery significantly, presently
</p>
<p>limited to 50% (Sabatier), and reduce the complexity of ECLSS O2 recovery.
</p>
<p>• MSFC and the University of Texas in Arlington (UTA) have jointly designed and fabricated a microfluid
</p>
<p>electrochemical reactor (MFECR) that operates at ambient conditions and utilizes a proprietary catalysis
</p>
<p>highly selective on reducing CO2 to ethylene (C2H4) at the cathode. At the same time, O2 is generated at
</p>
<p>the anode.
</p>
<p>• The MFECR would replace three pieces of hardware for future ECLSS architectures: the current CO2
</p>
<p>Reduction Assembly (CRA) (Sabatier reactor), the Plasma Pyrolysis Assembly (PPA), and the Oxygen
</p>
<p>Generation Assembly (OGA). It is designed to interface directly with the CO2 Removal Assembly (CDRA)
</p>
<p>and the Water Processing Assembly (WPA) to supply CO2 reactants and replenish them, respectively.
</p>
<p>This approach is expected to substantially improve the ISS ECLSS sustainability and reduce power and
</p>
<p>weight requirements.</p>
<p></p>

<p>ICES-2023-51 4

<p>• In 2016, NASA's Game Changing Development Program awarded University of Texas Arlington (UTA) a contract to develop a 
</p>
<p>Microfluidic Electrochemical Reactor (MFECR)
</p>
<p>• Developed based on the electrochemical reduction of CO2 to ethylene (C2H4) using water (H2O) as precursor and operates at ambient 
</p>
<p>pressure and just above the freezing point of H2O
</p>
<p>• Theoretical O2 recovery is 100% from metabolic CO2
</p>
<p>• An architecture of an Electrolytic O2 Recovery (EORe) system containing MFECR would reduce the complexity of the AR system by 
</p>
<p>eliminating three (3) pieces of hardware from the current baseline exploration architecture: 1) Sabatier, 2) Plasma Pyrolysis Assembly 
</p>
<p>(PPA), and 3) Oxygen Generation Assembly (OGA)

<p>EDU and Test Stand
</p>
<p>ICES-2023-51 707/19/2023
</p>
<p>108 mm diameter
</p>
<p>• The EDU is equipped with three chambers that have
</p>
<p>aligned serpentine paths to house the CO2, N2, and
</p>
<p>liquid electrolyte flows.
</p>
<p>• The electrolyte serpentine chamber is located between
</p>
<p>the CO2 and N2 chambers
</p>
<p>• The cathode, a gas diffusion layer (GDL) with Cu/Br
</p>
<p>catalysis deposited on the side facing the electrolyte is
</p>
<p>located underneath the CO2 serpentine chamber.
</p>
<p>• The anode, a GDE with Pt deposited on the side
</p>
<p>facing the electrolyte is located underneath the N2
</p>
<p>serpentine chamber.
</p>
<p>• The GDE is 0.032 mm thick and has two layers, the
</p>
<p>gas diffusion layer (GDL) and the macropore layer
</p>
<p>(MPL) coated with PTFE to make them hydrophobic
</p>
<p>and manage aqueous electrolyte permeation.</p>

<p>Future Work
</p>
<p>• Continue evaluating other composite anode configurations,
</p>
<p>including those equipped with platinized nickel foam, platinized
</p>
<p>titanium screen, and platinized titanium mass transfer layer.
</p>
<p>• Reduce the C2H4-CO EC onset by increasing electrolyte OH molarity
</p>
<p>and electrolyte type (KOH/LiOH).
</p>
<p>• Increase catalysis coverage on the GDE’s surface by performing
</p>
<p>electrodeposition on the outer surface of the GDE’s MPL and
</p>
<p>throughout the GDE.
</p>
<p>• Redesign the EDU based on the preliminary experimental outcome
</p>
<p>and model-based qualitative benchmark studies.
</p>
<p>• Evaluate two H2 separation systems that will recover H2 generated
</p>
<p>in the EDU</p>
