How NTP Works

Rover Phoebus 2A

• Propellant heated directly by a nuclear reactor and thermally expanded/accelerated 

through a nozzle

• Low molecular weight propellant – typically Hydrogen

• Thrust directly related to thermal power of reactor:  100,000 N ≈ 450 MWth at 900 sec

• Specific Impulse directly related to exhaust temperature: 830 - 1000 sec (2300 - 3100K)

• Specific Impulse improvement over chemical rockets due to lower molecular weight of 

propellant (exhaust stream of H2/O2 engine runs hotter than NTP)

Kiwi B4D Micro-explosions

• Kiwi B4D was shut down after 64 seconds full power 1000MW due to nozzle failure and extensive fire

• Possible cause was liquid air formed close to the liquid hydrogen inlet on the nozzle, flowing down and 

became trapped. Ozone was formed from liquid oxygen and built up with time.

• The decomposition of ozone can be explosive: ozone in liquid or solid state and the gaseous mixtures 

of concentrated ozone in oxygen (over 9.2% vol/vol) can explode further to an impact or if they are 

exposed to heat, a spark, sudden temperature or pressure changes or in the presence of impurities.[9]

• Kiwi B4E nozzle was modified with an inerted shroud to prevent liquid air from entering the interstices 

between nozzle tubes
Radiation may also affect other nearby liquids

[6,7,8,9]

SNP – NTP Lessons Learned 13

Pewee 1 had 135Xe Buildup 

• Pewee set records in power density and temperature. Operated at 503 MW for 40 minutes at 2550K 

with a peak to 2750K

• ~25k lbf of thrust

• Tested both NbC and ZrC coated fuel elements. ZrC outperformed NbC in reducing corrosion.

• The day following the final run, the concentration of 135Xe (fission product with short half-life) was so 

high that the reactor could not be made critical with control drums rotated to 180o based on delayed 

critical measurements. Virtually all Xe had decayed when the final measurement was taken one week 

after EP-III.

[6,11]

Engine design and operation to account for 135Xe

NF-1 Transverse View

Nuclear Furnace NF-1

• 44 MW in size and ran on GH2. 49 core elements.

• NF-1 test started in Summer 1972 and was last reactor test done before program canceled

• Six runs were made. Final two runs completed without incident

• Exit gas temperature above 2222K for 121 minutes and above 2444K for 109 minutes total

• Only Rover/NERVA reactor test with successful filtered exhaust before burning hydrogen in flare stack. 

• Composite fuel achieved better corrosion performance, while carbide fuel had cracked extensively near center of reactor

NF-1 on railroad car

NF-1 tested 49 fuel elements in close operating conditions as a full-scale reactor 
needing ~1000 fuel elements. Sub-scale demo of full-scale exhaust scrubber system

[2]

SNTP Lessons Learned

• Melting point of UC2-x is between 2700-2800K. Operating limit of baseline particle was 2500K. 

Advanced fuel development with mixed carbides having potential melt temperatures >3200K, but 

project was cancelled before final conclusions.

• CTE mismatch resulted in axial failure of hot frit. Double check CTE modeling.

• Major differences between nuclear and non-nuclear tests in both costs and results.

• SNTP completed Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) in 18 months for $2-3M.

• NTS selected. EIS final report info is being utilized with current ground test studies.

• Public meeting discussions is also valuable.

• Lessons learned from the SNTP Nuclear Element Test (NET) rig being used to develop a modification 

to the MIT reactor to test fuel elements in a 3GV pathway .

• Funded as a phase II STTR with Little Prairie Services and MIT.

SNP – NTP Lessons Learned 19

NASA Space Nuclear Propulsion (SNP) Project

Overall Nuclear Thermal Propulsion Strategy:

Develop a subscale nuclear engine that demonstrates a viable NTP propulsion system and is 

scalable to the Mars mission engine needs. Evolve to Mars engine system as a part of a Mars 

NP propulsive stage.

1. Develop a HALEU solid solution fuel form that can provide reactor exit temperatures >

2700 K (corresponds to > 900s Isp).

2. Design a reactor with an eye toward capability to be scaled to a size to support Mars 

mission engine needs. (Scaling is to be an engineering solution rather than further tech 

development.)

3. Design and develop a subscale engine (Key attribute goals: 10K-15K lbf thrust, 900s Isp)

4. Determine Demonstration option (either ground test or demonstration flight).

5. Identify testing and launch regulatory requirements and mitigate issues.

6. Work with CFM project to develop passive and active CFM systems to support NTP 

mission needs.

Goal is to demonstrate Engine (ground test or flight demonstration) Circa FY2027.

SNP – NTP Lessons Learned 21

SNP Lessons Learned

• SNP under the STMD is to mature critical NTP technologies to TRL 6 or TRL 7, not flight certification to TRL 8.

• Minimum demonstrations to advance the TRL

• Keep in mind what is needed by future customer. 

• Baselining a high assay low enriched uranium (HALEU) reactor design

• HALEU system analyses closes with selection of materials with low neutron absorption. (e.g., avoid materials like Rhenium). 

• New infrastructure being investigated to produce larger quantities of HALEU for multiple customers

• The larger the total thrust the shorter the mission burn time. Shorter burn times allow higher Isp based on the fuel endurance unless 

mission reusability is to be considered

• Keep in mind a fuel element design and fabrication process that is simple and not complex or time consuming.

• In 1964, Rover/NERVA tested 3 different reactors with ~4700 fuel elements not all the same and made at 3 different locations.

• Currently, it is not possible to construct new world class ground test facilities for NTP like Rover/NERVA had. Rely more on computer 

analysis and slight modifications to existing facilities. 

• Subscale Maturation of Advanced Reactor Technologies (SMART) reactor study utilizing lessons learned from Rover/NERVA Nuclear

Furnace and SNTP PIPET

• There are advanced NTP concepts being investigated, beyond traditional solid core, with low TRL but low AD2, with much higher Isp 

than 900 seconds
