<p>Innovation
• Hybrid Fusion Fission Reactor
</p>
<p>- Non-fissile, compact, scalable nuclear 
energy source sufficient to power and 
provide heat for melting and boring 
through icy shelves.
</p>
<p>• Robotic Probe
- Small, robust, long-lived electrical energy 
</p>
<p>and heat source
- Traditional nuclear power systems require 
</p>
<p>significant radioactive shielding
- Enriched actinide-based systems: 
</p>
<p>significant fabrication, safety, launch costs</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Impact
</p>
<p>• Probes for icy moons require unacceptable amounts of 238Pu isotope.
</p>
<p>• A small, low-mass, variable power source is needed.
</p>
<p>• New hybrid approach yields a variable output power source smaller than 
existing fissile reactors.
</p>
<p>• Non-fissile alternative to high-enriched uranium (HEU), low-enriched 
uranium (LEU), or high-assay, low-enriched uranium (HALEU) core saves 
uranium enrichment, security and launch safety costs.
</p>
<p>• Efficient operation with reactor thermal waste heat allows probe to melt 
and/or vibrate through ice shelf.</p>
<p></p>

<p>Takeaways
• Hybrid Fusion-Fast Fission Power system
</p>
<p>- No LEU, HEU, or HALEU necessary
- Built on NASA GRC1 and US Navy research2 published in Phys Rev C and elsewhere
- With scaling, suitable for ice crust penetration and power
- Variable output power possible so probe is throttleable
- Compact system supports small size of the probe
</p>
<p>• Recognition of Icy World ice-phase temperature and pressure changes
- Requires power/penetration flexibility
- Possible near-surface ice pools3
</p>
<p>• Combined ice melting/ultrasonic penetration
- Takes advantage of skin layer adjacent to probe
</p>
