<p>Brayton Systems Analysis Trends
</p>
<p>Increasing TIT:
• Larger temp ratio
• Greater efficiency
• Less Qin, smaller heat source
• Less Qrej, smaller radiator
But,
• TRL of advanced materials
• Greater thermal stress
• Possible life issues
</p>
<p>Increasing Power:
• Slight efficiency increase
• Linear area increase
• Large economy of scale benefits 
</p>
<p>in Brayton
But,
• TRL issues at high power
</p>
<p>Mass curve has steep slope at low 
power &amp; knee
</p>
<p>Increasing CIT:
• Higher heat reject temp
• Smaller radiator (µT4)
But,
• Smaller temp ratio
• Lower efficiency
• More Qin, larger heat source
</p>

<p>Brayton Rotating Unit 
</p>
<p>• First-ever Closed Brayton Cycle (CBC) power conversion 
system for space (1968-76)
</p>
<p>– 4 BRUs Fabricated by AiResearch and Tested at Lewis Research 
Center
</p>
<p>– Included Brayton Heat Exchanger Unit (BHXU) combined 
Recuperator and Cooler
</p>
<p>– 1 Unit (BRU-F) Modified with Foil Bearings
</p>
<p>• BRU Program Successfully Demonstrated:
– Manufacturing &amp; Assembly
– Jacking Gas Startup
– High Conversion Efficiency (&gt;25%)
– Material Compatibility
– Tilt-Pad Bearings
– Extended Life:  38000 Hrs Operation on One Unit Without 
</p>
<p>Degradation, Approx. 50000 Hrs Total on Four Units
</p>
<p>• Performance Parameters:
– Alternator Output 2.25 to 15 kWe
– Turbine Inlet Temp 1144 K (1600 F)
– Compressor Inlet Temp 300 K (80 F)
– Compressor Exit Pressure 310 kPa (45 psia)
– 36000 RPM, 1200 Hz, 208 V
– 65 kg BRU, 200 kg BHXU

– Operated up to 22 kW total output power, 950K turbine inlet,

<p>Space Brayton Technology Challenges
</p>
<p>• Biggest challenge is to finally get a Brayton converter into space!
</p>
<p>• Other key challenges include:
– Limited supplier base for niche design requirements
– Life and reliability demonstration (with modern test unit)
– Thermal interfaces at the heat source and heat sink
– Ground test complications due to thermal requirements
– Transient, off-nominal operations
– Achieving competitive mass (with Stirling) at low power level
– Space-rated electronics to process the electric power
</p>

<p></p>

<p>Summary
</p>
<p>• Long history for Brayton space power systems with significant hardware 
development and testing
– Glory days were 1960s and 1970s
– Technologies are well-suited for power levels from kilowatts to Multi-megawatts
</p>
<p>• Brayton technology experienced revivals for Space Station Freedom (late 1980s) 
and Prometheus (early 2000s)
– 25 kW SD system pursued, and 2 kW ground test demo completed
– 200 kW NEP system pursued, and variety of ground demos from 2 to 50 kW completed
</p>
<p>• Current NASA interests in Brayton are focused on low-power RPS (&lt;1 kW), mid-
power FSP (40 kW), and high-power NEP (&gt;1 MW)
– RPS Program built and tested a 300 W Brayton prototype with Creare
– Two contractor teams are pursuing Brayton options for FSP
– Brayton is the preferred technology for large-scale NEP systems
</p>
<p>• Continued NASA investments in turbine-based energy conversion technologies 
provides increased flexibility for future space systems
</p>
