<p>3
</p>
<p>Introduction
</p>
<p>• There is an interest within the aviation community to move in the direction of 
more certification by analysis.
</p>
<p>• As a response, the NASA Transformational Tools and Technologies project is 
currently looking into developing state-of-the-art computational tools for 
simulating and predicting performance of aerospace vehicles.
</p>
<p>• A subset of that research is exploring advanced Reynolds-averaged Navier Stokes 
(RANS) methods, such as Reynolds stress models and boundary layer transition 
models.
</p>
<p>• In order to investigate boundary layer transition, RANS simulations were 
performed of an axisymmetric cone that is representative of a generic hypersonic 
vehicle forebody.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>4
</p>
<p>Transition Model
</p>
<p>• The Langtry-Menter Shear-Stress Transport (LM-SST) transition model was used 
for the simulations.
</p>
<p>• It utilizes the k-ω SST formulation and solves the equations for
</p>
<p>• Turbulent kinetic energy
</p>
<p>• Specific turbulent dissipation rate
</p>
<p>• Turbulence intermittency
</p>
<p>• Transition Reynolds number </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>5
</p>
<p>Flow Solvers
</p>
<p>• Most simulations used the FUN3D flow solver as it contained the LM-SST 
transition model.
</p>
<p>• FUN3D is a node-based production level code that is developed and maintained 
at the NASA Langley Research Center.
</p>
<p>• It can solve the 2D/3D Euler and RANS equations for incompressible and 
compressible flows on unstructured grids.
</p>
<p>• A subset of “fully turbulent” turbulence models simulations used the Wind-US 
flow solver.</p>
<p></p>

<p>2D Flat Plate Revisited: Results
</p>
<p>• Transition is predicted to occur almost 
immediately at the lower Mach 
numbers while moving downstream at 
subsequent Mach numbers.
</p>
<p>• After Mach 2.0, the two codes start to 
diverge in their predicted transition 
locations.
</p>
<p>• The predicted transition location from 
the axisymmetric cone agrees 
reasonably well with the Mach 8.0 cold 
case.</p>

<p>14
</p>
<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>• The primary objective of this work was to explore the LM-SST transition model on 
an axisymmetric cone that was representative of a generic hypersonic vehicle 
forebody.
</p>
<p>• The transition model was able to reasonably predict transition for precursor 
simulations of a Mach 0.2 flat plate at two different turbulent inflow conditions.
</p>
<p>• The axisymmetric cone simulations showed high sensitivity of the inflow 
turbulent viscosity on the predicted transition location, with small variation in 
turbulence intensity.
</p>
<p>• Revisiting the flat plate at higher Mach numbers, the “fully turbulent” SST-V 
turbulence model predicted transition for Mach numbers greater than 2.0.</p>
