<div class="slide-content"><p>3</p>
<p>Goals of the on-orbit spectral calibration of OCI:</p>
<p></p>
<p>Comparing spectral observations of sunlight reflected by solar diffusers with predicted spectral observations of the reflected sunlight.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Comparing line positions and strengths with those derived from the TSIS solar reference spectrum and the diffuser BRDF, convolved with the OCI RSRs measured by GLAMR. </p>
<p></p>
<p>The on-orbit spectral calibration looks for shifts between the prelaunch RSRs and the on-orbit performance of the instrument.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Comparison of atmospheric absorption bands observed in spectra reflected by cloudtops with spectra reflected by the cloudfree ocean.</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>23</p>
<p></p>
<p>The RMS spectral shift in the Fraunhofer lines and atmospheric absorption bands between the measured and predicted spectra is 0.15 nm.</p>
<p></p>
<p>The residual spectral dispersion over the wavelengths of the Fraunhofer lines is 0.17 nm.</p>
<p></p>
<p>The band strengths of the measured and predicted spectral lines are essentially the same.</p>
<p></p>
<p>For the atmospheric absorption bands, the band strengths are higher over the ocean than over clouds, though the radiances are 1/8 the radiances over clouds.</p>
<p></p>
<p>The OCI spectral accuracy on orbit meets the instrument functional requirement of 0.5 nm.</p>
<p></p>
<p>The on-orbit spectral calibration of OCI has not shifted since calibration by GLAMR during the thermal vacuum test campaign.</p>
