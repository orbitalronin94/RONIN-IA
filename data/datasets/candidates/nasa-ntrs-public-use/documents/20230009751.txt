<p>INTRODUCTION
</p>
<p>• NASA has been studying Lunar and Martian surface propellant production for 
approximately 30 years.
</p>
<p>• Greater than 55% of any Lunar or Martian return vehicle is oxygen by mass.
</p>
<p>– Production on the surface decreases lander mass which decreases launch vehicle mass.
</p>
<p>• Oxygen production on Mars was demonstrated by Mars Oxygen ISRU Experiment (MOXIE) 
as a part of the Perseverance rover at rates between 1.5 and 11.2 mg/s.
</p>
<p>– Done on stationairy portion of the rover using carbon dioxide gas from the atmosphere.
</p>
<p>– Lunar production done either through mining water ice or reforming oxides within regolith.
</p>
<p>• Last step of propellant production is the liquefaction process.
</p>
<p>– Initially done within lander tanks, simply refueling them.
</p>
<p>• NASA created Cryogenic Fluid In-situ Liquefaction for Landers (CryoFILL) project to 
demonstrate liquefaction processes.</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>3
</p>
<p>CRYOFILL BACKGROUND
CryoFILL will demonstrate cryogenic capabilities on the Lunar and Martian 
</p>
<p>surfaces for landers, In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU), and the integration of 
</p>
<p>the two at a relevant scale, in a relevant environment with hardware that can 
</p>
<p>be used in ISRU End to End tests.
</p>
<p>• Human Lander System (HLS) Sustainable Lunar Architecture
</p>
<p>• In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU)
</p>
<p>Objectives
</p>
<p>• Design, build, and test a prototypical lander tank with a liquefaction system capable of 
incorporating prototype flight components as they are developed.
</p>
<p>• Demonstrate liquefaction processes in a relevant environment.
</p>
<p>• Provide data for validation of two-phase cryogenic fluid models in development.
</p>
<p>Current Status
</p>
<p>• Oxygen liquefaction demonstration complete on Prototype tank
</p>
<p>• Half scale liquefaction rate (1.1 kg/hr +) demonstrated on half scale 
(by surface area) tank
</p>
<p>• Incorporated Fiber Optic Sensing System (FOSS) for better understanding of 
ullage stratification 
</p>
<p>• Final report to be published as NASA Technical Publication, draft completed
</p>
<p>• Modelling of test data in progress 
</p>
<p>• Block 2 testing (with flight-like 90 K cryocooler) slipped to FY24 due to funding 
constraints
</p>

<p>EFFECT OF FLOW RATE
</p>
<p>Ran tests adjusting the flow rate as a 
function of time to see tank pressurization 
rates.
• Depressurization rates show much more 
</p>
<p>change over time than pressurization 
rates.
</p>
<p>When flow rate was set to 0.3 g/s (1.1 
kg/hr), pressure appeared to asymptotically 
approach a pressure, but the pressure was 
different when pressure approached from 
above than from below.
• Probably due to slight alterations in 
</p>
<p>neon flow loop and small difference in 
equilibrium states.
</p>
<p>• May have further collapsed if let run 
long enough.</p>

<p>PRESSURIZATION RATE ANALYSIS
</p>
<p>• Pressurization rate a linear function of 
mass flow rate.
• Used for setting transient test 
</p>
<p>operations rates.
• Some spread near 2.2 kg/hr, 1.1 kg/hr, 
</p>
<p>and 0.5 kg/hr
</p>
<p>• No tank pressure dependency in 2.2 
kg/hr test data, appears to be just noise 
in data
</p>
<p>• Perhaps some small levels of sensitivity 
in 1.1 kg/hr test data.
</p>
<p>• Strong dependance on tank pressure for 
0.5 – 0.6 kg/hr test data.</p>

<p>15
</p>
<p>CONCLUSIONS
</p>
<p>• Successful demonstration of oxygen liquefaction in a scalable lander tank and relevant lunar 
environments.
</p>
<p>• Demonstration of liquefaction completed with successful filling to &gt; 95% full without slowdown in 
liquefaction rate and maximum liquefaction rate of 1.56 kg/hr.
</p>
<p>• Demonstrated benefits of liquefaction at higher pressures increases liquefaction rate by 17% 
between 137 kPa and 275 kPa.
• Results show cryocooler efficiency (~12% change) is stronger influence than change in enthalpy (4% change) in effect of 
</p>
<p>liquefaction pressure/temperature.
</p>
<p>• Transients due to GOX Mass Flow Rate and Cryocooler Heat Removal were significantly more 
impactful than transients due to Environmental Temperature (factor of 4 lower pressurization rates).
</p>
<p>• Used steady-state results to predict transient pressurization rates within 10%.
</p>
<p>• Fill level not important in predicting liquefaction rates: 
• Changed pressurization rates slightly in transient tests.
</p>
<p>• Able to fill tank up to &gt; 98% full before liquefaction rate started to decrease.
</p>
<p>• Cryocooler flow inlet manifold around tank vent line.
</p>
<p>• Pressurization rate very similar, depressurization rate increase by factor of 4 at high fill level.</p>
