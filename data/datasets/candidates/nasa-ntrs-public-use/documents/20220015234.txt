SAWE Types of Hydrogen - Sources
• Brown

• Created through burning coal or lignite.

• Grey

• From natural gas through a process called steam reforming that combines natural gas with water (as 
steam) to create hydrogen gas

• Blue / Turquoise

• Ex: steam reforming with Carbon Capture

• Turquoise hydrogen is similar to blue hydrogen, using a process called methane pyrolysis to produce 
hydrogen and solid carbon, which can then be permanently stored and used.

• Green (is the gold standard environmentally)

• Produced from water electrolysis, powered by electricity generated from renewable sources, ex: 
hydro, solar or wind

SAWE Hydrogen Characteristics

• 6-things-to-remember-about-hydrogen-vs-natural-gas [1]
• Molecular Formula: pure element H2 vs molecular compounds CH4, JP-8

• No C, so: combining with oxygen to burn produces no CO2 
• Combining with air to burn though does produce nitrogen oxide (NOx)

• Molecular Weight: H2 is 2, Methane is 16, JP-8 is 180
• much greater leakage potential “Special consideration needs to be put into 

the materials used to reduce leakages through gaskets, valves or any sealing 
locations within a compressed hydrogen system”

• Flammability - % fuel req’d in Air to burn: H2 4 to 75, Methane 7 to 20
• Burns more readily – requires better combustion control

SAWE Hydrogen Characteristics
• 6-things-to-remember-about-hydrogen-vs-natural-gas [1]

• Flame Speed approx: H2 250 cm/sec,  Methane 35 cm/sec, JP-8 (30-60)
• another combustion control (design) issue, causes a backfiring phenomena 

• Adiabatic Flame Temperature: H2 4000F, Methane 3600F
• may require material changes, combustor cooling design changes

• Heating Value – Mass Basis, btu/lbm : H2 51,623,  Methane 21,518, JP-8 18,400
• 2.5 times as energy dense on a Mass Basis

• Heating Value – Volume Basis, btu/ft3 : H2 266,  Methane 881, JP-8 926
• requires 3 Times the Volume to contain the same energy content

Liquid Crystal Polymers
Why liquid crystalline polymers?
• Low CTE  (< 10x10-6 in flow direction)
• Temperature range (-250 to 400 oC)
• High rigidity and strength (modulus 10–24 GPa)
• Low dielectric material
• Excellent processability characteristics (melt or solution, different cure
methods are possible including UV, epoxy, maleimide…)

• Flame retardant (UL94 V0)
• Excellent retention of mechanical properties after radiation exposure
• Excellent barrier properties and inert to a wide range of solvents and
gases (moisture uptake < 0.03 %)

• LCP-GO or LCP-Clay nanocomposites possible 
(improving most of the properties listed)

Product forms
• Coatings (e.g. plasma spray)
• Composites (CF, glass with LCP/LCT as matrix resin)
• Thin films (tank liners)
• Foams (a foam-filled honeycomb sandwich structure was demonstrated)
