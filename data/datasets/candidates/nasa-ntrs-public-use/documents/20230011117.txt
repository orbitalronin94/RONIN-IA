<div class="slide-content"><p>Motivation for In-Space Inspection</p>
<p>The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) has key initiatives to establish outposts on the Moon and Mars</p>
<p>In-Space Manufacturing (ISM) provides a solution towards sustainable, flexible missions (both in-transit and on-surface) </p>
<p>On-demand fabrication, repair, and recycling capabilities.</p>
<p>In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) will be used to create launch/landing pads, habitats, shelters, roadways, etc.</p>
<p>Investment in capabilities to protect astronauts while building key infrastructure on extraterrestrial surfaces is crucial.</p>
<p>NASA will need to develop this complex space hardware and infrastructure with inspectability/Nondestructive Evaluation (NDE) in mind. </p>
<p>There is already an inherent need to inspect, verify and validate complex systems prior to flight (on earth).</p>
<p>There is a emerging need to inspect future ISM and ISRU hardware in its fabrication and service environment…space.</p>
</div>

<div class="slide-content"><p>In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU)</p>
<p>ISRU system concept for autonomous robotic excavation and processing of Mars soil to extract water for use in exploration missions</p>
<p>Ref. <a href="https://www.nasa.gov/isru/overview">https://www.nasa.gov/isru/overview</a>  and <a href="https://www.nasa.gov/nextstep/isru">https://www.nasa.gov/nextstep/isru</a> </p>
<p>ISRU involves hardware and/or operation that harnesses ‘in-situ’ resources to create products and services for robotic and human exploration</p>
<p>NDE Tools are required for ISRU resource assessment/prospecting and in-situ manufacturing.</p>
<p>New Nondestructive Evaluation (NDE) Tools are needed that will provide information to make decisions such as:</p>
<p>Compositional analysis of raw materials</p>
<p>Integrity, size and shape of ISRU AM-printed structures/habitats </p>
<p>X-ray CT concepts being considered for core drill and loose sample inspections.</p>
<p>Can help identify compositional features of interest, by % volume, which can be pivotal in decision making	</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide9_rId4"></div>
<p>GSFC x-ray Computed Tomography (CT) image slice of lunar basalt sample.</p>
<p></p>
<p>X-ray CT could be used to assess % volume of relative compositions, porosity/tortuosity, and much more.</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>NDE for ISM/ISRU Applications</p>
<p>Leverage NDE methods that have been successfully deployed on our spaceflight hardware:</p>
<p>X-ray Computed Tomography (CT)</p>
<p>NASA Goddard Space Flight Center (GSFC) has led efforts to investigate In-Space X-ray Computed Tomography (CT) for multi-use applications</p>
<p>Efforts include Concept Development and Trade Studies to assess feasibility for CT in different environments (ISS, lunar, Martian, crewed/non-crewed)</p>
<p>Infrared Thermography (IRT)</p>
<p>Proven On-orbit inspection of Space Shuttle Orbiter WLE; Ammonia leak on ISS</p>
<p>Could potentially be used to assess ISRU structures, landing/launch pads, berms, roads, </p>
<p>Integrated Vehicle Health Monitoring/Structural Health Monitoring (IVHM/SHM)</p>
<p>In-situ Air leak monitoring on ISS; likewise, could be used to monitor ISRU structure </p>
<p>Ultrasound (UT)</p>
<p>ISM/ISRU Piece-part inspection &amp; structural monitoring</p>
<p>CT image Sandwich panel core with insert fittings</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>NDE for ISM/ISRU Applications</p>
<p>Require NDE methods capable of:</p>
<p>Assessing ISRU structural dimensions, defects, damage, etc. as related to strength</p>
<p>Locating subsurface features such as structural reinforcements, voids, fractures, etc.</p>
<p>Active &amp; passive methods to monitor the “health” of the structure</p>
<p>Leverage NDE traditionally used for civil engineering applications: </p>
<p>Ground Penetrating Radar</p>
<p>Perseverance and Tianwen-1 Rovers</p>
<p>Accurate estimations regarding the depth of the top surface of lunar soil, which is an important way to determine the stability and strength of the soil foundation for developing lunar bases and research stations</p>
<p>Acoustic Emission</p>
<p>Structural monitoring to detect, locate, and characterize internal damage	</p>
<p>Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity Testing</p>
<p>Evaluation of compressive strength</p>
<p>Assessment of structural integrity and localization of defects and voids in regolith</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Key Challenges for In-Space NDE</p>
<p>Human Safety is Paramount … space is unforgiving. </p>
<p>Essential to develop ruggedized NDE systems that are highly reliable and accurate.</p>
<p>Development of Space-rated NDE Systems:</p>
<p>High sensitivity, small/compact, low-mass, simple to use, battery powered are desired.</p>
<p>Withstand harsh vibro-acoustic environment of launch</p>
<p>Radiation hardened</p>
<p>Thermal Environment</p>
<p>Electrical charging</p>
<p>Vacuum</p>
<p>Certification of Additively Manufactured (AM) ISM and ISRU hardware</p>
<p>Requires close partnership with Fracture Control, M&amp;P and Analysis disciplines is required in developing correlation of indication to verified defect.</p>
<p>NDE is just one piece of the puzzle</p>
<p>Astronaut NDE Inspector training/certification, Data Interpretation…</p>
<p>…many more challenges!</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Summary</p>
<p></p>
<p>Space is unforgiving! </p>
<p>Future In-Space NDE tools should be multi-purpose to address yet-to-be defined inspection needs. </p>
<p>In-Space NDE and in situ monitoring will play critical roles in the certification of hardware produced for ISRU/ISM applications.</p>
<p>Structural certification plans aren’t defined; however, active collaboration with Fracture, Materials and Analysis will be essential to quantifying NDE results</p>
<p>Space-rated NDE systems are needed!</p>
<p>Require NDE systems that are robust and highly reliable. </p>
<p>High sensitivity, small/compact, low-mass, simple to use, battery powered… </p>
<p>Consider partnership and collaboration opportunities with NASA.</p>
