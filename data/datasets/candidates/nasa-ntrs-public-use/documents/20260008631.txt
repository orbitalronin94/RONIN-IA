<p>Lunar Orbit Scope and Applications 
to Planetary Protection
</p>
<p>* To support planetary protection (PP) compliance and sustainable lunar exploration, we 
</p>
<p>investigate the natural dynamical behavior of low lunar orbits (LLOs) as a mechanism for 
</p>
<p>predictable disposal, contamination risk reduction, and stable staging architectures. 
</p>
<p>• Under the Committee on Space Research (COSPAR) planetary protection framework, 
</p>
<p>Moon missions are classified as Category II, IIa, and IIb, requiring assessment and 
</p>
<p>documentation of impact locations and forward-contamination pathways. Predictable 
</p>
<p>orbital decay and long-term stability are therefore critical for end-of-mission design.
</p>
<p>* We analyze families of LLOs spanning ~10–250 km altitude and 80°–100° inclination 
</p>
<p>(i.e., polar and near-polar) representative of pre- and post-descent staging orbits for 
</p>
<p>NASA’s Artemis Human Landing System (HLS) program and Commercial Lunar Payload 
</p>
<p>Services (CLPS) Initiative missions.
</p>
<p>* High-fidelity propagations via flight-proven astrodynamics software (STK and GMAT) 
</p>
<p>reveal two dominant behaviors. 
</p>
<p> 1) Naturally decaying trajectories produce deterministic surface impacts that cluster 
</p>
<p>    into geographically confined latitude–longitude corridors governed primarily by       
</p>
<p>    initial orbital geometry. 
</p>
<p> 2) Frozen and quasi-stable regimes exhibit multi-year to decadal lifetimes with     
</p>
<p>     negligible impact likelihood, forming candidate “graveyard” or staging orbits.
</p>
<p>• What is MAUNA? MAUNA automates high-throughput spacecraft 
</p>
<p>trajectory analysis by generating, executing, and post-processing 
</p>
<p>thousands of General Mission Analysis Tool (GMAT) propagations across 
</p>
<p>multidimensional design spaces. This replaces manually created and 
</p>
<p>modified scripts with a standardized, reproducible workflow and enables 
</p>
<p>systematic exploration of large orbital trade spaces.
</p>
<p>• Where does AI/ML actually enter? Mauna-generated high-fidelity 
</p>
<p>trajectory data provide a foundation for physics-informed machine 
</p>
<p>learning. For lunar impact prediction, machine learning (ML) can learn the 
</p>
<p>slow, long-term evolution of orbital states while known gravitational physics 
</p>
<p>constrain the solution; trajectories are then returned to high-fidelity numerical 
</p>
<p>propagation near the terminal event to accurately resolve the impact location.
</p>
<p>• What has NASA actually used this for / why does it matter? 
</p>
<p>The framework has been applied to lunar mission-design problems 
</p>
<p>supporting NASA’s Artemis HLS and CLPS programs, including high-
</p>
<p>fidelity low-lunar-orbit stability analysis, automated searches across hundreds 
</p>
<p>of thousands of candidate trajectories, and mapping of natural impact/disposal 
</p>
<p>behavior. This combination of automation, physics-based simulation, and ML 
</p>
<p>enables faster identification of stable orbital regimes and potential surface-
</p>
<p>interaction risks.
</p>
