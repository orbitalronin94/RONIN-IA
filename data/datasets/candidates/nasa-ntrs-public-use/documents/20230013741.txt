<p>Motivation and Objectives
</p>
<p>• Design of thermal protection system relies on aerothermal environments predicted (and 
margined) using continuum flow solvers.
• Flow solvers are usually applied up to            , i.e., altitudes well below Entry Interface {EI} altitude.
• Pressure &amp; heat pulses are closed at early time (from EI) via conservative extrapolation.
</p>
<p>• For reconstruction of aerothermal environments post-flight, restrictive design assumptions 
must be dispensed with.
• Use low-density/non-continuum flow solvers from EI up to the point where continuum flow assumption is 
</p>
<p>tenable for CFD solver.
</p>
<p>• Key questions:
• What is a possible metric to determine the appropriate altitude for a smooth hand off 
</p>
<p>from a low-density flow field solver to a continuum CFD solver?
• How well do non-continuum and continuum solvers compare in the region of overlap?
</p>
<p>• We first investigate a flow (of Ar, and CO2/N2 reacting mixture) past a hemispherical 
geometry, before turning our attention to Mars 2020 for which flight data are available.</p>

<p>Non-reactive Sphere Cases
</p>
<p>• We are only able to run one overlap case for the full M2020 3D geometry, 
since these are truly 3D (no symmetry in freestream flow):
• Little confidence in CFD higher than 80 km altitude (continuum breakdown; not using 
</p>
<p>slip boundary conditions here).
• Not computationally feasible to run DSMC lower than 80 km altitude.
</p>
<p>• Focus on 4.1 m radius hemisphere
• Stag. point environments of this geometry best replicate the environments at MTH01 of 
</p>
<p>M2020.
</p>
<p>• Start by investigating non-reactive flows (Argon), with freestream densities 
representative of the M2020 trajectory between h = 94.8 km and 58.0 km 
altitudes.</p>

<p>Non-reactive Sphere Cases: Takeaways
</p>
<p>• Investigated density conditions similar to M2020 from 94.8 to 58.0 km 
altitudes.
• Continuum breakdown effects as expected, although, in general, the 
</p>
<p>Boyd continuum breakdown criterion KnGLL &gt; 0.05 seems overly 
conservative.
• We propose that KnGLL = 0.5 might be a continuum breakdown criterion 
</p>
<p>better suited for shock and surface slip. 
• Surface heat flux is impacted at higher KnGLL around 2.0.
</p>
<p>• We now return to Martian atmosphere flows (CO2/N2) but keep the 4.1 
m radius sphere geometry.</p>

<p>Reactive Sphere Case Takeaways
</p>
<p>• Reactive flows show excellent agreement between SPARTA and DPLR for M2020 
conditions at h = 80.6 km after EI, for surface quantities.
• Corresponds to KnGLL larger than 2.0.
• Flow quantities still display breakdown around KnGLL of 0.5.
</p>
<p>• Notes:
• Lots of questions about accuracy of rotational relaxation rates remain.
• Also not shown here, but the chemistry model has a massive impact on the results.</p>

<p>16DSMC 2023 conference

<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>• A continuum breakdown occurring at KnGLL = 0.5 appears more appropriate for 
flow quantities. 
• Surface quantities are less impacted by breakdown than flow quantities.  
• KnGLL of 2.0 for non-reacting and higher for reacting cases.
</p>
<p>• Shock slip occurs before surface slip and may or may not impact surface 
quantity.
• Reacting flows appears more forgiving than non-reacting flows with respect to 
</p>
<p>breakdown.
• Relaxation and chemical models matter!
• For post-flight reconstruction efforts, accuracy is of the utmost importance, and 
</p>
<p>DSMC provided added accuracy compared to CFD for non-continuum 
conditions, despite the added cost.</p>
