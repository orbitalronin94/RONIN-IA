CERES and SYN1deg
The Clouds and Earth’s Radiant Energy System (CERES) satellites are part of NASA’s Earth Observing System (EOS). Its goal is to provide long-term global estimates of the shortwave and longwave radiative fluxes at TOA and the surface. 

CERES radiances are measured at a 20km2 footprint level and converted to fluxes using the CERES ADM based on imager properties from MODIS/VIIRS and GMAO atmosphere.
  SSF: A Level-2 product with instantaneous CERES footprint TOA fluxes 	at TOA with MODIS clouds and GEOS-4 atmospheric parameters.
The Level-2 CERES fluxes are then temporally averaged into daily and monthly fluxes and spatially averaged into 1˚x1˚ regions for the CERES Level-3 products:
SYN1deg: Provides daily and monthly CERES TOA fluxes with
    diurnal cycle based on geostationary derived broadband fluxes
    and compute in-atmosphere and surface fluxes.
  EBAF TOA: Provides net-balanced TOA fluxes. Global net flux is
    constrained by the ocean heat storage term. Provides spatially
    complete clear-sky fluxes.
 EBAF Surface: Provides surfaces fluxes consistent with the EBAF
    TOA fluxes

Motivation
SYN1deg combines the Single Satellite measurements from Aqua and Terra and adds geostationary measurement to more accurately measure diurnal changes. The goal of this research is to test how accurately we can reproduce CERES observations using geostationary data.

Methodology
To use geostationary satellite measurements we must convert their visible channels to shortwave broadband reflectance in and effort to match CERES measurements. We first start with Aqua and Terra Single Satellite Footprint data. The visible reflectances measured by MODIS are already angle-matched and coincident to CERES observations.
Local noon
Radiative Transfer Model
We employ a hyper-spectral RTM that produces radiances for many cloud and surface conditions under all angular conditions observed by GEO sensors














We then convolve these radiances to the spectral response functions of the visible imager that is to be compared to CERES observations.















Next we generate coefficients based on regressions between CERES and the convolved RTM data. These coefficients can then be applied to observational data to allow us to convert shortwave narroband reflectances to broadband reflectances.
