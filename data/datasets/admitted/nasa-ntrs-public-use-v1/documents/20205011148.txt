The EDS Technology

• The Electrodynamic Dust Shield (EDS) is an active 
dust mitigation/transport system for NASA’s 
Exploration missions.

• Uses dynamic electric fields to remove dust from 
surfaces.

• Multiple phases

• Can be made transparent, flexible, applied to metals

• Modes of Operation:
EDS can be operated continually for surfaces needed to be free of dust 

that are highly sensitive (optics, lenses, etc.)

EDS can be operated momentarily to remove settled dust for less 
susceptible systems (solar panels, radiators, etc.)

• Power Signal:
EDS operates between ~900-1200 volts in air, but could 

operate up to 4 kV in vacuum with efforts at minimizing that potential 
underway

Signals are 10 Hz square wave with fast rise times (still being optimized 
for vacuum)

EDS demonstration on a CLPS Mission

• Launch date: June 2023
• The main objective is to demonstrate the operation of two EDS Systems on 

the lunar surface:
• EDS for a small camera to be located under the lander’s deck
• EDS on a white aluminum panel to be located on a lander’s footpad

• The camera EDS will consist of indium tin oxide electrodes on a glass panel 
coated with a space-rated dielectric.

• The Footpad EDS will consist of thin copper electrodes on polyimide film with 
a white polyimide dielectric cover. This multilayer film will be affixed to an 
aluminum panel.

• The EDS panels will be activated and controlled with compact high voltage 
power supplies co-located with each EDS.

• A Digital Storage Unit (DSU) will provide power, data communications, and 
data storage for EDS and camera operations. 9
