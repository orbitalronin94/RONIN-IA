<div class="slide-content"><p>Solid-State Electrolytes</p>
<p>Advantages:</p>
<p>Solid-state electrolytes = low flammability</p>
<p>Wide temperature tolerance</p>
<p></p>
<p>Disadvantages:</p>
<p>Difficult to manufacture</p>
<p>Interface issues</p>
<p>Solid-Solid contact vs Liquid-solid</p>
<p>High density vs liquid (g/cm3)</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Material Selection</p>
<p>Lithium-Sulfur</p>
<p>Lithium metal is an ideal anode material</p>
<p>Lightweight (3860mAh/g), low potential, metallic</p>
<p>Sulfur has high capacity (1675mAh/g)</p>
<p>Reasonable potential above lithium (~2V)</p>
<p>Dissolution prevented in a solid-electrolyte</p>
<p>Solid-Electrolyte		25 micron separator:</p>
<p>Polymer ~1.2g/mL 		3 mg/cm2</p>
<p>Sulfide ~1.7 g/mL		4.25 mg/cm2</p>
<p>Oxide ~5.6 g/mL		14 mg/cm2</p>
<p>Oxides, such as LLZO, must be x3.3 times thinner than corresponding sulfide to achieve same weight penalty</p>
<p>Impact of Solid-Electrolyte (Sulfide):</p>
<p>Substantial gains in energy by controlling separating layer thickness</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusions</p>
<p>Lithium conducting Li6PS5Cl composite electrolytes could be manufactured with thicknesses between 25-50 μm through a scalable process.</p>
<p>Achieved thicknesses were in the practical range to make the chemistry competitive with current lithium-ion cells.</p>
<p>Binder and filler impacted conductivity, but ~20% was retained.</p>
<p>Significant reduction in thickness will lead to overall improvements in energy and power capability. </p>
<p>Length-scale of filler influenced conductivity losses</p>
