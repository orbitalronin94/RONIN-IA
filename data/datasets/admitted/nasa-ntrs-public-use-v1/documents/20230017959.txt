<p>01 Introduction
</p>
<p>Our experience with developing a language model for Earth sciences has yielded several key insights:
</p>
<p>1. Efficient Pipeline Management
Implementing a scheduler and monitoring system was crucial. It streamlined the workflow from data download to preprocessing, 
ensuring efficiency and allowing for timely error resolution.
</p>
<p>2. Handling Unstructured Data
The challenge of converting unstructured PDFs to text taught us the importance of selecting the right tools (PyPDF2 for non-OCR 
PDFs, Google Tesseract for OCR) based on the data's nature.
</p>
<p>3. Data Quality Assurance
Rigorous quality checks of the converted text were vital. This step ensured the reliability and accuracy of our data, impacting the 
model's effectiveness.
</p>
<p>4. Critical Preprocessing Steps
Text normalization and deduplication at document and sentence levels were essential for creating a clean dataset, underscoring 
the importance of meticulous preprocessing.
</p>
<p>5. Adaptation and Continuous Improvement
The monitoring system provided insights for ongoing improvements. Adapting the pipeline in response to these insights was key 
to enhancing its efficiency.
</p>
<p>6. Streamlined Collaboration
The project benefited from combining expertise in AI, Earth sciences, and data management, highlighting the value of 
interdisciplinary collaboration.
</p>
<p>02 Data Quality
For the development of our language model, we used 
publications from the American Meteorological Society 
(AMS) and a subset from the American Geophysical 
Union (AGU). These sources were chosen for their 
relevance to Earth science topics and the quality of 
content they provide. It is important to note that the 
data collected is exclusively utilized for a non-generative 
(encoder) model, ensuring that our focus is on analysis 
and understanding rather than generation, in alignment 
with responsible AI practices.
</p>
<p>Choice of AMS and AGU Publications:
</p>
<p>• Peer-Reviewed Content: Both AMS and AGU are 
known for their peer-reviewed publications, which 
ensures the accuracy and reliability of the content.
</p>
<p>• Coverage of Earth Sciences: These publications offer 
a wide range of topics within Earth sciences, making 
them suitable for creating a dataset that is both 
diverse and specific to our field of interest.
</p>
<p>Broader Knowledge Base:
While our language model also includes knowledge from 
other scientific domains like biology and astrophysics, 
this poster focuses specifically on the data collection 
process for Earth sciences. The diverse range of 
scientific knowledge in our model helps ensure its 
applicability and effectiveness across various scientific 
disciplines.
</p>
<p>03 Pre-processing
</p>
<p>Our data pre-processing pipeline was meticulously designed to handle the unique challenges posed by unstructured publications, 
primarily in PDF format, from AMS and AGU sources. The process involved several crucial steps to ensure the data's readiness for 
language model training:
</p>
<p>1. Conversion of PDFs to Text
1. Initial Step: Extracting text from PDF files, which involved different approaches based on the nature of the PDFs.
2.Tools Used:
</p>
<p>1.PyPDF2: Employed for non-OCR (Optical Character Recognition) PDFs, effectively handling standard text extraction.
2.Google Tesseract: Utilized for PDFs requiring OCR. Tesseract first converts PDFs into images at an adjustable resolution 
</p>
<p>and then extracts text from these images, accommodating for a variety of document qualities.
</p>
<p>2. Quality Assessment of Converted Text
1. Objective: To evaluate and ensure the accuracy of the text extraction process.
2.Methodology: Testing and comparing various tools including PyMuPDF (fitz), PDF Miner, PDFtoText, Tika, Tesseract, and 
</p>
<p>Amazon Textract to determine the most effective combination for our specific needs.
</p>
<p>3. Text Normalization
1. Purpose: To create a uniform structure in the text data, facilitating easier processing and analysis.
2.Processes Involved: Converting all text to a standard case (usually lowercase) and removing special characters and artifacts 
</p>
<p>that could interfere with text analysis.
</p>
<p>4. Deduplication
1. At Document Level: Removing duplicate documents to prevent redundancy in the dataset.
2.At Sentence Level: Identifying and eliminating repeated sentences within documents, ensuring each sentence's unique 
</p>
<p>contribution to the dataset.
</p>

<p>he collaboration between NASA and IBM Research has 
marked a significant milestone with the release of the 
HLS Geospatial Foundation Model (HLS Geospatial FM). 
This open-source geospatial artificial intelligence (AI) 
model, utilizing NASA’s Harmonized Landsat and 
Sentinel-2 (HLS) dataset, exemplifies the innovative 
application of AI in interpreting Earth observation data. 
Its diverse applications, ranging from monitoring land 
use changes to predicting crop yields, underscore the 
model's versatility and potential in addressing complex 
environmental challenges.
</p>
<p>As we build on this success, our focus now shifts to 
harnessing the power of AI in a new dimension: 
developing a specialized language model tailored for 
scientific discovery. This endeavor, a collaborative effort 
involving NASA’s Interagency Implementation and 
Advanced Concepts Team (IMPACT), IBM Research, and 
other key partners, aims to pioneer the application of AI-
driven language processing in the domain of Earth 
sciences.
</p>
<p>Our proposed language model seeks to leverage the 
principles of FMs, capitalizing on their ability to process 
and interpret large volumes of scientific text data. For 
that, It incorporates curated datasets from Earth 
Sciences, PubMed PMC, Astrophysics archives, and the 
Science Discovery Engine systems within the Science 
Mission Directorate. This poster specifically highlights 
our data collection methods and processes in Earth 
Sciences.
</p>
