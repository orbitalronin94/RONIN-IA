<p>Numerical Simulation of No-Vent Chill and Fill of a Large Liquid Hydrogen Tank</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>➢ No vent filling of cryogenic tank is an important part of in-space transfer of 
</p>
<p>cryogenic propellants for future space mission.
</p>
<p>➢ In microgravity, fluid transfer becomes challenging because liquid, instead of 
</p>
<p>settling at the bottom, may reside close to the vent valve and escape along with 
</p>
<p>vapor.
</p>
<p>➢ Therefore,  several no vent filling methods, such as Charge-Hold-Vent (CHV) 
</p>
<p>and Vented Chill and No Vent Fill (VCNVF), have been developed.
</p>
<p>➢ In this paper, we will present a numerical simulation of a VCNVF test conducted 
</p>
<p>at NASA/GRC of filling a large tank with liquid hydrogen.  
</p>
<p>➢ The simulation was performed using Generalized Fluid System Simulation 
</p>
<p>Program (GFSSP) developed at NASA/MSFC.
</p>

<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>➢ This paper presented model validation results of a GFSSP numerical model of 
</p>
<p>the non-vented filling of a large hydrogen tank.
</p>
<p>➢ Model accounts for different regimes of pool boiling heat transfer during tank 
</p>
<p>chilldown.
</p>
<p>➢ Model also accounts for condensation around spray droplet and liquid – vapor 
</p>
<p>interface.
</p>
<p>➢ GFSSP model captures the various stages of NVF, flash evaporation, boiling at 
</p>
<p>the wall, and condensation.
</p>
<p>➢ The model predicts tank pressure, filling rate, and wall temperature within 20%, 
</p>
<p>omitting errors due to the offset in the temperature for Test 20.
</p>
<p>➢ GFSSP model with new User Defined Functions (UDF) agree well with 
</p>
<p>Cryogenic NVF data across various tank sizes, flowrates, and initial wall 
</p>
<p>temperatures.
