Numerical Simulation of No-Vent Chill and Fill of a Large Liquid Hydrogen Tank











➢ No vent filling of cryogenic tank is an important part of in-space transfer of 



cryogenic propellants for future space mission.



➢ In microgravity, fluid transfer becomes challenging because liquid, instead of 



settling at the bottom, may reside close to the vent valve and escape along with 



vapor.



➢ Therefore, several no vent filling methods, such as Charge-Hold-Vent (CHV) 



and Vented Chill and No Vent Fill (VCNVF), have been developed.



➢ In this paper, we will present a numerical simulation of a VCNVF test conducted 



at NASA/GRC of filling a large tank with liquid hydrogen. 



➢ The simulation was performed using Generalized Fluid System Simulation 



Program (GFSSP) developed at NASA/MSFC.

Conclusions



➢ This paper presented model validation results of a GFSSP numerical model of 



the non-vented filling of a large hydrogen tank.



➢ Model accounts for different regimes of pool boiling heat transfer during tank 



chilldown.



➢ Model also accounts for condensation around spray droplet and liquid – vapor 



interface.



➢ GFSSP model captures the various stages of NVF, flash evaporation, boiling at 



the wall, and condensation.



➢ The model predicts tank pressure, filling rate, and wall temperature within 20%, 



omitting errors due to the offset in the temperature for Test 20.



➢ GFSSP model with new User Defined Functions (UDF) agree well with 



Cryogenic NVF data across various tank sizes, flowrates, and initial wall 



temperatures.
