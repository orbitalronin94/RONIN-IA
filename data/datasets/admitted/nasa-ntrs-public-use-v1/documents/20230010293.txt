<p>Spacecraft Charging
</p>
<p>• Spacecraft charging:
– Buildup of charge on surfaces, or 
</p>
<p>within materials, causes electrostatic 
</p>
<p>discharges, arcing. 
</p>
<p>– Varies with material, geometry, etc.
</p>
<p>– Can cause discharges on or 
</p>
<p>between surfaces at different 
</p>
<p>potentials (differential charging)
</p>
<p>• Dielectric materials:
– bad at distributing charge
</p>
<p>– susceptible to differential charging, 
</p>
<p>internal charging
</p>
<p>• largest differential potentials 
</p>
<p>on a spacecraft surface:
– between the sunlit and shaded 
</p>
<p>regions</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Dielectric Charging in Sunlight
</p>
<p>• The shadowed side is uniformly 
</p>
<p>charged to ~9500 V
</p>
<p>• Sunlit = charging to a positive 
</p>
<p>potential (as photoelectron flux 
</p>
<p>exceeds electron flux)
</p>
<p>• The sunlit grids ‘shadow’ certain 
</p>
<p>regions behind them (most 
</p>
<p>dangerous surface region)
</p>
<p>• Still a differential potential 
</p>
<p>between sunlit areas, and sunlit to 
</p>
<p>dark areas (sometimes very large)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>• There are a variety of 
</p>
<p>materials on launch 
</p>
<p>vehicles, and spacecraft
– Susceptible to differential 
</p>
<p>charging
</p>
<p>– particle flux varies across 
</p>
<p>different regions
</p>
<p>• Both can face a variety of 
</p>
<p>environments based on 
</p>
<p>trajectory
– Van-Allen Belt
</p>
<p>• Discharges could harm on-
</p>
<p>flight electronics, or even 
</p>
<p>break down materials
</p>
<p>Why is it important?</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>As a check: 
– can identify surface charging  
</p>
<p>‘danger-zones’ by plotting 
</p>
<p>trajectory
</p>
<p>– Use modeling software to 
</p>
<p>estimate potentials (NASCAP, 
</p>
<p>EMA Charge)
• Coupon style runs, more complex 
</p>
<p>runs
</p>
<p>What is required?
– Material properties
</p>
<p>• Found in databases like MAPTIS, 
</p>
<p>SCMD
</p>
<p>• Bulk conductivity, radiation-induced 
</p>
<p>conductivity, surface resistivity, 
</p>
<p>primary/secondary electron yield fits, 
</p>
<p>breakdown voltages, etc.
</p>
<p>– Trajectory, orientation
</p>
<p>– Geometry for more accuracy
</p>
<p>How to assess?</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>• Spacecraft charging analyses are inherently 
</p>
<p>difficult based on lack of material charging 
</p>
<p>properties
</p>
<p>• Data still being gathered and put into databases 
</p>
<p>(no fancy lookup tables as with mechanical 
</p>
<p>material properties) 
</p>
<p>• Same material data is even being re-measured 
</p>
<p>for further accuracy 
</p>
<p>• So, it is best to take a conservative approach 
</p>
<p>most of the time</p>
<p></p>
</div>
