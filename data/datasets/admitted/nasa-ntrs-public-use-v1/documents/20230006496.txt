How NTP Works



Rover Phoebus 2A



• Propellant heated directly by a nuclear reactor and thermally expanded/accelerated 



through a nozzle



• Low molecular weight propellant – typically Hydrogen



• Thrust directly related to thermal power of reactor: 100,000 N ≈ 450 MWth at 900 sec



• Specific Impulse directly related to exhaust temperature: 830 - 1000 sec (2300 - 3100K)



• Specific Impulse improvement over chemical rockets due to lower molecular weight of 



propellant (exhaust stream of H2/O2 engine runs hotter than NTP)

How did RIFT get Started?



• Summer 1960, Dr. Werner von Braun and team transferred from the Army to the new NASA Marshall 



Space Flight Center in Huntsville, AL. ~4600 employees and 1840 acres with existing facilities.[5]



• September 1960, contracts with Convair, Douglas Aircraft Company, Lockheed Aircraft Company, and 



the Martin Marietta Company to investigate the use of nuclear propulsion for upper stages.[2]



• April 29,1961 von Braun letter to Lyndon Johnson who had been asked by Kennedy to study what 



chances the United States had to win the moon race. In the letter, von Braun told Johnson that the 



“basic technology of nuclear rockets is still in its infancy.” Further, he called nuclear propulsion a 



“promising means to extend and expand the scope of our space operations in the years beyond 1967 or 



1968.” [6]



• May 25, 1961 Kennedy’s speech on landing on Moon and nuclear propulsion



• In June 1961, MSFC established a Nuclear Vehicle Projects Office to supervise and coordinate the 



development of high thrust nuclear propulsion rocket stages for future Saturn and other vehicle 



systems. Manage RIFT. [6]



• December 1961 studies completed and showed that a nuclear-powered rocket flight test could be 



launched as an upper stage of a chemical rocket in the Saturn class.[6]



• July 1962, Lockheed was selected to build the vehicle that would accept the Westinghouse/Aerojet 



NERVA engine. [6]

Why RIFT so Early?



• In early 1961, studies were showing a nuclear rocket third stage on 



SATURN could increase payload capability for orbital and escape 



missions by a factor of 1.5 to 2.5 times that of all-chemical vehicle.[7]



• Ten RIFT stages were planned to be made. One flight would not have 



a “live” NERVA. The other three would test transients and restart.[4]

• Kiwi B4A shutdown after reaching 500MW due to frequent flashes. 90% of reactor parts broken [12]



• Harold Finger halted further hot fire testing until vibration problem resolved. Dr Norris Bradbury (LANL Director) said stopping hot fire 



testing would kill the program. [2]



• Cold Flow test run with nitrogen and non-fissioning reactor and well instrumented. Vibration caused by interstitial flow. Problem resolved 



with better bundling of the core and seals. Final cold flow tests showed no vibration[2]



• Dr. Norris Bradbury wrote Harold Finger and said “insistence on a repeat KIWI B-4A cold flow test resulted in a damned interesting 



experiment! Perhaps we have all learned something!”[2]



Flow induced vibration problem. Non-nuclear cold flow system tests are valuable!

Cancellation of RIFT



• RIFT was cancelled late 1963 due to KIWI tests not going 
good and before KIWI B4D was tested. Also, national focus 
was landing on the Moon before the end of the decade.



• The NERVA engine program will be reduced to a ground test 
program. Reactor test programs planned for the coming year 
to be continued.



• MSFC will continue to study the design of a nuclear powered 
stage and the integration of this stage into the Saturn V 
launch vehicle with emphasis on operational applications



• Dr. Werner von Braun (MSFC Director 1960-1970) continued 
studies of NTP use on future space missions and approved 
special hot cells at MSFC for irradiating NTP components and 
PIE.
