<p>TECHNICAL MEMORANDUM
</p>
<p>STRESS CORROSION EVALUATION OF NITIHF ALLOY FOR THE INTERNATIONAL 
SPACE STATION ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL AND LIFE SUPPORT SYSTEM
</p>
<p>1.  INTRODUCTION
</p>
<p>NiTiHf, an alloy containing approximately 58% nickel, 39% titanium, and 3% Hafnium, 
was evaluated for stress corrosion cracking (SCC) resistance in support of the International Space 
Station (ISS) Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) as this alloy is considered 
a bearing material candidate for the Urine Processor Assembly (UPA), which is a system to recover 
water from urine in space to be used by the astronauts. NiTiHf is a shape memory alloy that is pro-
duced using powder metallurgy. The procedure involves subjecting the metallic powder to hot iso-
static pressure (HIP). This alloy was developed by NASA Glenn Research Center to replace 60NiTi 
(also known as Nitinol 60). The stress corrosion evaluation, performed at the Marshall Space 
Flight Center (MSFC) Materials and Processes Laboratory, involved testing in environments that 
are representative of service conditions as well as in a sodium chloride (NaCl) environment. The 
purpose of this investigation is to determine if  the material has adequate SCC resistance to be used 
in the ISS UPA system. In addition, the data is expected to be used to support future development 
of a standard or specification on this material. Previous stress corrosion evaluations performed at 
MSFC in support of the ECLSS program are included in references 1 through 3.   </p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>NASA Glenn Research Center supplied the fabricated samples for this evaluation, which 
were obtained from a 4.3-in diameter by 12.7-in long billet of NiTiHf. The material was a powder 
metallurgy ingot obtained from Carpenter Technology Corporation. The certificate of analysis for 
this material is shown in figures 1 and 2. Figure 1 contains details on how this material was pro-
duced and the chemical composition. Figure 2 contains optical micrographs of NiTiHf.

<p>After loading the specimens, they were placed inside 0.5-liter chemical-resistant plastic jars 
(figure 24), followed by the addition of the ECLSS testing fluids. The testing fluids were baseline 
pretreat, baseline brine, alternate pretreat, and alternate brine. The baseline fluids include sulfu-
ric acid and the alternate fluids include phosphoric acid. All solutions contain a small amount of 
chromic acid. The brine solutions were obtained by distilling  the pretreat solutions. Part of the 
water was removed resulting in more concentrated solutions. All the solutions had a pH of about 2. 
The word “pretreat” as used here refers to urine that was treated with flush water, chromium triox-
ide (oxidant), and the corresponding inorganic acid to control growth of microorganisms. The test 
solutions supplied were prepared by the Environmental Control and Life Support System Develop-
ment Branch at Marshall Space Flight Center (ES62).</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>14
</p>
<p>Figure 24.  Test setup for NiTiHf specimens tested in ECLSS fluids.
</p>
<p>The baseline pretreatment solution was prepared by adding 15.9 ml of baseline pretreat 
stabilizer and 265 ml of deionized water to one liter of urine (Donald L. Carter, email message to 
author, April 10, 2013). The baseline pretreat stabilizer consists of 54.5% deionized water, 9% chro-
mium trioxide (CrO3), and 36.5% sulfuric acid (H2SO4) by mass. The corresponding baseline brine 
solution is based on 85% water recovery of the pretreated urine. 
</p>
<p>The alternate pretreat was prepared by adding 17.5 ml of alternate pretreat stabilizer and 
265 ml of deionized water to one liter of urine (Donald L. Carter, email message to author, April 
10, 2013). The alternate pretreat stabilizer contains 2.36 ml of 85% phosphoric acid (H3PO4) and 
0.94 ml of 30% chromium trioxide (CrO3) oxidizer solution. The corresponding alternate brine 
solution is based on 85% water recovery of the pretreated urine.
</p>
<p>The alternate formulation was developed because the baseline formulation was causing pre-
cipitation of calcium sulfate dihydrate in the urine processor distillation assembly, which resulted in 
problems in the system. For detailed descriptions of the problems and how they were approached 
see reference 6. The stress corrosion testing in the ECLSS solution was accomplished by total 
immersion for 400 days. </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>15
</p>
<p>For comparison, a set of specimens was tested in 3.5% NaCl alternate immersion per 
ASTM G44.7 The tester, which is shown in figure 25, consists of a Ferris wheel type mechanism 
that has six plexiglass trays, a tank, a micro switch, a timer, and a 5-gallon glass bottle for deionized 
water. A 3.5% NaCl solution is prepared in the tank by dissolving 3.5 +/- 0.1 parts by weight of 
reagent grade NaCl in 96.5 parts of water. Deionized water is used to prepare the salt solution and 
to replenish the water lost by evaporation. The deionized water used exceeded the purity require-
ments of ASTM Specification D1193 type IV.8 The NaCl concentration is verified with a salinome-
ter. The pH of this solution when it is prepared should be between 6.4 and 7.2. If  necessary, the pH 
is adjusted with diluted reagent grade hydrochloric acid or reagent grade sodium hydroxide. The 
air temperature in the alternate immersion laboratory is maintained at 80 +/- 2 degrees Fahrenheit, 
and the relative humidity at 45 +/- 10%. Once the solution was prepared and the environmental 
conditions were set, the specimens were placed on the tester racks, equally distributed among the 
six trays and the test was started. The start date and time were recorded. The alternate immersion 
system rotates 60 degrees every ten minutes, allowing the specimens to be exposed to the solution
for ten minutes, followed by a drying period of 50 minutes. This cycle is repeated continually until 
the test is complete. Initially the plan was to test the specimens for at least a year in alternate 
immersion, however, they had to be removed after 200 days because the COVID-19 pandemic 
started. The center was closed during that time and the test could not be monitored any longer. 
However, 200 hours is considered sufficient time to detect any susceptibility to stress corrosion in 
alternate immersion, especially when most of the alternate immersion tests are run for periods of 
time ranging from 30 days (aluminum and low alloy steels) to 90 days (stainless steels, nickel, and 
titanium alloys), and occasionally for 180 days. During the time the tester ran, the specimens were 
inspected every working day. 
</p>

<p>The UTS values of the specimens tested unstressed and average values (AvUTSu) for each 
environment are presented in table 4. The UTS value of each sample tested with an applied stress 
(UTSs) was compared with the AvUTSu value for the corresponding environment; the results are 
presented in tables 5 to 9. The comparison consisted in obtaining the ratio of UTSs to AvUTSu for 
each environment. UTSs/AvUTSu is designated the stressed-to-unstressed UTS ratio in this report. 
A specimen with a stressed-to-unstressed UTS ratio of less than 0.75 can be considered a failure. It 
would indicate that the combined effect of stress and the environment was significantly greater than 
the effect of the environment only on the strength of the material. The acceleration in reduction 
in mechanical properties in SCC-susceptible materials is usually caused by cracks that grow as the 
stressed material is exposed to a corrosive environment. A material that shows that tendency at 
a specific stress level has a high probability of failing suddenly and prematurely by losing all the 
ability to carry the load. As seen in tables 5 to 9, none of the specimens had a stressed-to-unstressed 
UTS ratio that was less than 0.75, which is a positive result. The ratios ranged from 0.81 to 1.26, 
with 16 specimens with a ratio greater than 1 and 23 with a ratio less than 1. The overall average 
was 0.99 and the median value was 0.97. The UTS values ranged significantly, but that was also 
the case for the specimens tested initially in air, which suggests that the variation in strength is a 
characteristic of the material and that was not caused by the exposure. 
</p>

<p>4.  CONCLUSION
</p>
<p>The results of this evaluation do not indicate stress corrosion susceptibility of NiTiHf in 
complete immersion in the urine processing solutions (baseline pretreat, baseline brine, alternate 
pretreat, and alternate brine) or in 3.5% NaCl alternate immersion under the conditions of these 
tests. The ultimate tensile strength values varied significantly for this material, which is common 
for high hardness bearing materials. No deterrents were found for the use of this material for 
bearing applications at room temperature and at the test conditions. As a hard material, any parts 
fabricated from NiTiHf should be free of surface defects, especially if  it is going to be in tension, 
because flaws on this type of material combined with high tensile stress (applied or residual) may 
create the conditions for a fracture. This can be exacerbated by the fact that the specific ultimate 
tensile strength of the part can be unpredictable. The results of this work only apply to room 
temperature and should not be assumed that they will be the same at other temperatures. It can 
be concluded that this material has potential uses in the Environmental Control and Life Support 
System Urine Processor Assembly of the International Space Station. The generated data can also 
support the development of standards and/or specifications for this material or for the revision of 
existing ones, such as MSFC-SPEC-3706. Finally, as it is the case for any newly generated material, 
additional research is encouraged.</p>
