Creating Composite Biosignatures from Multiple-Method Data: Elemental, Isotopic, Reflectance, and Raman



Diana Gentry1, Abdullah Shahid2, Tao Sheng3, and Sunanda Sharma4



1NASA Ames Research Center 2North Carolina State University
3University of Pittsburgh 4Carnegie Institute of Science



Introduction:A biosignature is a data feature that in-
dicates past or present life. The Statistical Classification
of Biosignature Information (SCOBI) project is the first
attempt to algorithmically classify potential biosignatures
across multiple types of data. This approach is more rep-
resentative of data return from planetary exploration mis-
sions, which typically carry suites of multiple instruments
and can incorporate information from earlier missions and
observations.



Early work used a subset of ∼1200 measurements ei-
ther indicative or non-indicative of life [1]. Current results
use a improved data set of >4600measurements span-
ning 22 systems, with particular improvements in aque-
ous non-indicative samples (sterile ices) and environmen-
tal materials containing both indicative and non-indicative
material (mixed: snow, seawater, limestone). We sum-
marize here the results of applying five classification al-
gorithms, including supervised learning, to this multiple-
method data set. The data set is described in more detail
in [2], and the methods in [3].

Methods: Indicative samples included both au-
totrophic and heterotrophic bacteria, archaea, plant leaves,
and biogenic limestones and kerogens. Non-indicative
samples included meteorites, ices, and Mars regolith. The
classifiers were k-nearest neighbors (KNN), logistic re-
gression (LR), support vector machines (SVM), random
forest (RF), Gaussian naı̈ve Bayes (GNB), and a “voting”
sum of the prior five. Principal component analysis (PCA)
and n− 1 tests provided added introspection into the rel-
ative importance of each data type, sample, and feature.



We acknowledge the limits of extrapolating from
Earth life. Where possible, features were chosen to re-
duce dependence on Earth-like biochemistry; where not
possible, these features – such as the particular spectral
properties of chlorophyll, which has evolved in coordina-
tion with Earth’s surface insolation – were tagged to allow
future investigation into how their inclusion or exclusion
affects performance.



Results and Conclusions:The complete data set, us-
ing all four data types, resulted in ROC AUC = 0.78.
Individually, reflectance spectroscopy had the highest
AUC (0.78) and elemental abundance the lowest (0.69).



Three data type subsets (E+V+R, I+V+R, V+R) outper-
formed both the full set and the highest individual data
type (V+R AUC = 0.81), demonstrating that while com-
posite biosignature information can indeed improve on
single-data-type classification, more data can also mean
more opportunities to reinforce errors.



The performance of the different algorithms was vari-
able. RF yielded the highest overall performance, though
KNN was higher for several data type combinations and
the voting classifier was more consistent. SVM was gen-
erally the least accurate.



In n − 1 tests, Mg abundance had the biggest pos-
itive impact on AUC (+0.02), followed by Fe and P
(+AUC > 0.01). Raman mean intensity, Raman mean
peak width, N, and H had negative impact > 0.01 AUC.
(The confounding effect of H likely reflects that it is of-
ten not reported in the source data, especially for mineral
samples.) The lower apparent performance of elemental
abundance as an overall data type likely reflects this pres-
ence of both high- and low-value features, and indicates
that subsetting the data would likely significantly increase
performance. More advanced feature extraction methods
for reflectance and Raman data would likely further im-
prove these data types’ performance as well.



Carbonatite (non-indicative) and microbial mats (in-
dicative) were frequent outliers, with carbonatite being
misclassified in a majority of cases. When all indicative
samples were further subclassed as alive, non-alive, or
mixed, classification was generally less accurate for mixed
sample types – an important caveat, as these most closely
resemble potential planetary samples.

Future Work: The SCOBI data set will be released
through the Open Data Repository. These initial results
strongly justify further investigation, including with neu-
ral networks and other deep learning tools.
