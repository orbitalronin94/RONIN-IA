CHOMPTT
CubeSat Handling of Multisystem Precision Time Transfer

Technology Demonstration
• Stakeholders: NASA HEOMD-AES, STMD-SSTP, 

AFRL
• Project Manager: Anh N. Nguyen (formerly 

Belgacem Jaroux)
• Risk Classification: X-project (Note: University 

of Florida is the spacecraft owner and 
operator)

• Project Description:
Technology demonstration for enhanced GPS 
and future disaggregated navigation systems

• Status: Phase E (Mission Ops)
• Estimated Total Lifecycle Cost: ~$500K

L1 Requirements
• On-orbit demonstration of ground-to-space optical time transfer to a 3U 

CubeSat using COTS hardware

Key Performance Parameters
• Single optical time-transfer <200 ps time transfer error (6 cm) – in 

progress
• Clock drift of < 20 ns after 1 orbit (6 m) – complete

NASA ARC Roles and Responsibilities:
• Project Management and Systems Engineering
• Spacecraft development (EDSN/NODES SC bus)
• Collaboration on payload development
• I&T, I&T Facilities
• Collaboration on mission operations
• Delivery of one 3U spacecraft to the LSP for integration  
University of Florida Roles and Responsibilities:
• Payload development
• Mission operations and ground segment provider 
• Optical Facilities: TISTEF (UCF) Florida, EOS Australia, MEO France

Technical Implementation
• Delivery of 1x Flight Unit, 4kg, 3U CubeSat (in-house development, utilizes 

EDSN/NODES SC as bus)
• Nominal orbit: 500 km x 500 km, 85° inc.
• Mission Duration: 9 months (exceeded)
• Power: <1W orbit avg; 35 Wh EOL installed battery capacity
• GNC: NovaTel OEM615 GPS, magnetometer, IMU, 3x Faulhaber reaction 

wheels (EDSN/NODES experimental), and sun sensors and magnetorquers
embedded in solar panels

• Communications: UHF (437.56 MHz) Primary TT&C UL/DL, StenSat (437.56 
MHz) Beacon

Chief Engineer Responsibilities
• Formal Technical Authority

• Holds engineering requirements and approval for non-conformances, deviations, and waivers on flight 
articles

• Set engineering policy
• Anomaly resolution
• Risk Identification

• Individual projects/missions
• Systemic across the center

14

Chief Engineer: What’s it like?
• Large scope spanning multiple projects and disciplines

• Must shift gears frequently
• Rarely are you the expert

• Communicate at a detailed level with the working engineer;  then synthesize and distill 
for communication to high-level management

• Balance-balance-balance:  cost, schedule, system capability and risk
