<p>Space Nuclear Legacy Systems</p>
<p>Radioisotope Thermal Generators (RTGS) </p>
<p>June 29, 1961 – Transit 4A: The first U.S. spacecraft (U.S. Navy navigation satellite) to utilize nuclear energy powered by a RTG </p>
<p></p>
<p>Space power reactors</p>
<p>April 3, 1965 – System for Nuclear Auxiliary Power (SNAP) 10A: world’s first (and only US) reactor in space, 42 kWt / 0.6 kWe</p>
<p>Soviet reactors typically 100 kWt  3kWe (some systems &gt;150 kWt)</p>
<p></p>
<p>Space Propulsion </p>
<p>1959-1972 – RoverNERVA: Developed and tested Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP) reactors/engines, 100MWt – 5000MWt</p>
<p>Partial and full-power 25 to 250k lbf thrust</p>
<p>Max cumulative burns up to 100 minutes, specific impulse over 800s, peak fuel temperatures up to 2,750K</p>
<p>Maximum number of restarts 28 for a single engine</p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>5</p>
<p>What’s Changed</p>
<p>Landscape: Rapidly growing commercial space base and emerging cislunar economy  </p>
<p>Advances:  Improvements in nuclear materials, manufacturing methods, fuel forms, and reactor design </p>
<p>Space Policy Changes: National Security Presidential Memorandum (NSPM) 20 on the Launch of Spacecraft Containing Space Nuclear Systems, Space Policy Directive - 6</p>
<p>Clarified risk posture for government and commercial nuclear launches</p>
<p>Established ambitious goals for government development and demonstration of space nuclear power and propulsion capabilities</p>
<p>Focus on reactor developments that enable HALEU fuel (≤ 19.75% enriched) </p>
<p>Strengthen government investment through collaboration and commonality </p>
<p>Establish all-government launch and operation safety policies and procedures7 </p>
<p>Broaden engagement with domestic and international terrestrial nuclear industry</p>
<p>Enhance domestic investments in small modular reactors for terrestrial power production </p>
<p>Technology developments to date indicate use of HALEU can enable mass-optimized reactor designs</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>11</p>
<p>NASA Space Nuclear Propulsion</p>
<p>Provides a robust and reliable energy to both human and scientific exploration missions </p>
<p>Offers energy-dense systems with high ratios of power to mass and volume </p>
<p>Shares a strong interest from industry and other government organizations for space transportation</p>
<p>Propulsion: Speed, Maneuverability, Resiliency</p>
<p>On-demand orbital changes</p>
<p>Rapid transit for intercepts beyond GEO</p>
<p>Earth to Moon orbit in 1 day vs. 3 days</p>
<p>Human transit to Mars</p>
<p>Enables high-value deep space science missions</p>
