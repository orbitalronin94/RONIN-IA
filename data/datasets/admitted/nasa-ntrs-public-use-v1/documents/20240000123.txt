Space Nuclear Legacy Systems


Radioisotope Thermal Generators (RTGS) 


June 29, 1961 – Transit 4A: The first U.S. spacecraft (U.S. Navy navigation satellite) to utilize nuclear energy powered by a RTG 





Space power reactors


April 3, 1965 – System for Nuclear Auxiliary Power (SNAP) 10A: world’s first (and only US) reactor in space, 42 kWt / 0.6 kWe


Soviet reactors typically 100 kWt 3kWe (some systems >150 kWt)





Space Propulsion 


1959-1972 – RoverNERVA: Developed and tested Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP) reactors/engines, 100MWt – 5000MWt


Partial and full-power 25 to 250k lbf thrust


Max cumulative burns up to 100 minutes, specific impulse over 800s, peak fuel temperatures up to 2,750K


Maximum number of restarts 28 for a single engine








Transit 4A (with payloads)








Russian TOPAZ 


SNAP 10A 




















NERVA


Phoebus 2A: most powerful nuclear rocket engine ever tested is shown during a high-power test. The reactor operated for about 32 minutes, 12 minutes at power levels of more than 4.0 million kilowatts.

What’s Changed


Landscape: Rapidly growing commercial space base and emerging cislunar economy 


Advances: Improvements in nuclear materials, manufacturing methods, fuel forms, and reactor design 


Space Policy Changes: National Security Presidential Memorandum (NSPM) 20 on the Launch of Spacecraft Containing Space Nuclear Systems, Space Policy Directive - 6


Clarified risk posture for government and commercial nuclear launches


Established ambitious goals for government development and demonstration of space nuclear power and propulsion capabilities


Focus on reactor developments that enable HALEU fuel (≤ 19.75% enriched) 


Strengthen government investment through collaboration and commonality 


Establish all-government launch and operation safety policies and procedures7 


Broaden engagement with domestic and international terrestrial nuclear industry


Enhance domestic investments in small modular reactors for terrestrial power production 


Technology developments to date indicate use of HALEU can enable mass-optimized reactor designs
