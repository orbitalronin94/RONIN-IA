<div class="slide-content"><p>Introduction</p>
<p>Solid solution carbide fuel, (U,Zr)C, is an attractive fuel candidate material for space nuclear propulsion applications given its high temperature stability, high melting point, and hot hydrogen compatibility</p>
<p>Solid solution carbide fuels involving UC and refractory carbides TaC, ZrC, NbC were investigated in the 1960’s under the NERVA program</p>
<p>Studies suggest that low a UC content is crucial for enhancing operating temperatures along with hypo stoichiometric carbon content [1]</p>
<p>In this work, consolidated (U0.2,Zr0.8)C0.93 fuel samples were tested under hot hydrogen at 2,300°C (2,573.15 K) for a cumulative time of 3 hours with intervals at 30, 90, and 180 minutes</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide3_rId2"></div>
<p>Pseudo binary phase diagram for (U0.1Zr0.9)Cx [1]</p>

<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>(U,Zr)C Powder Feedstock</p>
<p>Powder feedstock of the composition U0.2Zr0.8C0.93 was produced via carbothermic reduction synthesis at Los Alamos National Laboratory [2]  </p>
<p>The powder feedstock was passed through a 200 mesh sieve and achieved a final particle size less than 74 μm</p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Sample Consolidation via Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS)</p>
<p>The U0.2Zr0.8C0.93 powder was introduced in a graphite die of 20 mm in diameter</p>
<p>The consolidation was performed via SPS</p>
<p>The peak temperature was 2,000°C and was held for approximately 10 minutes</p>
<p>The sample density reported was 98.64 % ± 0.06  TD</p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Hot Hydrogen Testing</p>
<p>The testing was completed at the Compact Fuel Element Environmental Test (CFEET) furnace at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center</p>
<p>The sample was exposed to inductive heating under atmospheric pressure with a hydrogen gas flow rate of 6 standard liters per minute (SLPM)</p>
<p>The tests were performed at 2,300°C (2,573.15 K) for a cumulative time of 3 hours in 30 minute increments after an initial interval of 10 minutes</p>
<p>The sample temperature was recorded through a quartz window with a multi-wavelength spectropyrometer (FAR FMP2/2X)</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Methods for Sample Analysis</p>
<p>Visual inspection was conducted after each hot hydrogen interval</p>
<p>Mass loss determination by weighing before and after each hot hydrogen interval</p>
<p>Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) were performed with: JEOL JSM-IT200</p>
<p>X-ray (XRD) diffraction analyses were carried out with an Aeris Research Edition powder diffractometer and HighScore+ software by Malvern Panalytical. Crystallographic data base: ICDD PDF-4+ 2020  </p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide7_rId2"></div>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Mass Loss</p>
<p>Visual inspection after the first intervals revealed an increased roughness in all surfaces </p>
<p>The largest mass loss occurs during the first run (~10 min) and can be attributed to rapid initial loss of carbon at the surface along with possible surface contamination being burned</p>
<p>The intervals after the 30 minute mark reported mass losses less than 7 mg per cycle as they tend to plateau in the remaining cycles. Such behavior is in agreement with other studies [4]</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>X-ray Diffraction</p>
<p>XRD data was collected on the powder feedstock prior to fabrication, on the consolidated sample, and after 30, 90, and 180 minutes of hot hydrogen exposure</p>
<p>The XRD data confirmed that the initial phase of U0.2Zr0.8C0.93 was preserved during sample consolidation and all cycles of hot hydrogen exposure. </p>
<p>The material did not undergo any chemical changes nor reacted with hydrogen during 3 hours of testing at 2,300 °C (2573 K)</p>
<p>These results are also in agreement with Kardoulaki et al., where the same fuel composition was tested at  2,327 °C in flowing hydrogen gas</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>SEM and EDS Analyses</p>
<p>SEM imaging was performed on the samples at 30, 90, and 180 minutes of hot hydrogen exposure</p>
<p>The predominant feature in the microstructure was thermal etching</p>
<p>The formation of surface porosity was observed after 90 and 180 minutes of hot hydrogen exposure</p>
<p>EDS analyses confirmed the presence of initial elements, zirconium and uranium in the fuel compound at the end of the test</p>
<p>Microcracks were observed in different areas of the sample after the 3 hours of testing, but no fragmentation or major breaking of the sample wafer occurred</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Discussion</p>
<p>The process parameters to densify U0.2Zr0.8C0.93 fuel via SPS proved to be successful while producing a high density sample with a density 98.64 % of the theoretical density (TD)</p>
<p>Upon completion of all hydrogen testing intervals, the sample density was reported as 90.01% TD</p>
<p>The cumulative mass loss recorded during the entire test (~3 hours) was less than 0.75% of the initial sample mass</p>
<p></p>

<p>A comparison of other fuel concepts undergoing hot hydrogen testing was completed. All data is evaluated after 30 minutes of hydrogen exposure. Even though the U0.2Zr0.8C0.93 fuel was tested at the highest temperature, it showed the lowest mass loss rate in this comparison</p>
<p>It demonstrated its exceptional compatibility under hot hydrogen while preserving its integrity and over 99% of its initial mass.</p>
<p>Regardless of the heating, cooling, and hot hydrogen flow during the cumulative 3 hours, the U0.2Zr0.8C0.93 sample did not display any fracturing</p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusion</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide12_rId2"></div>
<p>Bi-carbide U0.2Zr0.8C0.93 did not undergo any chemical changes nor reacted with hydrogen during 3 hours of testing at 2,300 °C </p>
<p>Despite the heating, cooling, and hot hydrogen flow during 3 hours, the U0.2Zr0.8C0.93 sample did not display any fracturing and preserved its structural integrity</p>
<p>The cumulative mass loss rates in this study is in agreement with the 16.81 mg/(m2·s) mass loss rate recorded by Kardoulaki et al. while testing (U,Zr)C fuel under very similar conditions.</p>
<p>The results from this work demonstrated the remarkable performance of solid solution refractory carbide fuels</p>
<p>In this study alone, this fuel concept shows a high temperature stability, hot hydrogen compatibility, and minimum mass losses after concluding hot hydrogen exposure for 3 hours </p>
<p>All those factors are essential for an NTP reactor, making solid solution refractory carbide fuel an excellent candidate for the application</p>
<p>Future work is being emphasized in evaluating the performance of different compositions with varying uranium contents in (U,Zr)C fuel</p>
<p></p>
