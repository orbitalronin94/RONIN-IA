Motivation



3



The NASA CERES project provides over 22 years of observed TOA fluxes and a suite
of datasets that include surface fluxes and cloud properties on different spatial and
temporal scales.



The Ed4 FluxByCldTyp data product is a new dataset that provides radiative fluxes as a
function of cloud pressure layer and optical depth bin (ISCCP-like) cloud types for
climate studies



As part of the effort to continue to improve accuracy and consistency of the
FluxByCldTyp fluxes, we used machine learning technique to calculate CERES sub-
footprint fluxes from MODIS narrowband radiances within mixed scene satellite
footprints



I will present preliminary results which show improvement over the current Ed4
algorithm that will be eventually become part of Ed5











The CERES Project



4



I. The Clouds and the Earth Radiant Energy System (CERES) instruments were
first aboard TRMM satellite launched in December 1997 and currently on
Terra, Aqua, NPP and NOAA20 polar orbiters.



II. CERES produces flux data products based on radiances measured in three
bands.



• SW (0.3-5μm)
• IR Window (8-12μm)
• Total channel (0.3-200μm)



LW = Total Channel – SW



III. Cloud properties are retrieved from the onboard imagers (MODIS or VIIRS)
measurements on the same satellite to assist CERES radiance to flux
conversion



IV. CERES produces a suite of datasets of different spatial and temporal scales
(https://ceres.larc.nasa.gov/)

Summary and Future work



• The DNN_rad/ADM and DNN_flux are developed to improve fluxes 
in FluxByCldTyp product. They both show improvement over Ed4.1



• The two methods give about the same results. DNN_flux is 
slightly better. 



• The DNN_flux method does not require the CERES ADM, thus 
greatly reduce code complexity.



• DNN will be incorporated in to FluxByCldTyp Ed5.
• Further improvement involves including more MODIS NB channels.
