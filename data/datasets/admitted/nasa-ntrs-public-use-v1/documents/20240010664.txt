PACE Ocean Color Instrument Functional Requirements


With an RMS spectral difference of 0.15 nm or 


a residual dispersion of 0.17 nm,


OCI meets the spectral accuracy requirement of 0.5 nm.

Goals of the on-orbit spectral calibration of OCI:





Comparing spectral observations of sunlight reflected by solar diffusers with predicted spectral observations of the reflected sunlight.





Comparing line positions and strengths with those derived from the TSIS solar reference spectrum and the diffuser BRDF, convolved with the OCI RSRs measured by GLAMR. 





The on-orbit spectral calibration looks for shifts between the prelaunch RSRs and the on-orbit performance of the instrument.





Comparison of atmospheric absorption bands observed in spectra reflected by cloudtops with spectra reflected by the cloudfree ocean.

Spectral Accuracy














23





The RMS spectral shift in the Fraunhofer lines and atmospheric absorption bands between the measured and predicted spectra is 0.15 nm.





The residual spectral dispersion over the wavelengths of the Fraunhofer lines is 0.17 nm.





The band strengths of the measured and predicted spectral lines are essentially the same.





For the atmospheric absorption bands, the band strengths are higher over the ocean than over clouds, though the radiances are 1/8 the radiances over clouds.





The OCI spectral accuracy on orbit meets the instrument functional requirement of 0.5 nm.





The on-orbit spectral calibration of OCI has not shifted since calibration by GLAMR during the thermal vacuum test campaign.
