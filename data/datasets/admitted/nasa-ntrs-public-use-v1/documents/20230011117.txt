Motivation for In-Space Inspection


The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) has key initiatives to establish outposts on the Moon and Mars


In-Space Manufacturing (ISM) provides a solution towards sustainable, flexible missions (both in-transit and on-surface) 


On-demand fabrication, repair, and recycling capabilities.


In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) will be used to create launch/landing pads, habitats, shelters, roadways, etc.


Investment in capabilities to protect astronauts while building key infrastructure on extraterrestrial surfaces is crucial.


NASA will need to develop this complex space hardware and infrastructure with inspectability/Nondestructive Evaluation (NDE) in mind. 


There is already an inherent need to inspect, verify and validate complex systems prior to flight (on earth).


There is a emerging need to inspect future ISM and ISRU hardware in its fabrication and service environment…space.

In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU)


ISRU system concept for autonomous robotic excavation and processing of Mars soil to extract water for use in exploration missions


Ref. https://www.nasa.gov/isru/overview and https://www.nasa.gov/nextstep/isru 


ISRU involves hardware and/or operation that harnesses ‘in-situ’ resources to create products and services for robotic and human exploration


NDE Tools are required for ISRU resource assessment/prospecting and in-situ manufacturing.


New Nondestructive Evaluation (NDE) Tools are needed that will provide information to make decisions such as:


Compositional analysis of raw materials


Integrity, size and shape of ISRU AM-printed structures/habitats 


X-ray CT concepts being considered for core drill and loose sample inspections.


Can help identify compositional features of interest, by % volume, which can be pivotal in decision making 





GSFC x-ray Computed Tomography (CT) image slice of lunar basalt sample.





X-ray CT could be used to assess % volume of relative compositions, porosity/tortuosity, and much more.

Leverage NDE methods that have been successfully deployed on our spaceflight hardware:

Efforts include Concept Development and Trade Studies to assess feasibility for CT in different environments (ISS, lunar, Martian, crewed/non-crewed)

NDE for ISM/ISRU Applications


Require NDE methods capable of:


Assessing ISRU structural dimensions, defects, damage, etc. as related to strength


Locating subsurface features such as structural reinforcements, voids, fractures, etc.


Active & passive methods to monitor the “health” of the structure


Leverage NDE traditionally used for civil engineering applications: 


Ground Penetrating Radar


Perseverance and Tianwen-1 Rovers


Accurate estimations regarding the depth of the top surface of lunar soil, which is an important way to determine the stability and strength of the soil foundation for developing lunar bases and research stations


Acoustic Emission


Structural monitoring to detect, locate, and characterize internal damage 


Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity Testing


Evaluation of compressive strength


Assessment of structural integrity and localization of defects and voids in regolith

Key Challenges for In-Space NDE


Human Safety is Paramount … space is unforgiving. 


Essential to develop ruggedized NDE systems that are highly reliable and accurate.


Development of Space-rated NDE Systems:


High sensitivity, small/compact, low-mass, simple to use, battery powered are desired.


Withstand harsh vibro-acoustic environment of launch


Radiation hardened


Thermal Environment


Electrical charging


Vacuum


Certification of Additively Manufactured (AM) ISM and ISRU hardware


Requires close partnership with Fracture Control, M&P and Analysis disciplines is required in developing correlation of indication to verified defect.


NDE is just one piece of the puzzle


Astronaut NDE Inspector training/certification, Data Interpretation…


…many more challenges!

Summary





Space is unforgiving! 


Future In-Space NDE tools should be multi-purpose to address yet-to-be defined inspection needs. 


In-Space NDE and in situ monitoring will play critical roles in the certification of hardware produced for ISRU/ISM applications.


Structural certification plans aren’t defined; however, active collaboration with Fracture, Materials and Analysis will be essential to quantifying NDE results


Space-rated NDE systems are needed!


Require NDE systems that are robust and highly reliable. 


High sensitivity, small/compact, low-mass, simple to use, battery powered… 


Consider partnership and collaboration opportunities with NASA.
