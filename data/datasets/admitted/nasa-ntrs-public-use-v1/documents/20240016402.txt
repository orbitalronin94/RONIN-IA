<p>Managing cryogens is a critical technology for NASA’s Artemis missions</p>
<p>Cryogenic fluid management (CFM) challenges</p>
<p>Storage and delivery: evaporative loss due to heat leakage, liquid location tracking</p>
<p>Pressure control: helium/autogenous pressurization, depressurization, cryocoolers</p>
<p>Tank filling/draining: draining and pressurization, jet and spray injection filling</p>
<p></p>
<p>NASA’s CFM Modeling Portfolio develops, enhances and validates computational fluid dynamic (CFD) and nodal tools for mission and operations design</p>
<p></p>
<p>Fluid Dynamics Branch (ER42) at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC) supports several CFM projects using CFD</p>
<p>Human Landing System (HLS), Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS), Space Launch System (SLS), etc.</p>
<p>ER42 has been validating Loci-Stream-VOF CFD solver to support current and future missions</p>

<p>Sharp Interface Turbulence Modeling</p>
<p>Stationary/quasi-stationary gas-liquid interface modeled with a fixed interface boundary</p>
<p>Velocity and stresses continuity</p>
<p>Significantly faster than volume of fluid method</p>
<p>Allows large number of numerical experiments</p>
<p></p>
<p>Modeling Evidence</p>
<p>Experiments, direct numerical simulation and large eddy simulation studies demonstrate</p>
<p>Diminished turbulence kinetic energy (TKE) on the gas side of the interface</p>
<p>Either unaltered or enhanced TKE on the liquid side of the interface</p>

<p>1. Autogenous Pressurization Test</p>
<p>Experiment</p>
<p>Performed at NASA MSFC using Engineering Design Unit tank with liquid hydrogen</p>
<p>The insulated double domed tank with 1.7 m diameter was filled to 90% volume</p>
<p>Uniform initial temperature of 20.345 K with saturation condition at the interface (1 bar ullage pressure)</p>
<p>Pressurized with room temperature gaseous hydrogen</p>
<p></p>
<p>CFD Model</p>
<p>Axisymmetric flow</p>
<p>Conjugate heat transfer</p>
<p>Temperature dependent thermal properties for the solids</p>

<p>Successfully validated Loci-Stream and the interface turbulence transport (ITT) modeling technique using autogenous pressurization and axial jet-based depressurization tests</p>
<p>ITT is crucial for reliable modeling of spaceflight CFM operations</p>
<p>The sharp interface model with simple ITT modeling is significantly faster than volume of fluid method and allows resolution of smaller scales</p>
<p>Demonstrated improved temperature predictions (and turbulence transport) compared to the RANS turbulence modeling using IDDES</p>
<p>Future work</p>
<p>Continuing to expand the validation to more CFM operations</p>
<p>Developing ITT model for volume of fluid method to simulate morphing interfaces</p>
<p>Validation against flight-scale tank experiments builds further confidence in reliability of our CFD tools for a wider range of CFM applications for supporting design/analysis of the long-term space flight missions</p>
<p>Insights gained from the validations are helping in-line support of Human Landing System spaceflight projects</p>

<p>Back-up</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Saturation pressure is very sensitive to the interface temperature</p>
<p>Phase change rate ṁ is very sensitive to the interface temperature.</p>
<p>For EDU tank autogenous pressurization experiment, 0.2 K error (1% error) in temperature results in 14% error in pressure</p>
<p>For K-site tank self-pressurization, 1 K error (5% error) in temperature results in 50% error in predicting tank pressure. For an unsteady problem, this is equivalent to being off by several hours of self-pressurization.</p>
<p>Must resolve thermal gradients due to heat exchange (latent and sensible) at the interface</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Experiments like those by <a href="https://www.dl.begellhouse.com/journals/5af8c23d50e0a883,6b4442405c7a2bae,23ace2da6ef09e09.html">Fabre et al</a> has shown that gas side turbulence is damped at the interface (“wall-like” behavior)</p>
<p><a href="https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article-abstract/2/10/1827/401812/The-effect-of-boundary-conditions-and-shear-rate?redirectedFrom=fulltext">Rashidi et al </a>show streak formations at the interface that are similar to the wall</p>
<p>DNS from <a href="https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article-abstract/8/6/1643/260075/Direct-numerical-simulation-of-near-interface?redirectedFrom=fulltext">Lombardi et al</a> shows turbulence on the gas side of the interface is wall-like, i.e., damped, but the turbulence on the liquid side is enhanced.</p>
<p>For liquids, “The mean velocity distribution, turbulence intensities, Reynolds stress and various other statistical measures are significantly altered compared to those in the wall region of channel flows”</p>
<p><a href="https://sites.chemengr.ucsb.edu/~ceweb/ce/people/faculty/banerjee/pdfs/jfm.pdf">Fulgosi</a><a href="https://sites.chemengr.ucsb.edu/~ceweb/ce/people/faculty/banerjee/pdfs/jfm.pdf"> et al</a> performed DNS analysis of stratified flow and found damped turbulence on the gas side interface similar to wall sublayer, but TKE is non-zero.</p>
<p>Importantly, they observed "similarity in the distribution of the turbulence intensities near the interface”. A general RANS model is plausible.</p>
<p><a href="https://doddm.com/publications/2020-ijmf-lj-md-jg-rt.pdf">Jofre et al</a> studies bubbly flow using LES and found that damping on the gas side of the interface produced satisfactory comparison to DNS in the near interface region</p>
<p></p>
<p>Summary</p>
<p>Gas-side interface turbulence: wall-like (Recall mesh requirements for heat transfer near a wall  need a low y+ mesh) </p>
<p>Liquid-side interface turbulence: either unchanged or somewhat enhanced</p>
