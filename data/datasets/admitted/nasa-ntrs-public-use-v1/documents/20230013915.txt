<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Markov Decision Process (MDP)
</p>
<p>Optimal Policy
</p>
<p>           Conditional probability of performing action a in state s such that
</p>
<p>       
</p>
<p>        the value function of this policy is greater than that of any other policy for all states</p>
<p></p>

<p>Evaluating the policy
</p>
<p>• Bellman Equation[1]
</p>
<p>• Value function of a state related to that of successor states
</p>
<p>• Iterative Policy Evaluation[2]
</p>
<p>• Bellman Eqn implies Vk = V𝜋 a fixed point in the iterative update
Backup diagram for V𝛑
</p>
<p>[1] On the Theory of Dynamic Programming, R. Bellman
[2] A Markovian Decision Process, R. Bellman</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Improving the policy
</p>
<p>• State-Action Value[1]
</p>
<p>• Expected return from following policy 𝛑(a|s) after action a in state s
</p>
<p>• Policy Improvement Theorem
</p>
<p>• For deterministic policies 𝛑 and 𝛑’
</p>
<p>Backup diagram for Q𝛑
</p>
<p>[1] Learning from Delayed Rewards, C. Watkins</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>PolicyEvaluation
• Input: Policy 𝛑(a|s)
• Initialize arbitrary V(s)
• Repeat until convergence:
</p>
<p>• Update V(s) via Bellman eqn
• Output: Value function V𝛑(s)
</p>
<p>PolicyImprovement
• Input: Value function V(s)
• Initialize arbitrary policy 𝛑(a|s)
• Repeat until convergence:
</p>
<p>• Update 𝛑(a|s) greedy w.r.t. V(s)
</p>
<p>PolicyIteration[1,2]
</p>
<p>• Initialize arbitrary policy 𝛑(a|s)
• Repeat until convergence:
</p>
<p>• V = PolicyEvaluation(𝛑)
• 𝛑 = PolicyImprovement(V)
</p>
<p>•Output: V*, 𝛑* [1] Dynamic Programming, R. Bellman
[2] Reinforcement Learning, Sutton &amp; Barto</p>

<p>Conclusions &amp; Outlook
</p>
<p>MDP based compilation
- Works with / without noise
- Beats Ross-Selinger in output length
- Scales as Poly(|S|.|A|) ~ Poly(|A|/ε)
</p>
<p>- Not as good as RS ~ PolyLog(1/ε)
</p>
<p>- Infeasible for multiple qubits, S ~ 2n
</p>
<p>- Improve T gate counts by rewarding them lesser
</p>
<p>Reinforcement Learning
- Use for larger qubit spaces
- Other state preparation problems
</p>

<p>Conclusions &amp; Outlook
</p>
<p>- Entanglement witnesses can efficiently verify entanglement in a state
</p>
<p>- Witnesses for QAOA-MaxCut require at most 3 sets of measurements 
</p>
<p>(once each in the X, Y and Z bases)
</p>
<p>- Such witnesses can also be used for other (e.g. GHZ) states
</p>
<p>- Witnesses for other QAOA states?
</p>
<p>- Is larger entanglement necessary for QAOA to solve combinatorial 
</p>
<p>optimization problems?</p>

<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>• It is possible to approximate continuous group gauge theories via 
</p>
<p>discrete subgroups
</p>
<p>• For U(1) x Z2, DN is a fairly good approximation for N ~ O(10)
</p>
<p>• Experimentally, we already have &gt; 80% estimated fidelity for D4 
</p>
<p>theory on a noisy QPU
</p>
<p>• More complicated gauge theories have more complicated discrete 
</p>
<p>subgroups, with more complicated gates to work out (future work)</p>
