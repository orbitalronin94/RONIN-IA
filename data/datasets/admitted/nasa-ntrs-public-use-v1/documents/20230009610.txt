<p>Motivation
</p>
<p>2
</p>
<p>Future space-flight missions will need:
</p>
<p>• sub-Kelvin detector cooling (at 40 mK and below) with higher heat loads than past missions
• significant cooling at Intermediate temperatures 0.04 K &lt; T &lt; 4 K (for Series Array SQUIDs, Filter &amp; 
</p>
<p>Termination banks, Cryogenic Mu Metal shield, other heat sinking…) 
</p>
<p>• NASA/GSFC is developing a flight-ready 4 K to 1 K to 0.03 K Continuous ADR (CADR) to meet these needs
</p>
<p>• In addition to its continuous operation, the major advantage of a CADR lies in its much higher cooling power per 
unit mass compared to its single-shot counterpart.
</p>
<p>• Various future missions are eyeing this technology, including probe missions such as the Line Emission Mapper X-
ray probe (LEM), the PRobe Far Infrared Mission for Astrophysics (PRIMA), and the Far-IR Spectroscopy Space 
Telescope (FIRSST). Additionally, there are under study flagship missions like the Habitable Worlds Observatory 
(HWO).
</p>
<p>X-IFU prototype array with more than 
3,000 pixels on a pitch of 260 µm. 
Courtesy of the X-IFU FPA team at 
SRON and NASA-GSFC</p>
<p></p>

<p>Design of the high T CADR
</p>
<p>7
</p>
<p>Stage 4A/4B:
</p>
<p>X2 AGGHS to thermally link to sink
X2 AGGHS to thermally link to intermediate T 
platform
</p>
<p>Operation in tandem to provide continuous cooling 
in a temperature range between 700 mK and 1.1 K
</p>
<p>Design of this stage is baselined from 
Hitomo/XRISM’s stage 3 with the following 
modifications:
</p>
<p>• Adapted to higher current 3A (Vs. 2A)
• Longer salt pill to accommodate required mass 
</p>
<p>of GLF
• Kevlar suspension replaced with Vespel
• SC magnet constrained flat against shield cap 
</p>
<p>(Vs. radially)</p>
<p></p>

<p>High T CADR shield design
</p>
<p>9
</p>
<p>Using 2V-Permendur (tradename Hiperco-50) as the 
shield
</p>
<p>Modeled the magnetic shield via Ampere and COMSOL 
to determine proper geometry
</p>
<p>Shield must not saturate at currents up to 3A in SC 
magnet
</p>
<p>Probe points 
10 cm away 
from top, 
bottom and 
outer mid-
section</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>High T CADR Suspension
</p>
<p>10
</p>
<p>Previously Kevlar was used to laterally (LSD: Lateral Suspension Device) constrain 
the salt pill in the bore of the SC magnet on one side of the pill and Vespel SP-1 for 
the other side.
</p>
<p>The Kevlar LSD is replaced with a Vespel SP-1 wheel and spokes design and rigorous 
mechanical and thermal analysis was carried to ensure the design meets requirements 
for vibration loading as well as thermal performance. </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>AGGHS testing and design
</p>
<p>11
</p>
<p>In order to verify that our existing flight AGGHS meets the 
minimum conductance required for the high T CADR, 
testing of an AGGHS was necessary.
</p>
<p>Troubles in test setup resulted in large errors, however 
within the errors conductance values were verified to fall 
within requirement.
</p>
<p>Need to refill switch to allow activation of getter at lower 
temp  lower conducted heat into gas and reentrant 
structure. (switch was previously tuned for 10 K operation)
</p>
<p>9 cm</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Hexapod structure
</p>
<p>12
</p>
<p>The 3 lower T CADR stages will need to 
mounted on a plate that is thermally 
isolated from the base 4 K plate.
</p>
<p>This requires careful design of the 
hexapod structure holding up the 
intermediate T plate to meet structural 
requirements for vibration loading and in 
the meantime minimize thermal loads to 
the intermediate T plate due to 
conduction.
</p>
<p>Iterative process was modeled via Python 
and Creo to find the optimum geometry, 
material, and positioning of the hexapod 
legs.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Low T CADR stages
</p>
<p>13
</p>
<p>• Hysteresis loss of all low T CADR 
stages are dumped at the intermediate 
T platform
</p>
<p>• These stages will use reentrant tubular 
suspensions as opposed to Kevlar. This 
allows for simpler assembly and 
integration.
</p>
<p>• Parasitic heat load into each stage must 
be minimized (less than 1 uW)
</p>
<p>• Unlike previous iterations, the lowest 
temperature stage salt pill will be 
suspended identically to other stages 
(SC magnet not integrated with salt 
pill)
</p>
<p>• Higher conductance heat switches will 
be used to fasten the cycle time
</p>
<p>• YbGG can be used instead of CPA for 
stage 3</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Stage 3 - YbGG
</p>
<p>14
</p>
<p>• Ytterbium Gallium Garnet (YbGG) has cooling capacity several 
times higher than Chromium Potassium Alum (CPA) per unit volume
</p>
<p>• Single crystal YbGG is procured and will be used in stage 3 of this 
CADR effort (pending test).
</p>
<p>• We will use He-3 to fill the voids in the salt pill bus to increase the 
conductance between the salt and the bus.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Overall magnetic shield
</p>
<p>15
</p>
<p>• Our shield concept will be similar to the one we designed and procured for the last SAT effort.
</p>
<p>• Use of high permeability material (A4K) to construct the shield wall and lids. Closeout shields 
designed for thermal straps exiting and leads entering the shield.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Old business…Dummy mass and SCHS vibe plan
</p>
<p>16
</p>
<p>Stages 3 thru 1 internal mechanical support:
</p>
<p>• For missions that don’t require an intermediate T platform, a viable pat would be to 
use the suspension system from the previous SAT effort (parasitic heat load from 4 
K sink).
</p>
<p>• The mechanical support for the salt pill on these stages has not been vibration 
tested and qualified yet. A plan to vibration test a Dummy Mass suspended 
identically to the actual stages is scheduled for late July 2023.
</p>
<p>SCHS:
• A plan to subject the SCHS to a vibration test is scheduled for late July 2023.
</p>
<p>• The SCHS will undergo post-vibe thermal tests once it survives vibration testing.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Conclusion
</p>
<p>17
</p>
<p>We are on a path to build the high T CADR stages, the AGGHSs, thermal 
straps, and the base plate to meet our first milestone.
</p>
<p>We then plan to move onto the detailed design of the low T CADR stages, 
including suspension, SC magnets, shield, etc. then proceed to building the 
stages, heat switches, thermal straps, etc.
</p>
<p>We will design our hexapod structure and have it analyzed by a structural 
analyst, after which we will begin fabrication and assembly of parts.
</p>
<p>We will conduct a thermal performance of the system, subject it to vibration 
loads commensurate with flight, and conduct post-vibe thermal test.</p>
