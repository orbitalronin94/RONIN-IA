<div class="slide-content"><p>Propelling the New Generation of Manned Spaceflight</p>
<p>Why use CFD to model Oxygen-Methane combustion applications at NASA?</p>
<p>Liquid Oxygen (LOX) and Methane (CH4) propellant fueled engines have been considered for In-Space Propulsion and a number of developing programs</p>
<p>Mars Lander concepts, Potential RL-10 replacement, SpaceX Raptor</p>
<p>Methane offers several advantages over fuels traditionally used by NASA and others in the space flight industry</p>
<p>Denser than Hydrogen, More energy per volume than H2; Less corrosive than Hydrazine</p>
<p>Methane Ignition presents unique and difficult challenges and is a significant risk for In-Space Ignition</p>
<p>Ignition of Hydrocarbon fuels is complex and stable combustion has been historically difficult for engine design</p>
<p>RL-10 was a H2/O2 Engine and presented significant ignition challenges at altitude, Methane would present additional challenges</p>
<p>Methane stored as a cryogenic liquid like H2, experiences phase changes at ambient temperatures and pressures unlike some other hydrocarbons, and requires a separate oxidizer unlike monopropellants</p>
<p>Shallow base of design experience in Space Flight propulsion applications</p>
<p>Empirical test-based experience focused on hydrogen, RP and hypergolic fuels</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>It’s Only Rocket Science</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide5_rId2"></div>
<p>Multiple choices for in-space propulsion system design</p>
<p>Cryogenic liquid propulsion systems basic elements</p>
<p>Turbine drives pumps</p>
<p>Pumps move propellants from tanks into feedlines at high pressure</p>
<p>Injectors direct propellants into combustion chamber</p>
<p>Combustion provides additional energy</p>
<p>Nozzle expands and accelerates gases, propelling spacecraft</p>
<p>Heat from the combustion chamber and nozzle drives the turbines</p>
<p></p>
<p>Our focus here is using CFD to model the combustion inside the Main Combustion Chamber to provide critical engineering metrics</p>
<p>Reliable Ignition – Difficult for In-Space environments</p>
<p>Stable Combustion – Injector design is critical</p>
<p>Sufficient Heat Transfer – Required to drive the turbine</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Computational Challenges of Combustion Modeling</p>
<p>Why use CFD to model combustion?</p>
<p>CFD-guided design can be cheaper and safer than an empirically-guided test development program</p>
<p>Why is combustion modeling considered to be computationally expensive?</p>
<p>Non-reacting CFD simulations have a significant set of base equations</p>
<p>Numerical methods used to solve 5 base PDEs that comprise the Navier-Stokes equations, plus turbulence closure equations and a species balance equation for multi-species </p>
<p>Finite-rate chemistry modeling of combustion adds additional equations</p>
<p>1 additional scalar equation for each additional species is added to the computation, practical chemistry models have 10-100+ species</p>
<p>Finite-rate models march slowly in physical time </p>
<p>Simulations typically require small time step sizes to be numerically stable</p>
<p>Flamelet Models provide an efficiency gain for combustion CFD simulations</p>
<p>Chemical reactions are computed for a fuel and oxidizer combination and tabulated prior to the CFD simulation</p>
<p>Combustion modeling in the CFD simulation achieved using just 2 new PDEs </p>
<p>Modest efficiency gain in time-step size </p>
<p>Ultimately, complexity, resolution and completion time of the CFD simulations is limited by computational resource availability</p>
<p>ER42 uses the NAS HPC resources at NASA Ames: Pleiades, Electra and Aitken Supercomputing Clusters</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Engineering Utilization of CFD Results on HPC Resources </p>
<p>Impact of Growing Computational Resources on CFD Simulation Fidelity</p>
<p>Effectively Utilize Complex Chemistry Mechanisms – Increases the accuracy of CFD simulations</p>
<p>Allows CFD Models to Include Complex Geometry and Finer Meshes – Improved spatial representation of the application in the CFD simulation</p>
<p> Improves Simulation Turn-around Time – Easier to engage with customers and provide actionable results</p>
<p></p>
<p>Introduction to three modern combustion CFD simulations conducted to support In-Space propulsion development projects</p>
<p>Ignition modeling of gaseous oxygen and methane flow inside a linear array chamber using LOCI\Chem</p>
<p>Project goal was to study to onset of transverse combustion instabilities</p>
<p>Finite-rate chemistry model with 12 species and 20 chemical reactions used to provide accurate flame speed propagation through the chamber</p>
<p>Mainstage combustion modeling of liquid oxygen and methane flow in an engine design concept using shear flow injector elements</p>
<p>Project goal was to extract heat flux distribution to chamber walls for a range of conditions to ensure that enough heat was extracted from the chamber walls to drive the turbine </p>
<p>Flamelet model utilized to enable efficient use of a large mesh and complex geometry</p>
<p>Mainstage combustion modeling of liquid oxygen and methane flow in an engine design concept using impinging flow injector elements</p>
<p>Project goal here was to quantify the heat release distribution in the primary combustion zone to quantify potential combustion instability modes</p>
<p>Flamelet model utilized to enable efficient use of a large mesh and complex geometry: Largest combustion CFD simulation completed by ER42 to date, &gt;100 Injector Elements and 600+ Million Volume Cells</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Liquid Oxygen and Methane Combustion Inside Concept Engine Chamber</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide10_rId4"></div>
<p>Blue Iso-surface shows where flame rapidly transitions from majority unburned O2 and CH4 propellant to majority combustion products such as CO2, CO and H2O</p>
<p>The green-yellow wall surface plot shows contours of heat flux into the chamber wall. The hardware designed to have higher heat flux at the aft end (right side) of the chamber which is uncommon in most chamber design concepts. Traditional, empirically derived design tools are not capable of accurately predicting this type of axial heat flux distribution down the chamber wall. The CFD-predicted wall heat flux was used to develop the turbine power cycle. Note: The sudden wall heat flux change halfway through the animation was part of a parametric study to help thermal analysts understand model changes due to fluctuating temperate at the solid walls. </p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Liquid Oxygen and Methane Combustion Inside Chamber with Impinging Injectors</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide11_rId4"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide11_rId5"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide11_rId6"></div>
<p>Blue Iso-surface shows where flame rapidly transitions from majority unburned O2 and CH4 propellant to majority combustion products such as CO2, CO and H2O</p>
<p></p>
<p>Cross-section shows temperature contours. Combustion gas temperature rapidly increases just downstream of blue iso-surface, vigorous combustion zone.</p>
<p></p>
<p>The green-yellow wall surface plot shows contours of heat flux into the chamber wall.</p>
<p>The impinging injector elements force rapid combustion of the propellants as indicated by the relatively short persistence of the blue iso-surface down the chamber. Peak combustion heat release occurs at a location just downstream of the iso-surface. Since the gases in the chamber are moving, this location has a time associated which is called sensible time lag. Sensible time lag is used in combustion stability calculations to predict frequencies and mode shapes of combustion instability events. CFD was able to provide this time lag parameter which is not well quantified in traditional combustion stability analysis methods for this injector type.     </p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Next Steps in ER42 CFD Combustion Modeling</p>
<p>CFD Model Fidelity Improvements</p>
<p>Equation of State robustness improvements – Enable robust simulations of two liquid propellants at sub-critical conditions such as engine start</p>
<p>Hypergolic Flamelet Model – Enable modeling combustion of two liquid hypergolic propellants which spontaneously ignite on contact</p>
<p></p>
<p>Harnessing Additional Computational Resources</p>
<p>Enable finer mesh CFD models to improve model spatial accuracy and better simulation temporal resolution while maintain impactful analysis completion times</p>
<p>Include additional engine geometry such as propellant manifolds – Critical for a CFD-only prediction of combustion instability events</p>
