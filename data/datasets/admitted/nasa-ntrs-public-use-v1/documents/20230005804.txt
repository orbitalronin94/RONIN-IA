Background



• NASA, ONERA and FAA recently completed a swept wing icing research 
effort called SUNSET II or SWIP.
– 65% scale high-speed Common Research Model (CRM65) swept wing geometry.
– Hybrid or truncated model designs for ice accretion tests of swept wing.
– Ice accretion and aerodynamic databases for App. C type MVD conditions.
– Preliminary comparisons of ice shape predictions from numerical tools.



• NASA and FAA collaborated on a follow-on effort to build and test a “full-
chord” version of the Midspan hybrid model.
– Compare full-chord and hybrid model ice shapes.
– Extend the icing conditions to larger MVD.
– First IRT test campaign conducted in February 2020 with results reported in AIAA 



Papers 2021-2678 and 2021-2679.

Objective and Approach



Objective
• Investigate the effect of large MVD conditions on the ice accretion a 



large-scale, swept wing.
• Extend the February 2020 database and repeat test conditions.
Approach
• Conduct ice accretion testing on full-chord Midspan model section of 



CRM65 wing in the NASA IRT.
• Use icing scaling parameters to compare small and large MVD cloud 



conditions.
• Measure ice geometry with 3D scanning technique and compute ice 



volumes.
• Measure ice mass.

Experimental Methodology



• Ice accretion testing was conducted using full-chord version of Midspan 
hybrid model based upon CRM65 wing.



• Full-chord model streamwise chord length = 136 inches (~3.5 m).



• Companion paper by Lee et al.* compares ice accretion collected on the 
hybrid and full-chord models under identical icing conditions.



*Lee, S., and Broeren, A.P., “Additional Comparison of Ice Shapes on Full-Chord and Truncated Swept Wing 
Models from January 2022,” SAE International Icing Conference, 20-22 June 2023, Submitted for Publication.

Experimental Methodology



• Icing tests were performed in the NASA IRT with small and large MVD 
clouds.



• IRT test section is 6 ft high by 9 ft wide by 20 ft long.
• Model was installed vertically from floor to ceiling.

Experimental Methodology



• Ice accretion was documented with photographs and 3D scans.
• A section of ice was removed and weighed.
• 3D scan data were post-processed to provide the Maximum Combined 



Cross Section (MCCS) and ice volume.

Summary



• Ice shape repeatability between February 2020 and January 2022:
– Better repeatability for MVD = 25 μm compared to MVD = 110 μm.
– Variations in ice mass and scanned iced volume for repeat conditions was approximately 



50% lower than stated uncertainty in IRT cloud calibration.
• Effect of attachment line freezing fraction on ice mass and volume:



– Ice mass increased with no reaching a peak near 0.4, followed by a decrease for MVD = 
25 μm and a plateau for MVD = 100 μm.



– The “total density” or “void fraction” values were in the range of 240 to 455 kg/m3—
consistent with previously reported values.



• Effect of MVD from 50 to 230 μm at fixed no and βoAc
– Main ice shape similar, more ice downstream at larger MVD, mostly on lower surface.
– Ice mass and volume increased with MVD—the “total density” or “void fraction” was 



approximately constant.
