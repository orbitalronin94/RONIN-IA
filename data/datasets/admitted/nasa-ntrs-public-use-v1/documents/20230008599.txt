Background
</p>
<p>• Minimum “reliably” detectable flaw size is starting point for 
fatigue and fracture damage tolerance analysis for 
fracture critical hardware
</p>
<p>• NASA requirement (and others) is that NDE provide 90/95 
Probability of Detection (POD)
</p>
<p>• One (of many) factors that can affect NDE POD is 
inspector-inspector variability
</p>
<p>• NASA addresses inspector variability by defining two 
types of NDE inspections for fracture critical applications
– Standard NDE
</p>
<p>– Special NDE
</p>
<p>This briefing is for status only and does not represent complete engineering data analysis 3</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Bill Prosser
</p>
<p>June 27, 2023
NASA Special NDE
</p>
<p>• Anything other than Standard NDE 
– Different methods than those defined as Standard
</p>
<p>– Flaw sizes smaller than designated at Standard
</p>
<p>– Materials/geometries not “similar” to that tested for Standard
</p>
<p>• Individual inspector(s) to perform inspection must 
demonstrate 90/95 POD capability
– Costly to test inspectors to demonstrate capability
</p>
<p>– Inspectors must periodically be retested to ensure capability is 
maintained
</p>
<p>• However, necessary in many situations when Standard NDE 
is not applicable
</p>
<p>This briefing is for status only and does not represent complete engineering data analysis 4</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Bill Prosser
</p>
<p>June 27, 2023
NASA Standard NDE
</p>
<p>• Conservative flaw sizes such that any qualified inspector 
“should” provide the 90/95 capability
– Individual inspector 90/95 POD demonstration not required
</p>
<p>– 5 specific NDE methods (PT, MP, UT, RT, and ET)
</p>
<p>– Documented in Tables in NASA Standard 5009
</p>
<p>• Standard NDE flaw sizes based (in part) on seminal NDE 
capability test program in early 1970’s performed by 
Space Shuttle Program (Bishop Study)
– Large number of flaws and multiple inspectors from multiple 
</p>
<p>companies
</p>
<p>– Intent was to select flaw size such that 95% of inspectors provided 
90/95 POD or better (i.e., 90/95/95 POD)
</p>
<p>This briefing is for status only and does not represent complete engineering data analysis 5</p>

Motivation for Current Study
</p>
<p>• NASA Standard NDE flaw sizes used by NASA (and others) 
for over 50 years
</p>
<p>• However, methodology to perform/analyze a Standard NDE 
demonstration never documented in NASA requirements
– Needed to include new NDE methods (e.g., digital radiography) and 
</p>
<p>reassess existing methods in light of technology improvements
</p>
<p>– Original methodology documented in test reports not consistent with 
more modern POD analysis methods (e.g., Mil-HDBK-1823)
</p>
<p>• Initial intent was to develop “new” Standard NDE 
methodology, baseline against historical data to show 
consistency, and document in NASA requirements
– Did not exactly go as planned!
</p>

<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Bill Prosser
</p>
<p>June 27, 2023
Reanalysis of 1970’s (Bishop) POD Data
</p>
<p>• Multiple issues 
– Same flaw sets used for all NDE methods – led to inadequate flaw 
</p>
<p>distributions to reliably estimate POD for some methods
</p>
<p>– Cracks etched even though etching only required for PT – may lead to 
nonconservative flaw estimates for methods like RT
</p>
<p>– Qualitative treatment of crack aspect ratio
</p>
<p>– Evolution of Standard flaw sizes to 5009 tables not well documented
• Some flaw sizes based on qualitative engineering assessment or 
</p>
<p>unreferenced additional POD studies
</p>
<p>– Study did not include blank specimens (i.e., without flaws) to estimate 
probability of false calls
</p>
<p>– Sorted Group Ascent Method (SGAM) used for original study is 
nonconservative relative to modern (1823) POD analysis methods
</p>
<p>8</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Bill Prosser
</p>
<p>June 27, 2023
SGAM Issue and Impacts
</p>
<p>• “Groups” NDE results by flaw size and performs statistical 
analysis to determine POD of flaw size group
– Inherent requirement is flaw sizes are constant within a group
</p>
<p>– Insufficient numbers of similar size flaws in original testing results in 
violation of assumption of constant flaw size in groups
</p>
<p>• Results in estimated a90/95/95 flaw sizes being 
nonconservative for UT, EC, RT, and PT – inspector 
coverage more like 50% (i.e., average)
</p>
<p>• For penetrant, miscalculated square root resulted in 
inspector coverage much closer to desired 95% - two 
wrongs make a right!
</p>

Summary of Historical Data Review
</p>
<p>• No clear traceable link to all entries in 5009’s 
Standard NDE table was found
– Table entries are a combination of POD studies and expert 
</p>
<p>judgement/experience
</p>
<p>• 5009’s Standard NDE flaw sizes are not consistently 
representative of 95% inspector coverage, and the 
deviation varies by technique and aspect ratio
</p>
<p>• 5009’s Standard NDE table has served its purpose 
well, and we recommend not updating the flaw sizes 
in the table, only clarifying their source and 
interpretation in 5009’s revision
</p>

<p>June 27, 2023
New Standard NDE Method Requirement
</p>
<p>• Standard NDE POD study shall consist of a MIL-HDBK-
1823A compliant POD study of a minimum of 10 inspectors 
that form a representative sample of inspectors
</p>
<p>• Individual inspector analyses shall be performed in 
accordance with MIL-HDBK-1823A and estimated a90/95 
flaw sizes for the individual inspectors shall be reported
</p>
<p>• Individual inspector POF shall be reported and are 
recommended to not exceed 1% POF with 50% confidence
</p>
<p>• Standard NDE flaw size shall be estimated as a function of 
the average and standard deviation of individual inspector 
a90/95 flaw sizes, and it shall represent the flaw size that 
90% of inspectors are expected to demonstrate at least 
90/95 detection capability
</p>

<p>June 27, 2023
Summary Comments
</p>
<p>• Historical review of original NASA Standard NDE POD study identified a 
number of issues and lessons learned
</p>
<p>• Resulted in a new methodology to update existing Standard NDE flaw 
sizes and add new NDE methods
</p>
<p>• Leverages lessons learned from Space Shuttle Program studies, NDE 
literature, and augments MIL-HDBK-1823A
</p>
<p>• Unified approach of a POD study through design, execution, analysis, 
and documentation
</p>
<p>• Intuitive to NDE practitioners and fracture analysts
</p>
<p>• First documented NASA Standard NDE approach
</p>
<p>• Addresses a long-standing gap in the Standard NDE body of knowledge, 
and it undergirds the continued usage of Standard NDE flaw sizes for 
NASA systems</p>
