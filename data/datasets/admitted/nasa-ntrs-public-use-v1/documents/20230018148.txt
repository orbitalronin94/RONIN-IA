Artemis I launch debris hazards routinely assessed


Two expected debris concerns identified and cleared


Tools and processes developed to assess Day-of-Launch (DOL) unexpected debris


Debris hazards increased due to tests and environments leading up to launch


Green Run, Wet Dress Rehearsals, Tanking Test, Hurricanes, Launch Attempts

Artemis I Launch Debris Overview

Expected Debris Assessments





SLS vehicle materials are less vulnerable to debris impacts than Shuttle materials; however,


Longer vehicle results in higher impact energies on aft components


Liquid engine locations closer to Booster nozzles increase risk of throat plug impacts


Expected debris includes thermal materials, ice, nozzle plug materials and other minor miscellaneous materials


Assessments of expected debris consist of 


Debris transport analyses


Impact assessments


Risk assessments for identified concerns


Identified issues reviewed by SLS Program for acceptance or mitigation


3











Two Expected Debris Concerns Accepted as Low Risk


Core Stage thermal protection system materials identified as concerns to the Booster Aft Separation Motor Single Fairing


Probabilities assessed for debris release, impact, and damage


Booster Throat Plug foam identified as concern to the liquid engine nozzles


Complex debris transport mechanism examined


Impact tests onto ‘waterfall’ performed for availability for transport


Fragment distribution assessed from test data 


Probability of damage calculated, given availability and transport to the nozzle surfaces


SLS Program determined the risks were low and acceptable

4








Day-of-Launch (DOL) Debris Process


Evaluating launch debris as a hazard assessment resulted in limited known design thresholds, requiring new process for DOL support


Developed DOL Launch Debris Process, coordinated with SLS Elements, Cross Programs, and imagery teams and processes


Debris Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) 


review scans to identify potential debris Items of Interest (IOIs)


perform needed assessments supporting risk evaluations, by Program management and mission management teams


DOL Debris Transport and Impact Assessment Tools developed


IOI evaluations coordinated over voice loops across four NASA Centers and several contractor sites


DOL Process exercised for multiple unexpected debris sources during simulations, tests, and launch attempts


Only one minor unexpected debris item required assessment on DOL





5








Debris Activities During Pre-Launch Tests


Core Stage Green Run Wet Dress Rehearsal and Hot Fire Tests


Four cycles of cryogenic loading/unloading, two hot-fire cycles, and a year of environment exposure


Cracks developed in TPS foam and were repaired as needed


Integrated Vehicle Wet Dress Rehearsals (WDR) 1 through 4


After Rollout and WDRs debris was observed and evaluated: foreign object debris, minor vehicle TPS damage, expected ice accumulation (potential debris), and unexpected tower debris


Unexpected items observed were corrected before launch


Launch Attempts 1 and 2 


Noted unexpected items on the tower, unexpected ice growth on the Core Stage, and TPS foam cracks at LH2/IT flange


All evaluated with DOL process or removed before launch





Artemis I Core Stage and Stennis B-Stand

Tanking Test Through Launch


Tanking Test


All cryogenic tanks successfully filled


Unexpected tower debris evaluated


Rollback for Hurricane Ian


Potential tower debris removed, and repairs completed in VAB


Weathering Hurricane Nicole


Imagery scans and walkdowns assured minimal unexpected debris


Minor tower softgoods damage mitigated to prevent debris release


One vehicle thermal material loosened and required assessment


Artemis I Launch, November 16, 2022


Areas of ice growth evaluated, confirmed within expectations


Several known TPS cracks monitored, previously assessed for risk


One minor TPS material assessed and cleared just before launch


7








Pre-Launch Risk Assessments


Planned and unplanned tests and processes increased the risk of debris release


Loads induced by cryogenic tanking cycles, from rollbacks, and from unexpected weather events resulted in two notable thermal materials damaged 





Foam cracks on the Core Stage indicated debris releases could be larger than previously defined for expected debris, and noted as Items of Concern (IOC)


Repair of most cracks was completed during roll-back to the VAB before launch


Remaining cracks were evaluated, and risk assessments completed


Thermal materials on Orion hardware were observed loosened in prelaunch events


Unexpected loose material from this location was evaluated and noted as an IOC


The loose materials were removed during roll-back to the VAB before launch


Remaining material was assessed as potential debris and risks evaluated





Risk assessments were completed and accepted by the SLS Program and mission management before launch





8











Conclusions


An integrated approach to DOL debris assessment across NASA SLS, Cross Program, and contractor organizations led to successful and efficient evaluations of unexpected launch debris and a successful Artemis I launch


Awareness of potential launch debris issues has improved, and mitigation activities more accomplished as a result of the activities leading up to launch


The many evaluations of expected and unexpected launch debris for Artemis I provide a sound basis for support to Artemis II and beyond
