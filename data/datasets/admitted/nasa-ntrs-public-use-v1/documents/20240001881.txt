Project Goal



 
#1- Exo-atmospheric deployment to an entry configuration of the 1m-class ADEPT.
 
Less than fully locked condition resulting in shape with less than 70-degree forebody cone angle.
 
Full, locked deployment before reaching 80 km altitude on descent, to 70-degree fore body cone angle achieving 6x greater drag area.



 
#2- Aerodynamic stability without active control of the 1m-class ADEPT in a flight configuration.
 
Does not tumble prior to M=0.8 while decelerating from peak Mach # (when Mach number is decreasing after passing through peak Mach number).
 
ADEPT does not tumble* before ground impact; Sign of pitch damping coefficient (Cmq) is determined; FF-CFD simulation tool is validated

 d. Sign of pitch damping sum

SR-1 Development Campaign Highlights


AERODYNAMICS DATABASE DEVELOPMENT


FREE-FLIGHT DYNAMICS TESTING


IMPACT TESTING


Drop testing enabled testing of various impact attenuators, characterization of impact load and demonstrated survivability of the on-board memory cards and battery design robustness.





Impact testing also enabled battery safety procedures to be rehearsed in the event of severe damage to the rechargeable lithium-ion batteries upon impact.








Assessed the dynamic behavior of ADEPT SR-1 at sub-sonic speeds, and aided in the selection of center of mass location to maximize likelihood of nose first impact. Testing performed in the Langley Vertical Spin Tunnel.








Static and dynamic aerodatabase predictions were used to perform preflight trajectory analysis of vehicle performance and helped inform risk and safety management.

Post-Flight Data Analysis


ADEPT SR-1 satisfied both Key Performance Parameters and met 5 out 6 Mission Success Criteria





Trajectory reconstruction simulated at 100 Hz using LV IMU, AVA IMU, AVA Magnetometer, radar tracking and atmospheric models. 





Total angle of attack remains below stability threshold of 20 degrees through M=0.4. 





The spin rate increase (from 44 deg/s to 370 deg/s) through supersonic deceleration was unexpected. Post flight analysis was unable to identify clear root cause.

GTA Results Summary & Findings


48


Results Summary:


GTA demonstrated reliable and repeatable ADEPT deployment


Only one unsuccessful deployment (caused by operator error – latch not reset)


Achieved fabric tension level requirement of >= 100 lbf/in at mid-gore location


Fabric showed no significant degradation from the repeated stow-deploy cycles


Demonstrated general manufacturability of an ADEPT aeroshell


Fabric to rib interface maintained tension & was mostly adequate – (see findings)


ADEPT GTA formed an acceptable deployed aeroshell shape (except possibly at trailing edge)


Findings / Lessons Learned:


Good alignment and structural compliance allowed for successful deployments despite the mechanism being over-constrained


Reduce over-constraints where practical


Fabric in-plane stiffness is very high (actually dominated over the structure lateral stiffness)


Need to make allowances for the structure to be adjusted to the fabric


Recommend that the fabric be allowed to rest on top of the ribs (not continuous attachment)


Allow some adjustability in final rib deployment angle to accommodate the as-built cone angle of the carbon fabric skirt (don’t expect to hit an exact rib deploy angle & desired tension level at the same time  deploy to the angle that provides the proper tension)


Trailing edge treatment (rib tip radius with single fabric layer stretched between) was unsatisfactory  recommend a different trailing edge design for a better outboard shoulder
