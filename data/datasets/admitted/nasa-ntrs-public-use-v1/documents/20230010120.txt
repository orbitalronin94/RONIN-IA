Motivation



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



- In supersonic flow, the presence of water vapor can impact data quality 
(if not characterized)
- “Condensation shocks”
- Mach number & total pressure decrease
- Static pressure & total temperature increase



- Mike Mills (AEDC) and Max Amaya (NASA ARC) have presented on this 
topic multiple times 



- As humidity-assessment instruments and methods for accounting for 
humidity in our tunnels were compared amongst several WTCWG 
members, it’s become apparent that there are subtle differences to 
discuss.

8x6 SWT



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



- Approximately < 50 feet of ¼” tubing between sample and sensor



- Two (2) Vaisala DMT143 
- Supposed to be insensitive to flow rate
- Anecdotally, dew point values decrease rapidly when the compressor 



starts up, even at relatively low Mach numbers 



- No pressure or flow rate measurements made on sample line @ 
hygrometer 
- Plan to recommend both measurements to be added as potential 



improvements, but not necessary.











8x6 SWT



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



- Historical rule of thumb: dew point < -15oF
- Acceptable at all Mach numbers



- 55oF of supercooling 
- Depending on the situation, low dew points 



may not be achievable for long at highest 
speeds, default to -15oF rule.



- Dry air typically turned “on” at Mach 1.4, 
but this can be delayed until data needs to 
be acquired or model visibility diminishes

10x10 SWT



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



- Larger diameter pipe (~2”) brings 
air sample from tunnel air heater 
station in settling chamber to 
reservoir tank (“air chamber”) 
- Air re-enters tunnel near end of 



contraction, upstream of flexwall.
- Approx. 50-75 feet of pipe/tubing 



between sample and hygrometers
- Small sample lines (~1/8”) 



transport sample from reservoir 
- Sample either pushed via tunnel 



pressure or pulled w/ vacuum pumps
- 0.5 L/min flow rate set











10x10 SWT



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



- Two (2) Stephens Analytical 
MicroView On-Line Hygrometers 
- Calibrated -60 to 0oC 



- GE Optica Hygrometer used as a 
check on the other two units.



- Dewpoint pressure-corrected to 
barometric pressure measurement
- Local total pressure measured at 



each hygrometer 



- Rule of thumb for testing: 
pressure-corrected dewpt < -15oF 











10x10 SWT



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



- Question: How accurate do we need our hygrometer measurement to be?
- 8x6 and 9x15 are using Vaisala’s and are happy with their simplicity, 



reliability, and ease of use. 
- Would it make sense for 10x10 to adopt these units, as well?



- We can assess what an error in dewpoint measurement would do to the 
calibrated Mach number in 10x10 using existing calibration relationships.











10x10 SWT



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



At -15oF & Mach 3 in 10x10 SWT:
- Stephens Analytical 



Hygrometer 
- 2σ ~= 1.2oC = 2.16oF 
- ~0.0012 Mach error



- A Vaisala dewpoint sensor 
- +/- 2.0oC = 3.6oF 
- ~0.0020 Mach error



The difference in calculated 
Mach number seems small but 
will need to be compared to 
other contribution sources.











Effect of SVP Model Choice



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



- There are several choices of Saturation Vapor Pressure (SVP) model 
which we can use when computing the humidity levels.
- Goff-Gratch (1945)
- Hyland & Wexler (1983)
- Buck (1996)
- Enhanced Buck (1981), etc.



- Does it matter which one we choose?



- Using simplified 1-D analysis1 with released latent heat of condensation2



under expected 10x10 SWT conditions:



1 Effects of Air Humidity in Supersonic Wind Tunnels, J. Lukasiewicz & J. K. Royle - ARC 2563 (1953)
2 Estimating Water Vapor Condensation Effects for Transonic and Supersonic Flow Fields, Robinson C. E. & Bauer R. C. (1985)

Effect of SVP Model Choice



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



At -15oF
- Mach number delta if using Buck 



Enhanced vs. Goff-Gratch: 
- ~0.0003 Mach (Re = 0.5e6/ft)



- Freestream total pressure delta:
- ~0.004 psi (Re = 2.5e6/ft) 



Seemingly insignificant difference in 
Mach number and total pressure 
(depends on the user/facility), would 
suggest using Buck Enhanced (1981):
- Relatively modern
- Enhancement factors to account for 



pressure levels 
- Definitely debatable, open to suggestions!











Conclusions & Future Work



National Aeronautics and Space Administration



- Choice of SVP Model doesn’t make a huge difference if properly used 
(i.e., frost-point vs. dewpoint, enhancement factors used properly, etc.)



- Future work:
- Add PT & flow rate sensors to 8x6 SWT Vaisala lines



- Add calculations for pressure-corrected dewpoint in 8x6 & 10x10 SWT 
(corrected to tunnel total pressure & corrected to atmosphere)



- Perform MUA following 10x10 full characterization (2025) to 
determine whether complex dewpoint system could be simplified.
