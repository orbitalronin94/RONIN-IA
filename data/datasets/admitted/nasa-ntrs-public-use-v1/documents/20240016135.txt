<div class="slide-content"><p>What is differential drag?</p>
<p>Propagation assumes a particular drag area.</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide2_rId3"></div>
<p>Changing a satellite’s drag has a net propulsive effect!</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide2_rId3"></div>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide2_rId3"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide2_rId3"></div>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Why does CARA Analysis need a decision aid?</p>
<p>CARA’s missions are seeing more and more conjunctions.</p>
<p>Missions without propulsion want to explore their options for collision risk mitigation.</p>
<p>(Missions with propulsion want to explore their options!)</p>
<p></p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide3_rId3"></div>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<p>Clark &amp; Highsmith | ‹#›</p>
<p></p>
<div class="slide-content"><p>The Δ-B-Cs of differential drag</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide4_rId3"></div>
<p>To compare satellites consistently, we use delta-BC:</p>
<p>Delta-BC = </p>
<p>So, Delta-BC of 2  3x drag area</p>
<p>Can be negative, to represent decreasing drag area.</p>
<p>Can be time-averaged to encompass duty cycles, etc.</p>
<p>BC: Ballistic Coefficient = (area/mass)</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<p>Clark &amp; Highsmith | ‹#›</p>
<p></p>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Can my satellite use differential drag?</p>
<p>~4,300 historical conjunctions with Pc &gt; 1E-4 at TCA-3 days.</p>
<p>Using CARA’s Maneuver Trade Space (MTS) tool, propagate with different delta-BCs.  Final Pc &lt; 3E-6?</p>
<p>There seems to be a clear breakpoint in the 500-600 km range, around delta-BC of 2.</p>
<p>Zooming in, 560 km is (close to) the 50% point.</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide5_rId3"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide5_rId4"></div>
<p></p>
<p>Pc: Probability of collision</p>
<p>TCA: Time of Closest Approach</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<p>Clark &amp; Highsmith | ‹#›</p>
<p></p>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Validation</p>
<p>To validate MTS’s analysis, we compared to another NASA mission that uses drag for risk mitigation.  (Delta-BC = 2.2, perigee ~520 km, ~48-hr maneuvers)</p>
<p>In most cases: qualitative agreement, tending conservative</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide6_rId2"></div>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<p>Clark &amp; Highsmith | ‹#›</p>
<p></p>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Validation</p>
<p>Of the 16 conjunctions we identified, 11 were remediated, which is consistent with expectations!</p>
<p>The one “optimistic” error was from a plan that was superseded by a shorter one (true negative).</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Application – Mission Design Support</p>
<p>A mission with very large solar panels asked us to evaluate differential-drag for risk mitigation.</p>
<p>Orbit not yet fixed, 500-700 km</p>
<p>Unfortunately, their mission constraints restrict their time-averaged delta-BC.</p>
<p></p>

<p>Clark &amp; Highsmith | ‹#›</p>
<p></p>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Other considerations</p>
<p>Positive vs. negative delta-BC</p>
<p>Usually more ‘upside’ going positive, but if the mission doesn’t have much delta-BC and has to make the most of it, CARA can advise them how to best reduce collision risk.</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide9_rId2"></div>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<p>Clark &amp; Highsmith | ‹#›</p>
<p></p>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusion</p>
<p>A mission:</p>
<p>Perigee below ~560 km </p>
<p>Able to triple its drag area </p>
<p>For 3 days straight</p>
<p>(or a greater factor for less time)</p>
<p>Can count on differential drag to remediate many high-interest conjunction events.</p>
<p></p>
<p>This is a very high bar to clear!  Much higher than formation maintenance, etc.</p>
<p>CARA is exploring other aspects of differential drag:</p>
<p>What efficacy is ‘worth pursuing’ vs. acceptable as a substitute for propulsion?</p>
<p>What counts as remediation?</p>
</div>
