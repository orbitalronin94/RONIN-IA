What is differential drag?


Propagation assumes a particular drag area.





Changing a satellite’s drag has a net propulsive effect!


































Clark & Highsmith | ‹#›






Why does CARA Analysis need a decision aid?


CARA’s missions are seeing more and more conjunctions.


Missions without propulsion want to explore their options for collision risk mitigation.


(Missions with propulsion want to explore their options!)

Can my satellite use differential drag?


~4,300 historical conjunctions with Pc > 1E-4 at TCA-3 days.


Using CARA’s Maneuver Trade Space (MTS) tool, propagate with different delta-BCs. Final Pc < 3E-6?


There seems to be a clear breakpoint in the 500-600 km range, around delta-BC of 2.


Zooming in, 560 km is (close to) the 50% point.











Pc: Probability of collision


TCA: Time of Closest Approach

Validation


To validate MTS’s analysis, we compared to another NASA mission that uses drag for risk mitigation. (Delta-BC = 2.2, perigee ~520 km, ~48-hr maneuvers)


In most cases: qualitative agreement, tending conservative

Clark & Highsmith | ‹#›






Other considerations


Positive vs. negative delta-BC


Usually more ‘upside’ going positive, but if the mission doesn’t have much delta-BC and has to make the most of it, CARA can advise them how to best reduce collision risk.










Clark & Highsmith | ‹#›






Conclusion


A mission:


Perigee below ~560 km 


Able to triple its drag area 


For 3 days straight


(or a greater factor for less time)


Can count on differential drag to remediate many high-interest conjunction events.





This is a very high bar to clear! Much higher than formation maintenance, etc.


CARA is exploring other aspects of differential drag:


What efficacy is ‘worth pursuing’ vs. acceptable as a substitute for propulsion?


What counts as remediation?
