Compressor Overview



• CO2 from the CDRA/4BCO2 is at a very low pressure, but Sabatier 
reactor requires it to be at a higher pressure
• Currently using mechanical compressor, but alternative technologies 



like Air-Cooled Temperature Swing Adsorption Compressor (AC-TSAC) 
might be better
• Lower mass/power requirements, less noise during operation, no rotating 



parts (less frequent maintenance/replacement), decreased fabrication cost 
and lead time

AC-TSAC Overview
• Zeolite pellets adsorb low pressure CO2 received from CDRA at low temperature and 



releases it at higher pressure at high temperature
• Sorbent takes ~25 minutes to heat up to 250°C, stays at 250°C for ~75 minutes, and takes 



~60 minutes to cool to room temperature
• Two beds is in heating/adsorption phase while the other is in cooling/production phase 



and then they switch to ensure CO2 is constantly being supplied to Sabatier reactor

Design Trade: Internal vs. External Heaters
• Internal resistive heaters are a potential point of 



failure (many wires going into the bed); complexity 
may be reduced with external heaters
• Using a simplified model (no fins), applied the same 



power internally and externally and compared 
sorbent heating rate and uniformity
• Using external heaters rather than internal not only 



reduces complexity but also has potential to improve 
temperature uniformity of the sorbent

Design Trade: Aluminum vs. Vapor Chamber



• What are vapor chambers?
• Heat pipes that efficiently spread heat in multiple 



directions
• Heat is applied to one end of the vapor chamber 



and the small amount of liquid trapped in the 
chamber evaporates
• Vapor flows throughout chamber and heats it, 



then condenses once it reaches cooler areas
• Liquid flows back to the heat source via capillary 



actions and cycle repeats
• Effective thermal conductivities can be 10,000-



100,000 W/mK

• Vapor chamber modeling
• For this paper, vapor chambers were modeled with material properties of aluminum

• Higher-fidelity modeling should be conducted to assess functionality

Design Trade: Number of Segments



• Increase in number of compartments
• Improves temperature uniformity due to less distance between heat spreading 



elements (e.g., aluminum or vapor chambers)
• Decreases heat rate due to increased thermal mass
• Decreases amount of sorbent that can fit within a given volume and thus the 



amount of CO2 that can be removed

Conclusions and Future Work



• Conclusions
• Created a thermal model of the AC-TSAC and validated it against test data
• Examined various model parameters to determine impact on future AC-TSAC 



designs
• External heaters should be considered to reduce system complexity and failure potential
• Increased thermal conductivity of vapor chambers improves temperature uniformity of 



sorbent
• Increased sorbent thermal conductivity should continue to be a focus of development



• Future work
• Continue validating current thermal model, accounting for heat loss 



mechanisms
• Model steady-state and cooling phases of the cycle
• Include pressurization at high temperature in model
