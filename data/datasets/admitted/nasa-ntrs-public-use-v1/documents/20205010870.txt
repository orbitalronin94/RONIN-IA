The PIAA Coronagraph uses a set of two aspheric mirrors to reshape
the evenly illuminated telescope aperture and generate a smooth
apodization that concentrates light in the center and tapers off
towards the edges. This principle is illustrated in Figure 2 where a
circular telescope’s PSF’s Airy pattern is removed by the PIAA
apodization. PIAA features two main advantages: the apodization is
lossless and can be used for aggressive inner-working angle (IWA)
designs. PIAA mirrors have been successfully manufactured by Axsys,
Tinsley, and NuTek with very small residual aberrations (<5 nm RMS).
Finally, our approach is to use circularly symmetric PIAA mirrors and
the DMs to eliminate diffraction due to telescope segmentation and
spiders. The central obstruction of LUVOIR-A requires a focal plane
mask with a complex focal plane.

The Large UV/Optical/IR Surveyor (LUVOIR) [1] is a next-generation
segmented telescope with an aperture ranging in circumscribed
diameter from 8-15 m. LUVOIR is planned to survey star systems
within 30 parsecs through direct imaging to detect and characterize
habitable exoplanets.

Due to its large size, LUVOIR presents technical challenges for the
direct imaging of exoplanets. First, LUVOIR will be a segmented
telescope which introduces additional diffraction challenges that
must be accurately controlled. Additionally, the LUVOIR-A design is
on-axis with a significant obstruction due to its secondary that must
be controlled using a coronagraph. LUVOIR-B will be an off-axis
telescope with segmentation. Finally, LUVOIR’s large diameter
increases its sensitivity to nearby stars – thus coronagraph designs
must be robust to tip/tilt sensitivity to efficiently survey nearby stars.

Here, we present a range of high-efficiency coronagraph designs
based on the Phase-Induced Amplitude Apodization (PIAA)
coronagraph [2]. First we present hybrid a PIAA-Vortex design that
uses a focal plane mask with an achromatic-vortex charge 6 that in
combination with the wavefront-control-system control both the on-
axis obstruction and the segmentation of the LUVOIR-A pupil. We also
present classical PIAA designs with an opaque focal plane mask for
the off-axis LUVOIR-B architecture optimized for robustness to tip/tilt
sensitivity. These designs are optimized for robustness to tip/tilt
sensitivity.

LUVOIR-B Classical PIAA Design
The hybrid PIAA-Vortex design for LUVOIR-A uses an optimized,
complex focal plane mask to deal with the enhanced diffraction rings
from the secondary obstruction. The focal plane mask is a vortex
charge 6 achromatic mask that provides robustness to tip/tilt and
other low-order aberrations, with a design enabled by the PIAA-
Vortex.

The LUVOIR-B features only segmentation so a classical PIAA design
with an opaque focal plane mask can be used. The focal plane mask
can be swapped with larger diameters to decrease the sensitivity to
tip/tilt as well as other low-order modes [5].

CONCLUSION

We have presented high-efficiency PIAA designs for both the LUVOIR-A
obstructed, segmented aperture and for the LUVOIR-B segmented
aperture. Both of these designs are being implemented in the SCDA
end-to-end simulator to demonstrate sensitivity to realistic thermal-
induced and line-of-sight segmentation errors.
