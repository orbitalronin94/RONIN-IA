<p>Hexagonal Boron Nitride as an Insulation Material
</p>
<p>• NASA Glenn Research 
Center (GRC) has spent 20 
years specializing in boron 
nitride-based research.
</p>
<p>• Applied widely in many 
applications commercial and 
industrial.

<p>– Aerospace and space 
components
</p>
<p>– Radiation Shielding
– Energy Storage
– Lubricants
– Thermoelectrics
– Cosmetics
– Paints
– Insulation materials
– Microelectronics

• Insulation
</p>

</p>

– Similar to graphite, hard and

</p>

– Hydrophobic
– Chemically stable
– Stable above 1000 oC in air
</p>
<p>• Lightweight

<p>Titania is Similar but More Interesting
• Titania has similarities to alumina
</p>
<p>– Similar sintering temperature
– Hot pressable and formable
– Lower Thermal Conductivity, but close (~7-10 W/m·K)
</p>
<p>Two major crystal forms, anatase and rutile
• Anatase
</p>
<p>– Lower density (3.78) lower than alumina
– Lower dielectric constant ~14/1101
</p>
<p>– Interesting semiconduction/ion conductivity properties, bandgap of 3.4 eV2
</p>
<p>– Longer e-hole pair lifetime can promote chemistry (better photocatalyst)
</p>
<p>• Rutile
– Denser form (4.23) higher than alumina
– More stable form
– Higher dielectric constant ~89/1701, bandgap of 3.0 eV2
</p>
<p>– Bright white, used in paints
</p>
<p>Preferentially makes small nanoparticles upon oxidation
– Allows for targeted final product control
</p>

<p>Method optimization
• TiCl3 is hard to work with
</p>
<p>– Rapidly reacts with wet air
– Low boiling point/sublimation
– Liquid at room temperature
– Likely to form oxide before removal or 
</p>
<p>hydration
</p>
<p>• Titanium tetraisopropoxide (TTIP)
– Reacts slower with air
– High boiling point (close to AlCl3)
– Can be diluted with isopropanol for optimal 
</p>
<p>coating
– Larger molecule
– Liquid at room temperature
</p>
