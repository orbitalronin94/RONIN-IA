Titania is Similar but More Interesting
• Titania has similarities to alumina



– Similar sintering temperature
– Hot pressable and formable
– Lower Thermal Conductivity, but close (~7-10 W/m·K)



Two major crystal forms, anatase and rutile
• Anatase



– Lower density (3.78) lower than alumina
– Lower dielectric constant ~14/1101



– Interesting semiconduction/ion conductivity properties, bandgap of 3.4 eV2



– Longer e-hole pair lifetime can promote chemistry (better photocatalyst)



• Rutile
– Denser form (4.23) higher than alumina
– More stable form
– Higher dielectric constant ~89/1701, bandgap of 3.0 eV2



– Bright white, used in paints



Preferentially makes small nanoparticles upon oxidation
– Allows for targeted final product control

Titania Precursors are More Complex

Method optimization
• TiCl3 is hard to work with



– Rapidly reacts with wet air
– Low boiling point/sublimation
– Liquid at room temperature
– Likely to form oxide before removal or 



hydration



• Titanium tetraisopropoxide (TTIP)
– Reacts slower with air
– High boiling point (close to AlCl3)
– Can be diluted with isopropanol for optimal 



coating
– Larger molecule
– Liquid at room temperature
