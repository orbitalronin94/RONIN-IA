<p>4Theory of Metallized Plastic Current Collectors
</p>
<p>• Internal short defect is fed rapidly 
</p>
<p>through solid metal (Al, Cu) film current 
</p>
<p>collectors
</p>
<p>• Polymer separator thermally breakdowns 
</p>
<p>and shrinks away creating higher internal 
</p>
<p>short risk between anode and cathode
</p>
<p>• Plastic substrate of current collector thermally 
</p>
<p>breaks down and isolates the electrochemically 
</p>
<p>active materials from the defect within 
</p>
<p>milliseconds
</p>

<p>As the nail compresses the can wall, the outer wind is locally compressed and the cathode PCC and NMC

<p>darkens (densifies) with light Al stripe absent while gap forms “inside” adjacent Cu foil. In the next frames,

<p>that 1st PCC area loses density. The pattern repeats for the 2nd and successive winds.

<p>We’re seeing the nail push NMC through the separator and into the graphite causing localized internal 
</p>
<p>short circuit (ISC) hot spot. This causes Al PCC to vaporize as the electrode densifies and then looses 
</p>
<p>density along with adjacent graphite.

<p>Probable PCC Mechanisms for Isolating Shorts
</p>
<p>• Nail front crushes outer winds into 
inner winds
– Causes anode/cathode contact
</p>
<p>– Active materials at nail interface 
discharge, get hot, fluidize, and 
generate a bit of gases
</p>
<p>– Vaporizing Al PCC near shorted 
materials
</p>
<p>– Active materials involved in short are 
electrically stranded from rest of JR
</p>
<p>• Nail edge stretches and cuts the 
polymer film
– NMC delaminates from stretched and 
</p>
<p>vaporized PCC at nail interface
</p>
<p>– NMC material at nail interface is 
sheared from JR NMC and stranded
</p>
<p>Stretched PCC cracks 
</p>
<p>NMC coating
Heat of crushed/shorted active 
</p>
<p>materials vaporizes the PCC
</p>
<p>Nail cuts, cracks, and shorts 
</p>
<p>active materials and vaporizes 
</p>
<p>PCC which strands NMC</p>
