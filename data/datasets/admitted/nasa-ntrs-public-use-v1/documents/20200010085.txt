An Initial Assessment of SAGE III/ISS Temperature and Pressure Retrievals
Michael Pitts, Larry Thomason, David Flittner, and Robert Damadeo
NASA Langley Research Center, Hampton, Virginia USA

Abstract
The Stratospheric Aerosol and Gas Experiment (SAGE) III instrument was deployed to the International Space
Station (ISS) in February 2017. SAGE III/ISS is the latest in the NASA lineage of solar occultation satellite
instruments designed to monitor the vertical distribution of aerosol, ozone and other trace gases in the upper
atmosphere. Originally built in the early 2000s, the SAGE III/ISS instrument required extensive testing and
refurbishment prior to deliver to ISS, including the replacement of the neutral density filter that has been
associated with some instrument response issues during the METEOR/3M mission. In particular, METEOR/3M
measurements of the oxygen A-band absorption feature near 762 nm that were to be utilized to retrieve profiles
of temperature and pressure were adversely affected by this instrument issue. Preliminary examination of the
SAGE III/ISS oxygen A-band spectra indicate that the measurements are of higher fidelity than from the
METEOR/3M instrument, which may permit successful retrieval of science quality temperature and pressure
products. In this paper, we will provide an overview of the temperature and pressure retrieval algorithm,
present preliminary analyses of the SAGE III/ISS oxygen A-band measurements, and an initial assessment of
the quality of the temperature and pressure retrievals.

During each sunrise and sunset encountered by the
orbiting spacecraft, the SAGE III instrument measures
the attenuated solar radiation through the Earth’s limb,
I(z,l), as a function of altitude and wavelength
• The exo-atmospheric solar radiation, Io(z,l), is also
measured during each event
• Atmospheric transmission is calculated as I/Io
• Transmission data is then used to infer vertical profiles
of O3, NO2, H2O, multi-wavelength aerosol extinction,
and temperature/pressure (O2 A-band)

Solar attenuator on SAGE III/Meteor 3-M
instrument was nearly plane-parallel and
unexpectedly acted like an etalon with moderate
reflectivity on one side
Glass substrate of ‘etalon’ had temperature
dependent index of refraction
Transmission characteristics of ‘etalon’ changed
during occultation events due to solar heating of
the attenuator
High frequency ‘etalon’ transmission pattern
modulated the O2 A-band and solar spectra in
unpredictable manner
Post-launch attempts to characterize and model
‘etalon’ had limited success and T/p products were
not science quality

• Preliminary Assessment:
– SAGE III/ISS O2 A-band measurements are of higher fidelity than SAGE III/Meteor 3-M with no
apparent instrument issues
– Sensitivity tests indicate small wavelength shift (~0.03-0.04 nm) in A-band channels relative to nominal
registration
– Preliminary retrievals are promising with near science quality products
