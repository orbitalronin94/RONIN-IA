<p>• Refueling spacecraft in orbit offers tremendous 
</p>
<p>benefits for increased spacecraft payload 
</p>
<p>capacity and reduced launch cost
</p>
<p>• Acquiring vapor-free liquid cryogenic 
</p>
<p>propellants from supply tanks and transferring 
</p>
<p>to receiving tanks is challenging:
</p>
<p>– Liquid, pressurant gas are not easily segregated in 
</p>
<p>microgravity environment
</p>
<p>» Phase separators must be reliable, have low pressure 
</p>
<p>drop, high turn-down ratio
</p>
<p>– Low surface tension of propellants, cryogenic 
</p>
<p>operating temperatures, large tank internal volumes
</p>
<p>• Among PMD types, screen channel LADs offer 
</p>
<p>higher resistance to gas ingestion under high 
</p>
<p>demand flow, adverse accelerations
</p>
<p>– Currently no flight cryogenic screen channel devices
</p>
<p>Background
</p>
<p>Propellant production, storage, and transfer 
</p>
<p>in-orbit is key to enabling the next generation 
</p>
<p>of advanced space missions.</p>
<p></p>

<p>• LN2 flow from primary tube containing LAD to 
</p>
<p>receiver tank driven by helium pressurant
</p>
<p>• During transfer testing, helium (and vaporized 
</p>
<p>LN2) vented overboard
– Heaters, secondary container used to ensure no cryogen is 
</p>
<p>expelled
</p>
<p>– Can be enclosed during zero-g, vented during interim periods 
</p>
<p>after LN2 settling
</p>
<p>• Testing proceeds in 15–30 s bursts of zero-g, 
</p>
<p>pauses when gravity restored
</p>
<p>• LN2 then driven back into primary tank from 
</p>
<p>receiver tank using pressurant
</p>
<p>• Testing repeated as time allows
</p>
<p>• Supplementary LN2 dewar to re-supply if needed
</p>
<p>• Goal to fully drain primary dewar using Creare 
</p>
<p>LAD over several successive zero-g cycles
</p>
<p>– Design of LAD prevents breakdown when gravity is 
</p>
<p>intermittently restored
</p>
<p>Concept of Operations
</p>
<p>Our test facility measures LN2 flowrate, pressure differential across 
</p>
<p>screened channels, liquid level within the primary dewar, and 
</p>
<p>records video of flow through sight-glasses to verify LAD operation.</p>

<p>• Expulsion efficiency is the key metric for gauging LAD performance
</p>
<p>– Expulsion Efficiency = (Initial Fill Volume – Liquid Volume @ Breakdown) / (Initial Fill Volume)
</p>
<p>• Verified in our testing by measuring liquid level during steady flight after 
</p>
<p>breakdown has occurred
</p>
<p>• Important to correct for aircraft angle of attack, dead volume inside tank, liquid 
</p>
<p>released during breakdown 
</p>

</p>
<p>determine tank orientation with 
</p>
<p>respect to gravity during steady 
</p>
<p>flight, important for using liquid 
</p>
<p>level measurement to determine 
</p>
<p>expulsion efficiency.</p>

<p>Summary of Key Improvements to Existing Parabolic Flight Payload
</p>
<p>Improvement Description
</p>
<p>Mitigate Tank Slosh between 
</p>
<p>Microgravity Periods
</p>
<p>Orient tank axis perpendicular to aircraft thrust axis, 
</p>
<p>reducing slush length from 60" (tank length) to 
</p>
<p>5" (tank diameter).
</p>
<p>Ensure Camera Stability
</p>
<p>Reduce passthrough resistance, increase passthrough 
</p>
<p>mechanical stability, and increase operating voltage of 
</p>
<p>CMOS cameras.
</p>
<p>Eliminate Temperature 
</p>
<p>Difference between Helium 
</p>
<p>Pressurant and LN2 in LAD
</p>
<p>Replace tubing connecting our helium pre-cooling coil 
</p>
<p>with a vacuum jacketed flex tube to greatly reduce heat 
</p>
<p>gain prior to entering our test article.
</p>
<p>Enhance Instrumentation
</p>
<p>(1) Switch to precision LN2 rated pressure transducers, 
</p>
<p>(2) add a second liquid level sensor, (3) add a pressure 
</p>
<p>sensor to the receiver dewar, (4) add temperature 
</p>
<p>measurements near screen surfaces.
</p>
<p>Reduce LAD Heat Leak and
