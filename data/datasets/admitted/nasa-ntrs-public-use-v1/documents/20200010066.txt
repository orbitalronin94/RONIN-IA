Background
 SLS EM-1 marks the first time an adaptive controller is to be
implemented on a human-rated launch vehicle
•The adaptive controller is intended to augment the performance of the
already well-tuned baseline linear controller.
•Done by extending the envelope of failures and flight anomalies.
 In a launch vehicle, the controlled error dynamics are lower in
frequency than the internal dynamics (flex, slosh) and parasitic
(engine) dynamics
•As we approach instability due to insufficient forward
loop gain (aerodynamics, thrust uncertainty), the error
dynamics diverge exponentially or at a frequency at
or below the design control frequency.
•As we approach instability due to excessive forward
loop gain, we observe the introduction of highfrequency spectrum in the control loop.
•These are general characteristics of the control of
damped complex roots with servo lag, delay, and
complex engine poles.
3

Background
Three key functionalities of the SLS Augmenting Adaptive
Control (AAC) algorithm
•Objective 1: Minimum adaptation when the baseline control system is
performing within the nominal envelope.
•Objective 2: Increase performance when tracking error becomes too
large.
•Objective 3: Decrease system overall gain to suppress undesirable high
frequency dynamics in the control path.

Demonstrate the key functionalities the AAC algorithm on
the Quad-Rotor hardware at the LaRC Autonomy Incubator
(AI)
•Dynamics of controlling a conditionally stable quad-rotor is analogous to
controlling an aerodynamically unstable launch vehicle.
•In theory, the AAC algorithm should be applicable
for a wide range of conditionally stable dynamical
systems

Background
AAC is a form of model reference adaptive control
• The forward loop gain KT is augmented by a signal Ka which comprises of low
frequency content in the reference model error signal, and high frequency
content that’s in the control signal.
• Multiplicative augmentation is easy to assess in terms of gain margin.
• Limits of adaptation (KT) are based on the stability margin requirements (+/- 6
dB) for the baseline linear controller.
• AAC is there to “extend” the nominal stability margins.

Conclusions and Ongoing work
Current Work:
•Better quantification of the system parameters
•Replicating other launch vehicle dynamics
– Use the Quad-Rotor to stabilize an inverted pendulum (IP).
– Use the AAC to extend the stability margins of the IP.
– Make the IP semi-rigid and attach sensor at tip (to mimic flexibility

and non-minimum phase behaviors).
