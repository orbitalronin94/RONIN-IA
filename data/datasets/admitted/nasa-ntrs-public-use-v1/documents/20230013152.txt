<div class="slide-content"><p>Solar Coronal Jets</p>
<p>Solar coronal jets have been regularly observed since the Yohkoh days (Shibata et al. 1992; Yokohama &amp; Shibata 1995 &amp; 1996; Shimojo et al. 1996; Nisticò et al. 2009, 2015; Cirtain et al. 2007; Savcheva et al. 2007; Moore et al. 2010; etc).  For reviews see Shibata &amp; Magara (2011), Raouafi et al. (2016), Hinode Review Team et al. (2019, section by Sterling), Shen (2021).</p>
<p>In polar coronal holes: size ~50,000 km x 8000 km; rate ~60/day (Savcheva et al. 2007).</p>
<p>Often have a “hot loop’’ at the jet’s base, located asymmetrically off to one side of the base.</p>
<p>Many jets show twisting motions during their growth phase (e.g., Pike &amp; Mason 1998, Kamio et al. 2010, Moore et al. 2015).</p>
<p>Sterling et al. (2015) argued that they form from “minifilament eruptions”; small-scale versions of large-scale filament eruptions.</p>
<p></p>

<p>What Causes Miniature-Filament Eruptions?</p>
<p>Several studies found flux cancelation leading to jets (e.g., Hong et al. 2011, 2014; Huang et al. 2012; Young &amp; Muglach 2014a,b; Adams et al. 2014; Muglach 2021).</p>
<p></p>
<p>Some other studies found jets from location of emerging flux+flux cancelation (e.g., Liu et al. 2011; Shen et al. 2012, 2017; Hong et al. 2012; Li et al. 2015), or perhaps strong convergence and shearing (Kumar et al. 2018).</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Possible Switchback Connection</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><div class="embedded" id="slide16_rId2"></div>
<p>GSFC/Adriana Marique Gutierrez</p>
<p>https://www.nasa.gov/feature/goddard/2021/switchbacks-science-explaining-parker-solar-probe-s-magnetic-puzzle</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>GSFC/Adriana Marique Gutierrez</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide17_rId2"></div>
<p>https://www.nasa.gov/feature/goddard/2021/switchbacks-science-explaining-parker-solar-probe-s-magnetic-puzzle</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p></p>
<p>A jet could launch a magnetic twist onto open field. </p>
<p></p>
<p>The resulting magnetic packet would propagate out along the field at the Alfvén speed. </p>
<p></p>
<p>That speed varies with distance from the Sun: ~1000 km/s in the corona, and ~100 km/s at 35 R_sun.</p>
<p></p>
<p>The wave packet, initially of size ~R_sun (Moore et al. 2015, Sterling &amp; Moore 2020), will feel different Alfvén speeds at its front and near-Sun sides; this Alfvén-speed gradient will result in the wave packet becoming more compact with distance from the Sun. </p>
<p></p>
<p>The result will be a feature that PSP could detect as a switchback.</p>
</div>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Jetlets</p>
<p>Smaller than jets, larger than spicules.</p>
<p>Seen in EUV, and in UV (IRIS).</p>
<p>First identified at plume bases (Raouafi &amp; Stenborg 2014).</p>
<p>Also occur in more general network locations (Panesar et al. 2018).</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Spicules</p>
<p>Chromospheric features, observed for well over 100 years.</p>
<p>Share some properties with jets (Sterling &amp; Moore 2016, Sterling et al. 2020a).</p>
<p>Jet-like appearance and dynamics.</p>
<p>Often spin.</p>
<p>Magnetic bases (Samanta et al. 2019).</p>
<p>Comparison lacks in other ways:</p>
<p>No clear evidence for erupting microfilament.</p>
<p>No confirmed base brightenings.</p>

<p>Summary</p>
<p>How does jet physics scale to different sizes?  (Large eruptions? ``Jetlets’'?  Spicules??)</p>
<p>Jets reach into the heliosphere, with white-light jets/narrow CMEs, and plasma outflows.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Approaching a good understand of coronal jets:  At least many jets are miniature filament eruptions triggered by flux cancelation.  AR jets are likely similar, but altered due to the more-complex AR field.</p>
<p>Might jets (and/or jet-like features) be the source of the PSP-observed switchbacks?</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p></p>
<p>PSP observations of switchbacks suggests that they are generated on preferred size scales, that of supergranules (Bale et al. 2021, Fargette et al. 2021), and on the size scale of granules (Fargette et al. 2021).</p>
<p></p>
<p>The three size scales for large-scale filament eruptions, jet-producing minifilament eruptions, and possible spicules produced from microfilament eruptions, reflect three natural magnetic size-scales on the Sun: active regions, supergranules, and granules. (Sterling et al. 2020.) </p>

<div class="slide-content"><p></p>
<p>Large-scale eruptions are like jet-producing eruptions, but on a larger scale (filaments ~30,000 — 105 km).</p>
<p></p>
<p>Jets occur from erupting minifilaments of size ~8000 km —18,000 km</p>
<p>(Sterling et al. 2015, Panesar et al. 2016, McGlasson et al. 2019).</p>
<p></p>
<p>It is plausible that the jet mechanism occurs on smaller size scales, with increasing frequency, with “jetlets” (widths ~4000 km, Raouafi &amp; Stenborg 2014; Panesar et al. 2018), and maybe even some spicules (widths ~ few x102 km; Sterling &amp; Moore 2016). </p>
<p></p>
<p> </p>
<p>If all can become switchbacks, the rates would be much larger than then the few x 100/day from canonical coronal jets.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Such jets might also contribute to the solar wind (Moore et al. 2011, Raouafi et al. 2023).</p>
