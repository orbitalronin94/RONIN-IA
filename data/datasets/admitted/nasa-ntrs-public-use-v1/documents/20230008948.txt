<p>Process Compensated Resonance Testing (PCRT)

NASA Marshall Space Flight Center
Engineering Directorate, Materials &amp; Processes Lab
Erin Lanigan, Delphine Duquette, Daniel Vaughan, Isabelle Sadowski (EM21)</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide1_rId3"></div>
<p>Fig 1: IN718 High Cycle Fatigue Specimen</p>
<p>Overview</p>
<p></p>
<p>PCRT uses resonant frequency – the natural frequency where a medium vibrates at the highest amplitude. PCRT measures the resonance frequencies of whole parts and compares the results to parts of the same type that are known to be nominal. </p>
<p>Off-nominal properties or defects in a part will affect its resonant frequency. This technology is an especially important technology as additive manufactured parts become more prevalent. </p>
<p></p>
<p>The nondestructive evaluation (NDE) of additively manufactured parts presents unique challenges and opportunities.  Additive manufacturing (AM) technology enables complex part geometries and morphologies, which test the limits of conventional NDE techniques. PCRT can potentially detect common AM defects such as porosity, lack of fusion, inclusions, oxides/corrosion, etc. </p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>	</p>
<p>Fig 2: IN718 Dogbone Specimen</p>
<p>How does PCRT work?</p>
<p>Set Up</p>
<p>Sending/Receiving transducers must be in contact with the part</p>
<p>Transducers sweep over a range of frequencies to identify resonant modes</p>
<p>Fixture &amp; Transducer set-up must remain consistent with each specimen</p>
<p>Channels must be in the same pattern </p>
<p>Hardware Verification Test can give a red/green light to indicate if the fixture is correctly set up</p>
<p></p>
<p>Analysis</p>
<p>Vibrant – the company that developed this system – has pattern recognition software to provide pass/fail analysis.</p>
<p>There are three main methods of analysis Vibrant uses:</p>
<p>Z-score Analysis</p>
<p>Characterizes part resonances relative to a reference population</p>
<p>Identifies Gross Outliers</p>
<p>Vibrational Pattern Recognition (VIPR)</p>
<p>Uses Mahalanobis-Taguchi System (MTS) </p>
<p>Part-to-Itself (PTI)</p>
<p>Metrics describe how a part changes due to processing operations (e.g. heat treated)</p>
<p></p>
<p>Process Sensitivities </p>
<p>Consistent part geometry is critical</p>
<p>Failed AM builds can result in incorrect height or shape</p>
<p>Messy build plate removal can result in incorrect shape</p>
<p>Changing build parameters will invalidate previous population</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>

<p>AM Dogbone Samples</p>
<p>Tensile test remnants are machined down for consistent size and shape</p>
<p>Parts are PCRT tested to determine if PCRT analysis aligns with mechanical test notes, which already determined if the build was good or bad. </p>
<p>Initial PCRT analysis (Z-score) shows distinctions between builds with outliers present. </p>
<p>Analysis showed PCRT capability to compensate for variables such as mass, height, and diameter to find true outliers</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide1_rId8"></div>
<p>Current Projects (continued)</p>
<p>HR-1 Defect Study</p>
<p>Two kinds of defects: seeded defects and laser power variation defects</p>
<p>Seeded defects of varying diameters</p>
<p>Laser power variation from 75%-125%</p>
<p>These defects replicate defects commonly found in AM builds such as:</p>
<p>Gas porosity trapped in powder feedstock</p>
<p>Fluctuations in laser power output</p>
<p>Build process errors</p>

<p>GSFC Fastener Study</p>
<p>Goddard Space Flight Center has an interest in using PCRT for flight fastener quality control</p>
<p>GSFC sent two lots of fasteners – Lot A and B – of two different sizes</p>
<p>Goal is to compare population of ‘good’ fasteners to population of ‘bad’ (defected) fasteners</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide1_rId9"></div>
<p>Fig 5: GSFC Lot A Fastener</p>
<p>HR-1 Defect Study</p>
<p></p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide1_rId10"></div>
<p>Defect study includes various types of NDE inspection, PCRT being one of them</p>
<p>Only two nominal parts per build</p>
<p>In the future, more nominal parts to be created to have better baseline established for PCRT</p>
<p></p>
<p>Z-score analysis found that AB and HT groups for 125% laser power showed large differences in Z magnitude</p>
<p>Build plate locations show that 125% power samples were on the edge of the build plate</p>
<p>PCRT could show variation across build plates</p>
<p>Demonstrates that PCRT can possibly identify main factors in material property anomalies. </p>
