Background


Managing cryogens is a critical technology for NASA’s spaceflight missions


Cryogenic fluid management (CFM) challenges
Long term propellant storage
Pressure control for nominal operations
Propellant transfer in microgravity
Conditioning propellants with cryocoolers


NASA’s CFM Modeling Portfolio
Develop, enhance and validate computational fluid dynamic (CFD) models


Propulsion Fluid Dynamics Branch at NASA Marshall supports several projects with CFD analyses
Human Landing System (HLS), Space Launch System (SLS), Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS), etc.


Develop and validate Loci-Stream-VOF CFD solver to solve complex thermo-fluid and multi-physics problems for CFM
Cryogenic tank operations/phenomena: self-pressurization, autogenous pressurization, helium pressurization, mixing (depressurization with jets, sprays, etc.), venting, ullage collapse, tank fill/drain, liquefaction, etc.

Computational Model


Eulerian Model


Two phase model: sharp interface method 


Turbulence Model: IDDES (Hybrid RANS/LES closure)


Asymmetric interface turbulence transport modeling: Wall-like turbulence on gas side of the interface


Phase change: Thermal equilibrium and Hertz-Knudsen-Schrage’s (kinetics-based)


Lagrangian particle modeling:


Spray cannot be resolved in Eulerian phase


Monte-Carlo modeling using parcel approach


Two-way momentum (Clift-Gauvin) and energy coupling (Ranz-Marshall) with Eulerian flow


Particle phase change: Thermal equilibrium and Hertz-Knudsen-Schrage model


Turbulence scattering: Sawford’s acceleration scattering model


Particle breakup model (Chryssakis and Assanis [2]): based on Weber number


Droplet-droplet collision model (O’Rourke [3]): based on collision Weber number, velocity vectors, and droplet size

Break-up Modeling is Crucial


Particle break-up and coalescence modeling is based on known flow physics


Depressurization rate is strongly affected by injected particle size


Large particle surface area enhances heat/mass transfer


Difficult to measure droplet sizes. They were not characterized in testing, but physics-based modeling does not require a priori particle diameter information


Particles with injection diameter of 1.7 mm are scaled 4x for visualization


Every 100th particle is displayed

Conclusion


Validated Loci-Stream and Lagrangian-Eulerian multiphase flow modeling technique for CFM Modeling Portfolio.


Identified important models crucial for reliable modeling


Asymmetric interface turbulence transport in Eulerian phase change modeling


Weber number based Lagrangian droplet break-up and collision model for physically accurate heat/mass transfer with Eulerian domain


Future work


Validate with VOF method for morphing interfaces


Validate VOF to Lagrangian particle conversion based on droplet size


A successful, and parameter-free validation against flight-scale tank test builds confidence in use of CFD methodology for CFM modeling for human spaceflight projects.
