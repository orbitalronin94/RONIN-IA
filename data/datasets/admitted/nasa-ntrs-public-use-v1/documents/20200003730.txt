Introduction : The science, applied science and educational communities have
a need for top-of-atmosphere (TOA) and surface radiative fluxes on a low latency
basis (within about 7 days of observation). Applied science uses for surface fluxes
include building energy performance monitoring and seasonal crop yield modeling.
The Clouds and Earth’s Radiant Energy System (CERES) FLASHFlux data products
address this need by enhancing the speed of the CERES processing using a baseline
spectral response and parameterized surface flux models to produce global fluxes
within a week of satellite observations. The CERES FLASHFlux provides two data
products: 1) an hourly [Level 2] Single Scanner Footprint (SSF) data separately for
Aqua and Terra observations, and 2) a daily [Level 3] Time Interpolated and Spatially
Averaged (TISA) 1ox1o gridded data that combines Aqua and Terra observations.

Annual State of the Climate: Since 2006, FLASHFlux data has been used to

determine the Earth Radiation Budget at the Top of Atmosphere. While FLASHFlux
data is not a climate dataset and long term analysis using only FLASHFlux data is
highly discouraged, we can normalized FLASHFlux data to climate dataset such as
CERES EBAF to assess most recent TOA flux variability in a long-term context. Below
highlights the steps that we use to accomplish an early estimate of CERES EBAF fluxes.

Step 1: Combining different FLASHFlux Versions

FLASHFlux TISA and CERES EBAF (Energy Balanced and Filled) are used in the
analysis of TOA anomalies for the annual “State of the Climate” report. We highlight
the methodology use to merge the two data products by correcting the bias in
FLASHFlux TISA and inter-calibrate to CERES EBAF. We also compare CERES
FLASHFlux SSF overpass and TISA radiative flux products to a set of surface
measurement sites that are globally distributed and assess agreement.

The CERES FLASHFlux data is always going through continuous improvement. Unlike formal CERES climate data products, FLASHFlux
products are more operational in nature; therefore, are updated as changes to input data and algorithms are made. One such
improvement is an update in the GEOS meteorology between Version 2 and Version 3. The updated meteorology caused a
discontinuity in the data products and requiring a normalization to better ensure monthly global averaged consistency.

The normalized FLASHFlux data can now be used to extend the CERES EBAF
observation. We use 18 years of CERES EBAF data to construct a monthly
climatology. Removing the monthly climatology from the hybrid monthly timeseries
provides the deseasonalized anomaly timeseries. Assessing this method by
comparing estimated and actual EBAF flux anomalies, we determined that using this
method provides an estimated flux anomaly within 0.2 Wm-2 uncertainty.

Global monthly mean deseasonalized time series
was constructed from the CERES EBAF (Energy
Balanced And Filled) Ed4 product for March 2000
to June 2019 and from the CERES Fast Longwave
and Shortwave Radiative Fluxes (FLASHFlux)
products for July 2019 to October 2019. A
normalization procedure results in a 2-sigma
monthly uncertainty of ±0.43, ±0.08, ±0.20 and
±0.50 Wm-2 for the OLR, TSI, RSW and total net
radiation, respectively.

Acknowledgements: This work was funded by the NASA CERES Project, Norman Loeb PI. Data were
obtained from the NASA Langley Research Center Atmospheric Science Data Center. Thanks go to John

• FLASHFlux data products contain the only publicly available global data set of
shortwave and longwave surface fluxes within about 7 days of observation, which can
be used to assess surface radiation budget variability in space and time.
• FLASHFlux surface fluxes show good agreement with ground measurements for TISA
products and are reliable for uses in applied science through the POWER web portal.
• The methods use to create a hybrid CERES FLASHFlux and CERES EBAF timeseries
provide a result that is within 0.2 Wm-2 uncertainty.
• FLASHFlux provides a reliable estimate of future TOA CERES EBAF fluxes; this year
the method projects a continued increased in the global net TOA flux through October.
