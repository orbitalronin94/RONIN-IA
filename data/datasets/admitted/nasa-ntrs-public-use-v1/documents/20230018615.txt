</p>
<p>Introduction
</p>
<p>• Liquid settling is often required before several operations 
</p>
<p>in cryogenic propellant tanks:
</p>
<p>• Engine restart
</p>
<p>• Propellant transfer
</p>
<p>• Venting
</p>
<p>• Pressurization 
</p>
<p>• Reaction control systems (or other propulsion systems) 
</p>
<p>may be pulsed to reduce the propellant mass expended. 
</p>
<p>• A vertical excitation is effectively introduced as a side effect.
</p>
<p>• The surface waves are known as Faraday waves.
</p>
<p>• Characteristics of Faraday waves were hypothesized to apply to 
</p>
<p>microgravity to design pulsed settling schemes without CFD:
</p>
<p>− Minimum excitation acceleration to produce Faraday waves
</p>
<p>− Minimum excitation acceleration to cause droplet ejection
</p>
<p>− Predictable surface mode shapes and wavelengths
</p>
<p>− Approximate surface wave amplitudes
</p>
<p>≈</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>3
</p>
<p>Introduction
</p>
<p>Problem
</p>
<p>• Surface instabilities can increase the heat 
</p>
<p>transfer and mixing between a hot ullage 
</p>
<p>and cold liquid.
</p>
<p>• Liquid can be ejected from the surface, leading 
</p>
<p>to inefficiencies or ullage collapse.
</p>
<p>• At worst, propellant may be unsettled by the 
</p>
<p>operation intended to prevent it.
</p>
<p>Solution
</p>
<p>• Assemble an engineering model to quickly 
</p>
<p>define regions of stability. 
</p>
<p>• Engineers can use the simple model as a 
</p>
<p>preliminary guide to 
</p>
<p>• Estimate an appropriate thruster output
</p>
<p>• Determine valve opening/closing response time
</p>
<p>• Select stable operating conditions (concept of 
</p>
<p>operations)</p>
<p></p>
