<p>Path to Sustainable Technologies in the Lunar Surface Environment Workshop - Lunar Dust Mitigation Guide
</p>
<p> Why the concern about lunar surface dust effects - sustained ops?
 Abrasive, adherent, sharp fine particulates - a problem for mechanisms?
</p>
<p> Ubiquitous - as discovered by the Apollo astronauts, completely avoiding lunar 
dust during surface operations would be nearly impossible.
</p>
<p> Electrostatics - potentially negatively or positively charged depending on solar 
illumination and charged particle flux, and in low gravity.
</p>
<p> Particle reactivity - chemically reactive fracture surfaces possible in hard vacuum.
</p>
<p> What should you be testing?
 Which dust effects are your primary failure concern?  Mechanical wear?     
</p>
<p>Optical effects / visibility?  Thermal control?  Combined effects?  
</p>
<p> How long *must* your asset operate?  Should it degrade gracefully?
</p>
<p> Are dust mitigation schemes to be tested?  Measured &amp; compared?
</p>
<p> How are you quantifying exposure / coverage / mitigations?
 Lunar surface particle size distribution extends down to nanometer range -
</p>
<p>optical microscopy resolves at best to ~micrometers.  Mass balance 
measurements typically cannot detect dust (&lt;20 µm dia.) differences.
</p>

<p>Lunar Dust Tolerance - Terrestrial Testing Concerns
</p>
<p> Test fidelity
 The simulant dust effects to be measured dictate the type and fidelity of testing
</p>
<p>needed, particularly if comparing mitigation scheme effectiveness.
</p>
<p> If dust mitigation is to be used, the approaches involve either avoiding lunar dust 
exposure, removing particulates as possible, or tolerating particulates during 
surface operations - or some combination of those three general approaches.
</p>
<p> Fidelity determinants
 Which simulant(s) to use, and particle size range of interest
</p>
<p> LSIC, and other websites - e.g., Univ. of Central Florida, Colorado School of Mines.
</p>
<p> Classifications and Requirements for Testing Systems and Hardware To Be Exposed To Dust in 
Planetary Environments, NASA Standard 1008 (2021), https://ntrs.nasa.gov
</p>
<p> Lunar Regolith Simulant User’s Guide, Schrader et al., NASA TM-2010-216446    (being updated!)
</p>
<p> Vacuum level required / achievable with simulant, if relevant.
</p>
<p> Simulant particle surface cleanliness / reactivity, if relevant.
</p>
<p> Electrostatic field environment, if relevant.
</p>
