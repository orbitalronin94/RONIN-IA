 Why the concern about lunar surface dust effects - sustained ops?
 Abrasive, adherent, sharp fine particulates - a problem for mechanisms?



 Ubiquitous - as discovered by the Apollo astronauts, completely avoiding lunar 
dust during surface operations would be nearly impossible.



 Electrostatics - potentially negatively or positively charged depending on solar 
illumination and charged particle flux, and in low gravity.



 Particle reactivity - chemically reactive fracture surfaces possible in hard vacuum.



 What should you be testing?
 Which dust effects are your primary failure concern? Mechanical wear? 



Optical effects / visibility? Thermal control? Combined effects? 



 How long *must* your asset operate? Should it degrade gracefully?



 Are dust mitigation schemes to be tested? Measured & compared?



 How are you quantifying exposure / coverage / mitigations?
 Lunar surface particle size distribution extends down to nanometer range -



optical microscopy resolves at best to ~micrometers. Mass balance 
measurements typically cannot detect dust (<20 µm dia.) differences.

Dust Mitigation Best Practices Guide Overview



 Technology Product Capability
 Develop an accessible, understandable, comprehensive, and refereed guide to 



avoiding lunar dust problems during surface operations, both for inexperienced 
mechanisms designers in academia or small companies *and* for seasoned 
mission veterans. 



 Extensive references into the detailed technical literature provide a “how to” 
lunar dust mitigation reference.



 Technical Capabilities 
 Interactions and input from potential Guidebook users in industry that are 



external to NASA during COVID 19, to be incorporated



 Identification and availability of technical expertise across NASA



 Exploration & Science Applicability 
 Publication and distribution to NASA stakeholders, aerospace industry, and 



academic partners 



 For use by designers, commercial partners, and service providers



 NASA missions targeted by the guidebook: CLPS, Artemis, Gateway, HLS

Lunar Dust Tolerance - Terrestrial Testing Concerns



 Test fidelity
 The simulant dust effects to be measured dictate the type and fidelity of testing



needed, particularly if comparing mitigation scheme effectiveness.



 If dust mitigation is to be used, the approaches involve either avoiding lunar dust 
exposure, removing particulates as possible, or tolerating particulates during 
surface operations - or some combination of those three general approaches.



 Fidelity determinants
 Which simulant(s) to use, and particle size range of interest



 LSIC, and other websites - e.g., Univ. of Central Florida, Colorado School of Mines.



 Classifications and Requirements for Testing Systems and Hardware To Be Exposed To Dust in 
Planetary Environments, NASA Standard 1008 (2021), https://ntrs.nasa.gov



 Lunar Regolith Simulant User’s Guide, Schrader et al., NASA TM-2010-216446 (being updated!)



 Vacuum level required / achievable with simulant, if relevant.



 Simulant particle surface cleanliness / reactivity, if relevant.



 Electrostatic field environment, if relevant.
