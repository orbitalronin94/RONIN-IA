<p>Background</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Silicon Carbide MOSFETS
</p>
<p>• Enticing option for converters
• Fast switching
• High efficiency
• Higher breakdown voltage 
</p>
<p>• Downsides include
• Higher short circuit current density 
• Smaller thermal capacitance
</p>
<p>• Small internal inductance and small on-state resistance causes 
large di/dt -&gt; Current rapidly increases during a short circuit 
event
</p>
<p>• Typically results in damage to the module</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Common Short Circuit Detection Methods
</p>
<p>• Desaturation (Desat)
• Measures the voltage at the drain of the 
</p>
<p>MOSFET
• When the voltage exceeds the specified 
</p>
<p>voltage, power is stopped to the device
• Can shut down a fault in several 
</p>
<p>microseconds
• Originally designed to work with IGBTs, 
</p>
<p>not SiC MOSFETs
• Much better at protecting IGBTs
• Better at limiting current during short circuit 
</p>
<p>events
S. Mocevic et al., “Comparison and Discussion on Shortcircuit
Protections for Silicon-Carbide MOSFET Modules: Desaturation Versus 
Rogowski Switch-Current Sensor,” IEEE Transactions on Industry 
Applications, vol. 56, no. 3, pp. 2880–2893, May 2020, doi: 
https://doi.org/10.1109/TIA.2020.2972816.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Problems With Common Methods
</p>
<p>• Some off the shelf SiC MOSFETS have a short circuit withstand 
time of &lt;2 microseconds
</p>
<p>• Desat at its fastest can shut down a fault in several 
microseconds</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Current Based Fault Detection
</p>
<p>• Proven to respond faster
• Main factor driving performance is current 
</p>
<p>transducer 
• Air cores – aka Rogowski Coil – desirable for flight 
</p>
<p>• Low mass
• Small footprint
• Trade-off: significant reduction in sensitivity compared to 
</p>
<p>magnetic cores
• Rogowski coil generates di/dt value of the sensed 
</p>
<p>current scaled by a factor of the mutual inductance
• Large magnitude current derivative of fault events 
</p>
<p>mitigates need for CT with large inductance
M. El-Shahat, E. Tag Eldin, N. A. Mohamed, A. EL-Morshedy, 
and M. E. Ibrahim, “Measurement of Power Frequency Current 
including Low- and High-Order Harmonics Using a Rogowski 
Coil,” Sensors, vol. 22, no. 11, p. 4220, Jan. 2022, doi: 
https://doi.org/10.3390/s22114220.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Prototype Design
</p>
<p>• Power Circuit
• DC bus – Vbus
• Load inductance – Lload
• Parasitic power loop inductance – Lpar
• Switching device – S1
</p>
<p>• Rogowski Coil – represented by coupled inductor
• Primary inductance – Lp
• Secondary inductance –Ls
• Coupling coefficient – k
</p>
<p>• Filtered by RC network
• R1
• R2
• C
</p>
<p>• Comparator w/ threshold voltage – Vth
• Output of comparator used to activate latching shutdown circuit - Vo</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Design Considerations
</p>
<p>• RC filter must provide filtering to ensure circuit does not trip 
during normal switching
</p>
<p>• Circuit must be sensitive enough to detect low-frequency faults
• Must have overlap between spectrum of faults that Rogowski based 
</p>
<p>and Desat based methods can detect
• The actual device needed to be small enough to fit in a 2.54 cm 
</p>
<p>space due to the space constraints on the converter</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>Test Results</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Prototype Implementation
</p>
<p>• Prototype 1 kV, 250 kW inverter w/ 
logic circuitry to latch fault
</p>
<p>• Rogowski coil fabricated using: 
• Adjustable plastic ring
• 200 turns of 0.45 mm diameter 
</p>
<p>magnet wire
• Entire assembly sealed w/ shrink 
</p>
<p>tubing
• Rogowski Coil weighs 7.2 grams 
</p>
<p>and has coil length of 9.398 cm</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Prototype Implementation Continued
</p>
<p>• In test configuration coil 
resulted: 
</p>
<p>• Self inductance – 10.7 μH
• Mutual inductance – approx. 60 
</p>
<p>nH
• Inverter configured with a 
</p>
<p>shorted load to induce low-
inductance fault
</p>
<p>• Approx 500 nH
• Set by parasitic inductance of 
</p>
<p>shorted load wire
</p>
<p>Rogowski 
CoilDC Link 
</p>
<p>Capacitors
</p>
<p>Fault Latching 
Circuitry</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Test Results
</p>
<p>• DC bus varied starting at 50 V, 
stepped up by 50 V to 1 kV
</p>
<p>• Fault current waveforms captured in 
intervals of 200 V
</p>
<p>• System prevented damage to all 
devices and responded to all fault 
events in 500 ns
</p>
<p>• System kept fault current low to 
prevent excessive voltage 
overshoot
</p>
<p>• Additional ringing past device 
shutdown attributed to resonant 
current – does not contribute to 
device heating </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Additional Findings
</p>
<p>• Need for fault latch signal to be isolated from noisy signals to 
prevent false tripping and proper latching
</p>
<p>• Propagation delay imposed by fault latch circuity needed to be 
small to ensure efficient shut down time </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Conclusion</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Conclusion
</p>
<p>• Investigated hard fault protection method for 1 kV SiC MOSFET 
utilizing a Rogowski coil
</p>
<p>• Electrical and thermal model used to verify functionality of 
circuit
</p>
<p>• Experimentally gathered low-inductance fault data collected on 
prototype 1 kV SiC based inverter
</p>
<p>• Combining Desat with this method can protect from the most 
common methods of FET failure, saving time and money when 
working with experimental SiC MOSFET converters</p>
