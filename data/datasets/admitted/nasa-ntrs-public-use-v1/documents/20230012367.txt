Background











Silicon Carbide MOSFETS



• Enticing option for converters
• Fast switching
• High efficiency
• Higher breakdown voltage 



• Downsides include
• Higher short circuit current density 
• Smaller thermal capacitance



• Small internal inductance and small on-state resistance causes 
large di/dt -> Current rapidly increases during a short circuit 
event



• Typically results in damage to the module











Common Short Circuit Detection Methods



• Desaturation (Desat)
• Measures the voltage at the drain of the 



MOSFET
• When the voltage exceeds the specified 



voltage, power is stopped to the device
• Can shut down a fault in several 



microseconds
• Originally designed to work with IGBTs, 



not SiC MOSFETs
• Much better at protecting IGBTs
• Better at limiting current during short circuit 



events
S. Mocevic et al., “Comparison and Discussion on Shortcircuit
Protections for Silicon-Carbide MOSFET Modules: Desaturation Versus 
Rogowski Switch-Current Sensor,” IEEE Transactions on Industry 
Applications, vol. 56, no. 3, pp. 2880–2893, May 2020, doi: 
https://doi.org/10.1109/TIA.2020.2972816.











Problems With Common Methods



• Some off the shelf SiC MOSFETS have a short circuit withstand 
time of <2 microseconds



• Desat at its fastest can shut down a fault in several 
microseconds











Current Based Fault Detection



• Proven to respond faster
• Main factor driving performance is current 



transducer 
• Air cores – aka Rogowski Coil – desirable for flight 



• Low mass
• Small footprint
• Trade-off: significant reduction in sensitivity compared to 



magnetic cores
• Rogowski coil generates di/dt value of the sensed 



current scaled by a factor of the mutual inductance
• Large magnitude current derivative of fault events 



mitigates need for CT with large inductance
M. El-Shahat, E. Tag Eldin, N. A. Mohamed, A. EL-Morshedy, 
and M. E. Ibrahim, “Measurement of Power Frequency Current 
including Low- and High-Order Harmonics Using a Rogowski 
Coil,” Sensors, vol. 22, no. 11, p. 4220, Jan. 2022, doi: 
https://doi.org/10.3390/s22114220.

Prototype Design



• Power Circuit
• DC bus – Vbus
• Load inductance – Lload
• Parasitic power loop inductance – Lpar
• Switching device – S1



• Rogowski Coil – represented by coupled inductor
• Primary inductance – Lp
• Secondary inductance –Ls
• Coupling coefficient – k



• Filtered by RC network
• R1
• R2
• C



• Comparator w/ threshold voltage – Vth
• Output of comparator used to activate latching shutdown circuit - Vo











Design Considerations



• RC filter must provide filtering to ensure circuit does not trip 
during normal switching



• Circuit must be sensitive enough to detect low-frequency faults
• Must have overlap between spectrum of faults that Rogowski based 



and Desat based methods can detect
• The actual device needed to be small enough to fit in a 2.54 cm 



space due to the space constraints on the converter

Test Results











Prototype Implementation



• Prototype 1 kV, 250 kW inverter w/ 
logic circuitry to latch fault



• Rogowski coil fabricated using: 
• Adjustable plastic ring
• 200 turns of 0.45 mm diameter 



magnet wire
• Entire assembly sealed w/ shrink 



tubing
• Rogowski Coil weighs 7.2 grams 



and has coil length of 9.398 cm











Prototype Implementation Continued



• In test configuration coil 
resulted: 



• Self inductance – 10.7 μH
• Mutual inductance – approx. 60 



nH
• Inverter configured with a 



shorted load to induce low-
inductance fault



• Approx 500 nH
• Set by parasitic inductance of 



shorted load wire

Test Results



• DC bus varied starting at 50 V, 
stepped up by 50 V to 1 kV



• Fault current waveforms captured in 
intervals of 200 V



• System prevented damage to all 
devices and responded to all fault 
events in 500 ns



• System kept fault current low to 
prevent excessive voltage 
overshoot



• Additional ringing past device 
shutdown attributed to resonant 
current – does not contribute to 
device heating 











Additional Findings



• Need for fault latch signal to be isolated from noisy signals to 
prevent false tripping and proper latching



• Propagation delay imposed by fault latch circuity needed to be 
small to ensure efficient shut down time 











Conclusion











Conclusion



• Investigated hard fault protection method for 1 kV SiC MOSFET 
utilizing a Rogowski coil



• Electrical and thermal model used to verify functionality of 
circuit



• Experimentally gathered low-inductance fault data collected on 
prototype 1 kV SiC based inverter



• Combining Desat with this method can protect from the most 
common methods of FET failure, saving time and money when 
working with experimental SiC MOSFET converters
