<p>Abstract
</p>
<p>Due to increasingly high DC link voltages and further 
advancements in the current density of silicon carbide (SiC) 
MOSFETs, it has become evident that conventional IGBT 
protection methods are not sufficient to protect these devices 
from overcurrent during low-inductance fault events. The use 
of an air core Rogowski coil topology was explored to see if it 
could mitigate these hard fault events. The design of this 
circuit resulted in safe shutdown of a low impedance phase-to-
phase fault, tested up to DC link voltages of 1 kV. 
</p>
<p>Background
• Silicon Carbide (SiC) MOSFETs – enticing option for 
</p>
<p>power converters 
</p>
<p>• Fast switching
</p>
<p>• High efficiency
</p>
<p>• High breakdown voltage
</p>
<p>• Tradeoffs
</p>
<p>• High short circuit density
</p>
<p>• Small thermal capacity
</p>
<p>• Small internal inductance and on-state 
resistance
</p>
<p>• Current rapidly increases during a 
short circuit event resulting in 
damage to device
</p>
<p>• Desaturation
</p>
<p>• Designed to work with IGBTs
</p>
<p>• Measures voltage at drain, stops power 
when voltage threshold is exceeded
</p>
<p>• Takes several microseconds to stop a fault
</p>
<p>• Many SiC MOSFETS have a short circuit 
withstand time of &lt;2 microseconds
</p>
<p>• Air Core / Rogowski Coil Based Fault Detection
</p>
<p>• Low mass 
</p>
<p>• Small footprint
</p>
<p>• Low inductance causes large current 
derivative of fault events and reduces need 
for current transducer with large inductance
</p>
