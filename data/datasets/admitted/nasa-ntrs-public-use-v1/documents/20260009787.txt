CBM 2 Prompt Propagation Tests ODA:
 Introduction



An Optimized Data Analysis or ODA is a final data analysis package for a test or test series. The primary goal 



is to develop and present key data, observations, and findings in a form that is most useful to the LOX/Methane 



Assessment Physics-Based Modeling and Engineering Models Teams, as well as test teams and stakeholders. 



This ODA expands and builds upon the Quick Look Data Analysis (QLDA) completed by the LMA Data 



Analysis Subteam (DAS) for each Prompt Propagation test. Please note that values reported in the ODA often 



differ from the preliminary values reported in the relevant QLDAs. This ODA represents the FINAL, most 



accurate data values and yield assessments for each included test event. 



This ODA provides analyses of the blast data recorded on the CBM 2 Prompt Propagation (CBM 2_PP01-04) 



Tests and associated baseline tests CBM 2_100_P01 and P02. The CBM designation refers to “Confined by 



Missile”, an explosion accident scenario wherein a propellant leakage occurs while being confined within the 



physical structure of a missile or launch vehicle. The PP (Prompt Propagation) tests each featured a nominal 



100 lbm of LOX/LNG in a stacked tank configuration, with a 25 lbm C4 explosive charge placed in contact with 



the bottom of the test article. The tests were designed to quantify and understand the contribution to yield from 



unmixed LOX/LNG should a detonation occur in close proximity (e.g., sympathetic detonation of an upper 



stage should a lower stage detonate).

CBM 2 Prompt Propagation Tests ODA:
 Introduction (Concluded)



The CBM 2_PP01-02 Tests evaluated prompt propagation for the case of LOX over LNG; for these tests 



LNG was in the lower tank with the C4 charge placed in contact with the bottom of the test article. The CBM 



2_PP03-04 Tests evaluated prompt propagation for the case of LNG over LOX; for these tests LOX was in 



the lower tank with the C4 charge placed in contact with the bottom of the test article. 



Baseline explosive tests (designated as P01 and P02) were also conducted featuring just the C4 explosive 



charge (P01) and the C4 charge placed under a water-filled test article (P02) as a means of establishing the 



relative contribution of LOX/LNG on Tests PP01-04. Analyses focused on blast overpressure data within the 



TB 700 window (10-0.5 psi peak pressure) in order to provide assessments of explosive yield. Note that 



explosive outputs are reported as equivalent explosive weight (EEW) in lbm of TNT and yield 



percentage with TNT=100% for a hemispherical surface burst condition.

CBM 2_PP ODA: Executive Summary



The following observations* are offered based on the results:



1. Assessed yields** for baseline Test P01 (C4 charge only) were within expectations for C4, given the 



cylindrical charge shape.



2. Averaged overpressure-based yield for baseline Test P02 (water-filled test article) were ~7% lower on 



a mass basis as compared to those for Test P01 (C4 charge only); averaged impulse based yield was 



~18% lower than P01 on a mass basis. This is most likely due to the presence of the test article/water 



mass altering the local blast field, which is believed to have affected positive overpressure phase 



durations similarly to the case of a propellant-filled article. It is not believed that the water mass 



adversely affected reaction of the C4 explosive; C4 has well-documented performance in underwater 



blasting applications.



3. Tests PP01 and PP02 (LOX over LNG) produced negligible overpressure-based yield above that for 



baseline Test P02; impulse-based yields on a percentage basis increased minimally (4-6%, just 



beyond the analyzed yield uncertainty bounds).



8



* Observations apply only to specific scenarios evaluated in this test series. Additional testing/modeling is required to determine broader applicability.



** Yields listed do not include uncertainty bounds, which are included in the individual test sections of this document. In all cases mass-based yields 



are in lb of TNT hemispherical surface burst yield; for percentage-based yields 100% = TNT hemispherical surface burst yield.











Observations* (continued):



4. On a percentage basis, Tests PP03 and PP04 (LNG over LOX) produced overpressure-based 



yields** 8-13% higher than baseline Test P02; impulse-based yields increased 17-21%.



5. A possible reason for the higher yields for LNG over LOX is that in this testing configuration, the 



LOX was in closest proximity to the C4 donor charge, potentially introducing O2 at sufficiently 



early times to enhance afterburning of the C4 detonation products. Afterburning contribution to 



yield is not representative of a likely prompt propagation scenario and thus caution is advised 



regarding the observed increase in yields reported herein for LOX in closest contact with the 



donor charge.



6. Higher impulse-based yields indicate that deflagration of the propellants was a primary 



contributor to yields for both configurations.

CBM 2_PP Tests Description



10











• Commercial launch providers are actively developing launch vehicles (LV) that will 



utilize LOX and LCH4 (a.k.a. liquid natural gas/LNG) as the rocket propellants. 



Insufficient testing has been conducted to fully characterize the explosive risk 



associated with the LOX/LNG propellant combination. 



• Preliminary testing suggests that these propellants may be highly energetic and 



extremely hazardous if not properly controlled. Therefore, it is necessary to conduct 



appropriate testing to define the explosive characteristics and safety criteria for safe 



handling and use. 



• The CBM 2 testing campaign is designed to simulate a launch vehicle failure case 



where propellants can mix due to the failure of common or intermediate bulkheads. 



To establish the NEW and the explosive environment produced from internal failures 



of operational system tankage these tests will determine the explosive hazard 



potential of the liquid propellant combination under confined mixing conditions.

CBM 2 Test Program Overview



The CBM 2 Test Program is being conducted as part of the NASA-USSF sponsored 



LOX/Methane (LNG) Assessment, or LMA. 



CBM 2 Test Program was designed to examine consequences of a static launch pad explosion 



with nominal propellant masses ranging from 100 to 20,000 lbm. CBM 2 consists of the 



following test series:



• PP 01-04 – evaluation of potential sympathetic detonation of propellants (prompt 



propagation)



• TT 01-04 – evaluation of a transfer tube-related failure



• CB 01-05 – focus on common bulkhead failure to simulate internal commodity mixing within 



a ruptured tank



All CBM 2 tests conducted at Eglin AFB, FL

Analysis Approach



Definitions:



QLDA – Quick-look Data Analysis. This is a preliminary analysis package for a test or test series that 



is conducted as quickly as possible upon availability of data. The primary intent is to provide 



actionable feedback to the test team and stakeholders as quickly as possible in order to improve 



future test design and/or data collection. QLDAs are typically produced within one week or less of 



data availability.



ODA – Optimized Data Analysis. A final data analysis package for a test or test series. Primary goal 



is to develop and present key data, observations, and findings in a form that is most useful to the 



PBM and EMT, as well as test teams and stakeholders. 



Note: A QLDA was developed for each Prompt Propagation (PP) test. This ODA includes the entire 



PP Test Series.











26



Analysis Approach: Overview



For all tests, a QLDA with pressure- and impulse-based EEWs was developed using the JPL Explosion Data 



Interface (JEDI) tool and analyses conducted by Aerospace Corp. These analyses included all reliable blast 



data recorded on the tests.



ERDC conducted manual, optimized data processing for pressure records within the TB 700 window for 



selected PP tests to confirm data processing accuracy. These data were then used to determine EEWs. The 



ERDC analyses served as an additional confirmation of the JPL and Aero-reported EEWs, were applicable. 



Note that for Test CB2_100_PP04, Aero did not conduct data analysis; for this test, ERDC conducted an 



automated analysis using the ERDC EDNA tool.



The data processing and analysis methods used by Aero, JPL, and ERDC, while different to some degree, fall 



within the range of accepted blast data analysis. Because of this, the analyses represent the spectrum of 



expected yield values and uncertainty bounds* that might reasonably be expected from the provided blast 



datasets. The following slides detail the analysis methods used by each entity. 



*Uncertainty represents 95% confidence bounds from the T-Student distribution, which describes the confidence that the true test yield falls 



within the reported range based on the methodology used. It does not consider uncertainty due to sensor error, far-field criteria, parameter 



selection, test-to-test variability, test behavior, test setup, comparison to TNT, or TNT equivalency model.











27



Analysis Approach: JPL



JPL utilized the JPL Explosion Data Interface* (JEDI) v1.0 for all analyses. JEDI provides 



automatic baseline offset corrections to the raw pressure waveforms. The records are then 



analyzed using exponential fits and user-directed processing methods, such as moving-



average smoothing to account for noise transients. JEDI typically reports the following values:



• Hand-selected (with/without SL-correction)



• Local maxima (with/without SL-correction)



• Exponential fit (with/without SL-correction)



• Moving average (with/without SL-correction)



• JEDI also identified questionable data records not suitable for use in yield determinations.



For the PRC Phase 1 Test Series, JEDI determined % TNT yield from the Kingery-Bulmash 



DOE TIC-11268 hemispherical model based on peak overpressures and positive phase 



impulses within the range 0.5-10 psi, where peak overpressure is hand-selected and both 



pressure and impulse are corrected for sea level conditions. All uncertainties reported 



represent 95% confidence bounds according to the T-Student distribution.



*Contact NASA JPL, Aerospace Nuclear Safety Engineering for information regarding this tool which was developed by Noelle Wells, Don Li, Shervin Taghavi and Elan Borenstein.

Analysis Approach: Aero



Analysis performed using Aerospace-developed tool that expedites data entry, reduction, analysis, 



and results formatting. Minor data cleaning automatically performed before analysis



– Data dropouts removed; pre-blast pressure tared as needed



– Identified questionable data records not suitable for use in yield determinations.



– Standardized to windless sea-level conditions



– Slant heights used for elevated gauge sensors



Three independent methods are used to assess pressure data and produce unique estimations of 



explosive equivalent weight (EEW) and yield



– Positive-phase impulse (PPI): integrates pressure over positive phase response



– Peak overpressure: pressure at ToA using best-fit curve



• Peak overpressures determined via exponential fit and Friedlander curve fit to positive pressure portion of 



waveform (Friedlander analysis results only are reported in this document)



– Time of arrival (ToA): yield from derived ignition time (not reported, for comparison only)



Far-field yield is used for overall explosive yield calculations



– Per TB 700-2, defined as region where overpressure is 0.5 – 10 psig

Analysis Approach: ERDC



ERDC conducted manual, optimized data processing of select pressure records within the TB 700-2 



window for certain tests.



The ERDC methodology utilizes manual, user-directed modifications to the raw recorded data time histories to 



minimize noise and extraneous readings. Processes typically included the following (as-needed):



1. Truncation of records to begin at zero-time and conclude when the pressure record returned to a stable 



baseline after the end of the negative overpressure phase.



2. Baseline correction. Typically, this consisted of a constant value added or subtracted from the record to 



provide a mean zero pressure reading prior to shock arrival. In some cases, a variable baseline correction 



was applied to better account for baseline drift during the pressure event due to thermal-induced sensor 



response or other factors.



3. Filtering via either low-pass filter application or the use of smoothing techniques to minimize noise 



transients that impeded accurate selection of pressure peaks.



ERDC also identified questionable data records not suitable for use in yield determinations and produced csv 



files of the final, processed records.

CBM2_100_P02 JPL Analysis Summary 



Aero and JPL-assessed yields were in good agreement



Yields were consistent with expected values for C4, considering the cylindrical charge shape and effect of the 



water-filled test article above the C4 charge.



Both overpressure-based and impulse-based yields were somewhat lower than for Test P01 without the water-



filled test article. This is believed to be due to the presence of the test article directly above the C4 charge, which 



serves to suppress initial upward blast propagation while directing more blast energy downward. This significantly 



changes the local blast field relative to the C4-only case.



Because of its similarity to the PP01-04 tests which feature a propellant-filled test article above the C4 charge, the 



yields from Test P02 were used as the basis for determining yields from the propellant contribution for those tests.

Observations*



1. Assessed yields** for baseline Test P01 (C4 charge only) were within expectations for C4, given the 



cylindrical charge shape.



2. Averaged overpressure-based yield for baseline Test P02 (water-filled test article) were ~7% lower on a 



mass basis as compared to those for Test P01 (C4 charge only); averaged impulse based yield was 



~18% lower than P01 on a mass basis. This is most likely due to the presence of the test article/water 



mass altering the local blast field, which is believed to have affected positive overpressure phase 



durations similarly to the case of a propellant-filled article. It is not believed that the water mass adversely 



affected reaction of the C4 explosive; C4 has well-documented performance in underwater blasting 



applications.



3. Tests PP01 and PP02 (LOX over LNG) produced negligible overpressure-based yield above that for 



baseline Test P02; impulse-based yields on a percentage basis increased minimally (4-6%, just beyond

5. A possible reason for the higher yields for LNG over LOX is that in this testing configuration, the LOX was

• Current state of uncertainty reporting:



– Individual analysts provide yield uncertainty purely from variance in far-field signal yields



• Tied to the data, but does not include all sources of uncertainty in the data



– Everything from the event itself to capturing of the data (e.g. sensor uncertainty) to interpretation (e.g. parameter, yield model)



– Matters most in the very far-field (yield is highly sensitive to small variations for peak overpressure < 1 psi)



» Why low-yield events are best reported as “low yield”



• Best to provide clarity on what this uncertainty is and is not wherever it is reported



• Statistical methods for determining uncertainty (e.g. T-Student distribution) only work for 



independent samples (e.g. x uncorrelated samples in the 0.5-10 psi range)



• When we begin merging yields and uncertainties from individual entities for a single test, we are 



dealing with correlated samples
– E.g. x samples analyzed one way merged with x samples analyzed another way are the same sample set



– This is no longer a statistical problem because it is not statistical variance, rather method differentiation



– Risks of treating this as a statistical problem: artificially increase the sample size; convoluted results; misrepresent the source of 



uncertainty; sideline the benefit of having several analysts in the first place
