<p>CBM 2 Prompt Propagation Tests ODA:
 Introduction
</p>
<p>An Optimized Data Analysis or ODA is a final data analysis package for a test or test series. The primary goal 
</p>
<p>is to develop and present key data, observations, and findings in a form that is most useful to the LOX/Methane 
</p>
<p>Assessment Physics-Based Modeling and Engineering Models Teams, as well as test teams and stakeholders. 
</p>
<p>This ODA expands and builds upon the Quick Look Data Analysis (QLDA) completed by the LMA Data 
</p>
<p>Analysis Subteam (DAS) for each Prompt Propagation test. Please note that values reported in the ODA often 
</p>
<p>differ from the preliminary values reported in the relevant QLDAs. This ODA represents the FINAL, most 
</p>
<p>accurate data values and yield assessments for each included test event. 
</p>
<p>This ODA provides analyses of the blast data recorded on the CBM 2 Prompt Propagation (CBM 2_PP01-04) 
</p>
<p>Tests and associated baseline tests CBM 2_100_P01 and P02. The CBM designation refers to “Confined by 
</p>
<p>Missile”, an explosion accident scenario wherein a propellant leakage occurs while being confined within the 
</p>
<p>physical structure of a missile or launch vehicle. The PP (Prompt Propagation) tests each featured a nominal 
</p>
<p>100 lbm of LOX/LNG in a stacked tank configuration, with a 25 lbm C4 explosive charge placed in contact with 
</p>
<p>the bottom of the test article. The tests were designed to quantify and understand the contribution to yield from 
</p>
<p>unmixed LOX/LNG should a detonation occur in close proximity (e.g., sympathetic detonation of an upper 
</p>
<p>stage should a lower stage detonate). 
</p>

<p>CBM 2 Prompt Propagation Tests ODA:
 Introduction (Concluded)
</p>
<p>The CBM 2_PP01-02 Tests evaluated prompt propagation for the case of LOX over LNG; for these tests 
</p>
<p>LNG was in the lower tank with the C4 charge placed in contact with the bottom of the test article. The CBM 
</p>
<p>2_PP03-04 Tests evaluated prompt propagation for the case of LNG over LOX; for these tests LOX was in 
</p>
<p>the lower tank with the C4 charge placed in contact with the bottom of the test article. 
</p>
<p>Baseline explosive tests (designated as P01 and P02) were also conducted featuring just the C4 explosive 
</p>
<p>charge (P01) and the C4 charge placed under a water-filled test article (P02) as a means of establishing the 
</p>
<p>relative contribution of LOX/LNG on Tests PP01-04. Analyses focused on blast overpressure data within the 
</p>
<p>TB 700 window (10-0.5 psi peak pressure) in order to provide assessments of explosive yield. Note that 
</p>
<p>explosive outputs are reported as equivalent explosive weight (EEW) in lbm of TNT and yield 
</p>
<p>percentage with TNT=100% for a hemispherical surface burst condition.

<p>CBM 2_PP ODA: Executive Summary
</p>
<p>The following observations* are offered based on the results:
</p>
<p>1. Assessed yields** for baseline Test P01 (C4 charge only) were within expectations for C4, given the 
</p>
<p>cylindrical charge shape.
</p>
<p>2. Averaged overpressure-based yield for baseline Test P02 (water-filled test article) were ~7% lower on 
</p>
<p>a mass basis as compared to those for Test P01 (C4 charge only); averaged impulse based yield was 
</p>
<p>~18% lower than P01 on a mass basis. This is most likely due to the presence of the test article/water 
</p>
<p>mass altering the local blast field, which is believed to have affected positive overpressure phase 
</p>
<p>durations similarly to the case of a propellant-filled article. It is not believed that the water mass 
</p>
<p>adversely affected reaction of the C4 explosive; C4 has well-documented performance in underwater 
</p>
<p>blasting applications.
</p>
<p>3. Tests PP01 and PP02 (LOX over LNG) produced negligible overpressure-based yield above that for 
</p>
<p>baseline Test P02; impulse-based yields on a percentage basis increased minimally (4-6%, just 
</p>
<p>beyond the analyzed yield uncertainty bounds).
</p>
<p>8
</p>
<p>* Observations apply only to specific scenarios evaluated in this test series. Additional testing/modeling is required to determine broader applicability.
</p>
<p>** Yields listed do not include uncertainty bounds, which are included in the individual test sections of this document. In all cases mass-based yields 
</p>
<p>are in lb of TNT hemispherical surface burst yield; for percentage-based yields 100% = TNT hemispherical surface burst yield.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Observations* (continued):
</p>
<p>4. On a percentage basis, Tests PP03 and PP04 (LNG over LOX) produced overpressure-based 
</p>
<p>yields** 8-13% higher than baseline Test P02; impulse-based yields increased 17-21%.
</p>
<p>5. A possible reason for the higher yields for LNG over LOX is that in this testing configuration, the 
</p>
<p>LOX was in closest proximity to the C4 donor charge, potentially introducing O2 at sufficiently 
</p>
<p>early times to enhance afterburning of the C4 detonation products. Afterburning contribution to 
</p>
<p>yield is not representative of a likely prompt propagation scenario and thus caution is advised 
</p>
<p>regarding the observed increase in yields reported herein for LOX in closest contact with the 
</p>
<p>donor charge.
</p>
<p>6. Higher impulse-based yields indicate that deflagration of the propellants was a primary 
</p>
<p>contributor to yields for both configurations.
</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>CBM 2_PP Tests Description
</p>
<p>10</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>• Commercial launch providers are actively developing launch vehicles (LV) that will 
</p>
<p>utilize LOX and LCH4 (a.k.a. liquid natural gas/LNG) as the rocket propellants. 
</p>
<p>Insufficient testing has been conducted to fully characterize the explosive risk 
</p>
<p>associated with the LOX/LNG propellant combination. 
</p>
<p>• Preliminary testing suggests that these propellants may be highly energetic and 
</p>
<p>extremely hazardous if not properly controlled. Therefore, it is necessary to conduct 
</p>
<p>appropriate testing to define the explosive characteristics and safety criteria for safe 
</p>
<p>handling and use. 
</p>
<p>• The CBM 2 testing campaign is designed to simulate a launch vehicle failure case 
</p>
<p>where propellants can mix due to the failure of common or intermediate bulkheads. 
</p>
<p>To establish the NEW and the explosive environment produced from internal failures 
</p>
<p>of operational system tankage these tests will determine the explosive hazard 
</p>
<p>potential of the liquid propellant combination under confined mixing conditions.
</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>CBM 2 Test Program Overview
</p>
<p>The CBM 2 Test Program is being conducted as part of the NASA-USSF sponsored 
</p>
<p>LOX/Methane (LNG) Assessment, or LMA. 
</p>
<p>CBM 2 Test Program was designed to examine consequences of a static launch pad explosion 
</p>
<p>with nominal propellant masses ranging from 100 to 20,000 lbm. CBM 2 consists of the 
</p>
<p>following test series:
</p>
<p>• PP 01-04 – evaluation of potential sympathetic detonation of propellants (prompt 
</p>
<p>propagation)
</p>
<p>• TT 01-04 – evaluation of a transfer tube-related failure
</p>
<p>• CB 01-05 – focus on common bulkhead failure to simulate internal commodity mixing within 
</p>
<p>a ruptured tank
</p>
<p>All CBM 2 tests conducted at Eglin AFB, FL
</p>

<p>CBM 2 Prompt Propagation Test Series Description
</p>
<p>CBM 2 Prompt Propagation Test Series Description
</p>
<p>The 100 lbm LOX/LNG prompt propagation tests (CBM2_100_PP01 to PP04) were designed to quantify and understand the
</p>
<p>contribution to yield from unmixed LOX/LNG should a detonation occur in close proximity (e.g., sympathetic detonation of an
</p>
<p>upper stage should a lower stage detonate).
</p>
<p>To simulate an explosion at the base of a LOX/LNG stage, 25lbm of C-4 was placed directly in contact with the bottom of a 
</p>
<p>test article containing a nominal 100 lbm of LOX/LNG propellant.
</p>
<p>LOX/Methane Test Article
</p>
<p>• Elevated test articles (bottom 3 ft above GL)
</p>
<p>• Testing both LOX over LNG and LNG over LOX
</p>
<p>• OD = ~10 in
</p>
<p>• Overall Length = ~57 in
</p>
<p>• Structural L/D ~5.7; Propellant L/D &lt; 5.7
</p>
<p>• Transfer Tube in Lower Tank:
</p>
<p>• L = 26.5 in
</p>
<p>• D = 1.0 in
</p>
<p>C-4 Charge
</p>
<p>C-4 Charge. 
</p>
<p>Roughly to scale
</p>
<p>• 25 lbm, 10-in diameter x 5.25 in high cylinder
</p>
<p>• Mounted on Base of Bottom Tank (LOX or LNG)
</p>
<p>• Density of C-4 ~ 1.6 – 1.72 g/m^3 Taken from Trip Report by D. Allgood</p>

<p>Analysis Approach
</p>
<p>Definitions:
</p>
<p>QLDA – Quick-look Data Analysis. This is a preliminary analysis package for a test or test series that 
</p>
<p>is conducted as quickly as possible upon availability of data. The primary intent is to provide 
</p>
<p>actionable feedback to the test team and stakeholders as quickly as possible in order to improve 
</p>
<p>future test design and/or data collection. QLDAs are typically produced within one week or less of 
</p>
<p>data availability.
</p>
<p>ODA – Optimized Data Analysis. A final data analysis package for a test or test series. Primary goal 
</p>
<p>is to develop and present key data, observations, and findings in a form that is most useful to the 
</p>
<p>PBM and EMT, as well as test teams and stakeholders. 
</p>
<p>Note: A QLDA was developed for each Prompt Propagation (PP) test. This ODA includes the entire 
</p>
<p>PP Test Series.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>26
</p>
<p>Analysis Approach: Overview
</p>
<p>For all tests, a QLDA with pressure- and impulse-based EEWs was developed using the JPL Explosion Data 
</p>
<p>Interface (JEDI) tool and analyses conducted by Aerospace Corp. These analyses included all reliable blast 
</p>
<p>data recorded on the tests.
</p>
<p>ERDC conducted manual, optimized data processing for pressure records within the TB 700 window for 
</p>
<p>selected PP tests to confirm data processing accuracy. These data were then used to determine EEWs. The 
</p>
<p>ERDC analyses served as an additional confirmation of the JPL and Aero-reported EEWs, were applicable. 
</p>
<p>Note that for Test CB2_100_PP04, Aero did not conduct data analysis; for this test, ERDC conducted an 
</p>
<p>automated analysis using the ERDC EDNA tool.
</p>
<p>The data processing and analysis methods used by Aero, JPL, and ERDC, while different to some degree, fall 
</p>
<p>within the range of accepted blast data analysis. Because of this, the analyses represent the spectrum of 
</p>
<p>expected yield values and uncertainty bounds* that might reasonably be expected from the provided blast 
</p>
<p>datasets. The following slides detail the analysis methods used by each entity. 
</p>
<p>*Uncertainty represents 95% confidence bounds from the T-Student distribution, which describes the confidence that the true test yield falls 
</p>
<p>within the reported range based on the methodology used. It does not consider uncertainty due to sensor error, far-field criteria, parameter 
</p>
<p>selection, test-to-test variability, test behavior, test setup, comparison to TNT, or TNT equivalency model.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>27
</p>
<p>Analysis Approach: JPL
</p>
<p>JPL utilized the JPL Explosion Data Interface* (JEDI) v1.0 for all analyses. JEDI provides 
</p>
<p>automatic baseline offset corrections to the raw pressure waveforms. The records are then 
</p>
<p>analyzed using exponential fits and user-directed processing methods, such as moving-
</p>
<p>average smoothing to account for noise transients. JEDI typically reports the following values:
</p>
<p>• Hand-selected (with/without SL-correction)
</p>
<p>• Local maxima (with/without SL-correction)
</p>
<p>• Exponential fit (with/without SL-correction)
</p>
<p>• Moving average (with/without SL-correction)
</p>
<p>• JEDI also identified questionable data records not suitable for use in yield determinations.
</p>
<p>For the PRC Phase 1 Test Series, JEDI determined % TNT yield from the Kingery-Bulmash 
</p>
<p>DOE TIC-11268 hemispherical model based on peak overpressures and positive phase 
</p>
<p>impulses within the range 0.5-10 psi, where peak overpressure is hand-selected and both 
</p>
<p>pressure and impulse are corrected for sea level conditions. All uncertainties reported 
</p>
<p>represent 95% confidence bounds according to the T-Student distribution.
</p>
<p>*Contact NASA JPL, Aerospace Nuclear Safety Engineering for information regarding this tool which was developed by Noelle Wells, Don Li, Shervin Taghavi and Elan Borenstein.</p>

<p>Analysis Approach: Aero
</p>
<p>Analysis performed using Aerospace-developed tool that expedites data entry, reduction, analysis, 
</p>
<p>and results formatting. Minor data cleaning automatically performed before analysis
</p>
<p>– Data dropouts removed; pre-blast pressure tared as needed
</p>
<p>– Identified questionable data records not suitable for use in yield determinations.
</p>
<p>– Standardized to windless sea-level conditions
</p>
<p>– Slant heights used for elevated gauge sensors
</p>
<p>Three independent methods are used to assess pressure data and produce unique estimations of 
</p>
<p>explosive equivalent weight (EEW) and yield
</p>
<p>– Positive-phase impulse (PPI): integrates pressure over positive phase response
</p>
<p>– Peak overpressure: pressure at ToA using best-fit curve
</p>
<p>• Peak overpressures determined via exponential fit and Friedlander curve fit to positive pressure portion of 
</p>
<p>waveform (Friedlander analysis results only are reported in this document)
</p>
<p>– Time of arrival (ToA): yield from derived ignition time (not reported, for comparison only)
</p>
<p>Far-field yield is used for overall explosive yield calculations
</p>
<p>– Per TB 700-2, defined as region where overpressure is 0.5 – 10 psig</p>
<p></p>

<p>Analysis Approach: Aero Correlation Fits
</p>
<p>TNT correlations map scaled distance (Z) to blast 
</p>
<p>parameters (peak overpressure, scaled impulse)
</p>
<p>– Aerospace’s UFC-3-340 curve fit to UFC-3-340-02 tabulated data 
</p>
<p>used for peak overpressure
</p>
<p>– Kingery &amp; Bulmash 1984 equation used for scaled impulse
</p>
<p>In Z vs blast parameter domain, correlation curve is static, 
</p>
<p>and data points shift based on assumed EEW
</p>
<p>– X values in units of [ft/lb1/3] shift left or right based on averaged 
</p>
<p>far-field EEW, as indicated in upper-right plot label
</p>
<p>– Y values are static based on analytically determined 
</p>
<p>overpressure/impulse
</p>
<p>Proximity of points to dashed line indicates how closely 
</p>
<p>test data aligns with TNT correlation
</p>
<p>– Points left of curve indicate yield was less than expected for those 
</p>
<p>sensors based on far-field EEW, and vice versa
</p>
<p>A higher EEW shifts 
</p>
<p>points to the left
</p>
<p>EEW set to far-field average, 
</p>
<p>so far-field data should 
</p>
<p>appear centered on the line
</p>
<p>A lower EEW shifts 
</p>
<p>points to the right
</p>

<p>Analysis Approach: ERDC
</p>
<p>ERDC conducted manual, optimized data processing of select pressure records within the TB 700-2 
</p>
<p>window for certain tests.
</p>
<p>The ERDC methodology utilizes manual, user-directed modifications to the raw recorded data time histories to 
</p>
<p>minimize noise and extraneous readings. Processes typically included the following (as-needed):
</p>
<p>1. Truncation of records to begin at zero-time and conclude when the pressure record returned to a stable 
</p>
<p>baseline after the end of the negative overpressure phase.
</p>
<p>2. Baseline correction. Typically, this consisted of a constant value added or subtracted from the record to 
</p>
<p>provide a mean zero pressure reading prior to shock arrival. In some cases, a variable baseline correction 
</p>
<p>was applied to better account for baseline drift during the pressure event due to thermal-induced sensor 
</p>
<p>response or other factors.
</p>
<p>3. Filtering via either low-pass filter application or the use of smoothing techniques to minimize noise 
</p>
<p>transients that impeded accurate selection of pressure peaks.
</p>
<p>ERDC also identified questionable data records not suitable for use in yield determinations and produced csv 
</p>
<p>files of the final, processed records.</p>

<p>• Current state of uncertainty reporting:
</p>
<p>– Individual analysts provide yield uncertainty purely from variance in far-field signal yields
</p>
<p>• Tied to the data, but does not include all sources of uncertainty in the data
</p>
<p>– Everything from the event itself to capturing of the data (e.g. sensor uncertainty) to interpretation (e.g. parameter, yield model)
</p>
<p>– Matters most in the very far-field (yield is highly sensitive to small variations for peak overpressure &lt; 1 psi)
</p>
<p>» Why low-yield events are best reported as “low yield”
</p>
<p>• Best to provide clarity on what this uncertainty is and is not wherever it is reported
</p>
<p>• Statistical methods for determining uncertainty (e.g. T-Student distribution) only work for 
</p>
<p>independent samples (e.g. x uncorrelated samples in the 0.5-10 psi range)
</p>
<p>• When we begin merging yields and uncertainties from individual entities for a single test, we are 
</p>
<p>dealing with correlated samples
– E.g. x samples analyzed one way merged with x samples analyzed another way are the same sample set
</p>
<p>– This is no longer a statistical problem because it is not statistical variance, rather method differentiation
</p>
<p>– Risks of treating this as a statistical problem: artificially increase the sample size; convoluted results; misrepresent the source of 
</p>
<p>uncertainty; sideline the benefit of having several analysts in the first place
