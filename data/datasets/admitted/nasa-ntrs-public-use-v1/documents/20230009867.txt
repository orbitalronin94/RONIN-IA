Launched in December 2017, the CERES Flight Model 6 scanned Earth for the first time, joining 5 other operational CERES instruments to extend a critical climate data record begun in 1997. CERES FM6 supports NASA’s continuing goal to understand and measure the solar energy reflected by Earth, the heat the planet emits, and the role of clouds in that process. The first data products were released to the public for the CERES Flight Model 6 (FM6) instrument on JPSS/NOAA-20 on June 13, 2018.  A year later the instrument on-orbit performance is excellent – functioning as designed.

Conclusions


NASA is observing earth radiation budget by CERES instruments since March 2000.


CERES instruments capture changes that influence radiation.


Frequency of large forest fire that influence clear-sky downward shortwave irradiance at a global scale is increasing.


Earth’s energy imbalance at the top-of-atmosphere is energy input to Earth’s climate system.


Both satellite and in situ observations show doubling of Earth’s energy imbalance from 2005 to 2019.








Contribution to Earth Energy Imbalance trend





The contribution depends on region


Cloud cover: e.g. low-level clouds are influenced by sea surface temperature increases





Surface: Sea ice extent and snow over land





Temperature: Increasing temperature reduces the imbalance





Other: e.g. increasing CO2 concentration reduces longwave emission to space.





Water vapor: increasing water vapor amount associated with a temperature increase increases absorption of solar radiation and reduces longwave emission to space.





Aerosols: decreasing aerosol loading increases absorption of solar radiation
