Research Motivation

• Distributed propulsion aircraft enabling 
Advanced Air Mobility (AAM) missions

▪ VTOL, STOL, and CTOL configurations

▪ Many control surfaces and propulsors

▪ Significant propulsion-airframe interactions

• Conventional methods fail to efficiently 
characterize complex aircraft

• Design of experiments (DOE) and response 
surface methodology (RSM) are essential

• Objective: investigate response surface 
experiment designs for complex aircraft 

• Application: aero-propulsive modeling suitable 
for flight dynamics simulation development

Response Surface Design Evaluation

Fraction of design space (FDS) plot.

• Pre-experiment design evaluation using: 

(1) statistical power, (2) correlation metrics, 

(3) prediction variance, (4) model parameter precision

• 𝐼-optimal and 𝐴-optimal designs are shown to have: 

▪ Highest statistical power for candidate model terms

▪ Lowest correlation among candidate model terms

▪ Lowest prediction variance across the design space

▪ Lowest model parameter standard errors

• FCCCD and distance-based designs generally have 

the worst design evaluation metrics

• 𝐷-optimal design generally has moderate values of 

design evaluation metrics

Sequential Design Evaluation

Concluding Remarks

• Distributed propulsion aircraft present new modeling challenges 

• DOE/RSM techniques provide efficient experiment designs that 

enable accurate characterization of complex aircraft

• 𝐼-optimal and 𝐴-optimal designs generally have the best pre-

experiment design evaluation metrics

• Sequential 𝐼-optimal base design had the best pre-experiment design 

evaluation metrics and yielded models with lower prediction error

• Nested 𝐼-optimal design approach is recommended for future aero-

propulsive characterization experiments for complex aircraft
