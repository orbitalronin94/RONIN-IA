<p>Introduction</p>
<p>ICESat-2 laser sends out about 2x1016 photons each 0.1 second; 96% of photons hitting a layer of snow gets back out of it after bouncing around inside the snow </p>
<p>In each 0.1 second, roughly 100,000 of those photons reach ICESat-2’s detector from snow surface </p>
<p>Our key finding: snow depth = ½ of the averaged distance those 10K or so photons traveling inside snow before coming out of it </p>
<p>Innovativeness: the random walk theory comes from other community (average distance of ants traveling inside their colony = 4 times of the volume / total surface area); </p>
<p>Monte Carlo radiative transfer calculations confirm that the theory applies to photons traveling inside snow, for various snow properties; </p>
<p>Snow depth derived from ICESat-2 measurements using this concept agrees with data ground-based  measurements</p>
</div>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Radiative Transfer Model Simulations suggest that laser light travels inside snow the same as ants traveling inside its colony (random walk): </p>
<p>Average distance light travels inside snow &lt;L&gt;  equals 4 * V / S  and it equals twice of the snow depth H  (Hu et al., 2022; Lu et al., 2022).   </p>
<p>assume A is the top part of snow surface area and A &gt;&gt; side area. ;   Snow volume = A * H</p>
<p>Snow surface area = areas of (top surface + bottom surface + sides) = 2A</p>
