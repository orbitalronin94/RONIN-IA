Introduction


ICESat-2 laser sends out about 2x1016 photons each 0.1 second; 96% of photons hitting a layer of snow gets back out of it after bouncing around inside the snow 


In each 0.1 second, roughly 100,000 of those photons reach ICESat-2’s detector from snow surface 


Our key finding: snow depth = ½ of the averaged distance those 10K or so photons traveling inside snow before coming out of it 


Innovativeness: the random walk theory comes from other community (average distance of ants traveling inside their colony = 4 times of the volume / total surface area); 


Monte Carlo radiative transfer calculations confirm that the theory applies to photons traveling inside snow, for various snow properties; 


Snow depth derived from ICESat-2 measurements using this concept agrees with data ground-based measurements























Uniform Incidence:


Valid for ALL scattering coefficients, scattering phase functions and physics shapes of the scattering media


Question: does the theory apply to space-based lidar measurements (different scattering geometry: nadir pointing laser beam and receiver) ?





Answer: Yes, through Monte Carlo simulations
