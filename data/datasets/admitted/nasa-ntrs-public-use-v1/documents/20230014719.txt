<div class="slide-content"><p>Cryogenic Propulsion Systems: A MSFC Strategic Imperative</p>
<p>The NASA strategic goals and initiatives rely heavily on cryogenic systems</p>
<p>Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) Strategic Framework – Go, Land, Live, Explore</p>
<p>Go – Cryogenic Fluid Management, Space Nuclear Propulsion, Advanced Propulsion</p>
<p>Land – Entry, Descent, and Landing; Large-Scale Lunar and Mars Global Access</p>
<p>Live – In-Situ Resource Utilization; Thermal Management Systems</p>
<p>Explore – Small Spacecraft Technologies</p>
<p>NASA Flagship Missions have historically been and continue to be dependent upon cryogenic system solutions</p>
<p>Human Lander System</p>
<p>Space Launch System</p>
<p>Space Nuclear Propulsion</p>
<p>MSFC is heavily committed to ensuring the success of these missions and the development of the associated mission-enabling technologies</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>MATCH Overview</p>
<p>Initially provides capability for liquid nitrogen and hydrogen, options for other cryogens in the future with minimal hardware change</p>
<p>All components LOX compatible, but lack appropriate cleanliness</p>
<p>Three dedicated flow legs</p>
<p>High Flow: Up to 250 GPM (LN2)</p>
<p>Medium Flow: Up to 125 GPM (LN2)</p>
<p>Low Flow: Up to 30 GPM (LN2)</p>
<p>Designed to interface with longstanding MSFC test facilities</p>
<p>Test Stands 300 and 400</p>
<p>CDA</p>
<p>4205 Cryo Lab</p>
<p>Supply Pressures up to 300psig</p>
<p>Higher pressure applications will require the addition of a boost pump downstream of the MATCH hardware</p>
