Project Goals
 




 
Goal #1
 
Develop and integrate an electronics manufacturing  system for multilayer electronic devices and sensors that can operate in microgravity and demonstrate their critical functions with parabolic flight testing that leads to a microgravity demonstration on the ISS.



 
Goal #2
 
Provide a technology maturation hub for microelectronics, sensors, and semiconductors commercialization development of a LEO Space Economy. 




 
Project Objectives 
 




 
Objective #1
 
Development, ground testing, and support of parabolic flight testing for direct ink write process and applications including via filling and chip placement. 



 
Objective #2
 
Development and ground testing of the Electrohydrodynamic Inkjet materials and process including feature resolution characterization, and parameter optimization.



 
Objective #3
 
Conduct an Interim Design Review of an Integrated Printed Electronics Printer Module.



 
Objective #4
 
Demonstrate the feasibility of printing electronic devices by a Laser Ablation Deposition process.



 
Objective #5
 
Development of laser curing for dielectric and conductive inks through a laboratory ground demonstration.

Update on ODME Partnerships & Collaborations: 


ODME Printer Module and Advanced Toolplate development for EDU and Parabolic Flight Unit (two separate ground dev units)


Redwire completed the Printer Module EDU on May 20. This unit has the identical Advanced Toolplate as the one on the Parabolic Flight Unit, which means the toolheads for the EDU are being flight validated.


Parabolic testing of the first 6 toolheads was completed in February. A redesign/optimization of the ink deposition tool was identified from the first flight campaign. ODME contracted for the upgrade and the newly optimized SmartPump tool was flight tested on August 19.


A newly developed Advanced Toolplate toolhead for Electrohydrodynamic (EHD) inkjet (semiconductors) has been developed in FY24 with ODME direction, with significant collaborative funding coming from STMD Flight Opportunities. This new tool was parabolic flight-tested August 20-22, 2024.


A new tool that was developed with significant finding from an InSPA SBIR award is an Electrodeposition (high performance communications and computing) head. Ground testing is in process with planned parabolic flight testing in early 2025.


ODME had significant participation and a leadership role in the NIST Advanced Manufacturing Roadmapping workshops in FY24. In FY24, ISM/ODME and Auburn University hosted an event in Huntsville, AL and the final roadmapping workshop was in Washington DC, attended by Curtis Hill ODME PI. A report will be generated and submitted to NIST Advanced Manufacturing office by fall 2024. A significant portion of this roadmapping focused on space and semiconductor manufacturing.


ODME CHIPS Semiconductor-related activities:


ODME is leading an Interagency working group to develop semiconductor manufacturing in LEO. Significant funding for wideband gap diamond semiconductors is expected in later FY24 or FY25 from Space Force for collaborative development.  


ODME is leading the collaborative integration with NSF for new SBIR awards focused on semiconductors and advanced materials for space manufacturing. Several new NSF Phase I SBIRs have already been awarded, with several others pending soon. Several SBIRs for space semiconductor related work have already been awarded.


A new Phase III SBIR project was kicked off with Goeppert, Inc. on November 2. This project is the development of a furnace and processing capability for fabricating high-quality MoS2 in microgravity. This new process and material may be an enabling technology for next-generation semiconductor transistors for higher packaging density and improved performance. Collaborators include MIT, Arizona State, and Texas A&M universities. The furnace has been completed and extensive material characterization is in process.


A new ODME-Texas A&M project started in Q2 FY24 to develop 2D semiconductor materials for applications in radiation hardening and ultimate transistor scaling.  This project is in parallel with and in support of the InSPA SBIR Phase III Goeppert – MIT development of a new microgravity furnace to fabricate space-optimized MoS2

ISM ODME Technical Progress and Results


10





ODME – EHD Inkjet development


Significant progress on the development of new inks for the EHD inkjet process and optimization of the nanoprinting process. A parabolic flight test campaign in August validated the integration of this process tool to the ODME Print Module Advanced Toolplate.


ODME – Dual Ablation Laser Post Processing


Development of the novel dual ablation system has been funded for FY24 by ODME. The research has already yielded the first 3D printed Schottky diode. This deposition system also has significant potential as a lunar (vacuum) manufacturing system for solar panel interconnects and other applications.


Flight Opportunities has awarded Auburn University one of the FY24 TechFlights awards in the ODME category. This will significantly increase the pace of development of this system and will result in two additional parabolic flight campaigns to validate this technology in zero G. NASA TechFlights 2023 Selections – NASA


ODME – semiconductor testing and characterization


Arizona State University is supporting the ODME SEADS semiconductor project with device testing, characterization, and feedback for materials optimization. New RRAM devices produced by EHD inkjet were tested in preparation for parabolic flight testing in August 2024. (INTEL)


Curtis Hill/ODME PI is a co-author of a new high-impact paper published by Stanford University, “Semiconductor Manufacturing in Low-Earth Orbit for Terrestrial Use.” This paper has been published (The Benefits of Semiconductor Manufacturing in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) for Terrestrial Use - NASA) as a summary of the Stanford Semiconductor Manufacturing in the Space Domain Workshop in March 2023. The paper already has over 6,000 views and 2,000 downloads.


ODME – Crew Health Sensing


ODME continued development of the AstroSense crew health wearable device in FY24. This includes the development of a sweat collection patch, integration of the sweat collection and printed cortisol sensor, and the final integration of the cortisol sensor with the AstroSense wireless wearable device. Further planned work includes miniaturization of the device (AstroSense Delta), integration with AFRL sensing of cortisol for pilots, and further automation of the manufacturing process.
