<p>• Satellite Orbital Debris Characterization 
Impact Test (SOCIT) hypervelocity impact 
test series
– December 1991 to January 1992
– Fourth test (SOCIT4) targeted a flight-ready 
</p>
<p>Navy Transit satellite bus
• Current breakup models like the NASA 
</p>
<p>Standard Satellite Breakup Model (SSBM) 
use SOCIT4 test results
– About 59% of the spacecraft’s total mass 
</p>
<p>analyzed
– ~810 recorded fragments or fragments groups
</p>
<p>• DebriSat experiment builds upon the 
previous impact test data by utilizing more 
modern materials typical of low Earth orbit 
(LEO) satellites today
– Better represents evolving materials and 
</p>
<p>technology that may fragment differently
• DebriSat's post-impact processing targets 
</p>
<p>collection of fragments greater than or 
equal to 2 mm
– 205K+ individual fragments recorded thus far
– ~60K have mass, dimensions, primary material, 
</p>
<p>shape recorded
</p>
<p>SOCIT &amp; DebriSat Impact Experiments
</p>
<p>SSBM well represented the major in 2009 
on-orbit collision involving Cosmos 2251 
</p>
<p>– a legacy spacecraft 
</p>
<p>High area-to-mass ratio materials not well 
represented in collisions with Fengyun-1C
</p>
<p>(2007) and Iridium 33 (2009)</p>

<p>3
</p>
<p>• Fragment database records involving shape assessment
– Mass, dimensions, volume, primary material, shape designation (from inspection)
</p>
<p>• Estimated size and shape
– DebriSat: point-clouds from imagery
</p>
<p>– SOCIT: manually measured with calipers
</p>
<p>• Mutually perpendicular Orthogonal-projection measurements (x ≥ y ≥ z) defined as:
– x is the longest projected dimension across the fragment
</p>
<p>– y is the longest projected dimension orthogonal to x
</p>
<p>– z is the longest projected dimension orthogonal to the x-y plane
</p>
<p>• Average of these orthogonal projections produces a characteristic length, LC, 
– A standard metric of size for space-debris observations
</p>
<p>– Independent of the aspect angle or remotely sensed object
</p>

<p>21
</p>
<p>• RCCs are an advantageous 
shape model
– Practical for hypervelocity-impact testing
– Characterize shapes w one parameter L:D
</p>
<p>• RCCs use different L:D estimators 
for rods, plates, and nuggets
– Inspectors’ shape designation predestines 
</p>
<p>the scope of population values
– Suggests that entire shape category might 
</p>
<p>be represented by a sample mean or 
simple distribution
</p>
<p>• Volume can be leveraged to provide 
more than one type of L:D estimate 
for intercomparisons
– Geometric (cylindrical) volume depends 
</p>
<p>only on physical dimensions
• May be an option for SOCIT data without 
</p>
<p>measured (bulk) volume
</p>
<p>– Mass-based volume (m/ρ) least dependent 
upon dimensional data
</p>
<p>• Likely the best option going forward for impact 
fragments bent during capture
</p>
