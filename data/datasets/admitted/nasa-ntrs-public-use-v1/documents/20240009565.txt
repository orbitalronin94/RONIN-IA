Results shown for Motor @ NRP











• Parametric modeling approach was developed to evaluate impact of MW-class EAP systems on 
vehicle-level performance for the TPH C-130H concept:



• Engineering-level models and mission profiles were created along with integrated approach to battery 
sizing and power management



• Based on payload, 2030-2050 EAP technology levels & cruise mode of operation:



• Motor @ NRP: 200—800 NM & 40-60% fuel savings [Maximize Fuel Savings]



• Turbine @ NRP: 600–1,000 NM & 25-35% fuel savings [Maximize Range]



• Key design constraints identified were battery and electric motor sizing:



• Trade-off between range performance and fuel-savings; increasing electric power draw results in larger 
battery size, off-loading payload allows for increased fuel/battery capacity



• Battery specific energy density improvements lead to simultaneous range/fuel savings improvements 
compared to baseline C-130H



• Insufficient motor power affects climb requirements and runway length (≥1.82 MW for TPH C-130H 
configuation) and insufficient battery capacity constrains range and cruise altitude (min. ~3.25 MWh)

Vehicle-Level Impacts of 



Aircraft Electrification



Advantages:



• Reduced fuel consumption → decreased CO2 & 



GHG emissions for short-haul missions



• EAP conversion efficiency (91.3%) higher than 



conventional gas turbine with Jet Fuel A (30%)



Disadvantages:



• Current pack-level battery specific energy 



density (~250 Wh/kg) 48x lower than Jet Fuel A 



(12,000 Wh/kg) → reduced payload and range 



capabilities



• EAP concept feasibility limited by current 



electrical system technology levels in terms of 



power density
