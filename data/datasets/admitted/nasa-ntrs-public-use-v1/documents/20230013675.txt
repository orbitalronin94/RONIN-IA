<p>Summary</p>
<p>Mars missions will require significantly more energy and much longer system service life, with more constrained departure windows, than lunar missions</p>
<p>Shorter total roundtrip duration missions could reduce crew health and performance concerns but pushes the challenge to the in-space transportation system</p>
<p>Four In-Space Transportation propulsion technologies are currently under consideration </p>
<p></p>
<p>Access Mars Transportation White Paper with this QR code or at www.nasa.gov/MoonToMarsArchitecture</p>

<p>15</p>

<p>Minimum Energy Long-Stay Class, ~ 3 Years Roundtrip</p>
<p>High Energy Short-Stay Class, ~ 2 Years Roundtrip</p>
<p></p>
<p>Increasing Energy = Exponential Increase in Vehicle Mass</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Higher energy Mars missions require vehicles that are multiple ISS mass equivalents</p>
<p>“When” is a very complicated decision to address. </p>
<p>What is an acceptable roundtrip duration for humans? 		What are the Surface Cargo pre-deployment needs?
How many rockets do we have and how often can we launch them? 	What transportation system performance can we afford? </p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>For human mission, we need to wait for alignment for BOTH legs of the trajectory to minimize energy</p>
<p>Higher energy missions can reduce total trip time at the expense of shorter Mars vicinity stay time</p>
<p>Energy needs also vary more, making these missions more challenging to plan</p>
