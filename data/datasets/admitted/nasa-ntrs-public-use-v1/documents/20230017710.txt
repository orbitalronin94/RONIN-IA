This paper explores the derivation of safe separation bounds for a heterogeneous group of =



Uncrewed Aerial Systems (UAS) that are assigned temporally deconflicted trajectories. Com-



pared to spatially deconflicted trajectories, temporal deconfliction can lead to higher traffic



capacities and a more efficient use of the available airspace. One challenge with this type of



deconfliction is that collisions can occur if some cooperating UAS are behind or ahead of sched-



ule. To overcome this risk, this paper derives a lower bound on the safety distance between



two heterogeneous UAS in the presence of bounded uncertainties. This safety distance can



be leveraged to inform trajectory generation algorithms. The proposed bound establishes a



rigorous safety margin when the fleet deviates from the planned trajectories, both temporally



and spatially. For its derivation the paper assumes the UAS implement a distributed coordina-



tion algorithm that allows the fleet to maintain their schedules synchronized within a bounded



temporal error, and a path-following algorithm that lets the vehicles track a target that moves



along the planned trajectory with a bounded spatial error.

II. Introduction
As the diversity and number of vehicles in the airspace increase, it is important to aid traffic controllers, automate



certain tasks, and certify that travel plans are safe within specified margins, even in the presence of uncertainties. In



addition, as the air traffic demand grows [1], increasing the traffic capacity of the existing infrastructure may require



less conservative solutions that maintain or improve the levels of safety that the aviation industry has historically



operated under. In this context of soaring traffic demand, the inclusion of unmanned systems in the airspace poses



an added challenge. In some scenarios, UAS operators resort to ad hoc safe separation bounds that are sometimes

of any UAS with the goal of moving closer to standardization. To this end, this paper proposes that UAS implement



two algorithms: a path-following algorithm that attempts to track a point in space with a bounded spatial error, and

embedded in the solutions of the trajectory generation problem. This use of a safety coefficient resembles standard



practices in structures and materials problems.



This paper is organized in three main sections. Section III captures the properties of the trajectories considered,



and the characteristics of the relevant underlying algorithms that control the UAS. These are formalized through a



series of assumptions. These assumptions are then used to define the problem of the design of safe separation bounds



for temporally deconflicted trajectories in the presence of uncertainties. Section IV provides a solution through the



derivation of a safe separation bound for each pair of heterogeneous UAS. Last, Section V presents the conclusions of



this paper, and suggests potential applications and extensions of the results presented.

V. Conclusion
This paper defines and solves a safe separation problem for a fleet of = heterogeneous UAS that are assigned



temporally deconflicted trajectories. This work assumes that the vehicles are equipped with a path-following algorithm



that can track a virtual target that slides along their trajectories within some bounded spatial error. To coordinate the



UAS as the mission evolves, even in the presence of external disturbances, the fleet is also equipped with a distributed



time-critical coordination algorithm. This protocol attempts to synchronize the vehicle schedules within a bounded



temporal error. The paper leverages these bounded spatial and temporal errors to propose a lower bound for the safety



distance between the vehicle trajectories. Embedded in the computation of the safe separation distance is a safety



coefficient that provides a normalized notion of the safety margins built into the trajectory generation problem. If



chosen within the bounds provided in this paper, the safety coefficients guarantee safe separation among all cooperating



UAS. Future work could leverage the proposed bound to identify areas of improvement in path-following, time-critical



coordination, and trajectory generation to safely improve the traffic capacity of the airspace. A clear extension of this



work could involve the design of well-clear geometries that resemble current standards as in detailed in [5, 6], instead



of the bounding sphere used here.
