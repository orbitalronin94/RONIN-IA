<p>INTRODUCTION
The NASA Satellite ClOud and Radiation Property retrieval System (SatCORPS) applies a suite of 
algorithms to meteorological satellite data to provide cloud properties, radiative fluxes and other 
parameters on a global scale.
</p>
<p>SatCORPS framework provides two separate data stream :
</p>
<p>Cloud Climate Data Records (supports NASA CERES/RBSP)
</p>
<p>• Global cloud analyses from MODIS, VIIRS and GEOsats since 2000 needed to determine 
Earth’s radiation budget and cloud feedbacks.
</p>
<p>• Algorithms designed for consistent application to 25+ satellites in the CERES record. 
</p>
<p>Operational applications and other research needs
</p>
<p>• Near-realtime and historical cloud analyses (mostly focused on global GEO’s).
</p>
<p>• Data used to support field campaigns, weather forecasting, cloud process research, model 
development (cloud parameterizations), etc.</p>

</p>
<p>Image quality – bad scan lines in radiance imagery Apply human visual or CNN QC for most cases and satellites; apply 
radiance reconstruction using KNN for severely corrupted images
</p>
<p>Day/Night Consistency (cloud optical properties) ANN to help overcome theoretical limits due to IR blackbody limit; 
KNN to extrapolate optical properties from daytime
</p>
<p>Data products in the solar terminator and sun-glint KNN to extrapolate information from surrounding space/time 
domain
</p>
<p>Assumption that clouds are single-layer and have vertically 
homogeneous phase and PSD’s
</p>
<p>New IWP/LWP parameterizations that better account for cloud 
vertical structure; ANN for multi-layer cloud retrieval methods
</p>
<p>Poor knowledge of land surface emission temperature 
(affects cloud mask and retrievals)
</p>
<p>DNN to correct model reanalysis skin temperature based on 
correlations with satellite-derived values in clear conditions
</p>
<p>Nighttime cloud detection in polar regions ANN trained with CALIPSO data for application to MODIS/VIIRS
</p>
<p>Cloud thickness and ceiling Parameterizations based on CloudSat/CALIPSO groundtruth; KNN
for satellite/ceilometer data fusion over U.S.

<p>Nighttime Cloud Optical Depth (COD)
</p>
<p>Problem:
• COD can be estimated theoretically from solar 
</p>
<p>channels over a wide range during daytime
• At night, only thin cloud retrievals are theoretically 
</p>
<p>possible (COD &lt; ~6)  due to IR blackbody limit
• Optically thick COD’s set to pre-assigned fill values 
</p>
<p>(not consistent with daytime retrievals)
• Other parameters derived from COD at night also 
</p>
<p>very inconsistent and less accurate than daytime
- Cloud ice and liquid water path
- Cloud geometric thickness and ceilings
- Radiative fluxes, especially SFC LW↓
- Aviation weather hazards, e.g. icing conditions
</p>
<p>2021 Annual Mean COD (DAY), Aqua-MODIS
Global Mean =  5.1 ; Non-polar Mean = 4.7
</p>
<p>2021 Annual Mean COD (NIGHT), Aqua-MODIS
Global Mean =  2.9 ; Non-polar Mean = 2.8
</p>
<p>Poor consistency between daytime and 
nighttime</p>

<p>Global Cloud Composites (GCC) STATUS
• A system is developed within the NASA SatCORPS to create hourly global composites of cloud properties 
</p>
<p>from modern GEO and LEO imagers on a 3-km grid.
</p>
<p>• A one-month (July 2021) version 1 test dataset has been produced for SatCORPS Stakeholders: 
https://satcorps.larc.nasa.gov/GCC-SNWG
</p>
<p>• Version 1 dataset derived with legacy SatCORPS cloud algorithms and empirical narrowband to broadband 
TOA radiative flux formulas.
</p>
<p>• A version 2 system is nearly complete (release date January 2024) 
• Used to produce annual historical datasets for SNWG ‘Clouds and Radiation’ stakeholders
• Operationalized to meet low latency operational needs within the weather community (e.g. 
</p>
<p>NCEP), sustainable energy industry (e.g. NASA POWER), DOE ARM and elsewhere.
</p>
<p>• Version 2 applies updated cloud algorithms that includes bug fixes, improved forward models, clear sky 
radiance maps, instrument calibrations, and atmospheric correction procedures.
</p>
<p>• Version 2 also applies AI/ML tools to correct level-0 satellite radiance artifacts (e.g. GOES-17 striping) and 
to derive more accurate and consistent level-2 cloud and radiation data products at all times of day
</p>
<p>• A computed TOA and SFC radiative flux component is in development that ingests GCC products into a 
radiative transfer modeling system (25-km hourly global grids). Release date: Spring 2024
</p>
<p>• Data products will be freely available, and geospatially-service enabled as ArcGIS Image Services and Open 
Geospatial Consortium (OGC) Web Mapping/Coverage Services for visualization and analysis.</p>
