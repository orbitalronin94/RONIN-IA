.
Confined By Missile (CBM)- Liquid Oxygen/Methane Assessment</p>
<p>Consideration of Liquid Oxygen (LOX) Liquid Methane (LNG) as the new primary rocket propellent in the space industry is an uncertain endeavor due to its lack of data and testing. The behavior of liquid methane in cryogenic environments is largely unknown due to its lack of historical usage.</p>
<p>There are safety concerns over its volatility and potential for catastrophic collateral damage in the event of premature mixing and ignition.</p>
<p>CBM testing will simulate a launch vehicle common bulkhead failure on the pad, allowing mixing of the propellants under confined conditions.</p>
<p>This testing will help to establish explosive limits as a function of propellent weight, a series of explosive tests with mixtures of Liquid Oxygen and Methane from 100 lbs to 150,000 lbs will be conducted.</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Elevated and ground-mounted blast sensors at a variety of distances and directions will collect both near-field/far-field measurements. High Speed Cameras (20,000+ frames per second) will be used to characterize the explosive center, blast waves, and primary fragmentation.</p>
<p>Testing goals include progressively larger explosions to identify the auto-ignition mass limit, quantify the explosive hazard of the propellants as a function of mixing time, and to collect sufficient data for predictive methods for extrapolating blast environments to larger quantities of propellant.</p>
<p>Small scale testing indicates that well mixed quantities of LOX/LNG form a solution called MOX and have TNT yields greater than that of C4 explosive.</p>
