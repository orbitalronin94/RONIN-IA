Reliable Ignition of LOX-Methane Propellants 
in Thermal Vacuum Environment
 TRL: Start 4 / Current 5
FY22 IRAD PROJECT Overview 
The goal of this project is to demonstrate reliable ignition of liquid oxygen-liquid methane at thermal vacuum conditions using a coil-on-plug (COP) sparking exciter. Testing was performed using EP’s Frost-Mint cryogenic propellant test stand, and an updated engine based on data collected from previous engine testing at Plum Brook Station, to determine conditions under which the cryogenic propellants will consistently and reliably ignite.

INNOVATION
No cryogenic reaction control system (RCS) has ever flown in space.
In previous thermal-vacuum (<-275°F) testing at NASA GRC Plum Brook, the team uncovered anomalous LO2/LCH4 engine ignition phenomena where flame kernels quenched at these ultra-cold hardware temperatures, leading to many pulses where the engines would not light. In addition, cryogenic propellants are baselined by HLS Appendix H partners, CLPS partners, are expected in App H proposals, and are the key use-case for future ISRU manufactured propellants. A reliable LO2/LCH4 reaction control system is an enabling technology for human lunar and Mars exploration.

Outcome 
In FY22 the project successfully completed the following milestones:
Modified Frost-Mint to include an integrated vacuum tube and liquid nitrogen cooling system enabling cold vacuum ignition testing
Designed, fabricated, and tested the Integrated GN2/LN2 Ultra-low-temperature Enclosure (IGLUE), a custom close-coupled heat exchanger shroud
Conditioned the thruster and propellants to the temperatures and pressures desired
Recreated no-light conditions experienced during vehicle-level thermal vacuum testing
(IN WORK) Modified the engine performance using trim orifices to demonstrate the appropriate “fix” for the no-light phenomenon
(IN WORK) Completed forward work described in AIAA-2017-4668

Infusion Space / Earth
Next steps include a final high-MR design iteration that produces a device that can ignite LO2/LCH4 in any conceivable environment. This work can directly inform HLS and CLPS partners by providing risk reduction efforts and anchored analyses to predict troublesome scenarios. Further anchor modeling of ignition quenching would be of interest to STMD, as well as HLS and CLPS contractors/subcontractors, and could include in-flight demonstrations of flight hardware, component verification and validation in flight-like environments for qualification or acceptance testing, and robust modeling efforts that predict no-go propellant or hardware conditions. Conference and/or journal papers will be written to disseminate the information gathered to all industry partners.
