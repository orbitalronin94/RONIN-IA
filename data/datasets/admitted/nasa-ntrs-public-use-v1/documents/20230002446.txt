<p>Two-coupled thermal-driven phenomena, Marangoni effect and thermal fractional decomposition under 
</p>
<p>high vacuum, previously observed by the authors could lead to an extraterrestrial mining operation that would 
</p>
<p>significantly reduce mechanical operation and allow in-situ product extraction directly from the mineral 
</p>
<p>without the necessity of neither mineral beneficiation nor use of terrestrial precursors. Thermal Marangoni 
</p>
<p>effect alone and coupled with fractional decomposition have been corroborated through paths  of 10 and 13 
</p>
<p>inches respectively on molten JSC-1A lunar regolith simulant presenting a novel and valuable potential for 
</p>
<p>extraterrestrial mining as the observed outcome (self -transportation via Marangoni effect and fractional 
</p>
<p>decomposition at higher temperature) will be more prominent on extraterrestrial surfaces under higher 
</p>
<p>vacuum and reduced gravity. A comprehensive 3D model built by the authors demonstrated to be a crucial tool 
</p>
<p>to determine the right location of the sample to optimize the gradient temperature along the wall of a long 
</p>
<p>tubular crucible enhancing the Marangoni effect as surface tension (the driving force for the thermal 
</p>
<p>Marangoni effect) depends on the temperature gradient.

<p>I. Introduction 
</p>
<p> The Marangoni effect is frequently observed while drinking wine with 12% or larger ethanol contains as a ring of 
</p>
<p>wine formed near the top of the glass generates droplets that fall back into the wine. In this phenomenon, known as 
</p>
<p>Tears of wine, the Marangoni effect is driven by the surface tension created via gradient on ethanol concentration and 
</p>
<p>temperature along the three-phase, liquid wine, solid wall, and gas air. Besides being observed while drinking wine, 
</p>
<p>the Marangoni effect is crucial for welding metals, manufacturing integrated circuits, and growing crystals. This paper 
</p>
<p>aims to establish that thermal Marangoni effect coupled with material decomposition under the high vacuum 
</p>
<p>conditions at the moon and asteroids have the potential to be crucial supporting extraterrestrial mining and other 
</p>
<p>interplanetary endeavors, as vacuum and reduced gravity are expected to augment the Marangoni effect on 
</p>
<p>extraterrestrial molten soil leading to sustainable extraterrestrial in-situ resources utilization (ISRU).  
</p>
<p>    Previous Marangoni effect assessment on molten lunar regolith via JSC-1A lunar regolith simulant under vacuum 
</p>
<p>generated a spontaneous upwards migration of a uniform molten thin-film front as well as at bulk scale that climbed 
</p>
<p>the crucible wall covering the entire wall’s surface and reaching the crucib le’s top end1. Further assessment on higher-
</p>
<p>temperature vacuum-driven decomposition of the uniform thin-film front as it climbed the crucible’s wall revealed a 
</p>
<p>significant decomposition of various simulant’s metal-oxide components into their respective oxygen and metal 
</p>
<p>elements strengthening the feasibility of extraterrestrial generation and fractional separation of oxygen and 
</p>
<p>metals/alloys with none or minimal need for terrestrial precursors. These two coupled phenomena, thermal Marangoni 
</p>
<p>effect and fractional decomposition, will be more prominent on extraterrestrial surfaces under much higher vacuum 
</p>
<p>and reduced gravity opening novel pathways in extraterrestrial mining and manufacturing 
</p>
<p>  The Thermal Marangoni effect alone and coupled with fractional decomp osition of metal oxides, further 
</p>
<p>segregation of the decomposed components, and oxygen (O2) release were observed on molten JSC-1A lunar regolith 
</p>
<p>simulant by the authors at Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC) and reported in this paper. The upwards migration 
</p>
<p>paths observed and reported in this paper, 10 and 13 inches respectively, are longer than those reported previosly1 
</p>
<p>validating further the potential utilization of these two coupled phenomena in  extraterrestrial mining. Segregation of 
</p>
<p>several decomposed components observed and reported here was neither observed previously 1.   
</p>
<p> Decomposition and release of O2 were confirmed by direct measurement of O2 via a 100 Atomic Molecular Unit 
</p>
<p>(AMU) Residual Gas Analyzer (RGA) installed on the downstream of the vacuum line outlet;  SEM/EDX analysis 
</p>
<p>was conducted on the melt samples removed at different positions in the upwards migration path to quantify the 
</p>
<p>composition of the melt and corroborate not only fractional decomposition but also further segregation.  
</p>
<p> The test stand designed and built by the authors can operate at temperatures as high as 1,600 Celsius and house 
</p>
<p>longer and wider tubular crucibles to carry larger samples with lengthier upwards migration paths. A comprehen sive 
</p>
<p>3D model built by the authors demonstrated to be a crucial tool to determine the optimal location of the tubular crucible 
</p>
<p>to obtain the largest temperature gradient along the crucible’s wall to boost the thermal Marangoni effect.  
</p>
<p>     Future extraterrestrial mining will require an operational approach completely different from the terrestrial one that 
</p>
<p>heavily relies on not only mechanical excavation and transportation but also on a stand -alone refinery process to 
</p>
<p>extract the mineral components. In this paper, the authors propose an extraterrestrial all-in-one mining process in 
</p>
<p>which the Marangoni effect would allow non-mechanical transportation of the extraterrestrial mineral to feed an also 
</p>
<p>in-situ pyrolysis-based refinery unit. 
</p>
<p>II.  Marangoni Effect 
</p>
<p>he Marangoni effect2 leads to the motion of liquids through a three-phase junction driven by surface tension 
</p>
<p>gradients generated by changes in composition and temperature, the main two variables that govern surface 
</p>
<p>tension. A surface tension gradient induced by local variations in composition is called solutal Marangoni effect3. 
</p>
<p>Surface tension gradients induced by changes in temperature are usually known as thermocapillary flow4, or thermal 
</p>
<p>Marangoni effect. In tears of wine5, a  classical example of solutal Marangoni effect, a  meniscus is formed at the three-
</p>
<p>phase junction between the wine glass walls, wine, and air then the liquid in the meniscus loosely clings to the surface 
</p>
<p>of the glass. The meniscus is formed because the walls of the glass have a hydroph ilic surface. Wine contains ethanol 
</p>
<p>that evaporates faster in the meniscus than in the flat bulk flat surface due to the meniscus higher surface area in 
</p>
<p>relation to its small volume. Therefore, it generates an ethanol concentration gradient between the men iscus and the 
</p>
<p>flat bulk causing a surface tension gradient that moves the meniscus up the walls of the glass. As the meniscus gets 
</p>
<p>more depleted of alcohol, which in turn causes a larger surface tension gradient, more wine gets pulled up the walls 
</p>
<p>of the glass until droplets form. Gravity takes effect and tears of wine run down the sides of the glass and back into 
</p>
<p>the flat bulk of the wine. 
</p>

<p>Thermal-driven Marangoni effect under vacuum causing upwards migration of oil caused by a temperature 
</p>
<p>gradient along the wall was reported by Tazioukov6. Experiments using different vacuum working fluids on metallic 
</p>
<p>surfaces, often used in vacuum technology, were used to prove the theoretical results. The results obtained provide a 
</p>
<p>better understanding of the surface migration process due to the temperature gradient and allow for control of the oil 
</p>
<p>film displacement. Kundan7 found that temperature gradients near the heated end of a wickless heat pipe in 
</p>
<p>microgravity might be high enough to generate a thermal Marangoni effect and lead thermocapillary forces that resist 
</p>
<p>the return flow of liquid from the cold end. The experiments were conducted at the International Space Station (ISS) 
</p>
<p>using a transparent heat pipe combination to generate images and thermal data.  
</p>
<p>III. Test Stand 
</p>
<p> A vertical-tubular Thermal High-Vacuum Chamber (THVC) has been designed and built to conduct the study and 
</p>
<p>characterization of both thermal-driven phenomena on molten extraterrestrial simulant samples, upwards migration 
</p>
<p>and fractional decomposition that are the fundamentals of the experimental work. Figure 1 illustrates a schematic (not 
</p>
<p>at scale) of the THVC that basically consists of (1) one meter long, 110-mm ID, 100-mm OD alumina tube, (2) a 508-
</p>
<p>mm long, 52-mm OD, 45-mm ID one-end-closed alumina tube housing the simulant sample and hanging from the top 
</p>
<p>SS flange, and 3) a top viewport equipped with a blue-laser (465-nm 
</p>
<p>wavelength) illumination system aimed to witness/record the upward 
</p>
<p>migration of the molten sample throughout the inner wall of the sample 
</p>
<p>holder tube.  The sample holder is long enough (508 mm) to allow the 
</p>
<p>molten sample to extensively migrate upwards and concurrently 
</p>
<p>decomposes into metals, alloys and oxygen driven by the fact that the 
</p>
<p>high vacuum present within the THVC allows the sample components 
</p>
<p>to decompose into their elements at lower temperature. Two SS flanges 
</p>
<p>are positioned at both ends of the main alumina tube, the top SS flange 
</p>
<p>is equipped with three ports, one is connected to the vacuum line, the 
</p>
<p>second one holds the viewport that allows the investigators to 
</p>
<p>witness/record the thermal-driven migration, and the third one is 
</p>
<p>connected to the Residual Gas Analyzer (RGA that measures gaseous 
</p>
<p>O2, water vapor, nitrogen (N2), carbon dioxide (CO2), and up to 100 
</p>
<p>Atomic-Molecular-Unit (AMU) gases. Two different vacuum setup 
</p>
<p>approaches are available to attempt reaching the highest vacuum level; 
</p>
<p>in the first approach two diffusion vacuum pumps are coupled to the top  
</p>
<p>and bottom sides of alumina tube and both connected with a scroll pump 
</p>
<p>as illustrated in Figure 1. In the second approach, the bottom diffusion 
</p>
<p>pump is eliminated while the top diffusion pump is replaced with a turbo 
</p>
<p>pump. SiC heater elements in direct conta ct with the main alumina tube 
</p>
<p>provides the heat source for the THVC. An insulation layer wraps the 
</p>
<p>heating elements and the main alumina tube providing the required 
</p>
<p>insulation along the heating zone. The one-end-closed tube housing the 
</p>
<p>sample is loaded on to the top of the main alumina tube by hanging it 
</p>
<p>from the top SS flange using two hooks. 
</p>
<p>The THVC is equipped with a single resistively heating zone intended to operate at temperatures on the order of 
</p>
<p>1,500 Celsius and 10-6 torr. The heating elements consisting of sixteen silicon carbide (SiC) rods are evenly spaced 
</p>
<p>forming a hot wall around the alumina tube. The alumina tube is sealed on both ends through silicone O-rings, 
</p>
<p>insulated by a set of concentric insulating tubes a nd blankets. Stable, programmable temperature control capability is 
</p>
<p>provided by a process controller system. 
</p>
<p>Key THVC’s process performance specifications include:  
</p>
<p>Work Volume:   100 mm diameter, approximately 20” long. 
</p>
<p>Operating Temperature: 1,600 Celsius Maximum 
</p>
<p>Maximum Ramp Rates: 20 Celsius per minute  
</p>
<p>Forced cooling Rate:  Process tube gas flow can cool sample 
</p>
<p>Operating Atmosphere: Vacuum down to 10-6 torr inside alumina tube. 
</p>
<p>The THVC’s equipment specifications include:  
</p>
<p>Process Chamber:  Alumina Tube, 1 meter long/110mm ID/100 mm OD Nominal

<p>VI. Summary 
</p>
<p>Thermal Marangoni effect alone and coupled with fractional decomposition of metal oxides releasing O2 have 
</p>
<p>been corroborated on molten JSC-1A lunar regolith simulant through long upwards migration paths, 10 and 13 inches 
</p>
<p>respectively validating further the proposed approach for extraterrestrial mining. These two coupled phenomena, 
</p>
<p>thermal Marangoni effect and fractional decomposition, will be more prominent on extraterrestrial surfaces under 
</p>
<p>much higher vacuum and reduced gravity.  
</p>
<p>The test stand designed and built by the authors can operate at temperatures as high as 1,600 Celsius and house 
</p>
<p>longer and wider tubular crucibles to carry larger samples with lengthier self -transportation path. The comprehensive 
</p>
<p>3D model built by the authors demonstrated to be a crucial tool to determine the optimal location of the tubular crucible 
</p>
<p>to obtain the largest temperature gradient along the crucible’s wall to boost the thermal Marangoni effect. SEM/EDX 
</p>
<p>analysis were conducted on melt samples along the upwards migration path to quantify and corroborate the fractional 
</p>
<p>decomposition and segregation of the melt.  
</p>
<p> Self-transportation, due to Marangoni effect, without fractional decomposition that would be required in the non -
</p>
<p>mechanical extraterrestrial mining approach was witnessed along 13 inches in the first case. Additionally, self -
</p>
<p>transportation coupled with fractional decomposition/segregation of the melt at higher temperature that would be 
</p>
<p>required for sustainable extraction/refinery and all-in-one extraterrestrial mining approach was also witnessed (and 
</p>
<p>quantified via SEM/EDX analysis) along 10 inches in the second case.  
</p>
<p> This novel approach might be an alternative to pursue sustainable environmental-friendly production of metals, 
</p>
<p>such as aluminum that generates more CO2 by weight than aluminum.       
</p>
