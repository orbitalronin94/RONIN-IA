<p>Quenching Two-Phase Pressure Drop
</p>
<p>• There are no models for predicting two-phase pressure drop 
</p>
<p>during transfer line chilldown
</p>
<p>▪ Controlling parameters and properties are very different for 
</p>
<p>cryogenic fluids compared to water and refrigerants
</p>
<p>▪ Existing models have only been developed for heating case
</p>
<p>▪ Most models have only been developed for room temperature 
</p>
<p>fluids
</p>
<p>▪ Existing models have not been validated against any cryogenic 
</p>
<p>quenching data
</p>
<p>▪ Fluid transient effects are not accounted for
</p>
<p>• Test a new 2m transfer section with detailed pressure drop 
</p>
<p>measurements &amp; flow visualization
</p>
<p>• Measure two-phase pressure drop across range of conditions
</p>
<p>• Develop a new UDF that accounts for frictional, accelerational, 
</p>
<p>gravitation, and transient pressure drop 
</p>
<p>• Feed UDF into higher order models to better predict flow rate 7</p>

<p>Universal Cryogenic Correlation Development and 
</p>
<p>Validation
</p>
<p>• In two phase flow literature, each new set of data yields a new set of correlations
</p>
<p>• Each correlation is generally only fit to a select group of data
</p>
<p>• Majority of correlations applicable only for room temperature liquids
</p>
<p>• Recent drive towards developing so-called universal correlations
</p>
<p>• Dimensionless groups generally fit to wide range of data
</p>
<p>• Requires careful filtering scheme
</p>
<p>• Universal correlations are highly desirable because they will enable design reference books for two 
</p>
<p>phase systems and simplify thermal/fluid models
</p>
<p>• Answers the question: With hundreds of correlations in the literature, which one do I use?
</p>
<p>• Aerospace Corp &amp; Purdue University developed the pioneering universal correlations for line 
</p>
<p>chilldown and flow boiling with heating, respectively
</p>
<p>• Correlations fed into GFSSP &amp; Thermal Desktop to predict: 1) location of ONB and CHF during 
</p>
<p>steady state heating, 2) set insulation requirements, 3) chilldown time, 4) chilldown mass
</p>
<p>11
</p>
<p>All correlations generally predict data within +/- 25% across all cryogens &amp; flow conditions
</p>
<p>Prediction of flow boiling heat transfer coefficients and zCHF improved by factor of 3-10 over base predictions</p>
<p></p>

<p>Injector Modeling
</p>
<p>• RGCT evaluated over a dozen models for how best to model saturated two-phase flow through 
</p>
<p>restrictions (valves, orifices, etc.)
</p>
<p>▪ Isentropic models
</p>
<p>▪ Isenthalpic models
</p>
<p>▪ Choked flow models
</p>
<p>• Needed to predict transient two-phase flow rate during tank chill &amp; fill, to bound expected flow 
</p>
<p>rate during prop transfer, and to determine choked flow conditions
</p>
<p>• Evaluated the models over historical data sets where two-phase flow was encountered at tank 
</p>
<p>inlet during tank chilldown
</p>
<p>• Explored sensitivities in operating near the saturation line for tank chilldown and fill
</p>
<p>• Work was leveraged by SpaceX on the 2020 Tipping Point to improve predictive performance for 
</p>
<p>multiple phases of their prop transfer, to improve mass flow rate predictions, and to reduce 
</p>
<p>uncertainty in propellant mass transferred
