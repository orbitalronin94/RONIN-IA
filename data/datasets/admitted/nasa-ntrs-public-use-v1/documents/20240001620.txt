<body><div class="slide-content"><p>NASA’s High-Temperature Superalloy: GRX-810</p>
<p>An Additively Manufactured (AM) Oxide Dispersion Strengthened (ODS) Breakthrough</p>
<p></p>
<p>Challenge</p>
<p>Conventional materials and processing limit the design and operating temperature of aerospace components.</p>
<p>Expected Impacts</p>
<p>Reduce fuel burn and lower operating costs </p>
<p>Reduce maintenance cost with increased durability</p>
<p>Create lighter and thinner engine parts </p>
<p>Provide new flexibilities for engine part design </p>
<p>Solution</p>
<p>Developed a material that can be AM and operate in temperatures above 2,000°F (1,093°C).</p>
<p>Used computational tools to discover the optimal alloy composition in less time and with lower costs than traditional trail-and-error processes.</p>
<p>This 3D printing manufacturing process is more efficient, cost effective, and cleaner than conventional methods.</p>
<p>Results vs. current state-of-the-art superalloys</p>
<p>ODS alloys offer significantly better higher-temperature capabilities</p>
<p>Next Steps</p>
<p>Publishing research on ODS alloy properties and characteristics </p>
<p>Manufacturing scale up to meet industry needs</p>
<p>35+ organizations interested in adopting Superalloy GRX-810</p>
<p>Issue licenses for Commercial Use</p>
