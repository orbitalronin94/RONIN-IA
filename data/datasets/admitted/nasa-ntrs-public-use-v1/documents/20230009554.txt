<p>BranchIntroduction
</p>
<p>• Future space-flight missions will need:
- sub-Kelvin detector cooling (at 50 mK or below) with higher heat loads 
</p>
<p>than past missions
- significant cooling at ~ 0.5 K – 4 K for optics/instrument
</p>
<p>• In 2016 NASA/GSFC proposed to develop a flight-ready 10 K to 0.05 K 
continuous adiabatic demagnetization refrigerator (CADR) to meet these needs
</p>
<p>• Original funding from NASA HQ for years 2017 – 2019
</p>
<p>• Additional intermittent funding through September, 2022
</p>
<p>• We report the progress on this effort
</p>

<p>Continuous
50 mK Stage
</p>
<p>• 4 K to 50 mK subsystem to be flight-worthy version of a lab CADR
• Heat switch between stages 1, 2 is superconducting; all others are gas-gap
• Salt pills:  gadolinium-gallium-garnet (GGG) and chrome potassium alum (CPA)
• Design included 10 K overall magnetic shield
</p>

<p>10 - 4 K CADR  Subsystem
</p>
<p>• Two parallel GGG salt pills alternating 
cooling/recycling
</p>
<p>• Includes two 10 K, shielded Nb3Sn 
magnets from Superconducting Systems
</p>
<p>• Includes four gas-gap heat switches      
(2 passive, 2 active)
</p>
<p>• Original target was 20 mW of 
continuous 4 K cooling
</p>
<p>• Early test with single stage suggested 
that 10 mW cooling more realistic
</p>
<p>• As 2-stage system began testing, one of 
the magnets failed.
</p>
<p>• Due to limited resources, this subsystem 
was shelved before arrival of 
replacement magnet 4</p>

<p>Branch4 K – 50 mK CADR Optimization
</p>
<p>11
</p>
<p>Many cooldowns, system modifications over 16 months of testing.
</p>
<p>• Magnets trained to appropriate operating currents after each cooldown from room temp.
</p>
<p>• Passive gas-gap heat switch fill levels tuned for appropriate on/of temperatures
</p>
<p>• CADR operating parameters tuned and varied to maximize heat lift
</p>
<p>• Two suspension legs (with earlier design) failed after 8, 12 cold cycles
- Epoxy joints sheared away from metal parts due to cycling
- We re-designed epoxy joints to include failsafe pins; replaced all legs
</p>
<p>• Sub-standard copper was used to fabricate the salt-pill-bus end pieces
- We assumed RRR = 50 – 100; actual values were 3.5 and 7;  (Pot Metal!)
- We re-made and replaced these parts;  RRR = 65
</p>
<p>• Our cryostat had a poor conductive path from cooler head to stage 4
- We bolted on flexible copper straps to enhance conduction
</p>
<p>• In June, 2023 we finally achieved nearly our target performance parameters</p>
<p></p>

<p>BranchTest Results Summary
</p>
<p>12
</p>
<p>• Useful cooling measured at different temperatures (rejecting to 4.0 K)
- 5.4 µW at 0.05K   (target was 6.0 µW)
</p>
<p>• “Closed” conductance of superconducting heat switch (SCHS) at 0.05K:  6 mW/K
- Will be compared with post-vibe value
</p>
<p>• “Open” SCHS conducts 4.6 µW from 0.3K to 0.08K
- Matches calculated value
</p>
<p>• Heat conducted into stage 1 @ 0.08K from 3.1K:  0.8 µW
- Calculated 0.33 µW; difference might be due to vibration heating?</p>

<p>BranchOptions to Improve Performance
</p>
<p>13
</p>
<p>• Use Gadolinium Lithium Fluoride (GLF) in Stage 4 salt pill
</p>
<p>• Use Yttrium Barium Gallium Garnet (YBGG) in Stage 3 salt pill
</p>
<p>• Use titanium 15-3-3-3 in all gas gap heat switch shells (in place of stainless steel)
</p>
<p>• Use ultra-pure annealed copper in SCHS
</p>
<p>• Increase effective Area/Length in Stage 1 copper linkage
</p>
<p>• Increase conductive link between Stage 4 and cryocooler in test cryostat</p>
