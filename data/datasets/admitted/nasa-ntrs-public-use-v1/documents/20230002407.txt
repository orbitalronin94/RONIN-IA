<p>Airports as Energy Nodes
Sustainable Energy Hubs to Benefit AAM Users and Local Communities
Challenge
- How can our existing infrastructure with thousands 
</p>
<p>of public-use airports simultaneously contribute to 
the growth of Advanced Air Mobility (AAM) and to the 
broader goal of net-zero emissions in all sectors?
</p>
<p>- How can airports handle the energy needs of AAM 
vehicles in the future?
</p>
<p>- How do we provide this energy in a sustainable, 
reliable fashion at low cost to users?
</p>
<p>- How do we leverage airport-hosted energy for non-
aviation use in the face of current regulations?
</p>
<p>Distribution of public-use airports in the CONUS
</p>
<p>Comparative land use in the CONUS vs. required area 
for net-zero electricity generation in 2035 (NREL)
</p>
<p>Expected Impacts
- AAM vehicles can catalyze renewable energy harvesting, 
</p>
<p>conversion, and distribution at airports
- Enhance community resiliency to power outages
- Reduce local emissions and encourage low- and 
</p>
<p>zero-emission aircraft and ground vehicles to 
recharge/refuel with renewable energy
</p>
<p>- Enhance macrogrid stability through onsite 
distributed storage
</p>
<p>- Generate “green” fuels from harvested energy and 
local feedstocks
</p>
<p>Partners and/or Participants
- National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL)
- NASA Aeronautics Research Mission Directorate Intercenter
</p>
<p>Systems Analysis Team (ISAT)
- Georgia Institute of Technology (via ISAT)
- Colorado Department of Transportation
- Newport News/Williamsburg Airport
- Colorado Springs Airport
</p>
<p>Proposed Solution
- Conduct hardware-in-the-loop (HWIL) demonstration of airport 
</p>
<p>microgrid that includes renewable harvesting (solar), onsite 
storage (battery and H2), onsite conversion (electrolysis), onsite 
liquefaction (LH2), and distribution to end-use (facilities, 
equipment, aircraft, and external)
</p>
<p>- Demonstrate use through different airport archetypes and 
operational scenarios, both nominal and off-nominal
</p>
<p>- Explore different aviation use cases and policy scenarios, 
including utility interaction for grid stability
</p>
<p>Results
- Developed cost-optimal trades for onsite power harvesting and 
</p>
<p>storage vs. grid upgrades for battery-electric regional air mobility 
(RAM) scenarios to date
</p>
<p>- Consider backup power generation and grid stability, as well as 
hydrogen life cycle in future iterations
</p>
<p>- Leverage NREL platforms for partial HWIL demonstrations
</p>
<p>Amortized cost of electricity for aircraft use
</p>
<p>Energy use and mix for battery-electric RAM aircraft 
for a scenarios in the northeast region (NREL)
</p>
<p>Next Steps
- Work with relevant stakeholders (airports, utilities, 
</p>
<p>regulators, aircraft operators, other end users) to define 
scenarios that enhance value propositions for all entities
</p>
<p>- Identify information to be targeted for regulatory change
- Publish reference architectures for different use cases
</p>
<p>POC: Nick Borer, NASA Langley Research Center</p>
