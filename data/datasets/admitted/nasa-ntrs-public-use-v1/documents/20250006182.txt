<p>1 | Background Probabilistic Fracture Analysis Challenges and Best Practices
</p>
<p>Potential Overconservatisms in Damage Tolerance
</p>
<p>• Two primary features of current practice that are potentially overconservative:
</p>
<p>1. Use of 𝑎90/95 as the initial flaw size 2. Safety factor of 4× on life
</p>
<p>Can we use probabilistic analysis to relax these conservatisms and demonstrate adequate 
design margins for reusable hardware?
</p>
<p>QUESTION
</p>
<p>➢ Originally implemented to account for uncertainty in crack growth 
rate, 4× is relied on to account for additional uncertainties.
</p>
<p>➢ NDE-detectable flaw may be larger than the size of flaws in the part
(see limited use of durability equivalent initial flaw size [1, 2])
</p>
<p>1. NASA-STD-5019A – Section 6.2.5 NFC Low-risk Parts
2. USAF Damage Tolerant Design Handbook*Plots are for illustrative purposes only – data is synthetic</p>

<p>1 | Background
</p>
<p>The Tradeoffs
➢ Increased complexity:
</p>
<p>• Safety factors rely on trusting “offline” testing and analysis to ensure appropriate 
conservatism and coverage of uncertainties → set it and forget it
</p>
<p>• Probabilistic results have an aura of precision → more responsibility on analyst to 
be rigorous and potentially modify approach on a case-by-case basis
</p>
<p>➢Need more data to calibrate/validate the stochastic model
</p>
<p>➢ Neither can predict unknown unknowns, but “overconservatism” of safety 
factors might save us where a precise probabilistic result may not.
</p>
<p>• Example 1: Underconservatism in COPV liner fatigue crack growth rates post-autofrettage
</p>
<p>• Example 2: Shuttle probabilistic analysis (anecdote)
</p>
<p>NASGRO Unconservative
</p>
<p>Rigor is key to a successful probabilistic analysis.
</p>

<p>Epistemic vs. Aleatory Uncertainty
• Types of uncertainty:
</p>
<p>• Aleatory: “Also known as statistical, stochastic, or 
irreducible uncertainty… that in principle cannot be 
reduced by additional physical or experimental 
knowledge.” [1]
</p>
<p>• Epistemic: Reducible uncertainty that is “due to 
simplifying model assumptions, missing physics, or 
basic lack of knowledge.” [1]
</p>
<p>• Useful to identify uncertainty that can potentially be 
</p>
<p>reduced with additional resources.
➢ Typically implemented as a double loop 
</p>
<p>Monte Carlo simulation
</p>
<p>➢ Results in interval results referred to as a “p-box”

<p>Example | FAA, Titanium turbine rotor disks
• Multi-year testing and characterization effort by the 
</p>
<p>Aerospace Industries Association (AIA) Rotor Integrity Sub-
Committee (~10 member companies)
</p>
<p>• Used POD curve to estimate candidate flaw distributions
</p>
<p>• Selected/adjusted flaw distribution to match field experience
</p>
<p>• Performed probabilistic analyses to guide relative risk 
reduction (e.g., through redesign) compared to an identified 
baseline “design target risk”
</p>

</p>
<p>2. Aerospace Industries Association Rotor Integrity Sub-Committee. "The development of anomaly distributions for aircraft engine titanium disk

<p>Example | USAF, Equivalent Initial Flaw Size (EIFS)

<p>observed fatigue cracks

calculation method
➢ Crack growth predictions based on the derived 
</p>
<p>distribution must use the corresponding forward 
calculation method
</p>
<p>• Relies on statistically significant number of 
observations of damage that has progressed to a 
quantifiable size in representative hardware
</p>
<p>See: USAF Damage Tolerant Design Handbook: Guidelines for the Analysis 
and Design of Damage Tolerant Aircraft Structures

<p>Conclusion
</p>
<p>• Probabilistic analysis can enhance models to provide 
significantly more information and potentially reduce 
overconservatism.
</p>
<p>• Primary tradeoffs are the increased complexity of 
analysis and data requirements.
</p>
<p>• Alternatives to probabilistic approaches for reusable 
spaceflight hardware, such as inspection intervals, should 
also be considered.</p>
