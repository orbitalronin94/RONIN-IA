<p>Electrolysis Comparison
</p>
<p>Pros
• Flight heritage (ISS)
• Efficient gas compression
• High operating pressures
• Relatively low operating temperature (~30 o C) 
</p>
<p>Cons
• Liquid water must be kept above 0o C during 
</p>
<p>lunar night
• Feedwater must be deionized
• Radiator is required to reject waste heat
• Oxygen dryer is required
</p>
<p>Proton 
Exchange
Membrane
</p>
<p>Solid
Oxide
Electrolyzer
</p>
<p>Pros
• Does not require deionized feedwater
• Oxygen is dry and pure
• Flight heritage (MOXIE)
• Should be able to survive lunar night with 
</p>
<p>minimal power
• Waste heat can be used to keep stack at 
</p>
<p>operating temperature
</p>
<p>Cons
• Low operating pressure (&lt; 30 psia)
• High operating temperature (~800 o C)
• Output gases are hot and may require more 
</p>
<p>liquefaction power and/or more radiator mass 
depending on heat recuperation efficiency 
</p>
<p>• Water will need to be vaporized twice, once for 
extraction and again for electrolysis</p>

<p>Hydrogen Liquefaction
</p>
<p>• Magnetocaloric and elastocaloric refrigeration systems exhibit 
potential for lunar cryogenic fluid management
</p>
<p>• Active Magnetic Regenerative Refrigeration (AMRR) systems 
leverage magnetocaloric materials, and their subsequent 
temperature changes when exposed to magnetic fields, to transport 
thermal energy
</p>
<p>– Rare earth element dependence, high magnetic field 
requirements, and poor heat transfer limit current 
refrigerants
</p>
<p>– Superparamagnetic high entropy alloys show promise in 
improving structural and functional performance of the 
materials
</p>
<p>• Elastocaloric Cooling (eC) systems leverage shape memory alloys, 
and their subsequent temperature changes when exposed to 
stresses, to transport thermal energy
</p>
<p>– Fatigue and temperature dependence limit current alloys
– Cryogenic superelastic alloys demonstrate increased 
</p>
<p>capability at low temperature, and longer service life
• Adoption of caloric refrigeration systems is largely limited by the 
</p>
<p>existence and/or availability of capable refrigerants, cost, mass, and 
technological readiness
</p>
<p>• Advancements in ferroic materials research (e.g. novel high entropy 
alloy compositions) stand to increase the viability of these systems 
by improving refrigerant performance and reducing both mass and 
cost
</p>

<p>Common to Mars ISRU 
</p>
<p> Producing both LOX and LCH4 on the moon would be a good “Moon to Mars” architecture for propellant production and can be accomplished if solid carbon is 
combined with hydrogen sourced from lunar water
</p>
<p> Current hydrogen liquefaction cryocooler technology will have to operate for over a year in order to see a return on landed mass (assuming 183 days of sunlight 
per year)[9]
</p>
<p> The use of solid carbon to drive a carbothermal reduction reaction has been demonstrated for terrestrial silicon production[10]
</p>
<p> Methane pyrolysis technology is being developed for terrestrial hydrogen and carbon production[11]
</p>
