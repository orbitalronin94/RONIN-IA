<div class="slide-content"><p>Space Nuclear Propulsion and Power: Brief Overview</p>
<p>NASA is evaluating multiple nuclear propulsion / power concepts for deep space / interplanetary missions</p>
<p>Propulsion applications: Nuclear thermal propulsion (NTP) and nuclear electric propulsion (NEP)</p>
<p>Power applications: Fission surface power (FSP)</p>
<p>Current design approach:</p>
<p>Modular component assembly using docking/berthing interfaces/methods derived from ISS</p>
<p>Launched elements sized structurally for handling launch loads and constrained by LV fairing volume</p>
<p>Mission CONOPS frequently involves “low-touch” approach to vehicle assembly / aggregation</p>
<p>Reactors are self-contained, launched as a single unit</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>What can ISAM do for SNPP systems?</p>
<p>Servicing</p>
<p>Enable a “Space/planetary drydock” approach to nuclear vehicle/component reuse and maintenance</p>
<p>Dedicated space infrastructure for maintenance/repair functions – offloading similar requirements from the vehicle itself</p>
<p>Increase in reactor/vehicle operational lifetime</p>
<p>Allows for improved data-gathering between missions on reactor performance/lifetime</p>
<p>Moves reactor servicing functions into space</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>What can ISAM do for SNPP systems?</p>
<p>Assembly</p>
<p>Seamless integration of components, allowing for reduced overall vehicle/power system mass</p>
<p>Less reliant on complex docking systems</p>
<p>Downsize structures that are designed for launch loads</p>
<p>Reduced gravity constraints = novel configurations</p>
<p>Variations/optimizations in radiator placements, sub-element positioning</p>
<p>Offloading assembly requirements onto external ISAM infrastructure reduces overall vehicle mass</p>
<p>Fewer unused redundancies in avionics/navigation/comms components</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>What can ISAM do for SNPP systems?</p>
<p>Manufacturing</p>
<p>Delivery of raw/recycled materials for manufacturing parts</p>
<p>Vacuum, microgravity environment can enable alternative assembly approaches </p>
<p>Novel nuclear support structures / shielding manufacturing</p>
<p>Symbiotic relationship w/ power-dense infrastructure potential offered by nuclear systems can enable cislunar manufacturing capabilities that exploit both the high power capacity and the space environment</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>The future of a space nuclear infrastructure integrated/enhanced with ISAM-based elements and approaches</p>
<p>Nuclear spacecraft with novel configurations enabled by in-space assembly “drydocks,” with reduced system mass and improved flight performance</p>
<p>Increased lifetime of vehicles and power systems with regular servicing</p>
<p>Recycling/manufacturing components to provide a sustainable, nuclear-enabled presence in space and on surface missions</p>
<p>Improved data-gathering on reactor/support components, to feed forward into nuclear propulsion / power design improvements</p>
