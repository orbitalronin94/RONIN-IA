<p>2
</p>
<p>Introduction &amp; Overview
</p>
<p>➢ Prior to flowing propellants, on-orbit leak tests are performed with helium gas
</p>
<p>➢ In flight propulsion and propellant transfer systems, the ability to accurately 
</p>
<p>characterize a propellant leak flow rate correlated from a measured gas leak 
</p>
<p>flow rate for a given configuration is of great importance
</p>
<p>➢ Depending on the size of the leak (varies by orders of magnitude), the gas 
</p>
<p>flow regime could be molecular, laminar or turbulent
</p>
<p>➢ From the measured pressure decay during helium leak testing, the ground 
</p>
<p>operators can estimate the unknown leak size and the initial leak flow rate
</p>
<p>➢ With the estimated leak size, the propellant leak flow rate can be determined  
</p>
<p>if the equivalence between the gas leak flow and propellant leak flow can be 
</p>
<p>deduced from an established (validated) theoretical model</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>3
</p>
<p>Schematic of a Leak (Gas or Liquid)
</p>
<p>➢ The tank volume is constant, and the mass in the tank decreases with time 
</p>
<p>due to leak flow (gas or liquid)</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>4
</p>
<p>Representation of an Arbitrary Leak Path
</p>
<p>➢ The leak flow rate is generally governed by the smallest leak diameter, path 
</p>
<p>length, surface tension, and surface roughness of the hole
</p>
<p>➢ Practical leak sizes vary widely (0.1 mm to 0.1 mm) leading to a wide range of 
</p>
<p>flow rates, and are generally unknown
</p>
<p>➢ The flow regime ranges from continuum (laminar/turbulent) to free-molecular</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>➢ The present data shows a nonlinear trend below about 5E-3 sccs GHe
</p>
<p>➢ On the other hand, Blair’s data shows a nearly linear trend in the entire range of GHe flow
</p>
<p>➢ The linearity predicted by the theory (see next slide) suggests that Blair’s data is likely 
</p>
<p>more accurate than the present data

<p>17
</p>
<p>Comparison with the Data of Blair &amp; Senear 
</p>
<p>➢ The present theory is compared with Blair’s data at three different supply pressures
</p>
<p>➢ The KSC test data at the lowest pressure tested (280 psig) is also shown
</p>
<p>➢ Good agreement between the theory and Blair’s data is seen for the GHe flow range of 0.001 to 1 sccs
</p>
<p>➢ Further test verification with water and GHe is needed along with other past data that may be available
</p>
<p>➢ An improved theory would be helpful to analyze other fluid combinations</p>
<p></p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>18
</p>
<p>Conclusions 
</p>
<p>➢ Test data are obtained for the equivalence between GHe and water leak flow rates 
</p>
<p>➢ A corresponding theory is developed considering non-continuum gas flow effects
</p>
<p>➢ The theory agrees satisfactorily with the test data of Blair for water in the GHe flow range of 0.001 to 1 sccs
</p>
<p>➢ Further testing and improvements to the theory are required to better understand the leakage behavior</p>
