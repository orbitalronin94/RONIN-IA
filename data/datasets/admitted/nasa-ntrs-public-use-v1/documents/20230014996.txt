2



Introduction & Overview



➢ Prior to flowing propellants, on-orbit leak tests are performed with helium gas



➢ In flight propulsion and propellant transfer systems, the ability to accurately 



characterize a propellant leak flow rate correlated from a measured gas leak 



flow rate for a given configuration is of great importance



➢ Depending on the size of the leak (varies by orders of magnitude), the gas 



flow regime could be molecular, laminar or turbulent



➢ From the measured pressure decay during helium leak testing, the ground 



operators can estimate the unknown leak size and the initial leak flow rate



➢ With the estimated leak size, the propellant leak flow rate can be determined 



if the equivalence between the gas leak flow and propellant leak flow can be 



deduced from an established (validated) theoretical model

Representation of an Arbitrary Leak Path



➢ The leak flow rate is generally governed by the smallest leak diameter, path 



length, surface tension, and surface roughness of the hole



➢ Practical leak sizes vary widely (0.1 mm to 0.1 mm) leading to a wide range of 



flow rates, and are generally unknown



➢ The flow regime ranges from continuum (laminar/turbulent) to free-molecular

16



GHe and Water Leak Flow Rate Correlation 



➢ The present data shows a nonlinear trend below about 5E-3 sccs GHe



➢ On the other hand, Blair’s data shows a nearly linear trend in the entire range of GHe flow



➢ The linearity predicted by the theory (see next slide) suggests that Blair’s data is likely 



more accurate than the present data 



Blair & Senear (1968)











17



Comparison with the Data of Blair & Senear 



➢ The present theory is compared with Blair’s data at three different supply pressures



➢ The KSC test data at the lowest pressure tested (280 psig) is also shown



➢ Good agreement between the theory and Blair’s data is seen for the GHe flow range of 0.001 to 1 sccs



➢ Further test verification with water and GHe is needed along with other past data that may be available



➢ An improved theory would be helpful to analyze other fluid combinations











18



Conclusions 



➢ Test data are obtained for the equivalence between GHe and water leak flow rates 



➢ A corresponding theory is developed considering non-continuum gas flow effects



➢ The theory agrees satisfactorily with the test data of Blair for water in the GHe flow range of 0.001 to 1 sccs



➢ Further testing and improvements to the theory are required to better understand the leakage behavior
