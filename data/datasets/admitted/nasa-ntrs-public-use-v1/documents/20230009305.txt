Introduction



07/19/2023 ICES-2023-51 2



• The SOA on oxygen recovery onboard the ECLSS at the ISS is a complex, heavy, and power-consuming



system that recovers approximately 50% of the oxygen (O2) from metabolic carbon dioxide (CO2).



• For future long-duration beyond LEO, O2 recovery systems must be highly reliable and efficient and



recover a minimum of 75% O2 from metabolic CO2. An alternative technology development effort



currently underway at NASA MSFC has the potential to increase O2 recovery significantly, presently



limited to 50% (Sabatier), and reduce the complexity of ECLSS O2 recovery.



• MSFC and the University of Texas in Arlington (UTA) have jointly designed and fabricated a microfluid



electrochemical reactor (MFECR) that operates at ambient conditions and utilizes a proprietary catalysis



highly selective on reducing CO2 to ethylene (C2H4) at the cathode. At the same time, O2 is generated at



the anode.



• The MFECR would replace three pieces of hardware for future ECLSS architectures: the current CO2



Reduction Assembly (CRA) (Sabatier reactor), the Plasma Pyrolysis Assembly (PPA), and the Oxygen



Generation Assembly (OGA). It is designed to interface directly with the CO2 Removal Assembly (CDRA)



and the Water Processing Assembly (WPA) to supply CO2 reactants and replenish them, respectively.



This approach is expected to substantially improve the ISS ECLSS sustainability and reduce power and



weight requirements.

• In 2016, NASA's Game Changing Development Program awarded University of Texas Arlington (UTA) a contract to develop a 



Microfluidic Electrochemical Reactor (MFECR)



• Developed based on the electrochemical reduction of CO2 to ethylene (C2H4) using water (H2O) as precursor and operates at ambient 



pressure and just above the freezing point of H2O



• Theoretical O2 recovery is 100% from metabolic CO2



• An architecture of an Electrolytic O2 Recovery (EORe) system containing MFECR would reduce the complexity of the AR system by 



eliminating three (3) pieces of hardware from the current baseline exploration architecture: 1) Sabatier, 2) Plasma Pyrolysis Assembly 



(PPA), and 3) Oxygen Generation Assembly (OGA)

EDU and Test Stand



ICES-2023-51 707/19/2023



108 mm diameter



• The EDU is equipped with three chambers that have



aligned serpentine paths to house the CO2, N2, and



liquid electrolyte flows.



• The electrolyte serpentine chamber is located between



the CO2 and N2 chambers



• The cathode, a gas diffusion layer (GDL) with Cu/Br



catalysis deposited on the side facing the electrolyte is



located underneath the CO2 serpentine chamber.



• The anode, a GDE with Pt deposited on the side



facing the electrolyte is located underneath the N2



serpentine chamber.



• The GDE is 0.032 mm thick and has two layers, the



gas diffusion layer (GDL) and the macropore layer



(MPL) coated with PTFE to make them hydrophobic



and manage aqueous electrolyte permeation.

Future Work



• Continue evaluating other composite anode configurations,



including those equipped with platinized nickel foam, platinized



titanium screen, and platinized titanium mass transfer layer.



• Reduce the C2H4-CO EC onset by increasing electrolyte OH molarity



and electrolyte type (KOH/LiOH).



• Increase catalysis coverage on the GDE’s surface by performing



electrodeposition on the outer surface of the GDE’s MPL and



throughout the GDE.



• Redesign the EDU based on the preliminary experimental outcome



and model-based qualitative benchmark studies.



• Evaluate two H2 separation systems that will recover H2 generated



in the EDU
