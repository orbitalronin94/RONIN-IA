1.0 Introduction 



Aluminum Alloy 2219 (AA2219) is an age-hardenable Al-Cu alloy that has been widely used 



throughout the aerospace industry for applications such as cryogenic tanks of launch vehicles 



because of its light weight, high strength, and fracture toughness at cryogenic temperatures.1 



AA2219 is available in all wrought products, and it can be supplied in the form of bars, plates, 



forgings, and extrusions in the full range of commercial sizes. Additionally, AA2219 is readily 



weldable and suitable for applications over a temperature range of –450 °F to 600 °F (–268 °C to 



315 °C). In the solution heat-treated and aged condition, its mechanical properties in the 500 to 
600 °F (260–315 °C) temperature range are superior to those of any other commercially 



available aluminum alloy. 



AA2219 continues to see frequent use in space and advanced aviation applications due to the 



practicality and availability of the material. As the lead-time required for forged products has 



increased, the use of thick plate products has become increasingly common for the manufacture of 



arbitrary structural shapes. However, the mechanical properties of AA2219 in thick product forms 



(thick plate greater than 3 inches) are not well documented. Specifically, little information is 



known about the mechanical properties of AA2219 thick plate in the short-transverse (ST) 



orientation. The ST properties are not controlled by the industry material procurement standards 



nor are they available in commonly used material property databases such as the Metallic Materials 



Properties Database and Standardization (MMPDS).15 



The design review process within a recent project identified the use of AA2219-T87 5-in thick 



plate for a variety of structural hardware. Through prior personal experience, the reviewer flagged 



a concern about the low ductility in the ST orientation of this material. Based upon these concerns, 



multiple material lots of thick plate AA2219-T87 from the same manufacturer were evaluated for 



their tensile performance. These tests identified irregularities in the ST ductility—with total 



fracture elongation varying from 1% to 7.35%. Thus, it is evident that a better understanding of 



the microstructure and mechanical properties for the thick plate material in the ST orientation is 



key to ensuring its safe use in critical applications. These early tensile test results prompted 



additional testing and data analysis to understand the root cause of the low ductility. Materials 



characterization techniques were utilized to observe the micro-structure of fractured tensile 



specimens and remnant plate material. The mechanism and root cause of low ST ductility in thick 



plate material was presented and discussed in a NASA TM.2 The drastic reduction in ST tensile 



ductility can be attributed to the presence of abundant, coarse Cu-rich θ particles (Al2Cu) at grain 



boundaries. Hard θ particles are stress risers during tensile testing, and the increase in stress 



concentration around the hard θ particles promotes crack initiation and leads to significant 



reduction in fracture elongation. 



During the latter stages of the investigation into the low ST tensile ductility, the NASA 



Engineering and Safety Center (NESC) team discovered an “edge effect,” where improved 



ductility was observed in tensile specimens obtained from material close to the plate’s edges in the 



width direction. Therefore, an in-depth metallurgical investigation was carried out to determine 



the root causes of the edge effect. The main objective of this investigation is to obtain a better 



understanding of the microstructural characteristics of 5-in thick AA2219-T87 plate material and 



the mechanism that leads to the unexpected edge effect. The mechanism and root cause of the edge 



effect in the thick AA2219 plate material is presented and discussed. 











2 



2.0 Edge Effect on Tensile Properties Across the Plate Width and Thickness 



The NESC team discovered a “through-width” edge effect in 5-in thick AA2219-T87 plates, where 



improved ductility and strength were observed in tensile specimens obtained close to the plate width 



edge. In contrast, ductility and ultimate tensile stress decrease considerably in tensile specimens 



obtained away from the plate width edge. The short transverse (ST) specimen orientation used for 



tensile testing to determine the edge effect is shown in Figure 1.

The strength of AA2219 is related to the size and volume fraction of the primary strengthening 



precipitate θ' (semi-coherent Al2Cu intermetallic compound). It was also discovered that the hard 



and brittle θ (incoherent Al2Cu intermetallic compound) particles tend to agglomerate along grain 



boundaries and promote brittle intergranular fracture in thick AA2219 plates.2 It is postulated that 



the edge effect phenomenon is likely related to the combined effects of (1) the plate temperature 



being lower at the plate edge (surface) than at the plate mid-plane during hot-rolling, (2) the rolling 



strain being higher at the plate edge (surface) than at the plate mid-plane during hot-rolling, and 



(3) the quench cooling rate being higher at the plate edge (near surface) than at the center of the 



plate during quench. In-depth metallurgical analysis was performed to determine the root causes 



of edge effect. 



3.0 Procedures and Methods 



3.1 Material and Metallography 



The AA2219 thick plate materials used in this study were in the form of a 5-in thick plate in T87 



temper condition. The nominal composition of the AA2219 plate material is shown in Table 1. 



Metallographic specimens were prepared by sectioning through the plate thickness to observe the 



grain structure variation on the L-ST plane (see Figure 1) as a function of plate thickness location. 



Metallographic specimens were ground and polished mechanically and then chemically etched 



using Keller’s reagent to reveal the microstructure. Optical microscopy was performed in the L-



ST plane using a Leica DMi8 optical light microscope for high-resolution montaging of the micro-



structure. Quantitative grain size analysis was performed using the intercept method per ASTM 



E112-13,3 which details the methods and requirements for grain size determination of specimens 



with non-equiaxed grain shapes. 



Table 1. Nominal chemical composition of AA2219 (wt%). 



 



3.2 Differential Scanning Calorimetry Analysis 



Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) analysis was performed using the DSC-Q20 from TA 



Instruments®. DSC samples were prepared from five different thickness locations, from t/2 to the 



plate bottom surface (BS), as shown in Figure 6. DSC was conducted in a purified argon 



atmosphere with a constant heating rate of 20 °C/min from 40 to 600 °C in order to measure the 



net heat flow change during melting of the Al-Cu eutectic phase (α-Al + θ). The endothermic peak 



that appears around 548 °C indicates the melting reaction of the Al-Cu eutectic phase.4 The Al-Cu 



eutectic content was quantitatively determined by calculating the net heat flow value of the eutec-



tic melting peak in each thickness location. The DSC effort aims to obtain a better understanding 



of whether DSC can serve as a time-saving screening method to determine the weak locations for 



problematic thick AA2219 plates.

4.0 Results and Discussion

thickness locations: t/6, t/3, 2t/5, t/2, 3t/5, 2t/3, and 5t/6. Figure 7 displays the representative 



through-thickness microstructure of lot 930071 in the plate width edge at four thickness locations. 



The microstructure consists of large, elongated grains that are typical of a rolled plate. No 



significant change in grain structure was observed across the plate thickness, as shown in 



Figure 7.

Figure 10 shows the measured grain aspect ratio through the plate thickness at the plate width edge 



and width center in lots 930071 and 930082. Most grains exhibit aspect ratios between 2.5 and 5. 



No significant change in the through-thickness grain aspect ratio was observed for either lot. As 



can be seen in Figure 9, there is a slight increase in grain size near the t/2 thickness location at the 



plate width center, which is likely caused by a lower degree of recrystallization at the mid-

in lot 930082, as shown in Figure 9. The difference in grain size between these two lots is likely 



caused by variations in the hot-rolling practice that resulted in a reduction in the driving force of 



recrystallization for lot 930071.

The effects of grain size on the ambient yield strength of metals has been established by the Hall-



Petch relation.6 The Hall-Petch relation predicts yield strength increases with a decreasing grain 



size for materials with grain sizes ranging from 1 mm to 1 µm. This is based on the observation 



that grain boundaries are obstacles that hinder the movement of dislocations. Therefore, grain size 



can influence the number of dislocations piled up at grain boundaries resulting in an increase in 



tensile yield strength. The relation between yield stress and grain size is described mathematically

As shown in Figure 9, a slight variation in grain size throughout the plate thickness was observed 



between the plate width edge and width center for both lots. At the plate width center location of 



lot 930071, the grain size from the near surface layer decreases first from t/6 to t/3, and then 



increases gradually from t/3 to 2t/5 and t/2. According to the Hall-Petch equation, the distribution 



of yield strength should be t/3 > t/6 > 2t/5. However, the through-thickness tensile data 



(see Figure 5) indicate t/6 has significantly higher yield stress and tensile stress than that at t/3 and 



2t/5. It is evident that there is no clear correlation between grain size and through-thickness yield 











10 



stress for lot 930071. One explanation for this is that AA2219 is a precipitation-strengthened alloy, 



and the strengthening contribution predominantly comes from precipitation of θ',2 instead of the 



grain size effect. Therefore, it can be confirmed that the contribution of grain size to the observed 



edge effect for thick AA2219 plate material is insignificant.

Figure 13 shows through-width hardness versus distance (from the plate width edge to center)

4.3.2 Effect of Rolling Temperature on Microstructure Evolution 



AA2219-T87 plate production begins with homogenization of a direct-chill (DC) cast ingot 



(~16 inches in height) at a temperature slightly below 1018 °F (548 °C) to reduce Cu segregation 



and transform the coarse Al-Cu eutectic phase to α-Al and θ phase.2 The ingot is cooled naturally 



in air after homogenization and quickly transported to a rolling mill. Hot-rolling starts when the 



ingot temperature decreases to approximately 750 °F (399 °C). Precipitation of θ can occur at grain 



boundaries during hot-rolling, which is very problematic for thick AA2219-T87 plate material. A 



2219 time-temperature-precipitation (TTP) diagram8 was used to project through-thickness 



microstructure evolution during hot-rolling, as shown in Figure 18. There are two C-curves that 



show the critical time (tc) and temperature that θ phase begins to precipitate at grain boundaries 



and in the α-Al matrix for AA2219 during heat treatment or thermo-mechanical processing. As 



shown in Figure 18, the ingot cooling from homogenization to completion of hot-rolling proceeds 



through a series of successive steps: a continuous cooling step from homogenization (the dotted 



red curve) to the start of hot-rolling; a hot-rolling step (the solid red curves); and a continuous 



cooling step to ambient temperature after hot-rolling (the dotted orange curves). The projected 



cooling curves are based on the assumptions that it would take the ingot 10 to 15 minutes 



(600–900 seconds) to cool down to 750 °F (399 °C) after homogenization and that hot-rolling lasts 



for approximately 10 min (600 s). The estimated ingot cooling curve from homogenization (red 



dotted curve), the isothermal hot-rolling curves (bold red lines), and the plate cooling curves after 



hot-rolling (orange dotted curves) are superimposed on the TTP curve of 2219 to illustrate the 



microstructure evolution that can occur from homogenization to completion of the hot-rolling step. 











16 



 
Figure 18. A 2219 TTP diagram and tc for the precipitation of θ at grain boundaries and in the 



α-Al matrix.8 Due to the inhomogeneous temperature distribution across the plate thickness, the 



temperature can vary by as much as 60 °C in a thick aluminum plate from plate surface to plate 



core.2 



It has been reported that the through-thickness temperature of a 6-in thick AA7075 plate can vary 



significantly up to 60 °C.2,5 Assuming the through-thickness temperature varies up to 60 °C when 



hot-rolling a thick AA2219 plate at 750 °F (399 °C), the through-thickness temperature distribution 



can vary significantly from the plate surface to the plate core, as shown in Figure 18. The entire 



plate enters the matrix θ formation C-curve during cooling to 750 °F (from homogenization), as 



shown in Figure 18. Precipitation of grain boundary (GB) θ is expected to occur near the plate core 



when the hot-rolling temperature-time curve (the plate core curve) intercepts the C-curve of grain 



boundary θ phase (at point S). Grain boundary θ phase can continue to precipitate at the plate core 



during hot-rolling. After completion of hot-rolling, precipitation and growth of grain boundary θ 



phase can continue at the plate core during natural cooling until the temperature drops below the 



C-curve of grain boundary θ phase (at point F). In contrast, the plate surface temperature is 



significantly lower than that at the plate mid-plane location, and the hot-rolling temperature time-



curve never intercepts the C-curve of grain boundary θ phase during hot-rolling. Therefore, a 



higher amount of grain boundary θ particles is expected to be present toward the plate mid-



thickness location. The observed through-thickness edge effect can be partially attributed to 



variation in through-thickness temperature distribution during hot-rolling that leads to 



inhomogeneous distribution of θ particles across the plate thickness. 



4.3.3 Effects of Rolling Strain and Temperature on Fragmentation of θ Particles 



There is a significant difference in the size, volume fraction, and distribution of θ phase (Al2Cu) 



at the edge (plate surface) and center (plate mid-plane) of a 5-in thick AA2219 plate.2 The 



differences in volume fraction and size of θ phase between the edge and center of the plate can be 



attributed to the variations in through-thickness rolling strain and temperature distribution. It has 











17 



been reported5 that there are considerable variations in temperature and effective strain at different 



layers of a thick aluminum plate during the hot-rolling process, as shown in Figure 19. The 



temperature and strain data shown in Figure 19 was generated using a 150-mm (5.9-in) thick 7050 



plate. It can be seen from Figure 19(a) that the temperature history of plate core and surface is 



quite different during hot-rolling. At the start of rolling, the surface temperature dropped rapidly 



because the surface layer of the plate was in direct contact with the rollers, which increased heat 



dissipation and resulted in a rapid temperature drop. In contrast to the surface behavior, the 



temperature of the core area increased slightly during hot-rolling as additional heat is generated by 



plastic deformation without immediate heat dissipation. 



 
Figure 19. Distribution of (a) temperature and (b) effective strain at plate surface and core during 



hot-rolling a 5.9-in thick 7050 plate.5 



In addition to the variation in temperature between plate surface and core, the effective rolling 



strain also varies through-thickness and affects fragmentation of θ particles in AA2219.5,9 Rolling 



deformation cannot be uniformly distributed through the whole plate thickness, and the surface 



layer is more deformed than the center layer. As shown in Figure 19(b), the effective strain 



increases drastically at the plate surface when the rolling starts. The maximum effective strain 



value is present on the surface. During hot-rolling of a thick AA2219 plate, rolling strain 



incompatibility will develop at the interface of the θ particles and α-Al matrix because of their 



differences in elastic constants and hardness, which causes stress concentration at the hard θ 



particles.10 When the rolling temperature is high (e.g., exceeds 410 °C), the deformation resistance 



of the material is reduced as the α-Al matrix is very soft. As a result, the rolling-induced stress is 



not sufficient to break up the hard θ particles. When the plate is hot-rolled at a lower temperature 



(e.g., 360 °C), stress concentration at the hard θ particles is expected to increase significantly 



because of the increase in the effective strain and flow stress of the material when the plate is 



colder. This explains why θ particles are more fragmented and dispersed toward the plate edge as 



the plate surface layers are subjected to lower rolling temperature and higher rolling strain during 



hot-rolling. In contrast, the hard θ particles tend to agglomerate near the plate center where the 



temperature is higher and rolling stress/strain is too low to consistently break them up. 



4.3.4 Effect of θ Particle Size and Shape on Cu Dissolution During Solution Treatment 



The dispersed and fragmented θ particles, which have sharp corners and edges, can be more 



thoroughly dissolved into Al-Cu solid solution during solution treatment because Cu diffusion is 



facilitated by the increased total superficial area of θ particles.9 In contrast, the coarse and agglom-



erated θ particles are harder to dissolve into Al-Cu solid solution than the finely dispersed θ 











18 



because the diffusion of Cu atoms through the coarse agglomerated θ particles is more restricted. 



Toward the plate surface, where θ particles are more fragmented and dispersed, solution treatment 



can take a significantly higher amount of Cu into the Al-Cu solid solution to maximize 



precipitation hardening after artificial aging. θ particles are coarser and more agglomerated toward 



the plate core, which is expected to lower Cu content in Al-Cu solid solution after solution 



treatment. The strength of AA2219 is related to the size, distribution, and volume fraction of the 



primary strengthening precipitate, θ'. The differences in Cu concentration (in Al-Cu solid solution) 



and the degree of precipitation hardening contribute to the observed strength variations (edge 



effects) across the plate width and thickness, as shown in Figure 2(a) and Figure 4(a). 



4.3.5 Effect of Plate Thickness on Quench Cooling Rate 



From the hardness profiles presented in Figure 13, it is apparent that the 5-in thick 2219-T87 plate 



is quench sensitive. The quench cooling rate in a thick aluminum alloy plate is thickness-dependent 



and is limited by the thermal conductivity of the material.11 The cooling rate at the mid-plane of a 



thick plate is naturally slower than that near the surface areas. Figure 20 shows the average cooling 



rates of aluminum alloy plates, with thicknesses ranging from 1.6 mm (0.06 in) to 250 mm (8 in), 



achieved by quenching at three different water temperatures and in still air. The cooling rates 



shown in Figure 20 are for the location most distant from the surface, which is the mid-plane 



location in a plate or sheet. The dashed red line indicates the theoretical maximum cooling rate 



that can be obtained at the plate mid-planes with different thicknesses. As shown in Figure 20, the 



cooling rate at 400–290 °C (750–550 °F) at the mid-plane of a typical 127-mm (5-in) thick plate 



is about 1.0 °C/s when immersing the plate into 82 °C water. In comparison, the cooling rate at the 



near surface location of a 127-mm (5-in) thick plate can be estimated from the thin plate data to 



increase to > 100 °C/s under the same quenching condition, which is more than a 100-fold increase 



in the quench cooling rate. Similar variation in quench cooling rate can be expected across the 



plate width. The plate width edge is expected to have a higher cooling rate than the plate width 



center because the width edge area is in direct contact with the quench medium. A higher quench 



cooling rate at the width edge can make the material harder after artificial aging by reducing θ 



precipitation and increasing the volume fraction of the primary strengthening precipitate, θ'. In 



comparison, lower quench cooling rate at the width center softens the material due to precipitation 



of higher volume fraction of θ phase that lowers the concentration of the primary strengthening 



precipitate, θ', after artificial aging.

4.3.6 Effect of Quench Cooling Rate on Microstructure Evolution for AA2219 Plate 



When AA2219 is held at the solution temperature (995 °F) to permit diffusion of Cu, most θ 



particles will dissolve, and Cu will be taken into α-Al solid solution. AA2219 requires a very fast 



quench cooling rate to maintain a supersaturated Al-Cu solid solution in α-Al matrix. It is difficult 



to achieve the desired fast quench cooling rates in the plate core for thick plate products. The 



atomic fraction of the Cu element that is bound in quench-induced θ precipitate reduces the amount 



of Cu solute available for the formation of the strengthening precipitate, θ', during artificial aging. 



Due to the combined effect of increased θ precipitation and reduced volume fraction of the primary 



strengthening θ' precipitate, several mechanical properties, such as tensile strength and ductility, 



are significantly affected. 



A quench-sensitive alloy, such as AA2219, generally has the nose of its matrix θ C-curve (the blue 



curve) shifted to the left (indicating very little time is required for θ precipitation to occur during 



quench cooling), which is the primary root cause of quench sensitivity for AA2219, as shown in 



Figure 21.8 A reasonable explanation for the high quench sensitivity of AA2219 is its high Cu 



content, approximately 6.4% for thick plates. The maximum solubility of Cu in α-Al matrix is 



approximately 5.2% Cu at 995 °F, as shown in Figure 22.8 Two schematic quench cooling curves 



(faster cooling at plate surface and slower cooling at mid-plane) are superimposed on the TTP 



diagram, as illustrated in Figure 21, to demonstrate the effects of quench cooling rate on θ 



precipitation. The faster quench cooling curve misses the nose of θ phase C-curve, and θ does not 



form during quenching. The slower quench cooling curve intercepts the C-curve at approximately 



450 °C (at point S), which is the onset of θ precipitation during quench cooling. θ can grow 



continuously until the material is cooled outside the θ forming C-curve at approximately 375 °C 



(at point F). The quench-induced θ precipitate has very little hardening effect in itself,12,13 and the 



precipitation of θ phase consumes Cu atoms in Al-Cu solid solution and reduces the amount of Cu 



solute available for the formation of the strengthening precipitate, θ', during artificial aging. This 



explains why abundant precipitation of θ phase is the primary root cause of quench sensitivity that 



contributes to reduced hardness and strength at near mid-plane locations for thick AA2219 plates.14

4.5 Edge Effect Root Cause Analysis Summary 



Based on the tensile data, hardness profiles, and microstructure analysis, the key production steps 



and microstructure evolutions that influence the observed edge effect for 5-in thick AA2219 plate 



are summarized in Figure 27. The formation of θ phase starts when the Al-Cu eutectic phase, which 



forms during the terminal stage of solidification in ingot casting, decomposes into α-Al and θ 



during ingot homogenization. Large θ particles can fracture into small θ particles during hot-



rolling, and the degree of θ fragmentation is rolling temperature and effective strain-dependent. θ 



particles are more fragmented and dispersed toward the plate surface, as the plate edge is cooler 



and has higher rolling strain than the plate center. In contrast, the hard and brittle θ particles tend 



to grow and agglomerate along GBs near the plate center where the temperature is higher and 



rolling stress/strain is too low to consistently break up the hard θ particles. AA2219 typically 



contains up to 6.4 – 6.5 wt% of Cu, and Cu does not go completely into α-Al solid solution during 



solution treatment at 995 °F. The solubility of Cu in α-Al matrix is approximately 5.2 wt% at 











26 



solution temperature of 995 °F (see Figure 22). Therefore, 1 – 2 wt% of undissolved θ particles 



will remain in the α-Al matrix. If the alloy is cooled slowly during quenching (after solution 



treatment), additional θ particles can form and the pre-existing θ particles have time to coarsen. 



There will be inadequate time for additional θ particles to precipitate when the plate is cooled 



rapidly during quenching after solution treatment. The precipitation and coarsening of θ phase 



during quenching decreases Cu concentration in α-Al matrix, which lowers the degree of 



precipitation hardening after artificial aging. Therefore, the key mechanical properties, such as 



tensile strength and ductility, near the plate core are affected by the combined effect of increased 



θ precipitation and reduced volume fraction of the primary strengthening precipitate, θ'. In 



summary, the difference in the precipitate microstructure at the edge and center of the plate results 



in the observed edge effect for 5-in thick AA2219 plate. 



 
Figure 27. The production steps for AA2219 and the key microstructure evolutions that 



contribute to the observed edge effects. GB stands for grain boundary. 



5.0 Summary and Conclusions 



(1) Through-thickness edge effect can be attributed to three factors: (1) rolling temperature is lower 



at t/6 (near surface) than at t/3 and 2t/5 (near mid-plane), which mitigates grain boundary θ 



precipitation at t/6 (near surface); (2) more pronounced θ fragmentation occurs at t/6 (near surface) 



than at 2t/5 (near mid-plane), as the effective rolling strain is higher at t/6 (near surface) than at 



2t/5 (near mid-plane), which results in more Cu available for precipitaion hardening during aging; 



(3) quench cooling rate is higher at t/6 (near surface), which inhibits precipitation of θ phase and 



enhances Cu supersaturation in α-Al matrix during quench cooling. The increased Cu 



supersaturation in α-Al matrix promotes θ' precipitation during artificial aging. As a result, the 



volume fraction of θ' is higher at the edge than at the center of the thick plate, and the content of 



brittle θ (Al2Cu) phase is lower at the edge (near surface) than at the center (near mid-plane). The 



favorable microstructure evolution during hot-rolling and quench cooling leads to a noticeable 



improvement in ductility and tensile strength toward the plate edge. 











27 



(2) Through-width edge effect is primarily caused by quench sensitivity as evidenced by a distinct 



through-width hardness gradient from plate width edge (higher hardness) to plate width center 



(lower hardness). Higher quench cooling rate at the width edge strengthens the material by 



increasing the supersaturation of Cu in Al-Cu solid solution and promoting precipitation of θ' 



during artificial aging treatment. Ductility is also higher at the width edge, as precipitation and 



coarsening of the brittle θ phase is suppressed due to faster quench cooling rate. In contrast, lower 



quench cooling rate at the plate width center promotes θ precipitation and decreases the super-



saturation of Cu in Al-Cu solid solution, which leads to reduced ductility and tensile strength (due 



to reduced θ' precipitation) after artificial aging. The favorable microstructure evolution during 



quench cooling leads to a noticeable improvement in ductility and tensile strength toward the plate 



width edge. 



(3) DSC is a viable time-saving screening method that can determine the weaker location through 



the plate thickness. Based on the variation in mass fraction of Al-Cu eutectic phase, the weakest 



location is projected to be between 3t/8 and t/3 locations, which is consistent with the results 



obtained from quantitative size and area fraction analyses of θ particle using SEM and the 



quantitative image analysis technique.
