<p>2
</p>
<p>Background
</p>
<p>• Officially named in 2019, NASA’s 
Artemis program aims to establish a 
sustainable architecture to enable 
long term human explorations of the 
Moon and later Mars
• As a part of this sustainable 
</p>
<p>framework, Gateway is envisioned 
as a habitable orbital outpost in 
cislunar space
• Gateway will support human 
</p>
<p>landings on the surface of the moon, 
serve as a communications hub for 
cislunar space, and support the 
development and maturation of 
technologies needed to continue 
human exploration on to Mars Image credit: NASA</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>3
</p>
<p>Background (continued)
</p>
<p>• The first elements of Gateway to be 
delivered will be the Power and 
Propulsion Element (PPE) and the 
Habitation and Logistics Outpost 
(HALO)
• PPE and HALO will be launched and 
</p>
<p>together as the Co-Manifested 
Vehicle (CMV) and complete their 
transfer to the Moon as a single 
stack
• The Ion Propulsion System (IPS) 
</p>
<p>aboard PPE will be used to push the 
CMV stack to Gateway’s chosen 
baseline orbit, an Earth-Moon L2 
Southern (L2S) Near Rectilinear Halo 
Orbit (NRHO)
</p>
<p>Image credit: NASA</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>4
</p>
<p>CMV Lunar Transit (Design Reference Mission - 5)
</p>
<p>• CMV will fly a low-thrust Lunar Transit 
trajectory[1]
</p>
<p>– Launch vehicle will deliver CMV to 33,700 x 200 
km parking orbit
</p>
<p>– 50 kW class PPE SEP system will transit CMV 
to the NRHO
</p>
<p>• Lunar Transit will be more than 365 days in 
duration
</p>
<p>– SEP system will be operating for more than 
2/3rds of transit
</p>
<p>• PPE is responsible for &gt; 3 km/s Δv
– PPE will raise the CMV out of the Earth’s gravity 
</p>
<p>well over &gt;300 days of nearly continuous 
thrusting[1]
</p>
<p>• CMV Lunar Transit trajectory is broken into 4 
primary phases
</p>
<p>1. Spiral Phase
2. Alignment Phase
3. Ballistic Phase
4. Insertion Phase</p>

</p>
<p>Reference Trajectory
</p>
<p>• Modified spiral phase model used 
as reference trajectory
• DRM-5 uses a thruster duty cycle 
</p>
<p>to account for a number of 
uncertainties and coast events
• Operational constraint dictates 
</p>
<p>electric thrusters be shut down 
during low-altitude perigee passes
</p>
<p>– These passes are not explicitly 
modeled in DRM-5
</p>
<p>– Occur entirely within the first 42 
days of the transit
</p>
<p>• New reference spiral phase is 
developed which explicitly models 
these low altitude passes</p>

<p>11
</p>
<p>Conjunction Avoidance Implementation
</p>
<p>• Typical mission paradigm involves a vehicle primarily under coast with short duration 
maneuvers separated by hours or days
• Missions where EP is used for station keeping (Gateway, geo-com, etc) maintain this 
</p>
<p>“coast dominant” CONOPS
• The Lunar Transit represents a departure from this norm, where the vehicle will be 
</p>
<p>primarily under thrust for &gt;300 days[1]
– Some commercial low-thrust GTO missions do not maintain a conjunction avoidance strategy 
– SMART-1 (which flew a similar trajectory to the Lunar Transit) launched in 2003 to a 42,000 x 7,000km 
</p>
<p>parking orbit
• Two primary methods by which to generate separation from the reference are 
</p>
<p>investigated:
– Interrupting a planned thrust arc with an additional inserted coast
– Utilize RCS to complete an impulsive maneuver
</p>
<p>• Preliminary conjunction avoidance strategy :
– Utilize coasting as the primary conjunction avoidance mechanism after MET +7 days
– Place additional coasts so that they bracket either apogee or perigee (or low altitude cutouts)
– Use RCS as a last resort (after MET +7) and place RCS burns at apogee when they are required</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>12
</p>
<p>Implementation Assumptions
</p>
<p>• Three recommended coast locations chosen for operational simplicity and ability to affect the 
trajectory
</p>
<p>– Bracketing apogee
– Before low altitude cutout
– After low altitude cutout
</p>
<p>• Impulsive RCS burn placed at apogee, firing along the velocity vector
</p>
<p>Able to generate separation within ½ revolution
Coasts near perigee generate larger separations but require a full 
revolution to take effect
</p>

<p>18
</p>
<p>Summary
</p>
<p>• Ballistic leverage is shown to be an effective alternative to propulsive Debris Avoidance 
Maneuvers (DAM’s)
• Inserted coasting, when used as a collision avoidance action, can generate significant 
</p>
<p>separations (on the order of 10’s of km) within a short time horizon (&lt;10 hours)
</p>
<p>BALLISTIC LEVERAGE
Pros Cons
</p>
<p>• Repeatable
• Does not use 
</p>
<p>propellant
• Minimal impact to Δv
• May have negligible 
</p>
<p>impact on ability to 
complete NRHO 
insertion at desired 
epoch
</p>
<p>• All ballistic leverage 
represents missed 
thrust
</p>
<p>• May have to thrust 
into reference 
targeting coasts to 
catch up to reference
</p>
<p>RCS LEVERAGE
Pros Cons
</p>
<p>• Effective at generating 
short horizon leverage
</p>
<p>• Boosting trajectory, 
meaning you correct 
down to the reference
</p>
<p>• Uses propellant, may 
be prohibitive if 
needed many times 
</p>
<p>• Utilizing RCS outside of 
its intended use case
</p>
<p>• Heavy constraints on 
implementation</p>
<p></p>

– In-track vs cross-track vs radial separation and what is required to generate a 
</p>

oRequires greater understanding of operations CONOPS, maneuver 
</p>
<p>planning, etc
– Effect of conjunction avoidance maneuvers on eclipse timing and duration
</p>
<p>oLunar Transit must abide by 90-minute eclipse duration constraint
– Ability to target back to a reference following a conjunction avoidance 
</p>
<p>maneuver
– Conjunction avoidance later in the mission (passing through GEO, etc)</p>
