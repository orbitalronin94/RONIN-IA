Conjunction Avoidance Implementation



• Typical mission paradigm involves a vehicle primarily under coast with short duration 
maneuvers separated by hours or days
• Missions where EP is used for station keeping (Gateway, geo-com, etc) maintain this 



“coast dominant” CONOPS
• The Lunar Transit represents a departure from this norm, where the vehicle will be 



primarily under thrust for >300 days[1]
– Some commercial low-thrust GTO missions do not maintain a conjunction avoidance strategy 
– SMART-1 (which flew a similar trajectory to the Lunar Transit) launched in 2003 to a 42,000 x 7,000km 



parking orbit
• Two primary methods by which to generate separation from the reference are 



investigated:
– Interrupting a planned thrust arc with an additional inserted coast
– Utilize RCS to complete an impulsive maneuver



• Preliminary conjunction avoidance strategy :
– Utilize coasting as the primary conjunction avoidance mechanism after MET +7 days
– Place additional coasts so that they bracket either apogee or perigee (or low altitude cutouts)
– Use RCS as a last resort (after MET +7) and place RCS burns at apogee when they are required

Implementation Assumptions



• Three recommended coast locations chosen for operational simplicity and ability to affect the 
trajectory



– Bracketing apogee
– Before low altitude cutout
– After low altitude cutout



• Impulsive RCS burn placed at apogee, firing along the velocity vector



Able to generate separation within ½ revolution
Coasts near perigee generate larger separations but require a full 
revolution to take effect

18



Summary



• Ballistic leverage is shown to be an effective alternative to propulsive Debris Avoidance 
Maneuvers (DAM’s)
• Inserted coasting, when used as a collision avoidance action, can generate significant 



separations (on the order of 10’s of km) within a short time horizon (<10 hours)



BALLISTIC LEVERAGE
Pros Cons



• Repeatable
• Does not use 



propellant
• Minimal impact to Δv
• May have negligible 



impact on ability to 
complete NRHO 
insertion at desired 
epoch



• All ballistic leverage 
represents missed 
thrust



• May have to thrust 
into reference 
targeting coasts to 
catch up to reference



RCS LEVERAGE
Pros Cons



• Effective at generating 
short horizon leverage



• Boosting trajectory, 
meaning you correct 
down to the reference



• Uses propellant, may 
be prohibitive if 
needed many times 



• Utilizing RCS outside of 
its intended use case



• Heavy constraints on 
implementation
