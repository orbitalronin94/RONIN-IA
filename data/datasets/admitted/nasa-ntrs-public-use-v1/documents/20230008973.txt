<p>MSR CAMPAIGN OVERVIEW
</p>
<p>For Planning and Discussion Purposes 5</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Mars Sample Receiving 
Project
</p>
<p>Due to the potential for past or present indigenous life forms on Mars:
</p>
<p>• Sample return portion of the MSR Campaign is classified as a Category V Restricted Earth Return 
(RER) activity;
</p>
<p>• Requires an EIS under 14 CFR 1216.306
</p>
<p>Two-tiered Programmatic Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) considers potential impacts 
associated with the return of Mars samples to Earth:
</p>
<p>• Tier I  focuses on the MSR flight elements SRL and ERO, plus ground recovery;
• Tier II focuses on transportation of samples and the Sample Receiving Facility
</p>

<p>POTENTIAL ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACTS EVALUATED IN
TIER I PEIS
</p>
<p>For Planning and Discussion Purposes 8
</p>
<p>• Potential impacts to these resources:
– Health and safety
– Land use
– Water resources
– Biological resources
– Hazardous materials 
– Cultural resources
– Air resources
– Geology resources
– Climate change
– Traffic
– Socioeconomics
</p>
<p>• NEPA process may require consultation with outside agencies:
– Historic Preservation Office and local Native American Tribes 
</p>
<p>regarding the National Historic Preservation Act
– U.S. Fish and Wildlife
– U.S. Corps of Army Engineers
– Possible cooperating agencies
</p>
<p>• US Air Force:  manages launch facilities at CCSFS and proposed 
landing site at UTTR
</p>
<p>• US Army:  responsible for transport via Biological Select Agent and 
Toxins (BSAT) protocols
</p>
<p>• Centers for Disease Control:  invited due to expertise with BSAT 
possession, use, &amp; handling
</p>
<p>• US Department of Agriculture:  invited due to expertise with BSAT 
transport &amp; protocols</p>

<p>RISK COMMUNICATION FOR THE MSR SRP (NEPA TIER II)
</p>
<p>For Planning and Discussion Purposes 22</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Mars Sample Receiving 
Project
</p>
<p>• A coordinated approach for communicating about potentially controversial topics with the media, 
the public, educators, legislators, and governmental bodies.
‒ Major elements: strategic thinking, advanced planning, cultivating an “open” mindset among project leaders 
</p>
<p>and partners, developing public trust through well-prepared, empathetic, visible spokespersons
‒ Goal: A shared, sharpened awareness of potential stakeholder concerns about the proposed action
‒ Key Principles: Be open, respectful, clear, accurate timely, accessible, interactive 
</p>
<p>• Recognizes the citizen’s role in considering the value and safety of major Federal endeavors.
‒ Good Risk Communication takes the initiative to create effective information products, and use popular 
</p>
<p>communications channels to facilitate the citizen’s role in Federal decision-making processes
‒ “Risk communication is successful only if it adequately informs the decision maker.” - National Research 
</p>
<p>Council, Committee on Risk Perception and Communication (1989)
• Demonstrates an agency’s commitment to proactive, open and informed dialogue between 
</p>
<p>experts and the public about environmental and/or matters of public safety.
‒ Promotes better-informed public discussion and (ideally) greater appreciation for agency’s decision 
</p>
<p>rationale
‒ Done effectively, risk communication builds mutual trust and is a “win-win” for the public and the agency
</p>
