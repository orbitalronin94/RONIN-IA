<p>Overview of NASA Fission Surface Power
</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS-2023)
</p>
<p>Lindsay Kaldon
Fission Surface Power Project Manager
</p>
<p>NASA Glenn Research Center</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>2
</p>
<p>• Nuclear power systems will enable robust exploration of the Moon 
and Mars
• Reliable and robust energy production is essential to human and scientific 
</p>
<p>exploration missions to the Moon (to support Artemis) and Mars
</p>
<p>• Fission power systems can provide abundant and continuous surface 
power in all environmental conditions on the Moon and Mars:
• Lunar night is 14.5 Earth days long and permanently shadowed regions may 
</p>
<p>contain water ice. Surface nuclear power is required for a sustainable lunar 
presence
</p>
<p>• Mars has recurring planet-wide dust storms that can last for weeks or months
</p>
<p>• A fission system designed for a capability demonstration on the Moon will 
be directly applicable to human Mars exploration
</p>
<p>• Recent analyses indicate that a Mars fission surface power system is 
likely to enable 2x less mass to be flown to space and be more reliable 
than a comparable solar power system in the 10 to 40 kWe class
</p>

<p>What is a Fission Surface Power System?
</p>
<p>4
</p>
<p>• The Fission Surface Power hardware development is part of NASA 
Space Technology Mission Directorate’s (STMD) Space Nuclear 
Technologies Portfolio
</p>
<p>• Fission Surface Power
• Nuclear Thermal Propulsion and Nuclear Electric Propulsion
</p>
<p>• It is an autonomous nuclear power system that is launched, placed 
on the Moon’s surface by a lander, transported to its operational 
location, activated, and connected to a user power interface up to a 
kilometer away
</p>
<p>• It will have a ten-year life, consisting of a 1-year demonstration 
followed by operational support for Artemis
</p>
<p>• It will be designed so it is extensible to Mars missions
</p>
<p>Key Design Characteristics
</p>
<p> 40 kWe output at 120 Vdc
 6000 kg mass limit, fits on a lander
 5 rem/year above background at 1km
 Operate on the lander, or be transported</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Concept Results (Glenn Research Center’s COMPASS Team):
 FSP System delivered in 3 pallets:  Reactor, Controller, User 
</p>
<p>Load Interface
 Reactor Pallet:
</p>
<p>– A HALEU-moderated reactor with radiation shielding to protect 
co-located plant equipment, nearby electrical controls (50 m), 
and crew habitation area (1 km) 
</p>
<p>– Power conversion consists of four 12 kWe Stirling converters 
(48 kWe gross)
</p>
<p>– Deployable radiators that leverage the International Space 
Station design but operate at higher temperature (&gt;400 K)
</p>
<p>– Shielded Ka-Band link for communications to Earth
 Controller Pallet:  Stirling electrical controllers, high-voltage boost 
</p>
<p>electronics, thermal management, spool and 50 m cable
 User Load Interface:  Electronics to convert high transmission 
</p>
<p>voltage to 120 Vdc for loads, thermal management, spool and 1 
km cable
</p>

<p>Fission Surface Power Summary
</p>
<p> NASA is working with the Department of Energy and their federally funded 
laboratories to establish a lunar fission surface power system
</p>
<p> NASA focus is on designing, building, and demonstrating a low enriched uranium 
fission surface power system that is directly applicable for Moon and Mars, scalable 
to higher power levels
</p>
<p> NASA will continue to be closely engaged with industry to seek innovative, unique 
design approached for fission surface power systems</p>
