<p>NASA Moon2Mars Architecture:  Strategy and Objectives
</p>
<p>Lunar 
Infrastructure (LI)
</p>
<p>Released 6 April 2023
</p>
<p>LI-1:  Develop an incremental Lunar power generation and distribution 
system that is evolvable to support continuous robotic/human operation 
and is capable of scaling to global power utilization and industrial power 
levels.
</p>
<p>MI-1:  Develop Mars surface power sufficient for an initial human Mars 
exploration campaign 
</p>
<p>• Human Lunar Return
</p>
<p>• Foundational Exploration
</p>
<p>• Sustained Lunar Evolution
</p>
<p>• Humans to Mars
</p>
<p>Mars
Infrastructure (MI)
</p>
<p>MI-1:  Develop Mars surface power sufficient for an initial human Mars 
exploration campaign </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>• Nuclear power systems will enable robust exploration of the Moon 
and Mars
• Reliable and robust energy production is essential to human and scientific 
</p>
<p>exploration missions to the Moon (to support Artemis) and Mars
</p>
<p>• Fission power systems can provide abundant and continuous surface 
power in all environmental conditions on the Moon and Mars:
• Lunar night is 14.5 Earth days long and permanently shadowed regions may 
</p>
<p>contain water ice. Surface nuclear power is required for a sustainable lunar 
presence
</p>
<p>• Mars has recurring planet-wide dust storms that can last for weeks or months
</p>
<p>• A fission system designed for a capability demonstration on the Moon will 
be directly applicable to human Mars exploration
</p>
<p>• Recent analyses indicate that a Mars fission surface power system is 
likely to enable 2x less mass to be flown to space and be more reliable 
than a comparable solar power system in the 10 to 40 kWe class
</p>

<p>Fission Surface Power (FSP) Overview 
</p>
<p>Need
Establish a durable, high power, sun-
</p>
<p>independent power source for                  
NASA missions
</p>
<p>Support Moon and 
Mars mission 
requirements
</p>
<p>Deliver a flight 
qualified Lunar 
demonstration 
</p>
<p>system
</p>
<p>Transition FSP 
technology to 
</p>
<p>industry
</p>
<p>Established FSP system design can be leveraged for subscale NEP flight demonstrator or terrestrial application
</p>
<p>• Develop a 40 kWe HALEU lunar fission power system for a launch date by the end of the decade
• Final design must show extensibility to support a Mars human exploration mission
• Project scope includes technology development, design, engineering, and delivery of integrated system
• Project near-term focus remains in technology development and formulation
• DOE/Idaho National Laboratory (INL) is managing nuclear industry design contracts</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Fission Surface Power (FSP) Project Overview
</p>
<p>Industry will design and develop the system in two phases:
</p>
<p> Phase 1: Completed Sept 2023
– Three industry teams worked 1-year contracts for initial designs
</p>
<p>– Deliverables included design documents, requirements, schedule                                           
and cost estimates for Phase 2
</p>
<p> Phase 2: 
– Planned to be a separate, open and competitive procurement
– Deliverables include an engineering development unit and flight unit
</p>

<p>Concept Results (Glenn Research Center’s COMPASS Team):
 FSP System delivered in 3 pallets:  Reactor, Controller, User 
</p>
<p>Load Interface
 Reactor Pallet:
</p>
<p>– A HALEU-moderated reactor with radiation shielding to protect 
co-located plant equipment, nearby electrical controls (50 m), 
and crew habitation area (1 km) 
</p>
<p>– Power conversion may consist of Stirling or Brayton converters
– Deployable radiators that leverage the International Space 
</p>
<p>Station design but operate at higher temperature (&gt;400 K)
– Shielded Ka-Band link for communications to Earth
</p>
<p> Controller Pallet:  power conversion electrical controllers, high-
voltage boost electronics, thermal management, spool, 50m cable
</p>
<p> User Load Interface:  Electronics to convert high transmission 
voltage to 120 Vdc for loads, thermal management, spool and 1 
km cable
</p>

<p>Summary
</p>
<p> NASA is working with the Department of Energy and their federally funded 
laboratories to establish a lunar fission surface power system that is directly 
applicable for Moon and Mars
</p>
<p> NASA-GRC technology investments are targeting key non-nuclear systems needs, 
including power conversion solutions, radiation hardening of electronics, and power 
distribution capabilities critical to nuclear systems
</p>
<p> NASA will continue to be closely engaged with industry, academia, and other 
external stakeholders (both domestic and international) to seek innovative, unique 
designs for fission surface power systems</p>
