<p>Introduction to CFEET</p>
<p>CFEET – Compact Fuel Element Environmental Test</p>
<p>Hot H2 furnace to test nuclear fuel and materials at temperatures ~2600K</p>
<p>Simulates the temperature and H2 atmosphere inside a nuclear thermal engine</p>
<p>First test on nuclear fuel and materials of interest prior to scale up and nuclear reactor testing</p>

<p>How CFEET Works</p>
<p>NASA-Marshall Space Flight Center – Fuel Development Plan –May 13, 2020</p>
<p>RF power supply sends an AC current through an inductor and the susceptor to be heated is placed inside the inductor. </p>
<p>Circulating eddy currents are induced within the tungsten susceptor.</p>
<p>Material is heated by thermal radiation that passes from the susceptor to the material.</p>
<p>These induced currents flow against the electrical resistivity of the metal, generating precise and localized heat without any direct contact between the part and the inductor.</p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>6</p>
<p>Validation</p>
<p></p>
<p>Pyrometers </p>
<p>Measures radiation from an object remotely</p>
<p>Essential to our work because most thermocouples cannot reach those high temperatures needed</p>
<p>Only thermocouple that comes close is Type K (-200°C to 1250°C )</p>
<p>Graphite analysis</p>
<p>No major reactions</p>
<p>Can maintain temperature at  2327°C (2500K)</p>
<p>Pyrometer Analysis</p>
<p>Williamson- temperature: 650-3000°C 700 and 800nm</p>
<p>Ircon- temperature: 600-3000°C 2 wavelength bands 850-1050nm 1000-1100nm</p>
<p>Far- temperature: 800-4000 °C  500-1000nm (500 channels)</p>
<p>Each pyrometer were placed with the exception of the Williamson was placed to monitor the sample and the susceptor</p>
<p>Final configuration: Far monitors the sample and the Ircon monitors the susceptor</p>
