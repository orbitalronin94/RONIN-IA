<div class="slide-content"><p>Genesis of NASA Radio Frequency Interference (RFI) Reporting</p>
<p>2007 Terra &amp; Landsat RFI events – not well reported</p>
<p>US Purposeful Interference Response Team (PIRT) established</p>
<p>NASA Space Asset Protection Program (SAPP), now Mission Resilience and Protection Program (MRPP), established</p>
<p>Implemented NASA “Unexplained Incident Escalation Procedure” with support from agency partners</p>
<p>Enrolled missions in detection and reporting</p>
<p>2019:  Institutionalized Agency-level requirement to report events</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>End of Mission (EOM) Experiments</p>
<p>Three NASA EOM experiment campaigns during the 2010s yielded the following findings to key questions:</p>
<p></p>
<p>Characterize vulnerabilities of space systems</p>
<p>Unprotected command links are susceptible to capable adversaries</p>
<p>Assess feasibility of threat actions</p>
<p>Modestly sophisticated threat systems can affect space assets</p>
<p>Identify signatures/indicators of threat effects</p>
<p>Command receiver telemetry is particularly useful in characterizing space events</p>
<p>Inform mission requirements and design trades</p>
<p>TT&amp;C responses in a contested environment should be considered in design trades</p>
<p>Validate hardware, software and CONOPS mitigations</p>
<p>Critical commands such as hardware commands merit special consideration</p>
<p>Improve flight ops security practices</p>
<p>Implement good security hygiene in command loads</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Identifying &amp; Implementing Protective Measures</p>
<p> 8</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<p></p>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Representative Threats to Civil Space Missions</p>
<p> 9</p>
<p>A representative set of threat actions below reflects emerging counterspace threats to a wide range of civil space missions:</p>
<p>Command link jamming – sufficient RF energy directed at the spacecraft in its command link frequency may preclude the ability to receive commands during the period of RF emissions.
</p>
<p>Command link intrusion – valid commands with proper encryption or authentication injected within the command path may lead to a temporary or permanent loss of control.
</p>
<p>GPS jamming – sufficient RF energy directed at the spacecraft at GPS signal frequencies may preclude the spacecraft GPS receiver from receiving GPS signals during the period of RF emissions.
</p>
<p>GPS spoofing – RF energy directed at the spacecraft at GPS signal frequencies with selected phasing may alter the navigation and timing solution of the spacecraft during the period of RF emissions.
</p>
<p>Cyber exploitation of critical project information (CPI) – adversary acquisition and use of CPI may enable the adversary to overcome system protections and induce harm to the system or to manipulate science data.</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<p></p>
<div class="slide-content"><p>NASA Technical Standard   NASA-STD-1006A</p>
<p> 10</p>
<p>Maintain Command Authority</p>
<p>Command Stack Protection: Programs/projects shall protect the command stack with encryption that meets or exceeds the FIPS 140, Level 1.</p>
<p>Backup Command Link Protection: If a project uses an encrypted primary command link, any backup command link shall at minimum use authentication.</p>
<p>Command Link Critical Program/Project Information (CPI): The program/project shall protect the confidentiality of command link CPI as controlled unclassified information (CUI) to prevent inadvertent disclosure to unauthorized parties.
</p>
<p>Ensure Positioning, Navigation, and Timing (PNT) Resilience</p>
<p>PNT Interference Recognition: If project-external PNT services are required, projects shall ensure that systems are resilient to the complete loss of, or temporary interference with, external PNT services.
</p>
<p>Report Unexplained Interference</p>
<p>Interference Reporting: Projects/Spectrum Managers/Operations Centers shall report unexplained interference to MRPP or to other designated notifying organizations.</p>
<p>Interference Reporting Training: Projects/Spectrum Managers/Operations Centers shall conduct proficiency training for reporting unexplained interference.</p>

<p>Vulnerabilities exist across the enterprise</p>
<p>STD-1006 did a lot to address some concerns across the eight newly assessed areas, still room for improvements</p>
<p>Space Security Best Practices Guide (BPG) focuses on three main thrusts</p>
<p>Software (both space and ground)</p>
<p>On-board computing, processing and bus</p>
<p>Preventions and mitigations through process</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Rationale Behind the BPG</p>
<p> 12</p>
<p>The BPG offers cyber vulnerability-based security principles and controls applicable to both space and ground segments</p>
<p>The principles are meant to be easily achievable regardless of project size, scope, or whether international, corporate, or university</p>
<p>The principles selected focus on a risk-based approach to mitigating potential impairments to mission success </p>
<p>Principles were identified as an initial starting point of the most critical implementations for missions to consider</p>
<p>The underlying security principles and associated controls were identified through an iterative process to address today’s cyber actor Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) used in attempts to compromise mission cyber capabilities</p>
<p>BPG is a starting point to address the cyber challenges to NASA mission success</p>
</div>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Systems Security Engineering</p>
<p>NASA is building a sustained capability for systems security engineering</p>
<p>Goal: systems that are appropriately protected from adversarial threats</p>
<p>…and still meet their primary objectives</p>
<p>Specialty sub-discipline of systems engineering, covers the entire system</p>
<p>Applies design principles from both engineering and security</p>
<p>Augmented with subject-matter expertise from other disciplines such as cybersecurity specializations (e.g., network security) </p>
<p>On an engineering team supporting a specific system, the Systems Security Engineer (SSE) will be the focal point for addressing system security topics for that system</p>
<p>Well aligned with other comparable efforts</p>
<p>NIST SP800-160 vol 1 rev 1, Engineering Trustworthy Secure Systems</p>
<p>OUSD R&amp;E Cyber Resilient Weapon Systems (CRWS)</p>
<p>Ongoing pilot with NIST to apply systems security engineering in space systems</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p> 17</p>
<p>The SSE integrates appropriate capabilities into the design and operations
of a system to be sufficiently resilient and able to meet its objectives</p>
