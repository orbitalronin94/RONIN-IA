<p>Classical Thermodynamics
• Evolution of systems in equilibrium through (reversible) 
</p>
<p>exchanges of matter and energy 

• Systems can be open, closed, isolated. Equilibrium 
</p>
<p>can be mechanical, thermal, chemical.

• Systems can be described by macroscopic state 
</p>
<p>variables (p, V, T, N_k …)

• 1st law: Energy

• 2nd law (closed systems): Entropy
</p>

<p>Dynamical Prescriptions 
• The constraints of the system of interest determine which thermodynamic 
</p>
<p>function is monotone (Zemansky 1957)

• Constant energy -&gt; Entropy increases.

• Constant pressure -&gt; Gibbs Free Energy decreases.

• Constant temperature -&gt; Helmholtz Free Energy decreases
</p>
<p>• Standard Mass Action Kinetics are consistent with these predictions (Krambeck 1970)

<p>IV. Implicit Thermodynamics
</p>
<p>20</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Implicit Thermodynamics
• Nonlinear stability proofs assume forward and backward 
</p>
<p>rates related through an equilibrium constant which 
satisfies the law of Mass Action.

• Limiting condition for more advanced chemistry 
</p>
<p>problems (high-temperature hypersonics with thermal 
non-equilibrium, radiation ...) 
</p>
<p>• Engineering practice (data-driven / empirical / reduced 
models) begs a two-fold inverse problem

</p>
<p>1. Does a data-driven thermo-chemical relaxation 
model inherently defines its own thermodynamics ?

</p>
<p>2. Would such “implicit thermodynamics” be 
compatible with the flow equations themselves ?
</p>
<p>21</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Implicit Thermodynamics
• YES (under certain conditions - Gouasmi &amp; Murman 2023)

• All numerical results actually obtained with curve-fit models
</p>
<p>• Implicit Entropy exists and does form a convex extension (Compressible Flow)
</p>
<p>• Implicit Free Energy (Generalized to arbitrary chemical systems) :
</p>

<p>Summary
• Computational physics grows with our ability to: 

• numerically enforce physical prescriptions within algorithms

• grasp the physics inherent to the models we use, be they theoretical or data-
</p>
<p>driven

• Time-integration schemes for thermo-chemical relaxation processes can greatly 
</p>
<p>benefit from enforcing thermodynamics (F, G, S) discretely.

• We argue that reverse-engineering the maths is possible

• relaxation model -&gt; “thermodynamics” 

</p>
<p>• This line of work heavily builds on classical thermodynamics notions, which are 
inherently limited (Prigogine &amp; Defay 1954). 

• Follow-up work building from broader microscopic/statistical stance. 
</p>
