Classical Thermodynamics
• Evolution of systems in equilibrium through (reversible) 



exchanges of matter and energy 

• Systems can be open, closed, isolated. Equilibrium 



can be mechanical, thermal, chemical.

• Systems can be described by macroscopic state 



variables (p, V, T, N_k …)

• 1st law: Energy

• 2nd law (closed systems): Entropy

Dynamical Prescriptions 
• The constraints of the system of interest determine which thermodynamic 



function is monotone (Zemansky 1957)

• Constant energy -> Entropy increases.

• Constant pressure -> Gibbs Free Energy decreases.

• Constant temperature -> Helmholtz Free Energy decreases



• Standard Mass Action Kinetics are consistent with these predictions (Krambeck 1970)

IV. Implicit Thermodynamics



20











Implicit Thermodynamics
• Nonlinear stability proofs assume forward and backward 



rates related through an equilibrium constant which 
satisfies the law of Mass Action.

• Limiting condition for more advanced chemistry 



problems (high-temperature hypersonics with thermal 
non-equilibrium, radiation ...) 



• Engineering practice (data-driven / empirical / reduced 
models) begs a two-fold inverse problem




1. Does a data-driven thermo-chemical relaxation 
model inherently defines its own thermodynamics ?




2. Would such “implicit thermodynamics” be 
compatible with the flow equations themselves ?



21











Implicit Thermodynamics
• YES (under certain conditions - Gouasmi & Murman 2023)

• All numerical results actually obtained with curve-fit models



• Implicit Entropy exists and does form a convex extension (Compressible Flow)



• Implicit Free Energy (Generalized to arbitrary chemical systems) :

Summary
• Computational physics grows with our ability to: 

• numerically enforce physical prescriptions within algorithms

• grasp the physics inherent to the models we use, be they theoretical or data-



driven

• Time-integration schemes for thermo-chemical relaxation processes can greatly 



benefit from enforcing thermodynamics (F, G, S) discretely.

• We argue that reverse-engineering the maths is possible

• relaxation model -> “thermodynamics” 




• This line of work heavily builds on classical thermodynamics notions, which are 
inherently limited (Prigogine & Defay 1954). 

• Follow-up work building from broader microscopic/statistical stance.
