Background








Self-pressurization


Liquid propellant and/or ullage is heated due to heat leakage to the propellant tank which gradually increases tank pressure


Must be understood and controlled for safety and to minimize propellant venting


Key dynamics


Low Reynolds number turbulent flow and natural convection, phase change


Nodal tools are unable to model and 3-D CFD analysis is required


Goals of validation


Primary: Time-accurate prediction of tank pressure for given heat leakage rate to the tank with reasonable margin of error


Secondary: Time-accurate temperature prediction across the tank

Loci-Stream


Pressure-based solver


Solid heat module for conjugate heat analysis


Two phase sharp interface method or VOF method for two phase flows


Two-phase Sharp Interface Method


VOF advection using geometric reconstruction is expensive and slow


Developed sharp interface method for quiescent interface


10-100x speed up  Allows testing for model sensitivities


Avoid stationary interface instabilities of VOF method


Improvement on “lumped gas liquid CFD” method


Allows simulation of ullage dynamics – Lump gas node replaced with 3-D gas CFD

Mesh and Timestep Sensitivity


All mesh are sufficiently refined to capture the pressurization of the tank

13


Flow Regime and Accommodation Factor Sensitivity


Laminar model lacks turbulence mixing and overpredicts interface temperature


SST model enhances mixing but still vastly underpredicts the mixing and consequently overpredicts the interface temperature and ullage pressure


The k-epsilon model performs best














Determined and modeled the most important features for self-pressurization to successfully transfer the learnings to space flight application


Liquid simulant convective currents induced by localized heating, heat transfer within the fluid and with the tank walls, and the phase change mass transfer at the interface


Validated Loci-Stream CFD solver for self-pressurization of ZBOT tank in normal gravity


Tank pressure, pressurization rate as well as temperature across the tank simulant, ullage and walls are predicted with very good accuracy


Evaluated model sensitivities to ensure validation is independent of modeling parameters and can be applied to same class of problem

Kinetics-Balance Method (Iterative)

Interface Turbulence Treatment


Experimental evidence: turbulence is damped and behaves somewhat similar to wall-bounded flow


Also, some evidence of asymmetric behavior


Wall-like on gas side


Slip-wall-like on liquid side


Simplified Egorov’s damping approach


Gas interface is always wall-like


Liquid interface is either wall-like or slip-wall-like
