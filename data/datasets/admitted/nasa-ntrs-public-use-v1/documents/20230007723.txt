31





ORBITAL FLIGHT TEST





OFT: December 20-22, 2019 


Launch: Cape Canaveral, SLC-41


Landing: White Sands Space Harbor, NM


Met ascent and descent, entry, landing flight test objectives

















Before transporting NASA crews to the space station, the providers were required to demonstrate its system capabilities with a flight test. Boeing and SpaceX offered two flight tests to the ISS, the first uncrewed and the second with crew. Following the anomalies experienced on OFT, Boeing offered a second uncrewed flight test: OFT-2.

Crew: Bob Behnken, Doug Hurley

Crew recovery by GO Navigator, Gulf of Mexico (off coast of Pensacola, FL due to TS Isaias)























33











CREW-1





Crew-1 Mission: November 15, 2020 - May 2, 2021 


Crew: Shannon Walker, Victor Glover, Mike Hopkins, Soichi Noguchi


Falcon 9 Block 5 launched from KSC, LC-39A


Docked to ISS - Resilience (C207): 167 days docked/168 days in orbit 


Port Relocate on ISS: April 5, 2021 from Forward to Zenith


Crew recovery by GO Navigator, Gulf of Mexico (off coast of Panama City, FL) 


First nighttime splashdown since Apollo 8


Crew-1 broke the record for the longest spaceflight by a U.S. crewed vehicle, surpassing the 84-day mark set by an Apollo capsule on the final flight to the Skylab 











34





CREW-2





Crew-2 Mission: April 23, 2021 - November 8, 2021


Crew: Megan McArthur, Thomas Pesquet, Akihiko Hoshide, Shane Kimbrough


Docked to ISS: Endeavour (C206.2) for 199 days 17 hours


Port Relocate on ISS: July 21, 2021 from Forward to Zenith


Crew recovery by GO Navigator, Gulf of Mexico (off coast of Pensacola, FL) 


First time that NASA astronauts flew in a combined crew with JAXA and ESA astronauts


First crewed flight of a reused Crew Dragon and Falcon 9 booster


Port Relocate on ISS: July 21, 2021, from Forward to Zenith


Broke previous on-orbit record by Crew-1


First direct crew handover (Crew-1/Crew-2)


First indirect crew handover (Crew-2/Crew-3)


First ISS fly-around since Space Shuttle























35





CREW-3


Crew-3 Mission: November 10, 2021 – May 6, 2022


Crew: Raja Chari, Tom Marshburn, Matthias Maurer, Kayla Barron 


Docked to ISS: Endurance for 174 days, 6 hours


Crew recovery by MV Shannon in Gulf of Mexico (off coast of Tampa, FL)


First reuse of Crew Dragon nose cone


Launched 2 days after Crew-2 landing


First indirect crew handover (Crew-2/Crew-3) following post flight assessment


This mission brought the total number of humans who have been to space to more than 600 with Maurer (600) and Barron (601)























36





CREW-4


Crew-4 Mission: April 15, 2022 – October 14, 2022


Crew: Kjell Lindgren, Robert Hines, Jessica Watkins, Samantha Cristoforetti


Docked to ISS: Freedom for 169 days, 16 hours


Crew recovery by MV Megan in Atlantic Ocean


First four flight Falcon 9 First Stage Booster for CCP 


New Spacecraft Freedom with reuse of more Dragon composite components 


Crew recovery planned in Atlantic Ocean or Gulf of Mexico 


























37





OFT-2


OFT-2: May 19, 2022 - May 25, 2022


Launch: Cape Canaveral, SLC-41


Docked to ISS: 4 days, 18 hours


Landing: White Sands Missile Range, NM 


Met all test flight objectives





























38





CREW-5


Crew-5 Mission: October 5, 2022 – March 11, 2023


Crew: Nicole Mann, Josh Cassada, Koichi Wakata, Anna Kikina


Launch Vehicle: Falcon 9 (first flight), Kennedy Space Center, Pad 39A


Docked to ISS: Crew Dragon Endurance (second flight): 156.5 days


Crew recovery by MV Shannon off the coast of Tampa


First female commander of a CCP mission (Nicole Mann)


First NASA flight of an indigenous woman (Nicole Mann, Wailacki Round Valley Indian Tribe) 


First flight of a Russian cosmonaut on a US commercial spacecraft, Anna Kikina


First mission as a part of the crew swap system of keeping at least one NASA astronaut and one Roscosmos cosmonaut on each of the crew rotation missions, ensuring the ability to maintain ISS if either NASA or Roscosmos are unable to fly for an extended period

CREW-6

Crew: Stephen Bowen, Warren "Woody" Hoburg, Andrey Fedyaev, Sultan Al Neyadi


Launch Vehicle: Falcon 9 (first flight), Kennedy Space Center, Pad 39A


Docked to ISS: Crew Dragon Endeavour (fourth flight) up to 180 days planned


Crew recovery planned in Atlantic Ocean or Gulf of Mexico


First flight of a United Arab Emirates astronaut, Sultan Al Neyadi


First four-time reuse of a Crew Dragon capsule
