<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Problem Setting
</p>
<p>• H-M teaming is a vast and open area, rich with uncertainties.
</p>
<p>• Commonly expressed sentiment:
• “Let humans do what they do best, and machines do what they do best”.
</p>
<p>• Works for well-defined environments.
</p>
<p>• Ultimate decision-maker is assigned a priori.
</p>
<p>• Facts:
• In new environments, decision-making capabilities and needs may change in real 
</p>
<p>time.
</p>
<p>• A single decision precedes Ɐ action.
</p>
<p>• Strictly rule-based behavior may be fragile. 
</p>
<p>• Q: In a dynamic environment, which agent to trust with a specific decision?
</p>
<p>• Working hypothesis: formal representation of H-M decision-making 
supports reasoning about the system and reduces uncertainty in 
outcomes.
</p>
<p>3
</p>
<p>Image credit: NASA APPEL Knowledge Services</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Effects of Decision Domain Attributes
</p>
<p>• Low time-criticality allows for deliberation and negotiation among solutions.
</p>
<p>• Low (context-dependent) complexity allows for a priori authority selection.
</p>
<p>• High time criticality and high complexity require a priori trust or ensuring reliable computation in real time.
</p>
<p>• Claim: formal problem representation needed in time-critical, safety-critical, and high-complexity domains.
4
</p>
<p>Problem complexity</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>A Critical Aspect 
of Uncertainty
</p>
<p>• In any system that involves 
decision-making: the ability of 
agents to arrive at satisfactory 
decisions and the attendant 
actions in time, as a function of 
problem complexity.
</p>
<p>• Reducing solvability uncertainty 
is always done via bounding 
problem complexity; e.g., current 
airspace.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>General Idea • Represent problem complexity in a computable, actionable form.
</p>
<p>• Build models of problem solution behaviors as a function of 
problem complexity. 
</p>
<p>• Detect approaches to unacceptable complexity.
</p>
<p>• Reconfigure system and/or decision-making; dynamic re-
assignment of authority in H-M teams.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Measure of Complexity
</p>
<p>• Tractability of the problem solution on a time budget, as a function of 
external and internal parameters.
</p>
<p>• The quality of solutions of the decision-making problem:
• Constraint satisfaction
</p>
<p>• Distance from optimality
</p>
<p>• Robustness (maximum uncertainty under which performance is guaranteed)
</p>
<p>• Serendipity (minimum uncertainty that would allow for great improvements 
in performance)
</p>
<p>• Other, context-dependent and formulation-dependent functions.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Conceptual 
Reasoning 
Framework
</p>
<p>8
</p>
<p>Given:
</p>
<p>• An agent and a goal
</p>
<p>Initialize: 
</p>
<p>• Set time t=0
</p>
<p>• Set initial sampling time interval Δt
</p>
<p>• Set initial look-ahead time T
</p>
<p>• Set initial look-ahead sampling time interval Δτ
</p>
<p>• Select initial problem-solving algorithm P
</p>
<p>• Select environmental complexity parameters C
</p>
<p>• Select initial environmental complexity model M
</p>
<p>• Select transition criteria
</p>
<p>• Select stopping criteria </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Conceptual
</p>
<p>Framework, 
cont.
</p>
<p>Do until (stopping criteria are satisfied)
</p>
<p>Acquire and assess complexity parameters C at time t
</p>
<p>Set τ = t 
</p>
<p>Do while (τ ≤ t + T)
</p>
<p>Estimate look-ahead complexity parameters at time τ
</p>
<p>τ = τ + Δτ
</p>
<p>End do
</p>
<p>Input complexity parameters into complexity model M; assess approach to 
phase transition 
</p>
<p>If (approach to transition detected) then 
</p>
<p>Reconfigure operations (add structure ˅ change agent)
</p>
<p>Else
</p>
<p>Assess system’s performance slack
</p>
<p>If (slack)
</p>
<p>Reconfigure operations (relax structure ˅ change agent)
</p>
<p>Else
</p>
<p>Continue present operations 
</p>
<p>End if
</p>
<p>End if
</p>
<p>Update time
</p>
<p>End do</p>

</p>

</p>

<p>• Optimality.
</p>
<p>• Robustness: robust 
solutions live in the

<p>Evaluating 
Approach
</p>
<p>15
</p>
<p>• Accurate detection of shifts in tractability and quality of solutions from 
nominal?
</p>
<p>• Clear ID of dependence of precursors (slowing down of solutions, shrinking 
of feasible regions) as a function of variables?
</p>
<p>• Low percentage of false positives?
</p>
<p>• Note: Comparative metrics are not available because there is no baseline 
SOA.
</p>
<p>Metrics
</p>
<p>• In shared-rules approaches, low risk.
</p>
<p>• In algorithmic decision-making, the risk of not detecting precursors in time 
is higher.
</p>
<p>• Mitigation: Lack of detectable precursors will point to the need for different 
control schemes. 
</p>
<p>Risks
</p>
<p>• Insights into practical sensitivities of a dense, heterogeneous system to 
control strategies and uncertainties.
</p>
<p>• A component in a toolbox for monitoring system controllability, efficiency, 
and safety, regardless of the overall control strategy.
</p>

<p>How Would Transfer of Authority Happen?
</p>
<p>• (After conditioning the system to minimize loss of solvability, uncertainty remains.)
</p>
<p>• Given: 
• Agents in an H-M team, Alice, Bob, and Charlie.
• Alice and Bob propose two actions.
</p>
<p>• Q: How should Charlie decide which action to approve, i.e., whom to trust?
</p>
<p>• Fact: Proposed actions are results of problem solving by Alice and Bob.
</p>
<p>• If all problem objectives and their priorities are well known, comparison of solutions is 
straightforward. In an H-M team, such knowledge may be limited due to, e.g.:
</p>
<p>• Incomplete problem formulation.
• Implicit, changing, arising objectives.
• ML: reward hacking.
</p>
<p>• Build a model of trustworthiness (ability to solve problems) as a function of solution 
algorithm and use it to inform authority re-assignment for specific decisions. 
</p>
<p>16</p>
<p></p>
</div>

</p>
<p>• Simulated Decision Makers

<p>• Opt1

</p>
<p>• Finished with violated constraints for 77 problems.

</p>

</p>

</p>
<p>• Declared failure twice due to exceeding maximum number of iterations.</p>

</p>
<p>• Simulated Decision Makers
</p>
<p>• Which to trust?
• If “self-awareness” has the highest priority, then Opt3.
</p>
<p>• If never “stalling” has the highest priority, then Opt1.
</p>
<p>• In the works
• Refined analysis of performance trends:
</p>
<p>• While computational complexity for arbitrary NLP is unpredictable, preliminary analysis shows trends 
related to function form (non-convexity, non-linearity). 
</p>
<p>• Complexity modeling for problems that differ by dimension alone (e.g., in ab initio traffic modeling) 
appear to be more amenable to detection of performance trends.
</p>
<p>• Testing of multilevel algorithm for acceptance of decisions.
</p>
<p>• Testing of multiobjective formulation via non-antagonistic game theory [Germeyer, 1986].
</p>
<p>N.B. Decisions from untrustworthy decision-makes must be accepted in the absence of alternatives and non-action. </p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Concluding Remarks
</p>
<p>• Data collection via simulation-based and physical experimentation serves to build 
complexity models for environments of interest and agents’ abilities to solve problems.
</p>
<p>• A “look-ahead” into complexity parameters and the resulting estimate of complexity 
(tractability, quality of solutions, and other objectives) informs reconfiguration of the 
system or re-assignment of decision-makers until complexity estimates change again.
</p>
<p>• Re-assignment depends on context and problem dimensions.
</p>
<p>• The approach pre-conditions the system to minimize the chances of losing solvability. 
</p>
<p>N.B. In H-M teams, while authority for a specific problem solution may be transferred to an 
M agent, continued participation of H agents is likely beneficial. 
</p>
