• Cryogenic Fluid Management (CFM) of propellant storage tanks is in the 
critical path of most envisioned NASA exploration missions, including 
missions to Mars.



• The Zero Boil Off Tank (ZBOT) Experiment series is undertaken to 
delineate the important and fundamental Physical Phenomena that 
govern key propellant tank pressurization and pressure control using a 
small-scale simulant-fluid experimental investigation in the Microgravity 
Science Glovebox (MSG) unit aboard the ISS. 



• An important goal of the investigation is to develop a comprehensive Two-
phase CFD model that captures the underlying physics of propellant tank 
with fidelity as demonstrated by validation against both microgravity and 
1g data.



• The ZBOT-1 Experiment flew in 2017 and collected valuable self-
pressurization and jet mixing microgravity data for a pure single 
component system. The data was used for nearly 30 model validation case 
studies. 



• The ZBOT-NC Experiment is scheduled to fly in early 2025 to investigate 
the effect of noncondensable pressurant gases on self-pressurization and 
jet mixing pressure control for a two-component system in microgravity.

This is accomplished while investigating: 



• The tank thermal stratification, self-pressurization and wall boiling as a function of



global and localized heat loads, fill levels, and the molecular weight and concentrations



of the noncondensable gases.



• The tank thermal destratification and depressurization rates as a function of jet



temperature, jet Weber numbers (velocities), fill levels, the molecular weight and



concentrations of the noncondensable gases.



The main goal of ZBOT-NC experiment is to determine impact of a non-condensable pressurant gas 
on the evaporation/condensation and transport processes that affect tank pressurization and 
pressure control in microgravity



To achieve our objectives, we intend to perform: 



a) High accuracy measurement of the ullage pressure, temperatures at multiple location in the liquid and on tank wall, 
and noncondensable gas concentrations; 



b) Visualization of the phase front through white light imaging; and 



c) Non-intrusive capture of the liquid temperature fields using a state-of-the-art in-house developed and customized 
Quantum Dot Thermometry (QDT) diagnostics.
