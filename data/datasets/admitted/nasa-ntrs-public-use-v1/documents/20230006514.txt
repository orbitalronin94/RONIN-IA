primary fuel qualification program for modern U.S.

fission product release fraction) is a key goal for 
the development of this fuel form in order to 
demonstrate that the fuel design serves as a 
fission product containment mechanism.

element of the space reactor development program

<p>Motivation
</p>
<p>• Fuel qualification is expected to be a major element of each space reactor 
development program, which encompasses all fuel fabrication, testing, and 
analysis activities to mature a space reactor fuel form capable of meeting 
performance and mission requirements. 
</p>
<p>• There is no existing qualified fuel form for any of the space reactors currently of 
interest to NASA and existing infrastructure dedicated to space reactor fuel 
development is limited.
</p>
<p>• Purpose of this study was to assess the readiness of terrestrial nuclear fuels for 
space applications, including identification of key technology development 
needs and related risk (technical, cost, schedule). 
</p>
<p>For NASA applications, it is desirable for fuel technologies to be sufficiently mature with an established fabrication 
infrastructure to reduce cost and schedule for fuel development
</p>

<p>Fission Surface Power Assessment - Key Findings 
and Takeaways
</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023 14
Assessment of Coated Particle Fuels for 
</p>
<p>Space Nuclear Power and Propulsion Systems
</p>
<p>• Due to higher uranium loadings, reactors will be smaller (lower volume and mass) with UO2
</p>
<p>or UN pellet fuels compared to TRISO fueled reactors. 
- Shielding (volume and mass) will scale proportionally with reactor volume
</p>
<p>- Technology development would be focused on cladding, moderator, and heat pipe development to meet reference system 
</p>
<p>parameters
</p>
<p>- If peak operating temperature requirement is reduced (&lt; 900 K): UO2 cores could be developed faster (infrastructure, 
</p>
<p>performance database, and fabrication methods exist). 
</p>
<p>- If the temperature cannot be reduced, the TRISO reactor design may be a better option
</p>
<p>• Key Reactor Technology Development Challenge: Moderator
- Moderator dimensional and compositional instability negatively impacts reactivity worth, power profile, and structural integrity of 
</p>
<p>the core
</p>
<p>- High temperature moderators will require technology development to establish net shape fabrication processes or extend the 
</p>
<p>existing performance database
</p>
<p>• Ten-year lifetime requires a combined system ground test to obtain a complete performance 
</p>
<p>database.
</p>
<p>- For demonstration missions: demonstrate integrated fuel, moderator, heat pipe integrity under reactor operations
</p>
<p>- For qualification: reliability, instrumentation, system interfaces need to be demonstrated</p>
<p></p>

<p>Nuclear Electric Propulsion Assessment - Key 
Findings and Takeaways
</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023 17
Assessment of Coated Particle Fuels for Space 
</p>
<p>Nuclear Power and Propulsion Systems
</p>
<p>• For NEP systems, we believe alpha to be the primary driver and thus minimizing reactor mass / volume is 
</p>
<p>of high interest. 
</p>
<p>- UN pellets best fuels candidate to meet this need. Development of refractory cladding would be required
</p>
<p>- Moderator development (high temperature) will likely drive schedule
</p>
<p>- Modified TRISO fuels offer good high temperature, high burnup characteristics. However, there no heritage from historic fuel 
</p>
<p>form development and has a limited terrestrial fuel performance database (not qualified under AGR)
</p>
<p>• Technology development would be required to establish fuel fabrication processes and performance database
</p>
<p>• Key reactor technology development challenges 
- Moderator
</p>
<p>- Structural Materials (cladding)
</p>
<p>- System Ground Test
</p>
<p>• Extensibility: UN is more extensible than both UO2 and TRISO fuel forms assessed for FSP.
</p>
<p>- UN was assessed to allow for the most compact reactor design and is capable of high operating temperatures. 
</p>
<p>- A common fuel form between FSP and NEP would enable a common fuel production line to be leveraged for both applications.
</p>
<p>- A common fuel form would reduce the design and testing needed to mature and qualify flight hardware which could reduce 
</p>
<p>overall cost and schedule for the development of both systems.</p>

<p>Nuclear Thermal Propulsion Assessment - Key 
Findings and Takeaways
</p>
<p>Nuclear and Emerging Technologies for Space (NETS) Conference 2023 20
Assessment of Coated Particle Fuels for Space 
</p>
<p>Nuclear Power and Propulsion Systems
</p>
<p>• Isp is the primary KPP driving the need for technology development. Modern NTP programs 
</p>
<p>will require higher performance fuels than historic programs.
- Current desired performance parameters (2700 K exit temperature, 10 hour lifetime, 5+ MW/L power density)  will require fuel 
</p>
<p>forms to exceed historic operating conditions 
</p>
<p>- Longer lifetime, higher temperature fuel performance is needed. A significant development effort is still necessary regardless of 
</p>
<p>fuel form selection
</p>
<p>• UC2 Derivative Fuels
</p>
<p>- Advantage: ability to leverage historic design and performance databases
</p>
<p>- Disadvantage: fuel melting point must be exceeded to meet KPPs (beyond demonstrated failure limits)
</p>
<p>- Technology development is required for this fuel form to demonstrate performance capability
</p>
<p>• Cercer (ZrC matrix) with Modified Coated Particle Fuels
</p>
<p>- Advantage: fuel form design leverages high melting temperature and hydrogen compatible materials to provide a path to higher 
</p>
<p>temperatures and longer lifetimes 
</p>
<p>- Disadvantage: no historic comparison or direct commercial development analog, fuel failure modes are unknown
</p>
<p>- Technology development is required for this fuel form to demonstrate performance capability</p>

<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>• Space reactors can leverage terrestrial fuel technologies. Ongoing advanced 
reactor development activities are maturing fuel forms capable of higher 
operating temperatures and burnups in the range desired for space reactor 
applications. 
</p>
<p>• Meeting high performance KPPs will require deviation from established fuel 
types which will introduce new technical risks and require technology 
development.
</p>
<p>• New testing infrastructure is needed to prove out reactor operations / reliability
- Reliability requirements will drive overall testing program
</p>
<p>- Power reactors require facilities dedicated for long lifetime testing
</p>
<p>- NTP reactors require unique facilities capable of handling the H2 propellant and unique 
reactor (engine) operations
</p>
