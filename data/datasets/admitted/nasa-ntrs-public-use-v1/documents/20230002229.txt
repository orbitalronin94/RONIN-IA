SSRG Initial Testing











SSRG Fatigue Study


Six subjects performed repetitive dynamic-gripping interspersed with isometric grip-holds under three conditions


Barehanded


Wearing pressurized Phase VI EVA SSG


Wearing pressurized SSRG


Results showed that the SSRG successfully reduced the neuromuscular effort needed to close the fingers of the space suit glove in more than half of subjects during two types of tasks


However, the SSRG required more neuromuscular effort to extend the fingers compared to a conventional SSG in many subjects


Subjects using SSRG stated they felt less fatigued during short periods of intense work compared to the SSG





Results show the potential of the SSRG as a grasp-assist device that can improve astronaut performance and reduce injury risk by reducing effort


Madden et al., ICES 2017

Portable Dynamometry


Joint strength assessments serve an important role in the medical rehabilitation domain, granting clinicians better insight into the progress of their patients


Similar assessments could be utilized by exercise physiologists, strength and conditioning specialists, and flight doctors to help maintain astronauts’ muscle function during missions


Isokinetic dynamometry is an accepted method of joint strength assessment in clinical settings, and NASA uses it as a tool to assess strength changes from pre- to post-flight


However, currently no practical means of obtaining individual joint strength data exists during spaceflight, precluding the ability to track changes in strength during missions


We developed a portable knee version to fill this gap


Comparison testing has shown that the Portable Knee Dynamometer has analogous repeatability to the state-of-the-art ground system and, overall, the peak knee torque measured by the PKD agrees very well for isometric and isokinetic contractions.


Device has been evaluated for proof-of-concept in microgravity

Sensorimotor Countermeasures Device


Sensorimotor system adapts to microgravity


Useless vestibular inputs


Main proprioceptive stimuli from treadmill


Future long-duration transit vehicles might not have a treadmill, so alternative sensorimotor training will be necessary


We are developing a Balance Board with pulldown actuation via a harness


The latest prototype has been evaluated for microgravity feasibility during a parabolic flight suite


Working toward an ISS technology demonstration unit

















Additional Developments


Upper Extremity Offload Device for Reduced Gravity Analog


Analog system capable of actively offloading the weight of a person (e.g. wearing spacesuit) or hardware to simulate alternate gravitational environments


Extremities still feel the pull of 1g, which can be fatiguing and/or uncomfortable, and provides an imperfect analog which could misinform suit design, etc.


We have developed a passive arm offload device to simulate the desired gravitational environment on the limbs


Now investigating making it “smart” with actuation and sensing
