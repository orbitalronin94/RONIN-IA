SHM
• Automated in-situ NDE monitoring of component state
– During flight or at set increments on ground
– Often requires integrated instrumentation

• Potential to reduce inspection time/cost
• Condition based maintenance
• Challenges:
– False negatives, false positives
– System validation/certification

Guided Wave SHM
• Based on guided wave (GW) propagation,
interaction, scattering from defects
• Physics of GW = long propagation
– But affected by environmental changes
(temperature, loading, etc)

• Highly studied area (many new publications
yearly)
• Multiple commercial companies worldwide
sell GW SHM hardware/software

Assessment
• Aim to aid in addressing SHM Challenges:
• Confidence in detectability
• Assessment of limitations

• Requirements on models
– Validated for relevant physics
– Resulting data represents key features of experimental system (e.g.,
speed of wave modes, amplitude)
• Necessary features will be based on the detection/quantification algorithm
being used

NDE Simulation: Forward and
Inverse Methods
• Forward modeling
– Simulate specific inspection
scenario with specific defect case
– Inform interpretation of data or
inform optimization of inspection
• Inverse modeling
– Start with experimental signal
– Use modeling to predict what
created that signal

‘CompCell’ EFIT
• 3D ‘Computational Cell EFIT’ is
modular code version of EFIT aimed
at complex geometries and written in
C++
• Allows easy discretization of complex
geometry
• Prevents wasted memory on empty
space (i.e., memory efficient)
• Uses pointers to modularize the EFIT
equations
• Uses MPI and OpenMP for
parallelization

FAA Panel & Simulation Case
• FAA AL 2024-T3 panel
• 3.175 m (125 in) long by 1.85 m (73 in) wide
• Skin thickness = 1.6 mm
• 0.1 mm wide 60 mm long vertical cut (‘crack’)
• Simulation case: 2-bays of the panel
• Δx = 0.1 mm, Δt = 7.9 ns
• 200 kHz excitation
• 8 hour run time on 400 cores Xeon 6148 Skylake
