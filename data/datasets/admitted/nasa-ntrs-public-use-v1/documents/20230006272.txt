<p>Points to make
</p>
<p>PIV measurements part of larger NASA Plug20 test program
– A validation dataset for internally mixed exhaust systems
– Features interchangeable internal mixers, and external plugs
– Dual-stream (BPR ≈ 4.5), single-stream flows covering 1.5 &lt; NPR &lt; 2.2, 0 &lt; Mf &lt; 0.3
– Contains PIV, background-oriented schlieren, phased array, far-field acoustics
</p>
<p>Observations/comparisons from PIV dataset
– Effect of internal mixing on plume
– Effect of external plug and plug length on plume
– Effect of flight on plume—dual-stream vs single-stream jets
</p>
<p>J. Bridges, M. P. Wernet, and G. G. Podboy, “Plug20 Test Report,” NASA Technical 
Memo NASA/TM—2021-10291, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210010291</p>

<p>PIV setup
Test in High Flow Jet Exit Rig of NASA Glenn Aero-Acoustic Propulsion Lab
0.4µm seeding in jet with 0.2µm seeding in the ambient flow.
Streamwise sheet in symmetry plane of jet
Two components of velocity measured using four camera (2x2) array, moved over 7 overlapping regions 
Total measurement region 0.5m x 2.2m, covering 14 nozzle diameters of flow with 1.2mm resolution.</p>

<p>11
</p>
<p>Summary
</p>
<p>PIV measurements of internally mixed, dual-stream exhaust system at reliable Reynolds number.
Flows measured include 
</p>
<p>– Dual-stream takeoff conditions (BPR ≈ 4.5) at NPR = 2, Mf = 0, 0.3
– Single-stream corresponding to fully mixed takeoff conditions and to unheated Ma = 0.9 with varying Mf.
</p>
<p>Observations/comparisons made from PIV dataset
– Better internal mixing produces plume closer to single-stream jet—lower TKE
– External plug reduces TKE in plume compared with internal plug
– Single-stream: Peak TKE mostly scaling with expected (Uj-Uf)2, uniform effect on turbulence strength, structure.
– Dual-stream: Interior shear layers produce more complicated flight effect than single-stream jets.
</p>
<p>Acoustic impacts of these flow effects covered in companion paper:
Bridges, J. “Diagnosing Noise Features of Internally Mixed, External Plug Exhaust Systems.” In 2023 AIAA Aviation Forum, 
AA-04, Jet Aeroacoustics I 12 June 2023.
</p>
