<p>Uniaxial Tension Testing of Niobium Alloy C103
</p>
<p>12
</p>
<p>Elevated Temperature Tension Testing
</p>
<p>−Wrought barstock in the  as-worked 
(extruded) and recrystallized condition 
was tested to compare to historical data 
and was found to be similar.
</p>
<p>− Printed materials exhibited dramatic 
differences in elevated temperature 
strength depending on post processing
</p>
<p>− As-printed condition has 2X yield strength 
of HIP’d condition
</p>
<p>− HIP’d material shows similar high-
temperature performance to 
recrystallized barstock</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Creep Testing of Niobium Alloy C103
</p>
<p>13
</p>
<p>Elevated Temperature Creep Testing
</p>
<p>−Wrought barstock in the recrystallized 
condition tested to compare to 
additively manufactured materials.
</p>
<p>− Creep testing is ongoing and needs a lot 
of time and specimens to determine 
material response over a wide range of 
temperatures and stresses.</p>
