<p>Impact of Geomagnetic activity on the upper atmosphere
</p>
<p>Energy deposited to the two polar cap, quickly 
globalizes and the whole upper atmosphere 
heats up and rises and descents with the storm
</p>
<p>Solar disturbances (Coronal Mass Ejection and other transients) 
impact the Earth’s magnetosphere transferring energy into the 
magnetosphere that ultimately is deposited in the upper 
atmosphere polar caps</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>

</p>
<p>How does the strength of the storm impact the upper atmosphere and its 
heating and cooling during geomagnetic storms

<p>Global Thermosphere Response
</p>
<p>1. Thermosphere responds quickly, nearly instantaneously at high 
latitudes. Both northern and southern polar regions respond 
simultaneously.
</p>
<p>2. The response propagates equatorward and it takes approximately 3 
hours for the enhancement to reach equatorial latitudes and to 
globalize. </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Summary of Results
</p>
<p>▪ The more intense the storm, the shorter the 
heating and cooling times. 
</p>
<p>▪ The most extreme storms presented the fastest 
heating and cooling times, reaching maximum 
intensification within 9.5 hr of onset and global 
cooling occurring at all latitude regions nearly 
simultaneously within 22 hr of onset. 
</p>
<p>▪ Cooling effects are attributed to high production 
of NO molecules that in turn play a major role in 
the radiative cooling of the thermosphere.
</p>
<p>▪ The more it heats up the faster it cools. </p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>The Nitric Oxide (NO) role during 
storms – Cooling matters too</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>SABER (Sounding of the Atmosphere using Broadband 
Emission Radiometry) on the TIMED mission
</p>
<p>• Energy input to the upper atmosphere results in high 
production of Nitric Oxide. The necessary elements are 
energetic electrons (precipitation) and heat (Joule Heating) for 
the chemical reactions that produce NO
</p>
<p>• Nitric Oxide (NO) is a most effective radiative cooling agent for 
the upper atmosphere</p>

<p>SUMMARY
➢ Severe and extreme storms show simultaneous heating at a 
</p>
<p>wide range of latitudes right at storm onset
➢ Thermospheric cooling times become shorter as the intensity of 
</p>
<p>the storm increases
➢NO radiative energy at above 100 km altitude is highly 
</p>
<p>correlated to storm activity and to the neutral density dynamic 
variation
</p>
<p>➢Orbital drag predictions are primarily challenged by inaccurate 
predictions of the storm heating and cooling times
</p>
<p>The hotter it gets the faster it cools. 
</p>
<p>NO at above 100 km plays an important role in the thermosphere 
thermal balance and impacts orbital drag predictions</p>
<p></p>
