Impact of Geomagnetic activity on the upper atmosphere



Energy deposited to the two polar cap, quickly 
globalizes and the whole upper atmosphere 
heats up and rises and descents with the storm



Solar disturbances (Coronal Mass Ejection and other transients) 
impact the Earth’s magnetosphere transferring energy into the 
magnetosphere that ultimately is deposited in the upper 
atmosphere polar caps

How does the strength of the storm impact the upper atmosphere and its 
heating and cooling during geomagnetic storms

Global Thermosphere Response



1. Thermosphere responds quickly, nearly instantaneously at high 
latitudes. Both northern and southern polar regions respond 
simultaneously.



2. The response propagates equatorward and it takes approximately 3 
hours for the enhancement to reach equatorial latitudes and to 
globalize. 











Summary of Results



▪ The more intense the storm, the shorter the 
heating and cooling times. 



▪ The most extreme storms presented the fastest 
heating and cooling times, reaching maximum 
intensification within 9.5 hr of onset and global 
cooling occurring at all latitude regions nearly 
simultaneously within 22 hr of onset. 



▪ Cooling effects are attributed to high production 
of NO molecules that in turn play a major role in 
the radiative cooling of the thermosphere.



▪ The more it heats up the faster it cools.

SABER (Sounding of the Atmosphere using Broadband

• Energy input to the upper atmosphere results in high 
production of Nitric Oxide. The necessary elements are 
energetic electrons (precipitation) and heat (Joule Heating) for 
the chemical reactions that produce NO



• Nitric Oxide (NO) is a most effective radiative cooling agent for 
the upper atmosphere

SUMMARY
➢ Severe and extreme storms show simultaneous heating at a 



wide range of latitudes right at storm onset
➢ Thermospheric cooling times become shorter as the intensity of 



the storm increases
➢NO radiative energy at above 100 km altitude is highly 



correlated to storm activity and to the neutral density dynamic 
variation



➢Orbital drag predictions are primarily challenged by inaccurate 
predictions of the storm heating and cooling times



The hotter it gets the faster it cools. 



NO at above 100 km plays an important role in the thermosphere 
thermal balance and impacts orbital drag predictions

NEMISIS is an integrated low-resource (mass, power, 
telemetry), multi-magnetometer system with high heritage 
that provides high accuracy magnetic field measurements 
regardless of spacecraft magnetic noise that GDC needs to 
measure energy input to the ionosphere-thermosphere 
system 



• Goal 1. Understand how large-scale and regional 
electrodynamics drive high-latitude ion and neutral structure 
and evolution.



• Goal 2. Understand the dominant pathways through which 
high-latitude electromagnetic energy forcing leads to global 
ionospheric-thermospheric dynamics.
