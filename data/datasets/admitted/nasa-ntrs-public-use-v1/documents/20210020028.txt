Abstract: 
Transition temperature testing of ferritic steels evaluates the ductile to brittle transition 

temperature, where the behavior of the steel changes from controlled ductile tearing to 
uncontrollable brittle fracture.  This testing is governed by ASTM (American Society of Testing 
and Materials) E1921 standard.  The calculations and plots required by this standard are 
iterative in nature and require significant effort to produce by standard hand calculations.  In 
addition to analysis of a complete data set, intermediate analysis during testing is beneficial to 
determine optimal test temperatures.  Given the exhaustive nature of calculating the transition 
temperature, being able to quickly update target temperatures is a significant benefit to not only 
the quality of tests, but the number of required tests as well.  T0 Test Evaluation Module 
(T0TEM) v1.5 is a software application created under the Layered Pressure Vessel (LPV) 
certification effort.  To reduce the analysis time of transition temperature test data sets produced 
for this effort, T0TEM was created to calculate the E1921 Master Curve along with all required 
data and validity checks.  By creating this program, hundreds of hours of analysis time were 
saved.  In addition, the creation of a standard program provided a significant improvement in the 
consistency with which results were reported.  T0TEM also calculates the results and plots 
required for the E1921 inhomogeneity annex to determine whether a material behaves in a 
homogenous manner.  This program was created by Levi Shelton and Cameron Bosley at 
NASA, with input and review from the E1921 committee.  The results generated by T0TEM were 
compared to the validation data sets provided by ASTM with good concurrence.  ASTM 
committee members have reviewed and applied T0TEM to other data sets with satisfactory 
results.  T0TEM was originally released to the NASA Software Repository and publicly via 
SourceForge in May of 2020.  Version 1.5 was released in April of 2021 with minor functionality 
updates and additional statistical analysis methods.

Introduction 
T0TEM (T0 Test Evaluation Module) is a software code that was created to efficiently 

analyze the large number of E1921 data sets being produced for the Layered Pressure Vessel 
(LPV) project.  The goal was to produce a program that could rapidly calculate and plot the 
master curve.  In addition, the software performs all necessary size adjustments, censoring, and 
validity checks as per ASTM (American Society for Testing and Materials) standard E1921.  
Recently, the addition of Annex X5 Treatment of Potentially Inhomogeneous Data Sets to ASTM 
E1921 (ASTM E08.07) created additional functionality of T0TEM to calculate master curve plots 
related to ferritic steels that exhibit inhomogeneity.  These methods assess whether the data set 
is likely to be inhomogeneous and they provide methods to characterize the inhomogeneous 
data.  All methods and formulas required for calculation of any part of E1921 are detailed within 
the standard.  T0TEM streamlines the data entry process and utilizes specialized root finding 
functions to speed calculation times as well as produce consistent data output files and plots. 

 

ASTM E1921 Background 
The test method delineated in ASTM E1921 is used to determine a reference 

temperature, T0 that characterizes the fracture toughness of ferritic steels that experience onset 
of cleavage cracking at elastic, elastic-plastic KJIC instabilities, or both.  The method uses the 
“Master Curve” concept to describe the transition in fracture toughness due to cleavage 
mechanisms as temperature decreases to the lower shelf.  The Master Curve method defines 
the ductile to brittle transition temperature of structural steels using static tests of fatigue 
precracked fracture mechanics samples. This is the recommended method when a limited 
number and size of specimens are available for testing.  The following figure shows a schematic 
of the ductile to brittle material behavior of ferritic steels with a superimposed Master Curve. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

 
 
 
 

 
 
 

Figure 1: Master Curve 



 

The statistical size effect (length of crack front) needs to be considered.  The weakest 
length statistics indicate that Pf will be a function of the volume of material tested, therefore the 
length of the crack front is important since a longer crack front increases the probability of 
fracture.  Two parameters to be considered are the thickness of specimen in testing, and the 
assumed or identified length of crack in structure.  E1921 test data is normalized using the 
statistical model to a 1T equivalent 25.4mm (1 inch) specimen thickness or crack length before 
evaluating the Master Curve parameter, Ko (and thus T0).

The application of E1921 to potentially inhomogeneous data sets is covered in Annex X5 
of E1921.  This annex provides calculation methods for analyzing material data sets that while 
still following the Master Curve theory, do not present in typical distributions and therefore need 
modified analysis to properly characterize the ductile-brittle transition temperature and turn it 
into a statistically usable value.  The two types of inhomogeneous data sets are bi-modal and 
multi-modal, and each comes with unique calculations and data validity requirements. 
 

From ASTM E1921 X5.3.2.1: “The bimodal toughness distribution applies to data sets 
that contain two toughness populations; the first population is more brittle than the second.  
Typically, such toughness distributions are encountered in heat-affected zone (HAZ) materials 
where the crack tip can sample the low- or high toughness material.  The combined bimodal 
toughness distribution is fully defined by three parameters: the reference temperature of 
population A, TA, the reference temperature of population B, TB, and the probability of sampling 
a specimen from population A, pA.  The probability of sampling a specimen from population B is 
equal to 1-pA.  Populations A and B are defined using the convention TB ≤ TA.” (ASTM E08.07) 
 

From ASTM E1921 X5.3.3.1: “The multimodal toughness distribution applies to data sets 
that contain randomly distributed toughness populations.  Typically, such cases are 
encountered in heterogeneous ferritic steels, for which the macroscopic heterogeneities are 
randomly distributed, or data sets of similar materials that are combined together.  The overall 
data set is composed of several populations that individually follow the master curve distribution.  
The combined distribution is fully defined by two parameters: the mean reference temperature 
of all populations (Tm), and the standard deviation around the mean (σTm).” (ASTM E08.07) 

 
 

T0TEM Development 
 T0TEM originated in 2017 as a Matlab script which was used to conduct analysis for the 
LPV program.  This simple script was created to speed test analysis and produce consistent 
plots and data output for material test reports generated as part of the program.  Following the 
addition of the inhomogeneity annex to E1921 and the round robin participation of NASA in its 
development, it was decided to combine all the E1921 analysis and functions into a more usable 
software package.  Matlab App Designer was selected as the platform for this task, since all the 
original scripts were created in Matlab.  An added benefit is the ability for external users to 
operate the software at no cost by using the free Matlab Runtime on Windows, Mac, and Linux.

T0TEM Features 
T0TEM’s integrated analysis and plotting tools allow the user to input raw data in the 

form of standard sample measurements and individual test values.  The sample measurements 
and values do not require modification prior to input which saves significant time usually 
required to perform size adjustments.  This data is then processed through the standard master 
curve evaluation, as well as the inhomogeneity evaluation and the results are plotted and 
exported in figures and tables as seen below.  In doing so, this software allows for large data 
sets to be processed quickly with all required outputs and plots generated automatically within 
seconds.   

Data input can be performed manually, by Excel sheet, or via .mat file.  Data may be 
entered in either US units or Metric, however uploading an Excel sheet with US units while the 
units switch is in Metric will produce an error.  Manually input data can be saved into a .mat file 
so repeated runs do not have to be re-entered each time.  Exported data can be copied with 
various delimiter types for .csv files or pasted into Excel.  Plots are saved as pictures directly 
from the plot interface and are a direct capture of the current screen where all bounds can be 
set.  All generated figures and plot legends are differentiated by shape or line type to allow for 
black and white publication.   
 The following figures show the layout and features of T0TEM v1.5.  Each feature is 
described in detail in the T0TEM user manual.  The graphical interface contains four tabs at the 
top that allow the user to navigate between the main program functions.  The first two tabs are 
used for conducting the basic transition temperature analysis as well as the inhomogeneity 
analysis.  These tabs contain all input and output functions, plot functions, and export capability.  
Next, the third tab allows the user to apply uncertainty conditions to the current data set and 
through a Monte Carlo analysis determine the effects of uncertainty within each part of the raw 
material test data.  Finally, the fourth tab is specially designed to receive multiple data sets as 
individual input files all at once.  This tab will calculate and report the most basic results for each 
data set in the tab.  Any individual data set from the fourth tab may be viewed in more detail by 
selecting the set and refreshing the first two tabs.

 In addition to the ASTM standard example problem agreement, T0TEM has been 
extensively beta tested by the E1921 community.  NASA is a long-standing member of the 
E1921 committee, and T0TEM has been used to analyze all transition temperature testing 
conducted as part of the Layered Pressure Vessel project.  T0TEM has produced satisfactory 
results in over 30 separate data sets which will be published as part of a future technical 
memorandum.  In each case, T0TEM produced results consistent with the proper analysis and 
results of each material test.  These data sets were initially analyzed with basic Matlab scripts, 
and re-run with each new version of T0TEM to ensure consistency.  This effort resulted in 
hundreds of individual analyses being run through T0TEM during its development. 
 Version 1.3 was initially distributed to select members of the E1921 committee for beta 
testing prior to public release.  During this test phase, members of the committee used T0TEM 
to analyze data on their own and were invited to comment on any issues they found.  Several 
comments were received regarding plotting functions as well as software bugs and output 
issues.  However, no critique of the validity of results was raised.  After version 1.4 was 
released publicly, more comments and questions were submitted.  Most of these comments 
were related to inhomogeneity analysis and limitations placed on lot sizes, as well as a few 
minor plot and functionality issues.  Again, no concerns with the validity of results were raised. 
T0TEM V1.5 is actively in use for an ASTM round robin study on reactor vessel materials under 
high rate testing.  Results have consistently shown agreement with analysis performed via other 
methods.  T0TEM has also been stress tested during an ASTM round robin censoring study 
evaluating problematic data sets against proposed new censoring methods.  During NASA’s 
participation in this study, T0TEM was used to baseline the results prior to evaluating different 
censoring methods.  In all cases except one, T0TEM produced satisfactory baseline results.  
Most importantly, for the one outlier, T0TEM did not give an incorrect result but rather a non-
result due to a determination of insufficient data.   

Conclusion 
 T0TEM v1.5 (current release) is the result of years of testing, evaluation, and 
improvement.  T0TEM’s functionality has improved significantly since conception and its 
accuracy has been constantly verified against the ASTM standard solutions during all phases of 
development.  It has performed hundreds of analysis iterations during the NASA Layered 
Pressure Vessel investigation which entailed over 30 separate lots of materials with many 
poorly behaving weld and parent data sets.  These data sets served to find several calculation 
issues in early beta testing, as well as issues with plotting and reporting.  At the time of this 
report, T0TEM has over 200 downloads worldwide and has received no significant comments or 
concerns over the accuracy of calculation.  It is somewhat difficult to completely characterize the 
overall certainty of T0TEM’s calculations in every scenario, for every data set.  Currently there 
are no standard approved software programs for performing E1921 calculations, it is the goal for
