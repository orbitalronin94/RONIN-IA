<p>Background</p>
<p>Managing cryogens is a critical technology for NASA’s spaceflight missions</p>
<p>Cryogenic fluid management (CFM) challenges
Long term propellant storage
Pressure control for nominal operations
Propellant transfer in microgravity
Conditioning propellants with cryocoolers</p>
<p>NASA’s CFM Modeling Portfolio
Develop, enhance and validate computational fluid dynamic (CFD)</p>
<p>Propulsion Fluid Dynamics Branch at NASA Marshall supports several projects with CFD analyses
Human Landing System (HLS), Space Launch System (SLS), Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS), etc.</p>
<p>Develop and validate Loci-Stream-VOF CFD solver to solve complex thermo-fluid and multi-physics problems for CFM
Phenomena: self-pressurization, autogenous and non-condensable tank pressurization, tank depressurization, ullage collapse, tank filling with jets/sprays, cryocooler operations, etc.</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Validation of MHTB Spray Bar Cooling with CFD with Eulerian-Lagrangian Particle Transport</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Modeling</p>
<p>Critical flow physics</p>
<p>Interface turbulence transport (ITT)</p>
<p>Phase change</p>
<p>ITT strongly influences propellant requirements, and consequently design of spaceflight missions</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Time and length scales for ITT is orders of magnitude smaller than spaceflight</p>
<p>Requires development and validation of computationally viable engineering model</p>
<p>[3] Chintan Patel. "Validation of Two Phase Flow Modeling Techniques in Loci-Stream Using Axial Jet Pressure Control and Autogenous Pressurization Experiments," AIAA 2025-1358. AIAA SCITECH 2025 Forum. January 2025</p>
<p>RANS – Reynold-average Navier Stokes</p>
<p>LES – Large Eddy Simulation</p>
<p>IDDES - Improved delayed detached eddy simulation</p>

<p>Physics Models</p>
<p>Two-phase model: Frozen interface</p>
<p>Turbulence model: IDDES (Hybrid RANS-LES)</p>
<p>Asymmetric interface turbulence transport model based on experimental and DNS evidence[3]</p>
<p>Phase change model: Hertz-Knudsen-Schrage (HKS)</p>

<p>Non-condensable Pressurization</p>
<p>Faster pressurization with helium – computationally more challenging </p>
<p>Cascading rows of Rayleigh–Taylor instabilities due to low density helium displacing higher density nitrogen</p>
<p>More complex vortical structures and more complex turbulent flow</p>
<p></p>
<p>Gas-Liquid Interface</p>
<p>Observed Dynamics from CFD Simulation – Helium mass fraction, Velocity Magnitude, Turbulence Viscosity Ratio, and Temperature</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Non-condensable Pressurization | Summary</p>
<p>Good agreement with pressurization data with  error in pressurant mass prediction (test had ±2.5% measurement accuracy)</p>
<p>Post-pressurization pressure drop prediction is unsatisfactory – resulting in 18.2% error in effective pressure increase</p>
<p>Phase change model is suspected to be inadequate for condensation under quiescent flow with non-condensable present</p>
<p>Pressure Change vs Time</p>
<p>Solution independent of spatial resolution, temporal resolution, accommodation factor of the phase change model, and inlet turbulence intensity</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusion</p>
<p>Validated CFD model for autogenous and helium pressurization operation</p>
<p>Careful interface turbulence transport modeling necessary for accurate predictions</p>
<p>Post-pressurization pressure drop is well predicted for autogenous pressurization but is unsatisfactory for helium pressurization</p>
<p></p>
<p>Further Work</p>
<p>Identify and address root causes for unsatisfactory post-pressurization pressure drop</p>
<p>Validate with moving and morphing interfaces – more complex CFM operations</p>
<p></p>
<p>Successful validation across propellant tanks of all sizes, different cryogens, and varying CFM operations using the same modeling approach demonstrate wide model applicability for supporting NASA’s future spaceflight missions</p>
