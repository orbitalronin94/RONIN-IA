Material Testing Results





In tensile testing at cryogenic and elevated temperatures up to 1400°F LP-DED NASA HR-1 exhibited good strengths and high ductility in both aging conditions



Yield and ultimate strengths were lower than wrought NASA HR-1, while fracture elongation was significantly higher than wrought. These trends were expected based on the composition changes made for LP-DED and the resulting microstructure. The low cycle fatigue life across temperatures was also in a range good for targeted applications



Tensile and low cycle fatigue tests in high pressure hydrogen exhibited no significant strength or ductility loss occurred in tensile testing and no loss of fatigue life was observed at 2% total strain for either aging cycle. In low cycle fatigue, LP-DED NASA HR-1 outperformed wrought in a high-pressure hydrogen environment

AM Adaptation of NASA HR-1





NASA HR-1 is a high-strength Fe-Ni based superalloy designed to resist hydrogen environment embrittlement (HEE), oxidation, and corrosion


Derived from JBK-75 and A286, NASA HR-1 was designed to have higher strengths to meet the needs of liquid rocket engine applications

Material Characterization Through Heat Treatment


Post-processing heat treatments are key to obtaining optimal properties for AM alloys and characterization of the microstructure informs on how alloys will perform


Stress relief, homogenization, solution, and aging cycles were developed to optimize the microstructure and properties of LP-DED NASA HR-1


Temperatures and times were adjusted to obtain desirable microstructure, mitigate Ti segregation observed, and prevent the formation of η-phase that is detrimental to the performance of NASA HR-1 in hydrogen environments


Two aging cycles were developed to adjust performance of NASA HR-1:


2-Step age for improved HEE resistance and LCF life 


1-Step age for higher strength


During characterization, various laser powers were examined to understand the impact of the LP-DED process parameters on the microstructure and properties to inform material application designs

Wrought NASA HR-1 was too expensive to produce traditionally, however additive manufacturing (AM) processes such as laser powder directed energy deposition (LP-DED) have enabled the material to be a feasible option for liquid rocket engine applications


The composition of NASA HR-1 was modified to adapt the alloy well to AM using PHACOMP analysis


Early in development of LP-DED NASA HR-1 it was observed that η-phase formed after aging, so the initial formulation was modified to the final formulation shown below to stabilize the matrix to help minimize η-phase formation and optimize properties


Modifications made in the final formulation were expected to improve fatigue life, thermal conductivity, ductility, and HEE resistance at the expense of strength

LP-DED NASA HR-1 in Application and Hot Fire Testing


Several manufacturing demonstration parts have been built using LP-DED NASA HR-1 to show versatility of the AM process and alloy


LP-DED NASA HR-1 nozzle extensions have been successfully hot fire tested from 2k lbf thrust class up to 35k under the RAMPT project and other test programs


Commercial space partners are actively building LP-DED NASA HR-1 nozzles for future engine applications


NASA HR-1 has also been used as a cladded jacket on combustion chambers and bimetallic joints between GRCop42 and LP-DED NASA HR-1 have been developed to expand the design space for combustion devices
