<p>Flight Reconstruction Approach and Summary
</p>
<p>• Compare calorimeter and radiometer Development Flight Instrumentation 
(DFI) data to pre-flight Best Estimated Trajectory (BET) models
– Determined plume induced radiative and total heating rates (q̇Rad, q̇Tot, ) utilizing serialized 
</p>
<p>calibration for each sensor location (15 rads, 35+ cals along CS and SRB base regions )
– Utilized collocated, symmetry DFI sensors to back-out the convective heat rate (q̇Conv) and heat 
</p>
<p>transfer coefficient at respective DFI locations
– Used T-0 as specified by Post-Flight BET and synced up all DFI using linear interpolation with BET
– No correction applied on Gas Temperature Probes (GTPs) 
– Artemis I reconstructed environments were compared against post flight models that used BET 
</p>
<p>inputs (e.g. motor conditions) 
– Base aerothermal environment reconstruction supported SLS Thermal and Stages
</p>
<p>Summary
– Core Stage
</p>
<p>• Radiation: Plume radiative heating environments were bounding
• Convection: Heat rate exceedances were observed at the base heat shield and RS-25 engine blankets, but 
</p>
<p>total heat load bounding at the base heat shield 
– Booster
</p>
<p>• Radiation: Plume radiative heating environments were bounding
• Convection: Heat rate and total heat load exceedances were observed at the inboard aft skirt</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>– All AR01 CS base aerothermal DFI were nominal 
– A total of 55 CS base aerothermal sensors (static pressure, 
</p>
<p>calorimeters/w TC, radiometers and gas temperature 
probes) were used in base heating flight reconstruction of 16 
DFI locations
</p>
<p>– Enabled adequate assessment of the convective heat 
transfer coefficient, cold wall heating and base flow physics
</p>

<p>Conclusions
• Base heat shield (BHS)  TPS cork combustion ignition and flame-out regimes occurred between ~T+14 s and ~T+75 s 
</p>
<p>(below 48,000 ft); attributes to &lt;40% of total heat load and highest rates; shows good agreement with Post-Green Run 
(PGR) models
– Peak cork combustion convective heat rates are greater than 5x the non-TPS-burning (modeled) rate
– Impacted inboard booster aft skirt heating
– Base heat shield (BHS) plume-induced radiation partially attenuated due to high TPS outgassing, smoke and cork 
</p>
<p>combustion products early in-flight
• Peak base gas recirculation spans between ~T+95 s and ~T+114 s and shows good agreement with PGR/pre-GR BET 
</p>
<p>models
– RS-25 – SRB plume recirculation at 40,000 ft (T+60 s) which led to high base pressure may have led to high 
</p>
<p>heating, but believe the dominant mechanism is cork combustion
– Peak recirculation regime spans an altitude between 86,000 ft and 128,000 ft 
– BET model shows peak recirculation at T+110 s (118,000 ft)
– Few poke-outs where AR01 flight data shows higher convection than prediction
</p>
<p>• Constant convective heating observed in choked flow regime during core-only flight (&gt;T+130 s); good 
agreement/conservatism observed between flight reconstruction and PGR/pre-GR BET models
</p>
<p>• EMHS reconstructed environments are much higher than the BET models &lt;75 s due to rain-shield and cork TPS burning; 
good agreement observed with BET models during core-only flight
</p>
<p>• Booster shutdown environments at the core stage were much less than flight, in most cases unobserved in comparison 
to Shuttle environments </p>
<p></p>
