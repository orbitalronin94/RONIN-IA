Supporting Spacecraft and Launch 
Vehicle Development 



• The Natural Environments (NE) Branch is within the MSFC Engineering Directorate’s 
Spacecraft and Vehicle System (S&VS) Department



• S&VS plans, performs and directs the technical Design, Analysis, Test, Evaluation, Verification, 
Integration, and Applied Research and Development of future Spacecraft and Launch Vehicle 
Systems



• Current programs we support - Space Launch System, Human Landing System, Mars Ascent 
Vehicle, Orion, Gateway 



• NE maintains expertise in terrestrial, space, and planetary natural environments 
definition, characterization, and analysis 



• Responsibility to disseminate this expertise to Agency programs and projects in support of 
environment definition, requirements development, vehicle development, operations, and 
sustaining engineering through program’s life cycle



• Branch has two teams; Terrestrial and Planetary Environments (T&PE) and Space Environments 
(SE) 



• T&PE has extensive expertise characterizing terrestrial surface and atmospheric 
(aloft) environments



• Winds, temperature, humidity, pressure, lightning, density, precipitation, radiant energy, 
aerosols, sea state, sea surface temperature 



• Maintains climatological databases for engineering analyses 3











Space Launch System (SLS)



• The four Natural Environments owned Launch Commit Criteria (LCC) (two 
temperature, one precipitation, and one wind) were updated and baselined for 
Artemis II.



• There were no changes from Artemis I to the temperature constraints for tanking or 
launch.



• The wind and precipitation LCCs were updated to only be monitored from L-3 hours to 
internal power instead of from L-7 hours to internal power.



• The wind placard was updated from an omnidirectional 24kt at 60ft placard to a 24kt to 
34.4kt at 60ft placard (graphic on next slide).



• Worked with KSC Weather office on updates to the Program Requirements 
Document for the Eastern Range.



• Added need for solar radiation instrumentation.
• Included High-Resolution (HR) balloon need and differentiated between Low-Resolution 



(LR) and HR by including expected effective vertical resolutions.

Orion/MPCV



• Analyzed landing availability impacts due to sea conditions due to reducing landing 
range along specified azimuths to mitigate the risk of heat shield damage on 
Artemis II. 



• Analysis revealed that using the nominal Design Specification for Natural Environments 
(DSNE) limits would be too constraining for landing availability, and that different zero-
margin constraints were used by different vehicle subsystems. 



• Determined and analyzed an additional constraint set along two entry azimuths at 
bounding entry interface stations with Orion Vehicle Integration Office, Crew Module 
Uprighting System (CMUS), and recovery personnel.

DOLILU Support



• Updating Profile Envision and Splicing Tool (PrESTo) and Weather 
Analysis Display Tool (WAND) ahead of Artemis II support.



• Updating user interface to improve human factors. 
• Adding capabilities within PrESTo to splice balloons together that end at differing 



heights, using the most up-to-date cycle balloons, and allowing a splicing of TDRWP 
straight into the Earth-GRAM monthly mean if no high balloon is available. 



• Training new team members to support DOLILU operations. 



• Working to implement new Orion DOLILU assessments into the process.
• DOLILU documentation will be updated to capture the new processes.
• Natural Environments will be creating an additional design profile for the Orion team 



during DOLILU operations.



• Received funding to support two DOLILU Joint Integration Sims with live-
balloon data in FY2024.



• Program schedule of November 2024 launch date.

On-Going Work



• AMPS replacement
• Working on estimating the Effective Vertical Resolution of the new Vaisala system 



based on simultaneous releases from Vandenberg Space Force Base.
• MW41 conditional certification - Validation of records on MW41 needs to be done if 



measurements from system are used for any analyses.
• Working on plan for NASA MW51 certification when Space Force completes operational 



acceptance of the system in 2024.
• Assess cold ambient temperatures for Orion post-vehicle recovery transport 



environments. 
• Concern exists that air temperature thresholds for propellent freezing could occur 



during transport from the landing site back to San Diego leading to hardware damage. 
• Received an action provide probabilities of air temperature being below specified 



criteria along representative routes.
• Complete the development of a tool to determine the probability of not 



exceeding abort sea conditions after including the vehicle’s probability of 
abort along the ascent ground track. Results are to be fed into tool to calculate 
an overall launch availability for natural environments.
