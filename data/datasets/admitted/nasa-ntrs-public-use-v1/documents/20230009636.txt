<div class="slide-content"><p>Background:  Cryogenic Boiling Heat Transfer Correlations 1/2</p>
<p>Most popular thermal/fluid design codes use correlations based on non-cryogenic room temperature fluids for cryogenic two-phase flow modeling</p>
<p>According to Mercado et al. [5]:</p>
<p>Pre-CHF boiling models overpredict cryogenic data, best case MSA of 397%</p>
<p>CHF models overpredict cryogenic data, best case MSA of 90%</p>
<p>Post-CHF boiling models underpredict cryogenic data, best case MSA of 127%</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Cryogenic boiling correlations exist in the literature, but most are correlated to a single data set and limited to there own set of hardware and operating parameters</p>
<p>With numerous amounts of one-off correlations, which correlation should be used for the designing and analyzing of cryogenic propellant transfer? </p>
<p>5</p>
<p></p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide5_rId3"></div>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Background:  Cryogenic Boiling Heat Transfer Correlations 2/2</p>
<p>Starting in 2018, Purdue University in collaboration with NASA Glenn Research Center developed the first-ever set of universal cryogenic flow boiling correlations</p>
<p>From over 53 independent reputable sources, carefully filtering of the data resulted in over 9,000 usable cryogenic data points</p>
<p>6</p>
<p>Fluids include:</p>
<p>He, H2, Ne, Ar, N2, CH4</p>
<p>Correlations developed:                                                                                           i</p>
<p>Onset of nucleate boiling (ONB)             Nucleate boiling</p>
<p>Critical heat flux (CHF)		   Rewet temperature                                Inverted annular film boiling (IAFB)	   Dispersed flow film boiling (DFFB)      Steady state two-phase pressure drop</p>
<p>Most of the data are predicted within 25%</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide6_rId2"></div>
</div>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Background:  Generalized Fluid System Simulation Program (GFSSP)</p>
<p>7</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide7_rId2"></div>
<p>System level CFD code developed at NASA in the early 90s </p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide7_rId3"></div>
<p>Fluid Nodes:  mass and energy equations are solved for pressures and enthalpies</p>
<p>Fluid Branches:  momentum equation is solved for flowrates</p>
<p>Fluid-to-Solid Conductors:  conjugate heat transfer</p>
<p>Solid Nodes:  solid energy equation is solved for wall temperature</p>
<p>Fluid Boundary Nodes</p>
<p>Solid-to-Solid Conductors</p>
<p>User subroutines are added for advanced physics</p>
<p>Fluid network  }</p>
<p>Solid network  }</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusions</p>
<p>Using GFSSP with universal Purdue University cryogenic flow boiling correlations:</p>
<p>Glickstein and Whitesides [1]  CH4 has SMAPE of 14.5%</p>
<p>Lewis et al. [2] N2 has SMAPE of 22.2%</p>
<p>Qi et al. [3] N2 has SMAPE of 26.0%</p>
<p>When predictive errors occur, the chief culprit is the type and location of the CHF</p>
<p>CHF predictions are excellent for Glickstein and Whitesides [1]</p>
<p>CHF predictions are excellent for Lewis et al. [2] (only one mis-prediction of CHF type)</p>
<p>For Qi et al. [3] CHF is usually predicted but not observed.</p>
