<p>Quantum Computing at NASA
</p>
<p>What is and isn’t Quantum Computing
</p>
<p>Quantum computers do many
fewer operations per second
than classical computers
</p>
<p>They cannot solve NP-hard
problems
</p>
<p>They are not on the verge of
solving useful problems
</p>
<p>They can provide insight into
classical solutions
</p>
<p>Quantum communication is
not faster than light
</p>
<p>Quantum computers utilize
additional operations
</p>
<p>They can decrease
computational scaling for
some problems
</p>
<p>They are programmable with
limited quantum knowledge
</p>
<p>Lucas T. Brady (KBR at NASA Ames) Quantum Artificial Intelligence and NASA March 23, 2023 3 / 20</p>
<p></p>
</div>

<p>Quantum Computing at NASA
</p>
<p>Why Quantum Computing at NASA
</p>
<p>NASA confronts computational challenges and bottlenecks with
missions, limiting scopes and aims
</p>
<p>Even if every atom on earth were a
processor, we could not run some
quantum-able computations
classically
</p>
<p>Quantum computers offer a
more efficient option
</p>
<p>We have a zoo of existing
applications
</p>
<p>Near-term is a lot more
heuristic
</p>
<p>This is potentially Green, with
lower energy consumption
</p>
<p>Lucas T. Brady (KBR at NASA Ames) Quantum Artificial Intelligence and NASA March 23, 2023 4 / 20</p>

<p>Current State of the Field
</p>
<p>Quantum Supremacy
</p>
<p>Quantum Supremacy: completing a task on
quantum hardware that cannot be done with
existing classical compute power
</p>
<p>Achieved by a Google – NASA – ORNL
collaboration
</p>
<p>The task was mathematical and not at all
practical
</p>
<p>We have long term applications but are on
the hunt for short term applications
</p>
<p>QuAIL is focused on hybrid approaches
</p>
<p>F. Arute et al. (2019),
</p>
<p>“Quantum supremacy using
</p>
<p>a programmable
</p>
<p>superconducting processor”,
</p>
<p>Nature 574, 505-510
</p>
<p>Lucas T. Brady (KBR at NASA Ames) Quantum Artificial Intelligence and NASA March 23, 2023 7 / 20</p>

<p>The Hunt for Near-Term Algorithms and Applications

<p>Noisy Intermediate Scale Quantum (NISQ)

long term

materials to be useful

<p>Deep Dive into One Application
</p>
<p>Problem Setup
</p>
<p>We want to use quantum machine learning
to analyze satellite imagery
</p>
<p>We are focusing on wildfires and vegetation
properties that indicate risks
</p>
<p>We want to create a quantum-assisted
image-to-image translation algorithm
</p>
<p>We are starting with quantum-ready models and incorporating
quantum where available
</p>
<p>Focus on Quantum Variational Autoencoder and later on

<p>Deep Dive into One Application
</p>
<p>Quantum Latent Space
</p>
<p>The latent space is small and so the best place to plug in a small
quantum computer
</p>
<p>We are using a Quantum Ising Born Machine
(QIBM)
</p>
<p>The QIBM is the same connectivity as a
Boltzmann machine but with quantum
instead of thermal fluctuations
</p>
<p>The quantum computer automatically
importance samples
</p>
<p>Training of a Born Machine is possible and relies only on easy to
produce quantum measurements
</p>
<p>Lucas T. Brady (KBR at NASA Ames) Quantum Artificial Intelligence and NASA March 23, 2023 16 / 20</p>

<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>Problems and Hurdles with QML
</p>
<p>Quantum systems are currently small and noisy
</p>
<p>Avoiding hype and finding problems with useful advantage
</p>
<p>Barren Plateaus
Regions of the control landscape where the gradients of our variational
parameters are decreasing exponentially with system size
</p>
<p>Cerezo, M., Sone, A., Volkoff, T. et al. Cost function dependent barren plateaus in shallow parametrized quantum circuits. Nat
Commun 12, 1791 (2021).
</p>
<p>Lucas T. Brady (KBR at NASA Ames) Quantum Artificial Intelligence and NASA March 23, 2023 18 / 20</p>

<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>Reasons to Hope in QML
</p>
<p>Quantum computers will only get bigger (10-20 years till the
technology really matures)
</p>
<p>Even a square root speed up can be a lot
</p>
<p>This is producing novel results in classical computing as well
</p>
<p>Try it and see: this is a novel and more powerful computing
paradigm that will soon be testable at scale
</p>
