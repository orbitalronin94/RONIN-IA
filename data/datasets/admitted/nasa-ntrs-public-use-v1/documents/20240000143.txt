<p> Background
</p>
<p>• Since the LOFTID project is an experimental technology demonstration, it was desired to drive the aeroshell in 
the reentry environment to temperatures that adequately prove the technology and validate the thermal 
response models, at an acceptable risk 
</p>
<p> Objective
</p>
<p>• Make informed aeroshell design, flight design, and flexible thermal protection system (FTPS) performance 
reliability trades while preventing excessive margin
</p>
<p>• Probabilistic uncertainty analysis informs design and risk for a specific project reliability requirement
</p>
<p> Aeroshell heat load capacity and flight heat load target driven by initial entry state
</p>
<p> FTPS size and required thermal margin  
</p>
<p> Risk of aeroshell exceeding flight allowable temperatures 
</p>
<p> Approach
</p>
<p>• Probabilistic uncertainty analysis is carried out using an end-to-end Monte Carlo process where three separate 
Monte Carlo simulations are sequentially run: 
</p>
<p> Entry trajectory/aerothermal model, reentry vehicle (RV) thermal model, FTPS thermal model
</p>
<p> Uncertainties in the trajectory and aeroheating model are propagated into the FTPS and RV thermal 
models to quantify the resulting probability distribution of the aeroshell thermal response 3
</p>

<p>Max FTPS/IS Interface Temperature Uncertainty 
Sensitivity Breakdown
</p>
<p>18
</p>
<p> A sensitivity analysis was performed using a Sobol indice approach
</p>
<p> Material thermal and aeroheating uncertainties significantly outweigh trajectory and model error uncertainties
</p>
<p> The top four contributors which make up 75% of the influence on the overall max FTPS/IS interface temperature variance are aftbody 
aeroheating, total forebody aeroheating, thermal conductivity of insulator layers 1-3, and the outer fabric thermal conductivity
</p>
<p>Breakdown by Discipline: Breakdown by Property:</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p> The probabilistic thermal margin process is a departure from the traditional RSS approach of combining 
conservatively bounding-case entry-trajectory and aeroheating environments to drive FTPS sizing
</p>
<p>• A study showed that the RSS method would have called for at least 26% more FTPS insulation
</p>
<p> The probabilistic uncertainty analysis results informed mitigations needed to achieve acceptable thermal 
performance reliability without applying unnecessary margin which would be counterproductive for a 
technology demonstration mission.
</p>
<p>• The process allowed for more informative risk and design trades to drive strategic atmospheric entry 
trajectory parameter selection
</p>
<p> A favorable aeroshell thermal response to the flown entry environment supplied useful flight data to 
demonstrate the technology application and to validate thermal models, so they can be developed as 
better HIAD design tools for future EDL applications
</p>
<p>• FTPS was sized and entry trajectory designed with adequate but not overly conservative margin
</p>
<p>• The hottest point on the inflatable structure exceeded the minimum project goal
</p>
<p>• No in-flight failures due to thermal overload 
</p>
