</p>
<p>as a Flight Experiment in Simulated Lunar-Gravity
</p>

</p>
<p>the thermal vacuum ASSIST chamber at KSC that provides an
</p>
<p>Atmospherically Sealed Simulator for In-situ System Testing.

</p>

<p>Background
Regolith conveying will be an essential task for supplying regolith
</p>
<p>feedstock to In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) reactor systems (Fig. 1)
</p>
<p>on the Moon and Mars [1-3]. The Vertical Lunar Regolith Conveyor
</p>
<p>(VLRC) is a technology being developed at NASA Kennedy Space
</p>
<p>Center (KSC) as a regolith transport task for the GCD ISRU FLEET
</p>
<p>project led by NASA Glenn Research Center (GRC).
</p>
<p>➢Technology Product Capability
</p>
<p>• Demonstrate feasibility of a vertical lunar regolith conveyor
</p>
<p>(VLRC) for transferring regolith (including icy regolith) from the
</p>
<p>lunar surface to an ISRU processing plant on a lander deck
</p>
<p>➢Technical Capabilities
</p>
<p>• Aims to understand the effects of particle size sorting and heat
</p>
<p>transfer during regolith transport along the conveyor
</p>
<p>➢Exploration &amp; Science Applicability
</p>
<p>• Applicable for moving regolith from the ground to the inlet of ISRU
</p>
<p>processing plants that extract oxygen (O2) from regolith or for
</p>
<p>extracting water from icy regolith
</p>

<p>Experiment Objectives:​
</p>
<p>1.Convey different lunar regolith simulants at different inclinations in simulated lunar-gravity 
</p>
<p>2.Record regolith flow using tracer particles and COTS cameras to calculate mass flow rates​
</p>
<p>3.Measure power consumption usage for two conveyor stack methods (stick/slip and eccentric)
</p>
<p>4.Conduct the experiment in a vacuum environment (&lt; 10 Torr).
</p>

<p>Goals &amp; Objectives
</p>
<p>➢Goal: Determine the optimum method of conveying regolith
</p>
<p>vertically around a helical surface under lunar gravity
</p>
<p>conditions in vacuum
</p>
<p>➢ The NASA Flight Opportunities program selected the VLRC to fly
</p>
<p>on a future Blue Origin New Shepard suborbital launch vehicle to
</p>
<p>study regolith transport physics in a relevant vacuum environment
</p>
<p>in a vacuum chamber under simulated lunar gravity conditions and
</p>
<p>to advance its technology readiness level (TRL) for future space
</p>
<p>applications.
</p>
<p>➢ NASA KSC developed multiple single-loop versions of the VLRC for
</p>
<p>the flight experiment to study the effects of test loop geometry on
</p>
<p>different granular materials under identical conveyance conditions
</p>
<p>simultaneously.
</p>
<p>➢ A conventional two degree of freedom helical vibratory approach
</p>
<p>using eccentrically weighted rotors and a stick-slip approach are
</p>
<p>being evaluated
</p>

</p>
