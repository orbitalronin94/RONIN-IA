<p>A Solar Sail Shape Modeling Approach for Attitude Control Design and Analysis</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide1_rId3"></div>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Introduction</p>
<p>Solar radiation pressure (SRP)-induced disturbance torques are a major design driver for solar sails, impacting all aspects of the attitude control system, including control and momentum management actuators</p>
<p>Traditionally, the center of mass (CM)/center of pressure (CP) offset of a flat sail has been the primary metric for predicting disturbance torques [2]</p>
<p>It was discovered during NEA Scout mission development that deformed sail shape effects were a significant contributor to the overall SRP-induced disturbance torques [3]</p>
<p>The strongest determinant of the sail shape is the deflection of each of the four booms</p>
<p>A significant known and predictable effect on boom deflections is the thermal distortion caused by the gradient across the booms from the sun-facing side to the space-facing side</p>
<p>2</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide2_rId2"></div>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><div class="embedded" id="slide3_rId2"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide3_rId3"></div>
<p>Background: NEA Scout and Solar Cruiser Modeling</p>
<p>The Solar Cruiser sail modeling methodology derives from NEA Scout [3]</p>
<p>Structural FEM created including tension and thermal distortion effects</p>
<p>Rios-Reyes/Sheeres generalized sail model [1] numerical implementation</p>
<p>FEM with ~66,000 elements transformed into 39 tensor coefficients for force, 45 tensor coefficients for torque</p>
<p>Computationally efficient force and torque calculation </p>
<p>The NEA Scout model was scaled and adapted for Solar Cruiser </p>
<p>Out-of-plane tip deflections were the biggest driver of disturbance torques</p>
<p>Magnitudes &gt;5x than a flat sail of identical dimensions, and &gt;2x for an identical sail with in-plane tip deflections only</p>
<p>Simple scaling left a lot of uncertainty in its ability to bound the problem, so a higher fidelity methodology was developed for Solar Cruiser</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Solar Cruiser Methodology Overview</p>
<p>Thermal modeling results dictate boom deflections which define the “nominal” sail shape used as a starting point</p>
<p>Uncertainty/error terms are defined and varied across a large parametric sweep</p>
<p>The nominal shape is modified for each error term, producing a large database of shapes</p>
<p>Rios-Reyes/Scheeres generalized sail model [1] process is used to create sail tensors for each shape</p>
<p>Forces and torques are calculated for each shape across Solar Cruiser mission attitudes</p>
<p>Force and torque data is post-processed to select two bounding shapes: maximum pitch/yaw (in-plane) torque and maximum roll (out-of-plane) torque</p>

<p>Assumptions and Boundary Conditions</p>
<p>Booms deflect parabolically, increasing from root to tip</p>
<p>Membranes deflect according to a “billowing” shape, captured using a 2D sinusoid with maximum deflection occurring at quadrant centroid</p>
<p>Non-sail membrane surfaces and in-plane deformations or asymmetries are neglected. Wrinkles are not directly modeled but effect is captured through optical properties [3]</p>
<p>Fixed-fixed boom/membrane interface at the roots and tips without any separation or interference</p>
<p>Boom tips and sail membranes can deflect out of plane in any arbitrary direction with respect to each other</p>

<p>Parameter Descriptions and Variations</p>
<p>Nominal Boom Tip Deflections</p>
<p>Out-of-plane, increasing parabolically from root to tip. The magnitude and direction (out of the sail plane and toward the sun) were held constant.</p>
<p>Membrane Deflections</p>
<p>Out-of-plane, billowing shape with peak/trough at the centroid of each quadrant. The magnitude was varied (0-5cm), and the direction varied in the sail out-of-plane axis.</p>
<p>Boom Tip Deflection Errors</p>
<p>Random/uncertain out-of-plane boom tip deflections due to manufacturing and assembly tolerances, tension changes in the membrane, and thermal load uncertainties. The magnitude was varied (0-2x), and the direction varied in the sail out-of-plane axis.</p>
<p>Center of Mass Offsets</p>
<p>The difference in the center of mass in-plane position relative to the designed geometric center, due to manufacturing and assembly tolerances (0-2cm).</p>
<p>Attitude</p>
<p>SIA varied from 0 to 17 degrees (Plane Change Demonstration mission target) and clock angle varied from 0 to 360 degrees.</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Solar Cruiser Design Considerations</p>
<p>Disturbance torques from worst-case shapes were considered bounding for the purposes of requirements derivation</p>
<p>Sail shape requirements specified to ensure the as-manufactured flight sail deformations would remain within the modeled deformations</p>
<p>Tip deflections</p>
<p>CM offsets</p>
<p>Reaction wheel torque capability (including 100% margin, 2x control authority)</p>
<p>Momentum management actuator design</p>
<p>The disturbances were accounted for in the integrated sailcraft model for requirements verification by analysis</p>
<p>Plant dynamics, control system, flight software </p>
<p>Sail tensors were integrated into the model, allowing computationally efficient force and torque calculation at simulated attitudes</p>
<p>11</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><div class="embedded" id="slide12_rId2"></div>
<div class="embedded" id="slide12_rId3"></div>
<p>Solar Cruiser Design Considerations</p>
<p>Momentum management requirements</p>
<p>To desaturate momentum in the pitch/yaw RSS (in-plane) axes, Solar Cruiser utilized an Active Mass Translator (AMT)</p>
<p>The torque predictions drive design considerations such as AMT range of motion, rail orientation, and bus mass allocation</p>
<p>For roll (out-of-plane) momentum management, Solar Cruiser utilized two actuator systems</p>
<p>Reflectivity Control Devices (RCDs) (primary) </p>
<p>Indium Field Emission Electric Propulsion Microthrusters (IFMs) (backup)</p>
<p>Roll torque predictions drive requirements on RCD surface area, as well as IFM propellant mass needed</p>
<p>12</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Conclusions</p>
<p>The deformed sail model results demonstrate considerably higher expected induced disturbance torques compared to the simplified assumption of a flat plate sail with a CM/CP offset</p>
<p>Accurate prediction of these disturbances is crucial when designing the spacecraft attitude control system</p>
<p>The disturbances drive all aspects of ADCS, ultimately impacting sailcraft characteristic acceleration and mission design as required masses grow</p>
<p>It is recommended to begin medium/high fidelity modeling as early as possible in the design cycle, even in the early mission concept phase</p>
<p>13</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Forward Work</p>
<p>The current approach yields a medium-fidelity model where inputs and assumptions on local conditions (e.g., nominal boom tip deflection, tip error uncertainty, and membrane deflection) drive the global sail shape</p>
<p>Forward work is currently ongoing to improve modeling fidelity</p>
<p>Cooperative effort including MSFC, LaRC, Redwire, and NeXolve</p>
<p>Iterative process where interactions between the boom deformations and the membrane deformations are increasingly refined</p>
<p>Top-down approach - “global “boom thermal/structural modeling outputs computed, information used in “local” membrane quadrant models</p>
<p>Assumed membrane shapes will be replaced with more physically realistic models</p>
<p>Sail tensors will be regenerated and attitude control metrics reassessed with high-fidelity shapes</p>
