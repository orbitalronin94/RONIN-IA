<p>Motivation
</p>
<p>2
</p>
<p>• Predictions of chilldown in cryogenic transfer lines 
requires improved understanding of boiling process and 
surface heat transfer
</p>
<p>➢ Due to operation close to the Critical Point; Large Variability in 
Properties including Specific Heat and Thermal Conductivity
</p>
<p>➢ Physics of Nucleation/Microlayer and bubble dynamics not 
well understood  
</p>
<p>• Accurate modeling of critical heat flux and departure 
from nucleate boiling  critical to estimating surface 
temperature history and quenching
</p>
<p>➢ Difficult to characterize heat flux directly with correlations; 
predictions come with large error bands and uncertainty 
</p>
<p>➢ Data for water does not correlate well due to much larger 
thermal effects of phase change
</p>
<p>➢ Higher Reynolds number results in increased sensitivity of 
heat transfer to roughness
</p>
<p>Boiling Curve
</p>
<p>Innovative solutions to complex problems</p>
<p></p>

<p>Motivation (Contd.)
</p>
<p>3
</p>
<p>• In collaboration with Prof. Emilio Baglietto at MIT as part of a NASA funded program,  
</p>
<p>advanced boiling sub-models  for cryogens are being developed
</p>
<p>➢ Experimental test program at MIT in liquid nitrogen to generate data for cryogenic fluids 
</p>
<p>➢ New boiling correlations for cryogens being developed from test data
</p>
<p>• Present effort employs earlier models developed for water that have been adapted 
</p>
<p>for cryogenic fluids by employing thermodynamic similarity scaling
</p>
<p>• Sub-grid models with thermodynamic scaling have been tested and demonstrated 
</p>
<p>for line chilldown in both liquid nitrogen and liquid hydrogen
</p>

</p>

</p>
<p>• Generalized Preconditioned Formulation
➢ Solves for pressure and temperature instead of 
</p>
<p>density and internal energy to overcome stiffness 
issues. Pressure and temperature are always well-
defined even for stiff systems
</p>
<p>➢ Techniques used for traditional “density” based 
codes can be used for this formulation now by 
casting it in time-marching form
</p>
<p>• Versatile Thermodynamics
➢ Matrices retain derivatives in generalized form
</p>
<p>➢ Allows specification of generalized EOS routines 
without modifying the code numerical formulation
</p>
<p>➢ Specifically, enthalpy can be a function of pressure 
and temperature e.g., cryogenic fluids
</p>
<p>➢ Speed of sound of generalized fluid determined by 
the eigenvalues of matrix [A]

<p>Overview of Issues with Hydrogen Boiling
</p>
<p>• There is a need for improved  understanding of hydrogen boiling 
</p>
<p>characteristics for more efficient storage and transfer
</p>
<p>• Hydrogen behaves very differently than other cryogens such as 
</p>
<p>liquid nitrogen
– Chilldown in liquid hydrogen is very rapid and the traditional “knee” in surface temperature 
</p>
<p>curve is not prominent
</p>
<p>– Hydrogen exhibits annular flow with liquid later on the wall and two-phase flow in core as 
</p>
<p>opposed to inverted annular flow in liquid nitrogen
</p>
