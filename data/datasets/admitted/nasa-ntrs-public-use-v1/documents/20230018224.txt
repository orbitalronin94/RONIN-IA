• Predictions of chilldown in cryogenic transfer lines 
requires improved understanding of boiling process and 
surface heat transfer

from nucleate boiling critical to estimating surface 
temperature history and quenching



➢ Difficult to characterize heat flux directly with correlations; 
predictions come with large error bands and uncertainty 



➢ Data for water does not correlate well due to much larger 
thermal effects of phase change



➢ Higher Reynolds number results in increased sensitivity of 
heat transfer to roughness

• Generalized Preconditioned Formulation
➢ Solves for pressure and temperature instead of 



density and internal energy to overcome stiffness 
issues. Pressure and temperature are always well-
defined even for stiff systems



➢ Techniques used for traditional “density” based 
codes can be used for this formulation now by 
casting it in time-marching form



• Versatile Thermodynamics
➢ Matrices retain derivatives in generalized form



➢ Allows specification of generalized EOS routines 
without modifying the code numerical formulation



➢ Specifically, enthalpy can be a function of pressure 
and temperature e.g., cryogenic fluids



➢ Speed of sound of generalized fluid determined by 
the eigenvalues of matrix [A]

Overview of Issues with Hydrogen Boiling



• There is a need for improved understanding of hydrogen boiling 



characteristics for more efficient storage and transfer



• Hydrogen behaves very differently than other cryogens such as 



liquid nitrogen
– Chilldown in liquid hydrogen is very rapid and the traditional “knee” in surface temperature 



curve is not prominent



– Hydrogen exhibits annular flow with liquid later on the wall and two-phase flow in core as 



opposed to inverted annular flow in liquid nitrogen



• Simulations of liquid hydrogen line chilldown experiments conducted at NASA 



(Hartwig et al.) were simulated to understand numerical sensitivities and 



improve understanding of physics



Hartwig J.W., Styborski, J. et al. “., “Liquid Hydrogen line chilldown experiments at high Reynolds Numbers II. Analysis ” Intl 
Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer, 156, 2020.

Thermal Properties of Stainless Steel 



Thermal Conductivity Specific Heat



• Lower thermal conductivity sustains large temperature gradient in solid for the same heat flux



• Similarly lower specific heat leads to larger temperature drop with time



• Combination of these effects leads to more rapid rate of temperature drop
