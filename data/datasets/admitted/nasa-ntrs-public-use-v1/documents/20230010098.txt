Overview


Achieving robustness to uncertainty is a problem that must be addressed on all levels to achieve success.


We first examine controller design for uncertain quadrotor UAV dynamics.


Complexity of UAV model serves as good test case for uncertainty management.


We present three state-of-the-art control methods and demonstrate a new way to integrate them to achieve safe control.


We then explain one heuristic standard of UAV safe operation and propose a method to integrate the standard into a controller.





We formulate the issue of robust control in the context of Control Lyapunov and Control Barrier functions, then proceed to use existing methods to yield a new process.

Adaptive Model-Free Control Impacts


Evolution of unknown system matrix and dynamical matrix allows system to “learn” its own dynamics.


Matrices can be saved as initial estimates for future flights.


Dynamical matrix requires estimation – and thus some knowledge of system dynamics, even if ideal.





Historical error tracking allows system to adjust to hardware degradation over time.


Sensor error addressed and compensated for.


Sudden failures, or large and intermittent errors, are not compensated for.

Adaptive Model-Free Control: 
Assumptions and Limitations


AMFC learned matrices cannot compensate for sudden, short-duration, disturbances.


Can reduce accuracy of both system and dynamical model.


Thus: assume short-duration disturbances are limited.





Conversion from “desired” orientation to maintain safety, to needed torques, can result in safety-critical control effort not being reached.


Issue is mitigated when desired control efforts are weak.


Thus: assume exact boundaries of safety never reached, strong control efforts not needed for safety maintenance.

“Well Clear” Principle


Uncertain, higher-order, safety-critical controllers are more effective when able to give small control corrections.


Small corrections are easier to track, involve less nonlinearities.


Unexpected disturbances less of an issue, at a very safe point to start with.





“Well clear” standard dictates a distance to maintain, which can be maintained with low-power maneuvers.


Ideal for uncertain autonomous operation


“Soft” safety boundary can be violated without danger, reached again when system recovers from disturbance

Well Clear Pitfalls


Low risk tolerance can lead to computationally infeasible airspaces, and thus counterproductive results.


Computationally infeasible, or expensive, airspace leads to poor control of aircraft, increasing risk.





Monte Carlo simulation performed to determine well-clear time-to-collision thresholds between nonreactive UAVs for varying risk tolerances.


1% risk tolerance: 260 seconds separation


5% risk tolerance: 59 seconds separation


Leads to obvious issues for short-ranged, low-powered sensors equipped on UAVs.

Well Clear/Risk Level Contributory Factors


Risk is not equal for all types of UAVs. Thus, well clear collision thresholds may change over time, depending on vehicle characteristics and environment.





Aircraft “riskiness” discretized as acceptable number of fatalities per hour of operation: 2e-7 for manned aircraft, proposed at 1e-8 for UAVs.


Ground impacts: Aircraft size, mass, speed, material, population density (probability of ground fatality)


Air impacts: Aircraft cross section, trajectory, velocity, exposure time, probability of fatality given collision





Can extend to space systems through debris risk, manned operations, equipment cost, etc.

HBCA Requirements


Traditional collision avoidance measures require up to 1 TB of memory, and intensive computing resources. Simplified (but robust) alternatives can be as small as 500 MB.


Feasible for mid-sized UAVs running off Single Board Computers (SBCs).


Offline logic table formulation required for each model of UAV.





Sensor characterization required, uncertainty model under different conditions needed to produce robust recommendations.


Similar requirements as control loop itself.


Still does not address “unknown unknowns” risk from undetected aircraft – airspace coordination still needed to avoid surprises.

Operational Safety Assumptions and Limitations


Operational safety approaches hold similar sensor and dynamical vulnerabilities as controller-based assurance.


Key difference: nominal state is more conservatively safe and error estimators have more time to adapt to environment.





We assume feasible obstacle and goal tracking, to yield existence and position (at least).


“Unknown unknowns” cannot be hardened against on the control level – offline coordination is needed (see: airways, airspace classes).

Applying to Space Systems


Demonstrated method of uncertainty management is general.


Adjustments to system dynamics and controls formulations must be made, however.


AMFC control heavily dependent on recognizing sources of uncertainty for estimation and refinement, requires a complete system model.


AHOCBF-CLF likewise scales in derivation complexity with system dynamical properties and possible control efforts.





Candidate systems have hardware limitations as well.


System update time must be short to allow for error correction and safe control.


System sensors must be filtered sufficiently to avoid high-amplitude noise, leading to error estimation destabilization.
