<div class="slide-content"><p>Overview</p>
<p>Achieving robustness to uncertainty is a problem that must be addressed on all levels to achieve success.</p>
<p>We first examine controller design for uncertain quadrotor UAV dynamics.</p>
<p>Complexity of UAV model serves as good test case for uncertainty management.</p>
<p>We present three state-of-the-art control methods and demonstrate a new way to integrate them to achieve safe control.</p>
<p>We then explain one heuristic standard of UAV safe operation and propose a method to integrate the standard into a controller.</p>
<p></p>
<p>We formulate the issue of robust control in the context of Control Lyapunov and Control Barrier functions, then proceed to use existing methods to yield a new process.</p>

<p>Is bounded above and below:</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Adaptive Model-Free Control Impacts</p>
<p>Evolution of unknown system matrix and dynamical matrix allows system to “learn” its own dynamics.</p>
<p>Matrices can be saved as initial estimates for future flights.</p>
<p>Dynamical matrix requires estimation – and thus some knowledge of system dynamics, even if ideal.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Historical error tracking allows system to adjust to hardware degradation over time.</p>
<p>Sensor error addressed and compensated for.</p>
<p>Sudden failures, or large and intermittent errors, are not compensated for.</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Adaptive Model-Free Control: 
Assumptions and Limitations</p>
<p>AMFC learned matrices cannot compensate for sudden, short-duration, disturbances.</p>
<p>Can reduce accuracy of both system and dynamical model.</p>
<p>Thus: assume short-duration disturbances are limited.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Conversion from “desired” orientation to maintain safety, to needed torques, can result in safety-critical control effort not being reached.</p>
<p>Issue is mitigated when desired control efforts are weak.</p>
<p>Thus: assume exact boundaries of safety never reached, strong control efforts not needed for safety maintenance.</p>

<p>Robust controllers still assume a level of vehicle reliability.</p>

<p>No sudden failures (fundamental system dynamics do not immediately change).</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>“Well Clear” Principle</p>
<p>Uncertain, higher-order, safety-critical controllers are more effective when able to give small control corrections.</p>
<p>Small corrections are easier to track, involve less nonlinearities.</p>
<p>Unexpected disturbances less of an issue, at a very safe point to start with.</p>
<p></p>
<p>“Well clear” standard dictates a distance to maintain, which can be maintained with low-power maneuvers.</p>
<p>Ideal for uncertain autonomous operation</p>
<p>“Soft” safety boundary can be violated without danger, reached again when system recovers from disturbance</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>“Well Clear” Definition Steps</p>
<p>Formulate some collision risk function</p>
<p>Sampling timestep</p>

<p>Sensor reliability (based on estimation error)</p>
<p>Define a context-dependent acceptable level of risk</p>
<p>Solve for the relative-state crossover point from acceptable to unacceptable risk and set as “well clear” objective.</p>
<p>Take steps to maintain “well clear” distance with all other obstacles.</p>
<p>Can be formulated as maintaining well-clear with respect to the closest obstacle each time</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Well Clear Pitfalls</p>
<p>Low risk tolerance can lead to computationally infeasible airspaces, and thus counterproductive results.</p>
<p>Computationally infeasible, or expensive, airspace leads to poor control of aircraft, increasing risk.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Monte Carlo simulation performed to determine well-clear time-to-collision thresholds between nonreactive UAVs for varying risk tolerances.</p>
<p>1% risk tolerance: 260 seconds separation</p>
<p>5% risk tolerance: 59 seconds separation</p>
<p>Leads to obvious issues for short-ranged, low-powered sensors equipped on UAVs.</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Well Clear/Risk Level Contributory Factors</p>
<p>Risk is not equal for all types of UAVs. Thus, well clear collision thresholds may change over time, depending on vehicle characteristics and environment.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Aircraft “riskiness” discretized as acceptable number of fatalities per hour of operation: 2e-7 for manned aircraft, proposed at 1e-8 for UAVs.</p>
<p>Ground impacts: Aircraft size, mass, speed, material, population density (probability of ground fatality)</p>
<p>Air impacts: Aircraft cross section, trajectory, velocity, exposure time, probability of fatality given collision</p>
<p></p>
<p>Can extend to space systems through debris risk, manned operations, equipment cost, etc.</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>HBCA Requirements</p>
<p>Traditional collision avoidance measures require up to 1 TB of memory, and intensive computing resources. Simplified (but robust) alternatives can be as small as 500 MB.</p>
<p>Feasible for mid-sized UAVs running off Single Board Computers (SBCs).</p>
<p>Offline logic table formulation required for each model of UAV.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Sensor characterization required, uncertainty model under different conditions needed to produce robust recommendations.</p>
<p>Similar requirements as control loop itself.</p>
<p>Still does not address “unknown unknowns” risk from undetected aircraft – airspace coordination still needed to avoid surprises.</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Operational Safety Assumptions and Limitations</p>
<p>Operational safety approaches hold similar sensor and dynamical vulnerabilities as controller-based assurance.</p>
<p>Key difference: nominal state is more conservatively safe and error estimators have more time to adapt to environment.</p>
<p></p>

<p>“Unknown unknowns” cannot be hardened against on the control level – offline coordination is needed (see: airways, airspace classes).</p>

<p>Applying Lessons Learned to Uncertainty in Extreme Environments</p>
<p>77</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Applying to Space Systems</p>
<p>Demonstrated method of uncertainty management is general.</p>
<p>Adjustments to system dynamics and controls formulations must be made, however.</p>
<p>AMFC control heavily dependent on recognizing sources of uncertainty for estimation and refinement, requires a complete system model.</p>
<p>AHOCBF-CLF likewise scales in  derivation complexity with system dynamical properties and possible control efforts.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Candidate systems have hardware limitations as well.</p>
<p>System update time must be short to allow for error correction and safe control.</p>
<p>System sensors must be filtered sufficiently to avoid high-amplitude noise, leading to error estimation destabilization.</p>
