Environmental Effects and Coatings Branch (LME)



LME is engaged in R&D related to environmental effects on materials durability……regardless of mission or environment



Core Competencies:
• Fundamental High Temperature Materials – chemistry, thermodynamics, and kinetics thru experimental and first principles 



analytical methods
• Computational Modeling – DFT, CALPHAD, Machine Learning
• Durability Testing in Extreme Environments
• Advanced Coatings – Thermal barrier coatings (TBC), Environmental barrier coatings (EBC), diffusion barriers, thermal 



barriers
• Space Environments – Atomic oxygen, UV damage, Planetary atmosphere/surface



Current Focus:
• Environmental barrier coatings (EBC) for ceramic matrix composites (CMC) (long-life 2400°F / next-gen 2700°F) – Plasma-



spray physical vapor deposition, slurry processing
• Fundamental thermo-chemistry database development
• Validated Life Modeling of CMC/EBC relevant to failure modes in engines
• Space Environments testing and flight projects



• Planetary – Lunar dust adhesion & thruster plume erosion 
• Low earth orbit – Flight experiments, Atomic oxygen erosion

Aerogels for Thermal Insulation



• High specific surface area (SSA), 
high porosity, and low density
• SSA: 200 – 1000 m2/g 



• Porosity: 90 – 99.9% 



• Low thermal conductivity
• Low as 0.009 W/(m•K) in atmosphere 



and 0.003 W/(m•K) under vacuum



• Low density = Low solid conductivity



• Pore sizes ≤ mean free path of gas 
= Low gas convection



• Versatile synthesis adaptable to a 
wide array of metal oxide compositions

Surfactant Templating of ZrO2 Aerogels for 
Improved Pore Stability



Structure directing agents can be used to control the pore structure of materials in the 
mesopore range



• Leads to well-ordered structures, uniform pore diameters



• Hard (porous membrane) vs. Soft (surfactant) templates



• Used to produce hollow spheres or fibers, nanotubes, mesoporous silica, gels, aerogels, etc.



Example: Formation of mesoporous silica nanotubes

Surfactant Templating of ZrO2 Aerogels for 
Improved Pore Stability



Surfactants template the aerogel structure 
• Added to sol prior to gelation (multiple of CMC)



• Washed out during supercritical drying and oxidized during heat treatment, leaving pores behind



• Not all pores are directly templated by surfactant
