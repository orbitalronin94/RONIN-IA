Project Overview


Project Description:   This project will demonstrate the ability to use a two-stage active cryocooler system to reduce/eliminate LH2 boiloff over long duration storage, and demonstrate the advantages over a single stage approach.  It is funded by NASA’s Space Technology Mission Directorate’s Technology Demonstration Missions Program as part of the Cryogenic Fluid Management Portfolio.


Why Does It Matter:  With the next generation of in space vehicles being developed to take humans to the moon and beyond, such as the NASA led HLS initiative,  vehicles will need to store liquid hydrogen for extended periods of time safely, while minimizing the system mass required to achieve Zero Boil-Off (ZBO).  The two-stage approach can theoretically reduce both system mass and power requirements.

We have multiple input and output parameters to control with parallel PID loops


We want to achieve a narrow band in the final output variable to claim successful zero boil off has been reached (testing application) 


ZBO is defined as saturation temperature and constant pressure in the tank with all valves closed 


Longer cooling cycles, which may help lower power usage associated with cryocooler startup, would require a wider band in tank pressure (mission application) 


Mission challenges could include: 


Rapid ullage collapse during engine burn


Rapid pressure increase (hot gas feedback from engine burn)


Rapid change in heat flux in cooling circuit resulting in unstable PID loops 


Special challenges for nuclear thermal propulsion including neutron heat load, especially if propellant tanks are used as partial shielding 


Conops may vary between short burns vs. long burns 


Solar heating (unequal distribution over surface, roll rate) 


If helium pressurant is used, modeling is complicated

Description of Advanced Model Base Control System 








Two chilled helium flow loops: Broad Area Cooling (BAC) at ~80K and Tube on Tank (TOT) at ~20K


Each loop has two PID controllers: Flow (cryofan speed) and Heater (electric heater to set lift) 


Cryogenerator has constant motor speed for both cryocoolers (lift vs. temp follows performance curve) 


Two sets of heaters on tank wall: recovery (high power for fast warmup between tests) and heat leak (low power to artificially increase tank heat load)

Why Use Parallel PID Loops


We assume that using parallel loops for the two loops is justified because they are sufficiently isolated by the MLI that feedback between the loops will be below other feedback noise and can be neglected


We assume that using parallel loops for the fan speed and line heaters (TOT and BAC) is justified because the time constant between the two can be configured to a few orders of magnitude difference


The difference in time constant alone may not be sufficient so may need to add a filter to one or both of the loops 


The Overwatch application will help keep the loop from oscillating out of control; this will help to simplify the control while giving the user better control than trying to actively react to changing conditions 


Lab testing before the actual test will be conducted to verify assumptions

Overview of Overwatch Program


Protects the system from positive feedback oscillations 


The controller will revert to the last manual setting for the output for the cryofan speeds and line heater voltage output


Will include a limiter on how far the tank wall temp can vary from the tank liquid temperature based on predicted steady state pressure


A preprogrammed step down for conditioning the cooling loop is being considered 


Overwatch Indexing Value Table


The Overwatch Indexing Value Table is an attempt at accounting for the two-phase nature of the propellant at the storage conditions


It attempts to account for the fact that based on the condition of the ullage, different equations of state are better model fits


By having the PID value correlated to the current conditions of the tank, TOT, and BAC this will help dampen out any higher order terms that could cause the pressure control system to become unstable 


Overwatch Deadband control


Will utilize the energy balance equation and ideal gas law (or REFPROP tables) to predict where the tank pressure is likely to be in the next set amount of time


Can set more aggressive PID values as needed to keep the pressure within the defined set limits (prevent RV from going off or tank collapse) 











Help to prevent and stop an un event from vent propellant 


Keep simple with Non iterative equation or look up table information


Updates a PID loop value to provide the actions when interrupt event happens or at set times


Based off tank pressure, propellant mass, estimated heat loads(most like will have learn this one meaning it will refine based on error in prediction)

Possible Additional Characterization Testing


Testing IMLI Response at 4205





Testing at Liquid Hydrogen temperature in the ESTF vacuum chamber in 4205 lab 108 is a challenge but can be done.


The BAC would be chilled to 80 K as planned for the testing at 300


The TOT would be chilled to 20 K as planned for the testing at 300


The tank would be prechilled by running cold LN2 fill, GN2, or wall cooled with LN2 spray


A GHe purge after the nitrogen chill, then use the TOT to finish chill to 20K 


The tank could be filled with (a media such as stainless BBs) (could try to mix media types to get different density, fill levels, and heat capacities) to increase heat capacitance to closer to that of LH2 in the final test, or left empty


One goal would be to develop model correlation in response models of the cooling loop and the IMLI for use in future testing of control logic of such LH2 systems without the need to use LH2
