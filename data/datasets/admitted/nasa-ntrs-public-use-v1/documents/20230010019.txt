<div class="slide-content"><p>4</p>
<p>Project Overview</p>
<p>Project Description:   This project will demonstrate the ability to use a two-stage active cryocooler system to reduce/eliminate LH2 boiloff over long duration storage, and demonstrate the advantages over a single stage approach.  It is funded by NASA’s Space Technology Mission Directorate’s Technology Demonstration Missions Program as part of the Cryogenic Fluid Management Portfolio.</p>
<p>Why Does It Matter:  With the next generation of in space vehicles being developed to take humans to the moon and beyond, such as the NASA led HLS initiative,  vehicles will need to store liquid hydrogen for extended periods of time safely, while minimizing the system mass required to achieve Zero Boil-Off (ZBO).  The two-stage approach can theoretically reduce both system mass and power requirements. </p>

<p>90K Cryocooler Power and Mass</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>5</p>
<p>We have multiple input and output parameters to control with parallel PID loops</p>
<p>We want to achieve a narrow band in the final output variable to claim successful zero boil off has been reached (testing application) </p>
<p>ZBO is defined as saturation temperature and constant pressure in the tank with all valves closed </p>
<p>Longer cooling cycles, which may help lower power usage associated with cryocooler startup, would require a wider band in tank pressure (mission application) </p>
<p>Mission challenges could include: </p>
<p>Rapid ullage collapse during engine burn</p>
<p>Rapid pressure increase (hot gas feedback from engine burn)</p>
<p>Rapid change in heat flux in cooling circuit resulting in unstable PID loops </p>
<p>Special challenges for nuclear thermal propulsion including neutron heat load, especially if propellant tanks are used as partial shielding </p>
<p>Conops may vary between short burns vs. long burns </p>
<p>Solar heating (unequal distribution over surface, roll rate) </p>
<p>If helium pressurant is used, modeling is complicated </p>
<p>     Challenges for Controlling 2-Stage Cooling Zero Boil-Off System</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>8</p>
<p>Description of Advanced Model Base Control System </p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Two chilled helium flow loops: Broad Area Cooling (BAC) at ~80K and Tube on Tank (TOT) at ~20K</p>
<p>Each loop has two PID controllers: Flow (cryofan speed) and Heater (electric heater to set lift) </p>
<p>Cryogenerator has constant motor speed for both cryocoolers (lift vs. temp follows performance curve) </p>
<p>Two sets of heaters on tank wall: recovery (high power for fast warmup between tests) and heat leak (low power to artificially increase tank heat load) </p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>9</p>
<p>Why Use Parallel PID Loops</p>
<p>We assume that using parallel loops for the two loops is justified because they are sufficiently isolated by the MLI that feedback between the loops will be below other feedback noise and can be neglected</p>
<p>We assume that using parallel loops for the fan speed and line heaters (TOT and BAC) is justified because the time constant between the two can be configured to a few orders of magnitude difference</p>
<p>The difference in time constant alone may not be sufficient so may need to add a filter to one or both of the loops </p>
<p>The Overwatch application will help keep the loop from oscillating out of control; this will help to simplify the control while giving the user better control than trying to actively react to changing conditions </p>
<p>Lab testing before the actual test will be conducted to verify assumptions </p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>11</p>
<p>Overview of Overwatch Program</p>
<p>Protects the system from positive feedback oscillations </p>
<p>The controller will revert to the last manual setting for the output for the cryofan speeds and line heater voltage output</p>
<p>Will include a limiter on how far the tank wall temp can vary from the tank liquid temperature based on predicted steady state pressure</p>
<p>A preprogrammed step down for conditioning the cooling loop is being considered </p>
<p>Overwatch Indexing Value Table</p>
<p>The Overwatch Indexing Value Table is an attempt at accounting for the two-phase nature of the propellant at the storage conditions</p>
<p>It attempts to account for the fact that based on the condition of the ullage, different equations of state are better model fits</p>
<p>By having the PID value correlated to the current conditions of the tank, TOT, and BAC this will help dampen out any higher order terms that could cause the pressure control system to become unstable </p>
<p>Overwatch Deadband control</p>
<p>Will utilize the energy balance equation and ideal gas law (or REFPROP tables) to predict where the tank pressure is likely to be in the next set amount of time</p>
<p>Can set more aggressive PID values as needed to keep the pressure within the defined set limits (prevent RV from going off or tank collapse) </p>
<p></p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>13</p>
<p>Possible Additional Characterization Testing</p>
<p>Testing IMLI Response at 4205</p>
<p></p>
<p>Testing at Liquid Hydrogen temperature in the ESTF vacuum chamber in 4205 lab 108 is a challenge but can be done.</p>
<p>The BAC would be chilled to 80 K as planned for the testing at 300</p>
<p>The TOT would be chilled to 20 K as planned for the testing at 300</p>
<p>The tank would be prechilled by running cold LN2 fill, GN2, or wall cooled with LN2 spray</p>
<p>A GHe purge after the nitrogen chill, then use the TOT to finish chill to 20K </p>
<p>The tank could be filled with (a media such as stainless BBs) (could try to mix media types to get different density, fill levels, and heat capacities) to increase heat capacitance to closer to that of LH2 in the final test, or left empty</p>
<p>One goal would be to develop model correlation in response models of the cooling loop and the IMLI for use in future testing of control logic of such LH2 systems without the need to use LH2</p>
<p>Safer option than running with hydrogen; ESTF is not approved for LH2 tests</p>
