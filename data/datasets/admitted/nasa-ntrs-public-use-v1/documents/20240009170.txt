North American T-28 Trojan (N502NA)











North American T-28 Trojan (N502NA)



• Utilized to calibrate guidance beam generated by NASA Wallops 
radar
• My first flight test experiment and as flight test engineer
• First exposure to NASA Wallops assets and personnel
• Lessons: 
• Flight test techniques and protocols were not taught in the academic 



curriculum – on the job training (OJT) mentoring required
• Research Pilots are different from everybody else, including Flight Test 



Engineers











Helio Courier











Helio Courier



• Leased vehicle used to evaluate light STOL operations
• Published first NASA publication (NASA TM)
• Lessons:



• Select the appropriate aircraft for the experiment
• Piloted Induced Oscillations (PIO) can corrupt your results
• Mentorship is essential/critical
• Teamwork is essential (“You cannot do it all by yourself”.)
• Manage by walking around
• Take detailed notes
• Do not write NASA reports like an English major – introduction to the “Grammar 



Ladies”
• Even an experiment with marginal results can result in an informative report











DeHavilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter 
(N508NA)











DeHavilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter, 1 of 2



• First mission - selected to evaluate crosswind landing gear during 
STOL operations at NASA Wallops (aircraft leased from 
manufacturer)
• Lessons:
• NASA cannot perform all modifications inhouse – DeHavilland designed, 



fabricated and installed the crosswind landing gear
• Detailed records are required during solicitation, award and performance 



of the contract
• Be prepared to accept results that differ from the a priori expectations
• Be prepared for industry to not incorporate your results
• Prepare detailed cockpit check lists for the FTE and coordinate with the 



pilot’s check list











DeHavilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter, 2 of 2



• Second mission - used to evaluate airborne commercial lightning 
detector during Storm Hazards 1978 in Norman, OK (aircraft 
purchased by NASA prior to Storm Hazards Program)
• Lessons:
• Be prepared to change the sampling paradigm – instead of flying the 



aircraft in the vicinity of thunderstorms, the aircraft was operated from a 
parked position on the ramp, and let the storms come to the aircraft
• Be prepared for the vendor to challenge your results











General Dynamics F-106B Delta Dart 
(N816NA)

















General Dynamics F-106B Delta Dart (N816NA)
• Selected to collect direct-strike lightning data (electromagnetic) and the correlation of 



other storm hazards to aircraft lightning strikes during Storm Hazards 1979-1986
• My first multiagency research mission (NOAA and FAA)
• Lessons:



• Become an SME in the subject: “The library is over there, and the phone is on your desk.”
• Select an aircraft with appropriate safety features: twin intakes, autoignition, overhead canopy rail, 



canopy glass with imbedded gold foil, ejection seats, excusive use of certified Jet-A/JP-5 fuel
• Consult with lightning SMEs regarding additional precautions: seal all fasteners in fuel tanks and 



conduct “hot and cold zap” tests of the aircraft on the ground before each season
• Flight in such an aircraft required extensive crew training (e.g., physiological (altitude chamber), 



emergency egress (ejection seat and water survival) and cockpit procedures)
• Repeat lesson - Be prepared to accept and defend results that differ from the a priori expectations:



• Aircraft trigger the lightning strikes, do not intercept them
• Aircraft lightning strikes do not correlate with the areas of heaviest precipitation and turbulence
• Aircraft lightning swept attachments to the aircraft exterior are not limited to the sharp edges and along the 



fuselage

















Learjet Lear 28/29 (N566NA)











Learjet Lear 28/29 (N566NA)



• Selected for Joint NASA/USAF Airborne Field Mill Program to 
correlate airborne electric fields with ground-based 
measurements to develop Launch Commit Criteria for Natural 
and Triggered Lightning
• Mission created following lightning strike to NASA Johnson T-38 



over southern California and loss of Atlas Centaur during launch 
from Cape Canaveral
• Last mission in which I served as a Flight Test Engineer
• Lesson
• Lessons learned from the previous Storm Hazards Program were 



applicable to this mission











Boeing 757-100 (N557NA)











Boeing 757-100 (N557NA)
• Acquired to replace NASA Boeing 737 as transport category aircraft for ARMD-



funded missions
• RSD designed, fabricated and installed an Aft Flight Deck (AFD) from which 



the aircraft could be flown with the ship’s pilots in the cockpit providing safety 
oversight
• Incorporating the AFD was the most complex task undertaken by RSD 



including the B-777 (no ties into the ship’s flight controls or Flight 
Management System)
• ARMD cancelled effort due to change in the Agency’s priorities
• Lessons:



• NASA as a Federal agency is subject to politics
• Be flexible –



• Removed AFD and installed it in Building 1268 complex as a fixed-base simulator
• Began supporting SMD-funded missions











Cirrus SR22 (N501NA)











Cirrus SR22 (N501NA)
• Selected by NASA along with the Lancair Columbia 300 (LC40) to 



support the Advanced General Aviation Technology Experiment(AGATE); 
however, AGATE partners required NASA to provide only one aircraft –
RSD selected the SR22.
• Equipped with the GA Baseline Research System (GABRS) and UAS 



Surrogate capability (both designed, fabricated and installed by RSD)
• Recently added modified commercial belly pod to contain EPA science 



instrumentation (nadir viewing)
• Lesson:



• Even “simple” GA aircraft can accommodate highly complex research 
instrumentation systems











Beechcraft B200 (N529NA)











Beechcraft B200 (N529NA)
• Selected as a Mission Management asset for the Center. Later transitioned to 



research aircraft for ARMD and SMD-funded missions
• Dual nadir portals installed by vendor with the FAA approval (STC) for the 



modifications
• B200 supported first SMD-funded missions performed by RSD
• SMD missions are bid and proposal versus ARMD, which was directed 



research
• Supported Heliophysics/SMD by serving as imaging platform for sounding 



rocket launches
• Supported first RSD foreign deployments: Canada, Greenland, South Korea, 



Spain and Gabon
• Lesson:



• Flight test techniques and protocols utilized by RSD were applicable to SMD missions











Dassault HU-25A Guardian (N524NA) 











Dassault HU-25A Guardian (N524NA)



• Former U.S. Coast Guard aircraft acquired to provide NASA with 
business class aircraft to support science missions: “weapons 
bay”, pylons and existing workstation
• RSD installed multiple external science probes and internal racks 



as necessary
• Lesson:
• Repeat: Flight test techniques and protocols utilized by RSD were 



applicable to missions on these aircraft











Gulfstream C-20B Gulfstream III (N520NA)











Gulfstream C-20B Gulfstream III (N520NA)
• Acquired from U.S. Air Force to replace HU-25A, providing RSD with 



global reach: Greenland, South Korea, Taiwan, Philippines, Thailand, 
Sao Tome and Principe and South Africa
• Dual nadir portals designed, fabricated and installed by commercial 



vendor
• Dropsonde design based on design provided by NASA Johnson –



beginning of Integrated Product Team for Gulfstream aircraft at NASA 
Langley, Johnson and Armstrong
• Lessons:



• Repeat: Flight test techniques and protocols utilized by RSD are applicable to 
missions on this aircraft
• Repeat: Detailed records are required during solicitation, award and 



performance of the contract











Boeing 777-246ER (N577NA)











Boeing 777-246ER (N577NA)
• Acquired by NASA to replace the DC-8, which was maintained and operated by 



NASA Armstrong. Provides NASA with large transport category aircraft for 
deploying multiple payloads simultaneously globally
• Initial baseline being designed by multi-Center IPT with support from 



commercial vendors
• Portals to be installed off site by commercial vendor
• Initial Operational Capability scheduled for end of CY25
• Lessons:



• Repeat: Flight test techniques and protocols utilized by RSD are applicable to missions 
on this aircraft (in particular, the B200, B-757, HU-25A and Gulfstream III)



• Repeat: Detailed records are required during solicitation, award and performance of the 
contracts



• Close coordination between the members of the IPT are essential (analyses, design 
reviews with minutes, ICDs, action items with closures)











Top Ten Lessons from My Flight Test Career
• Safety first
• Mentorship is essential/critical
• Teamwork is essential
• Maintain close coordination between the members of your IPT (analyses, 



design reviews with minutes, ICDs, action items with closures)
• Take detailed notes – documentation
• Consult with the SMEs in your subject area as you become a SME
• Apply test techniques and protocols from your prior missions to your current 



mission as appropriate and applicable
• Be prepared to accept and defend your results but be open to valid criticism
• Select the appropriate vehicle for your mission, considering capabilities and 



safety
• Prepare yourself for the mission
