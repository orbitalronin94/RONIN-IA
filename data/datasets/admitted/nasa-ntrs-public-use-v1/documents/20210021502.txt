Spiderweave vs. Stitched Joint Aeroshell
Joint Protective Layer Development

• Stitched Joint (ST): The legacy construction style sews individual panels of fabric
together using high-density, structural stitching.

• Tie-Layer is required to hold individual panels of fabric together.

• Spiderweave (SW) : The novel weave architecture allows for continuous fibers to
span across the rib region.

• The Tie-Layer is not required, but included in Spiderweave stack-up for
similitude in making thermal response comparisons – minimal stitching used.

ACO Task Load Testing
Load testing is used to address mechanical portions of Spiderweave ACO Task Objectives 1, 2 & 3:

Ø Objective #1:  Assess & compare Spiderweave mechanical performance vs. Stitched construction.
Ø Objective #2:  Develop improved measurement technique for carbon fabric/joint strength.
Ø Objective #3:  Develop rib protective layer techniques.  (Load test data use to compare P/L options.)

Ø Motivation behind Objective #2:
Ø Fabric / joint strength testing after the 2015 SPRITE-C arc jet test yielded inconsistent results.

Ø Articles had varying protective treatments and were subject to different test conditions
Ø Fabric/joint samples cut as individual strips for strength testing
Ø Removal, cutting & attaching grips thought to impart additional damage to the samples
Ø Testing as flat strips didn’t replicate aeroshell loading (different angles and constraints)
Ø Residual strength results varied from approx. 15% to 99% of the pristine joint test strength
