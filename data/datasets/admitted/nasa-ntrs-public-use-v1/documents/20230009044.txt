Low Leakage Cryogenic Valves
David Eddleman, Scott Kramer, Andrew Smith, Robert Walker / ER14



Background:
___



• Internal leakage poses a significant challenge for aerospace cryogenic valves in 
meeting the demands of future long-duration missions utilizing cryogenic 
propellants.



• Large vehicles for long-duration missions are projected to require valve sizes ranging 
from 3 inches to 10 inches. Current cryogenic valves are typically used on launch 
vehicles and have internal leakage rates up to 2,000 SCIM for 10-inch valves.



• Surface imperfections and misalignments can create leak paths for cryogenic fluids, 
which are more prone to permeating between (or even through) components due 
to their small molecule size.



• Activity Goal: Create large valves with significantly improved internal leakage rates 
(ideally below 1.0 SCIM) while at liquid hydrogen temperatures.



Low Leakage Relief Valve (LLRV):
• The Low Leakage Relief Valve is a normally-closed, piloted relief valve with a 3-inch 



nominal seat diameter. The LLRV is configured in a right angle (inlet to outlet). The 
pilot valve cracks at 20 psid, and the main valve will open fully before 25 psid. 



• The LLRV is intended to demonstrate the application of the self-aligning seat and 
poppet to pilot operated relief valves, with vent valve fluid conditions (gas).



• Demonstrated internal leakage rates ≤ 1.0 SCIM during LN2 testing and LH2 testing.



Concept:
___



• ER14’s design incorporates a self-aligning spherical poppet and conical seat, 
allowing 5 degrees of freedom through a spring-loaded ball joint. This configuration 
minimizes non-uniform seat stress and potential misalignments between the metal 
poppet and polymer seat.



• Three test valves (relief, isolation, and pre-valve) were developed to demonstrate 
the technology in various sizes, environments, and applications.



• LN2 Testing performed at the Component Development Area (CDA) to establish 
baseline performance and implement required changes prior to LH2 testing.



• LH2 Testing performed at Test Stand 300 (TS300) in the East Test Area.



Low Leakage Isolation Valve (LLIV):
• The Low Leakage Isolation Valve is a normally-open, bellows-actuated isolation 



valve with a 3-inch nominal seat diameter. The inline configuration with pneumatic 
actuator allows sealing force to vary by changing actuator pressure.



• The LLIV is intended to demonstrate the application of the self-aligning seat and 
poppet to fill-and-drain style valves in high flow, liquid environments.



• High internal leakage rates were initially observed during LN2 testing but were 
subsequently resolved. Demonstrated internal leakage rates ≤ 1.5 SCIM during LH2
testing.



Low Leakage Large Pre-Valve (LPV):
• The Low Leakage Large Pre-Valve is a normally-closed, hydraulically actuated 



isolation valve, with an 8-inch nominal seat diameter. The LPV is configured in a 
right angle (inlet to outlet), with the actuator port located at the top of the valve.



• The LPV is intended to demonstrate the application of the self-aligning seat and 
poppet to large, main propulsion system pre-valves.



• Demonstrated internal leakage rates ≤ 1.5 SCIM during LN2 testing and ≤ 5.0 SCIM 
during LH2 testing.
