<p>Overview</p>
<p>2</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>SLS vehicle generates environments during launch that must be mitigated by water systems on the Mobile Launcher, known as the Ignition Overpressure and Sound Suppression (IOP/SS) system</p>
<p>In preparation for Artemis I launch, flight rationale had to be built using a number of tests and models</p>
<p>Verify the integrated system works as intended </p>
<p>Verify that mitigation of liftoff acoustics does not generate additional adverse phenomena</p>
<p>Flight operation of SLS IOP/SS is a complex mix of geometric, fluid, and acoustic interactions</p>
<p>Multi-phase flow with significant gradients to water sheets/spray/mist</p>
<p>RS-25s begin to ignite around T-6 seconds, entraining surrounding fluids</p>
<p>Booster ignition at T-0 seconds with ignition shock propagation and plume development in short succession</p>
<p>Testing and analysis are ultimately partial models for this system of systems</p>
<p>Only practical full test is Artemis I launch itself</p>
<p>Artemis I flight rationale required leveraging all available tests</p>
<p>SLS-specific tests and historic water systems evaluated compared to the SLS system to develop 7-elements of flight rationale</p>
<p>Solid technical understanding</p>
<p>Condition relative to experience base</p>
<p>Bounding case established</p>
<p>Self-limiting aspects</p>
<p>Margins understood</p>
<p>Assessments based on data/testing/analysis</p>
<p>Interactions with other elements/conditions</p>

<p>Conclusions</p>
<p>Use of water for ignition overpressure mitigation and sound suppression has been common in the launch vehicle industry for many decades with no demonstrated history of safety-of-flight incidents as a result of use</p>
<p>Several launch vehicles are analogous but with notable differences</p>
<p>Space Transportation System (STS) is most useful for SLS, with limitations, while providing 136 successful flights (for IOP/SS)</p>
<p>Historical Data Review</p>

<div class="embedded" id="slide9_rId3"></div>
<p>9</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>STS Sound Suppression Water System (SSWS) uses same water tower and valve complex as SLS (without bypass valves), same risers, same pad</p>
<p>Water distributed to IOP water nozzles in separate plume holes for Orbiter RS-25s and each Booster</p>
<p>Note, IOP water to Booster plume holes added between STS-1 and STS-2, using water redirected from flame trench side pipe, and integrated externally to the MLP</p>
<p>Rainbirds for sound suppression were located on the Booster side of the MLP, similar in design and function to SLS</p>
<p>Crest water directed over flame diverter like SLS, but pointed in two directions to accommodate plumes from Boosters and RS-25s directed to either side</p>
<p>STS SSWS Overview</p>

<p>STS Tests of Interest – Water Flow Tests</p>
<p>12</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>FRFs performed prior to first flight of each orbiter, with Challenger requiring a second and Discovery receiving a second prior to return to flight</p>
<p>Columbia (OV-102) – 20 February 1981</p>
<p>Challenger (OV-099) – 18 December 1982 and 25 January 1983</p>
<p>Discover (OV-103) – 2 June 1984 and 10 August 1988 (STS-26)</p>
<p>Atlantis (OV-104) – 12 September 1985</p>
<p>Endeavour (OV-105) – 6 April 1992</p>
<p></p>
<p>FRFs required modifications to mobile launch platform and SSWS to mitigate on-pad plume heating and acoustic environments</p>
<p>Some legs of SSWS appear to be capped (Booster IOP, rainbirds) to provide enough water to RS-25s and trench for FRF duration</p>
<p>Thermal barrier on deck between RS-25s and Boosters</p>
<p>STS Tests of Interest – Flight Readiness Firings</p>
<p>13</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Ongoing test campaign to improve the IOP/SS performance for Artemis II  and Artemis III</p>
<p>Successfully completed 7 out of 8 planned flows</p>
<p>Final configuration test planned for early 2024</p>
<p>Areas of focus</p>
<p>Increasing overall flow rates to the SS system</p>
<p>Improving flow patterns to minimize interference with ML 0 Deck imagery assets </p>
<p>Current Effort: Artemis II ISVV-2 Water Flow Testing</p>

<p>14</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>
<div class="slide-content"><p>Artemis I flight rationale was achieved through utilizing the art of partial modeling</p>
<p>Successful launch on November 16th, 2022</p>
<p>Although this was a night launch, substantial imagery was captured to verify the performance of the fully integrated IOP/SS system, complete with interactions with the SLS vehicle, plumes, and environments</p>
<p>IOP/SS system performed primary function of mitigating the liftoff environments generated by the SLS vehicle</p>
<p>NASA is preparing to take the next step by sending crew on board Artemis II</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>The hope of the authors is that this summary of NASA’s IOP/SS water systems will be of benefit to the engineers of tomorrow designing the next generation of launch vehicles</p>
