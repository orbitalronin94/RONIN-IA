<p>In Situ Process Monitoring Functions
</p>
<p>There are two main functions of in situ process monitoring:
</p>
<p>Process Control Function
</p>
<p>• Quantitative analysis of part quality
• Requires a known correlation between 
</p>
<p>indications, physics of the process, and 
actual defects in the finished part
</p>
<p>• Need to know probability of detection
• Extra step – verify actual size, 
</p>
<p>location of created defects
</p>
<p>• Need to treat it like NDE – believe and 
investigate every indication
</p>
<p>• Can’t dismiss anything as a false 
positive unless proven
</p>
<p>vs.
</p>
<p>Part Quality Function
</p>
<p>• Real-time warning of build problems
• Use to check for process drift
• Monitor effects of parameter 
</p>
<p>changes, spatter, etc. 
</p>
<p>• Not counting on it for quantitative 
part quality metrics or defect 
detection
</p>
<p>• May help tell you where to look 
for a problem, but would 
require verification with NDE</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>When considering the use of in situ process monitoring for part 
qualification, there are a few aspects that challenge the current paradigm.
</p>
<p>Closed-loop process control
</p>
<p>• Often, defect observations are indirect
</p>
<p>• Directly observing process variation, inferring final defect
</p>
<p>• Must understand physical basis for measured phenomena
</p>
<p>• Need to prove a causal correlation from measured indications to defect state
</p>
<p>• Probability of detection (POD) study must include secondary verification of created defects
</p>
<p>• Current NASA qualification logic based on a locked process
</p>
<p>• For real-time parameter changes, a new approach is needed
</p>
<p>In-process vs. post-process NDE
</p>
<p>no longer 
nondestructive
</p>

<p>Passive in situ process monitoring may be used as a 
quantitative indicator of part quality, if qualified.
Language:
</p>
<p>“Prior to use as a quantitative indicator of part quality for part acceptance, passive 
in-situ process monitoring technologies shall be qualified by the CEO to the 
</p>
<p>satisfaction of NASA in a manner analogous to other NDE techniques.”
</p>
<p>Rationale:
• “All processes that are used to establish quantifiable quality assurance metrics 
</p>
<p>are qualified against established criteria to verify detection reliability, calibration, 
and implementation. If in-situ monitoring techniques are employed for such 
purposes, the need for such qualification is unchanged.”
</p>
<p>(emphasis added)
</p>
<p>Qualified In Situ Process Monitoring</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Passive in situ process monitoring may NOT replace NDE.
Rationale (cont.):
</p>
<p>• “Certification of a passive in-situ monitoring technology relies upon a thorough
understanding of the physical basis for the measured phenomena, a proven causal 
correlation of the measured phenomena to a well-defined defective process state, and a 
proven level of reliability for detection of the defective process state.”
</p>
<p>• “If qualified in the manner stated above, an in-situ process monitoring technique can be 
used to complement NDE in the Integrated Structural Integrity Rationale of the PPP. At this 
time, even a qualified in-situ process monitoring method cannot be considered a complete 
replacement for NDE.”
</p>
<p>• “Even if qualification is not desired, the use of in-situ process monitoring is encouraged as 
a source of process control data. This data can also be used to help guide targeted 
inspection.”
</p>
<p>(emphasis added)
</p>

<p>• What: The In-Situ Project
• Correlating In-situ monitoring data and Non-Destructive Evaluation (NDE) methods to characterize defect populations in 
</p>
<p>Laser Powder Bed Fusion (L-PBF) material
</p>
<p>• How: Analyze defect detection capabilities of in-situ monitoring by comparison to 
traditional NDE using flaws created with controlled off-nominal build parameters 
and verified by metallography
</p>
<p>• Create samples with known defects on EOS M290
• Record layer to layer AM process quality with EOState Optical Tomography Monitoring system 
• Compare results using North Star Imaging X5000 Mini-/Micro-Focus CT and UES RoboMet serial sectioning system
</p>
<p>• Why:
• Supports developing roadmap for qualifying in-situ monitoring technologies to support NASA-STD-6030 certification 
</p>
<p>approach
• Proven causal correlation between indications in in-situ monitoring data and final state of the part
</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>• Current L-PBF process, like any process, can generate material that contains 
defects:
</p>
<p>• gas porosity trapped in the powder feedstock
• random fluctuations in laser power output
• error in the build process: skipped layer or short feed
</p>
<p>• Defects have detrimental effect on the properties of the material produced:
• crack initiation sites
• reduce safe-life of the component
</p>
<p>• Traditional NDE methods are not fully adapted to AM:
• Limitations in reliably detecting defects in large or complex part geometries
</p>
<p>• Development of new approach is necessary:
• Pairing data from in-situ monitoring with NDE 
</p>
<p>Introduction</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>1. Develop L-PBF material defect detection and characterization methodologies
2. Understand the influences of build parameter variations on the resulting 
</p>
<p>material defect populations.
3. Correlate detected defect populations with tensile and fatigue properties.
4. Characterize the effect of heat treatment on the defect populations and their 
</p>
<p>detectability with mini-CT.
5. Evaluate part-to-part and build-to-build defect population repeatability.
6. Establish a preliminary probability of detection for the available in-situ 
</p>
<p>monitoring tools and for mini-CT.
7. Evaluate seeded defect methodologies for realistic defect creation.
8. Baseline defect populations for an established AM process as defined in NASA-
</p>
<p>STD-6030 Qualified Material Process (QMP).

<p>The current certification approach does not accommodate 
the use of adaptive systems.
</p>
<p>Two issues for verification:
</p>
<p>• Monitor the process using sensors (e.g. meltpool thermal signature) and change a 
machine/process parameter (e.g. laser power) to optimize the response
</p>
<p>• Currently available in many directed energy deposition (DED) systems
</p>
<p>1. Verify the sensor performance, algorithm and machine response
</p>
<p>2. Verify the physics – does controlling this parameter result in a good part?
</p>
<p>Adaptive (Closed-Loop) Systems
</p>
<p>(control system)
(materials)
</p>
<p>“Closed-loop process control based on adaptive in-situ monitoring technologies that alter the defined AM 
process in response to monitored phenomena are not currently applicable technology per this NASA 
</p>
<p>Technical Standard and cannot be used without prior approved tailoring.”
</p>
<p>Statement A: Approved for public release; distribution is unlimited.</p>

<p>For certifying closed-loop control systems, NASA can leverage the 
expertise of the spacecraft control systems community.

<p>Black box issue:
</p>
<p>1. Verify the accuracy of the sensor data
</p>
<p>2. Verify the software/algorithm processing and response
</p>
<p>3. Verify that the changed parameter responds correctly
</p>
<p>• For commercial systems, machine parameters may not be known, 
algorithms may not be accessible
</p>
<p>• Ideal approach: collaborate with machine manufacturers
</p>
<p>• Can also develop transfer function by studying inputs/outputs
</p>
