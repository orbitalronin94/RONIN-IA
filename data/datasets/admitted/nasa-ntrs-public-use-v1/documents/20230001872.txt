<div class="slide-content"><p>Do we need to worry about microbes leaking from our suits and habitats?</p>
<p>BACKGROUND</p>
<p>Some organisms can survive exposure to space!</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide43_rId2"></div>
<p>We also know that all crewed, pressurized volumes will leak or vent </p>
<p>Cyano-bacteria, lichen and fungi survived  up to 500+ days outside ISS </p>
<p>Tardigrades survived extended ISS exposure…and then reproduced</p>
<p>ISSUE </p>
<p>But we don’t know what’s actually leaking/venting from our current systems, how long those organisms could survive,  or how far they may travel under destination conditions</p>
<p></p>
<p>Does proximity to a warm spacecraft matter?</p>
<p>The answers will drive element design (i.e. closed vs. open ECLS), where we place elements, and who/how we collect science samples</p>
<p>How close can crew get without compromising science? </p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide43_rId3"></div>
<p>How far could our hitchhikers spread?</p>
