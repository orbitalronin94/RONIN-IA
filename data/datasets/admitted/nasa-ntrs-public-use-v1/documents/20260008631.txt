Lunar Orbit Scope and Applications 
to Planetary Protection



* To support planetary protection (PP) compliance and sustainable lunar exploration, we 



investigate the natural dynamical behavior of low lunar orbits (LLOs) as a mechanism for 



predictable disposal, contamination risk reduction, and stable staging architectures. 



• Under the Committee on Space Research (COSPAR) planetary protection framework, 



Moon missions are classified as Category II, IIa, and IIb, requiring assessment and 



documentation of impact locations and forward-contamination pathways. Predictable 



orbital decay and long-term stability are therefore critical for end-of-mission design.



* We analyze families of LLOs spanning ~10–250 km altitude and 80°–100° inclination 



(i.e., polar and near-polar) representative of pre- and post-descent staging orbits for 



NASA’s Artemis Human Landing System (HLS) program and Commercial Lunar Payload 



Services (CLPS) Initiative missions.



* High-fidelity propagations via flight-proven astrodynamics software (STK and GMAT) 



reveal two dominant behaviors. 



 1) Naturally decaying trajectories produce deterministic surface impacts that cluster 



 into geographically confined latitude–longitude corridors governed primarily by 



 initial orbital geometry. 



 2) Frozen and quasi-stable regimes exhibit multi-year to decadal lifetimes with 



 negligible impact likelihood, forming candidate “graveyard” or staging orbits.



• What is MAUNA? MAUNA automates high-throughput spacecraft 



trajectory analysis by generating, executing, and post-processing 



thousands of General Mission Analysis Tool (GMAT) propagations across 



multidimensional design spaces. This replaces manually created and 



modified scripts with a standardized, reproducible workflow and enables 



systematic exploration of large orbital trade spaces.



• Where does AI/ML actually enter? Mauna-generated high-fidelity 



trajectory data provide a foundation for physics-informed machine 



learning. For lunar impact prediction, machine learning (ML) can learn the 



slow, long-term evolution of orbital states while known gravitational physics 



constrain the solution; trajectories are then returned to high-fidelity numerical 



propagation near the terminal event to accurately resolve the impact location.



• What has NASA actually used this for / why does it matter? 



The framework has been applied to lunar mission-design problems 



supporting NASA’s Artemis HLS and CLPS programs, including high-



fidelity low-lunar-orbit stability analysis, automated searches across hundreds 



of thousands of candidate trajectories, and mapping of natural impact/disposal 



behavior. This combination of automation, physics-based simulation, and ML 



enables faster identification of stable orbital regimes and potential surface-



interaction risks.
