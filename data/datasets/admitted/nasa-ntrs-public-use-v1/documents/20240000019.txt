Time-averaged concentration of H2 gas at exit not dependent on leakage location. Minor variation in spatial distribution of H2 compared to variation in test data.

Artemis I H2 leakages have occurred at the 8 inch and 4 inch QD interfaces within the interior plate cavity.





Helium (Ghe) is used to purge the cavity. The combined Ghe/H2 gas is vented to the atmosphere outside the umbilical plate.





The Ghe purge is designed to dilute the H2 leakages to a non-hazardous concentration. 


Artemis I experienced multiple Launch Commit Criteria (LCC) violations due to H2 leakages overwhelming the purge system.


ER42(22-073)

















Flammability of H2/Helium/Air Mixtures





Flammability Diagram H2-He-Air


For a nominal umbilical leak scenario, the H2/GHe exhaust gas is not flammable when mixed with external air


LCC violation occurs when exhaust gas exceeds 4% H2


The lower flammability limit of H2 in air is 4%


A 4% H2 / 96% Ghe mixture is not flammable in air


The flammability diagram and empirical data show that ~8% H2 is required to produce a flame when mixed with air 


CFD simulation used to model the flammable volume of gas generated by the H2/Ghe exhaust mixing with air to provide additional information beyond the graphical flammability limits


CFD simulations visualize location of flammable gas and potential H2 accumulation regions for various flow conditions


Goal was to inform risk acceptance of an Artemis I launch waiver that allowed for a 10% H2 concentration exhaust exiting the umbilical plate cavity 


8% H2/ 92% Ghe gas mixture just skirts the edge of the flammable region in the diagram when mixed uniformly with air. 10% H2 or greater falls well into the flammable mixture region.

CFD Analysis Impact and Artemis I Observations


20


CFD analysis showed that at 10% H2 concentration, the vented exhaust gas could potentially support a sustained combustion event if ignited


No credible ignition source was identified in the flammable gas volume





Impact to Artemis I


CFD analysis of the umbilical exhaust venting was used to develop an Artemis I flight waiver


The waiver allowed for a 10% H2 concentration in the exhaust gas before violating the LCC


The waiver was used during the successful Artemis I flight on Nov. 16th 2022
