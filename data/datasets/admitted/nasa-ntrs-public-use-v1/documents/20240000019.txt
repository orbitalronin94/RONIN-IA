<div class="slide-content"><p>CFD Analysis Impact and Artemis I Observations</p>
<p>11</p>
<p>CFD/VCE analysis of CAPU exhaust risk showed a failure of the HBOI system was unlikely to produce a catastrophic consequence for the vehicle. </p>
<p>During Artemis I launch, HBOI systems performed nominally</p>
<p>CAPU exhaust gas ignited and burned continuously through launch sequence</p>
<p>No off-nominal H2 pops observed</p>

<p>H2 Leakages Occur Inside SLS Umbilical Plate Cavities</p>
<p>Cavity purged with Helium, H2 concentration monitored during purge</p>
<p>Umbilical plate purge system tested at Launch Equipment Test Facility (LETF)</p>
<p>Tests produced inconsistent H2 concentration results that varied by leak location and test-to-test</p>
<p>Known flow rates of H2 leakage gas and Helium purge gas</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Final CFD Findings</p>
<p>15</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide15_rId2"></div>
<p>Time-averaged H2 Concentration at New Hazardous Gas Sampling Location</p>
<p>Time-averaged concentration of H2 gas at exit not dependent on leakage location. Minor variation in spatial distribution of H2 compared to variation in test data.</p>
<p>CFD simulation results eliminated incomplete H2/Ghe mixing as the cause of the test data inconsistencies. Seal leakage eventually identified as the root cause.</p>
<p>Umbilical plate seal and Ghe purge system were redesigned with recommendations from CFD analyses. CFD results and test data agreed for final redesign.</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>16</p>
<p>SLS CS LH2 Umbilical Plate External System and Hazard Description</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide16_rId2"></div>
<p>Umbilical Plate External View</p>
<p>Fill/Drain Cavity</p>
<p>Helium Purge (2)</p>
<p>Bleed, </p>
<p>4 inch Umbilical QD</p>
<p>Fill/Drain, </p>
<p>8 inch Umbilical QD</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide16_rId3"></div>
<p>Fill/Drain Cavity Purge External Exhaust Port</p>
<p>Exhaust Port to External Atmosphere</p>
<p>Haz. Gas Sample Port to Mass Spectrometer</p>
<p>Artemis I H2 leakages have occurred at the 8 inch and 4 inch QD interfaces within the interior plate cavity.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Helium (Ghe) is used to purge the cavity. The combined Ghe/H2 gas is vented to the atmosphere outside the umbilical plate.</p>
<p></p>
<p>The Ghe purge is designed to dilute the H2 leakages to a non-hazardous concentration.    </p>
<p>Artemis I experienced multiple Launch Commit Criteria (LCC) violations due to H2 leakages overwhelming the purge system.</p>
<p>ER42(22-073)</p>
</div>
<div class="slide-master-content"></div>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Flammability of H2/Helium/Air Mixtures</p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide17_rId2"></div>
<p>Flammability Diagram H2-He-Air</p>
<p>For a nominal umbilical leak scenario, the H2/GHe exhaust gas is not flammable when mixed with external air</p>
<p>LCC violation occurs when exhaust gas exceeds 4% H2</p>
<p>The lower flammability limit of H2 in air is 4%</p>
<p>A 4% H2 / 96% Ghe mixture is not flammable in air</p>
<p>The flammability diagram and empirical data show that ~8% H2 is required to produce a flame when mixed with air </p>
<p>CFD simulation used to model the flammable volume of gas generated by the H2/Ghe exhaust mixing with air to provide additional information beyond the graphical flammability limits</p>
<p>CFD simulations visualize location of flammable gas and potential H2 accumulation regions for various flow conditions</p>
<p>Goal was to inform risk acceptance of an Artemis I launch waiver that allowed for a 10% H2 concentration exhaust exiting the umbilical plate cavity </p>
<p>8% H2/ 92% Ghe gas mixture just skirts the edge of the flammable region in the diagram when mixed uniformly with air. 10% H2 or greater falls well into the flammable mixture region.</p>
<p>ER42(22-073)</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>CFD Analysis Impact and Artemis I Observations</p>
<p>20</p>
<p>CFD analysis showed that at 10% H2 concentration, the vented exhaust gas could potentially support a sustained combustion event if ignited</p>
<p>No credible ignition source was identified in the flammable gas volume</p>
<p></p>
<p>Impact to Artemis I</p>
<p>CFD analysis of the umbilical exhaust venting was used to develop an Artemis I flight waiver</p>
<p>The waiver allowed for a 10% H2 concentration in the exhaust gas before violating the LCC</p>
<p>The waiver was used during the successful Artemis I flight on Nov. 16th 2022 </p>
