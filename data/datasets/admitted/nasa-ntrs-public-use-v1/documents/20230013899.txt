<p>NASA’s risk management (RM) framework entails two complementary processes:
</p>
<p>Risk-Informed Decision Making (RIDM)
</p>
<p>To inform decisions (e.g., SE decisions) through better use of risk and uncertainty 
information in selecting among alternatives
</p>
<p>Continuous Risk Management (CRM)
</p>
<p>To manage individual and aggregate performance risks over the course of the 
development and implementation phases of the life cycle to assure that defined objectives 
are met
</p>
<p>Consistent with the philosophy of “risk leadership” set forth in NPD 1000.0, RM processes are anchored 
to the established risk posture for objectives
</p>

<p>Application of “Risk Leadership” per NPD 1000.0C
</p>
<p>NASA Directive 1000.0C provides guiding principles of risk leadership at NASA
</p>
<p>“NASA leadership/employees are undergoing a cultural shift in risk understanding and acceptance, with the Agency actively implementing a philosophy of ‘Risk 
Leadership’ that has the goal of increasing ‘decision velocity’ within a proper risk posture”
</p>
<p>“There is a recognized need to define an appropriate risk posture along the lines of ‘how safe is safe enough’. For instance, the ASAP* noted in its recommendation 2009-
01-02a: “[a risk-informed design] approach is viable only if a common understanding of ‘sufficiently safe’ exists”
</p>
<p>“NASA’s diverse program/project portfolio necessitates a range of risk profiles tuned to the purpose of the program/project/sub-project elements within the portfolio.”
</p>
<p>“Risk Leadership is not just with respect to NASA’s engineering workforce, rather it involves all aspects of implementing missions, including the legal team, the resources 
managers, the procurement specialists, etc.; all of whom contribute to the risk posture”
</p>
<p>Accordingly, our RM Directive, NPR 8000.4C :
</p>
<p>Identifies the responsibility of NASA managers for the formulation risk leadership principles that establish the risk posture appropriate for the execution of effective and 
timely risk related decision making and affirms the corresponding responsibility for risk managers at all levels to implement such principles onto practical definition of risk 
posture and risk tolerance limits 
</p>
<p>Asserts that the manager of a NASA organizational unit must ensure the content of the unit’s Risk Management Plan be consistent with the risk leadership principles the 
organizational unit is expected to apply
</p>
<p>The NPR establishes a requirement for traceability of risk acceptance decisions to the risk posture established for projects or missions 
</p>

<p>Relationship between Risk Leadership and Risk Posture / Risk Tolerances
</p>
<p>Risk Leadership is exerted by managers by setting and 
communicating across a project or activity an appropriate 
Risk Posture for objectives
</p>
<p>Risk Posture is the expression of an overall attitude towards 
risk in the execution of a project or activity 
</p>
<p>Risk Posture is translated into the specific levels of Risk 
Tolerance set for all the key Project/Activity Performance 
Measures
</p>
<p>Depending on the project or activity nature and context, 
Risk Tolerance levels may be expressed quantitatively or 
“qualitatively” (e.g., with qualitative terms such as “high,” 
“medium,” “low,” corresponding to order-of-magnitude 
definitions) 
</p>
<p>I.e., as quantitative or qualitative expressions of allowable 
probability of performance deficits
</p>
