Motivation


Cruise drag reduction of simplified high-lift system


3.3 count reduction in excrescence drag – 2.25% reduction in fuel burn 


Hartwich et al., NASA/CR-2016-21170 








Takeoff L/D improvements of AFC aileron


2.5% – 5% net improvement in L/D at takeoff


Shmilovich et al., NASA Technical Report, document ID 20220006731





2








We are motivated by the fact that AFC is a technology that has the potential to improve the aerodynamics efficiency of a commercial transport.





Two examples are shown.





In the first, Hartwich et al showed that a 3.3 count reduction in excrescence drag is possible by applying active flow control to simplify a high-lift system





In the 2nd example, Shmilovich et al indicate that a 2.5% to 5% net improvement in takeoff L/D (lift-to-drag ratio) may be possible by applying AFC to a deflected aileron.

Experiment Description: Model





M = 0.1





Rec = 750,000





df = 0° and 20°





30% c flap


3


c = 0.3048 m








The test was performed in the Basic Aerodynamics Research Tunnel located at the NASA Langley Research Center. 





The model tested is a 30 deg swept wing, semispan model that is sidewall mounted in the Tunnel. The chord is 0.3 meters and the semispan is 0.61 meters. 





It has an N-A-C-A 0015 cross-section normal to the leading edge. 





The flap was tested at deflection angles of 0 and 20 degrees in this study and the results are included in the paper. 





The results obtained with the flap deflected to 20 degrees will be presented. 





Transition dot of 0.2mm applied to the upper surface at around 1.14cm from the LE to achieve a fully turbulent BL





The model has the capability of LE Actuation – the slot for it was left opeN

On the left hand side, we present the lift increment versus momentum coefficient or cmu supplies to the array of SWJ actuators with wing at an angle of attack of 4 deg. The figures indicates that we were able to achieve a lift increment of 0.4 using a cmu of approximately 2.5%. 


____________





On the right hand side we show Cp distributions. We decided to focus on the cmu value that generated a Cp of 0 at the TE. The cp distribution on the right hand side Indicates that with cmu=0 the flow over the flap is separated. 





As cmu is increased we see a nice pressure recovery over the flap and a trailing edge cp equal to 0 is achieved with a cmu of approximately 1%. 


______________________ 


For the remainder of the presentation the SwJ AFC results presented used a Cmu=1.1%. This Cmu value generated a cmu of 0.26 which represents a 26% improvement in CL at this angle of attack.

Summary


Active flow control (AFC) applied to a 30° swept wing with an NACA 0015 cross section normal to the leading edge.





Sweeping jet (SWJ) actuators with a Cm = 1.1% attached the flow when the flap deflected 20° and Rec=750,000. 





Flow visualization and particle image velocimetry (PIV) combined with force and moment data aided in understanding the flowfields with and without AFC.





Spanwise component of flow caused streamwise vortices produced by SWJ actuator to be inclined toward wing tip.
