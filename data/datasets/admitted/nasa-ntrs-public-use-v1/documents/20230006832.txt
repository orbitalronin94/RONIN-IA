<p>• This talk presents an overview of RST slitless
</p>
<p>spectrometers: high resolution grism and low 
</p>
<p>resolution prism assemblies.  
</p>
<p>• Combined with selected bandpass filters, grism
</p>
<p>and prism will perform the high latitude wide 
</p>
<p>area and time domain surveys to understand 
</p>
<p>the mystery of dark energy.  The details are 
</p>
<p>covered in the presentation : “Science Drivers of the Nancy 
</p>
<p>Grace Roman Space Telescope Grism and Prism” by Joshua Schlieder.
</p>
<p>• The following slides start from the formulation of optical concept design based 
</p>
<p>on the grism and prism requirements driven by the science goals.  It emphasizes 
</p>
<p>the challenges that the design and development encountered and the solutions to 
</p>
<p>overcome the challenges.
</p>

<p>• The main challenge for both grism and prism is to meet the image quality 
and wavefront error due to:
– Wide Field Of View: more than 100 x larger than the wide field camera of Hubble Space 
</p>
<p>Telescope (HST).
</p>
<p>– Wider spectral range from 750 nm to 1800 nm.
</p>
<p>– Located in a non-collimated f/8 space.
</p>
<p>– To fit into a tightly allocated space as two elements of the filter wheel.
</p>
<p>• The paper describes the three major development and implementation  
phases:
– Optical Design
</p>
<p>– Fabrication and alignment
</p>
<p>– Calibration and characterization.  
</p>
<p>• The challenges and solutions of each phase are briefly addressed.  The 
accomplishment of each step is summarized.
</p>
<p>Overall Challenges</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>606/04/2023
</p>
<p>• It was determined at a very early stage that Grism is the RST’s slitless
spectrometer with a compound multi-element assembly.  So it has gone 
through three develop phases: Prototype, EDU and ETU, and finally flight 
assemblies.
</p>
<p>• Challenges: 
</p>
<p>– To provide required spectral resolution implies that the grism needs to provide 
a diffraction limited optical design. 
</p>
<p>– The geometric aberration introduced by a grating in non-collimated space is 
proportional to wavelength.  It is impossible to be compensated with the 
shape and refractive index of the powered prism.
</p>
<p>• Solution:
</p>
<p>– Introduce second diffractive surface to compensate the chromatic aberration 
from the grating. 
</p>
<p>– Final grism design is consist of 3 elements: a parallel plate with diffractive 
surfaces on both sides plus 2 powered prisms.    
</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>806/04/2023
</p>
<p>• The prism assembly was added to WFI much later.  So it only went through 
ETU and flight phases.  Both use the same optical design.
</p>
<p>• Challenge:
</p>
<p>– The large FOV and the wide wavelength range still pose a great challenge for 
the design for aberration control. Furthermore, the output beam from prism 
assembly is required to be zero deviation at the desired wavelength. 
</p>
<p>– A rarely seen phenomenon had appeared during the initial design: in the 
middle of the spectrum, the dispersion direction is reversed, which means a 
part of the spectrum was overlapped.
</p>
<p>• Solution
</p>
<p>– The refractive index and dispersion (Abbe number) selection of the two 
materials is critical for a successful design.  The high index material mainly 
provides the required dispersion, and the low index material provides the zero 
deviation.   The final material selection is S-THI1 and CaF2.  This combination 
ensured a monotonic spectrum and the required spectral resolution. 
</p>
<p>Optical Design: Prism assembly</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>906/04/2023
</p>
<p>Optical design: Prism assembly
</p>
<p>CaF2
</p>
<p>S-THI1
</p>
<p>Flight prism
</p>
<p>Two element design with all spherical surfaces.
</p>
<p>One is made of S-THI1 glass to provide the desired
</p>
<p>spectral dispersion, the other one is made of CaF2 
</p>
<p>to make the beam zero deviation at the selected wavelength.
</p>
<p>The prism design meets both dispersion and wavefront requirements
Details of Grism and prism optical design are covered in presentation: “Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope 
</p>
<p>(RST) Grism and Prism: Optical Design” by Guangjun Gao, present by Catherine Marx</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>1006/04/2023
</p>
<p>• Challenge:
</p>
<p>– Grism includes two diffractive surfaces, which 
</p>
<p>indicates that high diffraction efficiency is needed.
</p>
<p>– Element inspections and assembly alignment posed 
</p>
<p>a big challenge for the three elements: a flat with double 
</p>
<p>sided diffractive surfaces and two powered prisms.
</p>
<p>• Solution:
</p>
<p>– Using multistep plasma etching technology to make high efficiency and wide band diffractive 
</p>
<p>surfaces.
</p>
<p>– Use Computer Generated Hologram (CGH) with specially designed fiducials on it for each 
</p>
<p>element. On the other hand, fiducials and references were also carefully added to element 
</p>
<p>itself.
</p>
<p>– Precision metrology were performed on each element.
</p>
<p>– Using the grism optical model and recorded fiducial data on each element to align the whole 
</p>
<p>grism assembly.  The precision metrology tools and creative methods are applied.
</p>
<p>Fabrication and Alignment: Grism</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>1106/04/2023
</p>
<p>• Challenge 
</p>
<p>– A lot of the prism’s challenges are similar to grism’s that needs CGH and 
</p>
<p>precision metrology.
</p>
<p>– An unique challenge posed to prism is the refractive index uncertainty of 
</p>
<p>S-THI1 that has significant impact on image and dispersion scale.
</p>
<p>– The other prism is CaF2, which is not only difficult to fabricate, but also 
</p>
<p>has a much higher CTE than other optical materials.
</p>
<p>Fabrication and alignment: Prism
</p>
<p>• Solution
</p>
<p>– A lot of the prism’s challenges are similar to grism’s that needs CGH and precision 
</p>
<p>metrology.
</p>
<p>– An unique challenge posed to prism is the refractive index uncertainty of S-THI1 that has 
</p>
<p>significant impact on image quality and dispersion scale.
</p>
<p>– The other prism is CaF2, which is not only difficult to fabricate, but also has a much higher 
</p>
<p>CTE than other optical materials.
</p>
<p>Details of Grism and prism fabrication and alignment are covered in presentation: “Alignment and wavefront testing 
</p>
<p>results of the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Grism and Prism assemblies” by Margaret Dominguez</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>1206/04/2023
</p>
<p>• Main tasks: diffraction efficiency and throughput, encircled energy, radiometric 
calibration, bandpass filter edge measurement, and dispersion scale.
</p>
<p>• Challenge:
</p>
<p>– Even though all tests have challenges, the spectral accuracy and wavelength knowledge is the most 
challenging test.  This is because the slitless spectrometers strongly rely on the edge sharpness of the 
wide bandpass filter and the dispersion scale to determine the absolute wavelength.  In effect the slope 
of the edge is not steep enough to provide a decisive wavelength, plus it is field and polarization-
dependent.
</p>
<p>• Solution
</p>
<p>– Calibration testbed has been carefully designed and aligned to provide better than diffraction limited 
beam for Grism/prism assemblies.
</p>
<p>– White light laser, Super continuum source from NKT, was used to provide point source with continuum 
spectral coverage in our wavelength range (700 – 2000 nm).    
</p>
<p>– Comb filters were designed to provide a series spectral lines with the desired spacing and bandwidth. 
</p>
<p>– FFT based Optical Spectral Analyzers (OSA) were used to calibrate source wavelengths.  The OSAs 
were calibrated by an Argon calibration source.
</p>
<p>– Innovative data collection and analysis methods were used to get the final edge measurements and 
dispersion scale. 
</p>
