<div class="slide-content"><p>Historical Context: Apollo 11</p>
<p>During the final descent of Apollo 11 to the lunar surface, the automatic guidance system targeted a landing point that was deemed unacceptable by the crew</p>
<p>Crew switched to manual control and landed on a relatively level surface bounded by a boulder field and crater</p>
<p>Touchdown conditions were well within the design envelope</p>
<p>Sink/Horizontal (uphill) Velocities: 1.8 and 2.2 ft/s</p>
<p>Attitude (Roll/Pitch/Yaw): -1.4/0.8/15.3 deg</p>
<p>Attitude Rates: -2.86/0/-0.57 deg/s</p>
<p>Landing Slope: 4.5 deg (design limit: 12 deg)</p>
<p>Entire touchdown sequence from initial contact to at-rest ~ 1.5 seconds</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Summary</p>
<p>Work extends Walton, et.al. approach from Apollo to include slosh dynamics</p>
<p>Analytical treatment of collision and vehicle dynamics using the theory of generalized impulse and momentum presented in Kane and Levinson’s textbook</p>
<p>Captures the fundamental dynamics and provides analyst with intuition into the problem</p>
<p>Active control significantly increases robustness to slosh dynamics at touchdown (as expected)</p>
<p>Increase control power, reduced latency and deadbands improves performance</p>

<div class="slide-content"><p>Overview of Current Work</p>
<p>Current work extends the Walton, Herr, and Leonard approach, analytical assessment of slosh on tipover stability</p>
<p>Planar/Rigid Vehicle</p>
<p>During a collision (inelastic), impulsive forces are produced which immediately stop the foot; vehicle rotates about the pinned leg until the other leg strikes the ground</p>
<p>Model slosh as a compound pendulum </p>
<div class="embedded" id="slide13_rId2"></div>
<p>Unstable</p>
<p>Stable</p>
<p></p>
<p></p>
<p>Due to the addition of a second body, conservation of mechanical energy cannot be used to establish critical values of post-collision angular velocities of the vehicle and pendulum that will result in toppling. </p>
<p>Instead, a two-step procedure is adopted in this analysis :</p>
<p>1) Theory involving generalized momenta and impulses described in Dynamics: Theory and Application of Kane’s Method, is used to predict the post-collision angular velocities of the vehicle and pendulum</p>
<p>2) Solve the coupled 2 degree-of-freedom post-collision equations of motion numerically to determine if toppling occurs</p>
