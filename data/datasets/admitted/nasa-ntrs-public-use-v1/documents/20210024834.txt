Motivation

➢ Shortcomings of RANS-based transition models for the Common Research Model with Natural Laminar Flow (CRM-
NLF) configuration (AIAA Paper 2021-1430)

• significant underprediction of the laminar extent

• Increased stability due to compressibility not accounted for in existing mainstream models (empirical transition
correlations within these models were formulated for low-speed flows)

Big Picture:

Blend the “physics-based” methodology for transition modeling with the RANS-based approaches to create a robust, reliable, user-
friendly, and cost-efficient approach for integrated modeling of laminar-turbulent transition (CFD Vision 2030 goal)

Objectives

➢ Modify selected correlations within these transport-equation-based transition models to account for compressibility
effects

• Models should return to their original behavior at low subsonic speeds and remain valid for original cases used in
their calibration & validation

• SST-based Langtry-Menter model and SA-based Amplification factor transport equation model as implemented in
NASA’s OVERFLOW CFD solver

• Utilize previous works on linear stability analysis to achieve this

➢ Evaluate resulting improvements in transition modeling for transonic CRM-NLF configuration

• Use reference data based on experiment + transition predictions based on stability analysis)

➢ Extend previously calibrated, stability-based dual N-factor criterion* to predict the transition front for all angles of
attack from the experiment

• Also: PSE marching carried along streamlines as opposed to grid lines in previous work for two AOAs (Paredes et
al. AIAA Paper 2021-1431)

AIAA SciTech 2022

Summary

 Proposed simple modifications to baseline SST-2003-LM2009/2015 and SA-AFT2017b to account for stabilizing effect of compressibility 

• The models returns to original form at low speeds 

• Applicability limited to Me <1.2 (validity of isentropic approximation to determine edge Mach number)

• Substantial improvements in accuracy of predicting transition front for the transonic CRM-NLF configuration

 Stability analysis carried out with PSE marching along streamlines

• Comprehensive assessment of transition in CRM-NLF based on nonparallel disturbance amplification

• Stability-based dual N-factor criterion predicted transition fronts that matched well with those inferred from the CRM-NLF experiment 

for all the conditions studied

• Provided additional evaluation for assessing the modifications to the RANS-based transition models

Future Work
➢ Careful evaluation of the compressibility correction and dual N-factor criterion across wide set of problems

➢ More reliable estimate of edge Mach number (extends applicability of model to Me < 2)

➢ Improve some of the simplifications assumed while formulating compressibility corrections
